# Writing about art: annotation and analysis

> **In the exam:** There is no written paper. Your writing is evidence across **all four AOs** in both components (72 marks each). Annotation is how you show AO1 (critical understanding), AO2 (reviewing and refining), AO3 (recording insights) and sometimes AO4 (explaining your final intentions). Examiners read your sheets with the assessment grid, so concise, specific annotation can lift a band.

## What you need to know

- The AO3 grid refers to recording "ideas, observations and insights related to personal work and **the work of others** through **visual and other methods**" - writing is one of those "other methods".
- AO1 rewards "critical understanding of the context of own ideas and the sources" and "**analyse and make connections**".
- AO2 rewards "refine work, driven by **insights gained through exploration of ideas and reflection**" and "a process of experimentation **and review**".
- You must be able to analyse artwork using **content, form, process and mood** (CFPM), use the **formal elements**, and use correct vocabulary.
- Annotation must be **short, specific and useful**, written beside the work it describes, not a separate essay.

## Notes

### Why annotate?
Images show *what* you did. Annotation shows *why* you did it, *what you learned* and *what you will do next*. Examiners cannot see inside your head; annotation is how you earn the "reflection" and "insight" marks.

### The CFPM framework for analysing any artwork
| Letter | Question | Look at | Example vocabulary |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C**ontent | What is it of? What is it about? | Subject, symbols, title, narrative | portrait, symbol, narrative, theme |
| **F**orm | How is it put together visually? | Line, tone, colour, shape, texture, pattern, composition, scale | contrast, rhythm, balance, focal point, cropped, perspective |
| **P**rocess | How was it made? | Materials, techniques, tools | impasto, glaze, hatching, wet-on-wet, lino cut, layering |
| **M**ood | What does it make you feel and how? | Atmosphere, emotion, message | tense, calm, nostalgic, chaotic, melancholy |

**Best answers join the four together:** "The *dark, heavy shadows* (form) in Käthe Kollwitz's charcoal (process) create a *sombre, oppressive* mood (mood) that stresses the mother's grief (content)."

### The golden pattern: POINT → EVIDENCE → EXPLAIN → LINK
Use **P-E-E-L** for each annotation:
- **Point**: what you noticed or did.
- **Evidence**: where you can see it (use specific features).
- **Explain**: the effect or reason.
- **Link**: how it affects your own work or next step.

*Example (analysing Van Gogh's* The Starry Night, *1889):* "Van Gogh uses thick, swirling brushstrokes (point). The impasto paint catches the light and the curved marks lead the eye across the sky (evidence). This makes the night feel restless and alive, with the calm village below as contrast (explain). I will use directional brushwork in my own sky study to show movement (link)."

### Annotating your own work
For each experiment or study, aim for three short parts:
1. **What I did**: materials and technique ("Soft pastel over a watercolour wash.")
2. **What happened / what I notice**: honest evaluation ("The wash dried unevenly, giving a mottled texture that suits the rough bark.")
3. **What next**: decision ("I will keep the wash but use oil pastel for stronger highlights.")

Evidence of **decisions** is gold: "I rejected… because…", "I chose… to…".

### Sentence starters
| Purpose | Starters |
|---|---|
| Describe | The artist uses… · The composition is… · The dominant colour is… |
| Explain | This creates… · This suggests… · This draws the eye to… · This emphasises… |
| Compare | Whereas… · In contrast… · Both artists… but… |
| Evaluate | This was successful because… · It did not work as I hoped because… |
| Plan | Next I will… · To improve this I will… · This has influenced me to… |
| Link to theme | This relates to my theme of ___ because… |

### Level-up vocabulary (use accurately)
- **Line:** expressive, fluid, angular, contour, gestural, hatching, cross-hatching
- **Tone:** contrast, gradation, chiaroscuro, highlight, shadow, high-key/low-key
- **Colour:** complementary, analogous, saturated, muted, warm/cool, tint, shade
- **Texture:** impasto, smooth, rough, layered, tactile
- **Composition:** focal point, cropped, rule of thirds, symmetry, foreground/background, viewpoint, scale
- **Mood:** melancholy, tension, serenity, unease, nostalgia

### Practical layout tips
- Write next to the item described, not in a block at the bottom.
- Handwriting or typed; both are fine if it is **neat and readable** at A2 size.
- Use a different colour pen or a label box so that it is not lost.
- Refer to the artist's **name, title, date** the first time.
- Quality over quantity: 2–4 focused sentences per study often beat a paragraph.
- Spelling of artists' names and technical terms counts for clarity: check them.

### Example: analysing Guernica (Picasso, 1937)
**Content:** a response to the bombing of the Basque town of Guernica during the Spanish Civil War; a screaming horse, a bull, a mother holding a dead child, a fallen soldier. **Form:** large scale (about 3.5 × 7.8 m); a limited palette of black, white and greys; fragmented, angular shapes; a strong focal point of the lamp and the horse's head. **Process:** oil paint on canvas, influenced by Cubism. **Mood:** horror, chaos, grief; the lack of colour feels like newspaper reports. It hangs in the Museo Reina Sofía in Madrid, so you can see it first-hand.

## Key terms and definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Annotation | Written notes next to your work explaining what you did, why, and what you learned |
| Analysis | Examining how and why an artwork is made, and its effect |
| Content | The subject matter and meaning of a work |
| Form | The visual elements and how they are arranged |
| Process | The materials, techniques and tools used to make the work |
| Mood | The feeling or atmosphere created |
| Evaluate | Judge how successful something is, with reasons |
| Insight | A useful understanding gained from experimenting or researching |

## Worked examples

**1.** Annotate this experiment in 3 sentences: you tried a lino print of a plant on orange paper and the print looks flat. [3]

*Model:* "I printed black ink on orange paper to test a warm background, but the plant shapes look flat because I carved only outlines (1). Next time I will carve hatched lines inside the leaves to create tone (1) like in the prints of Käthe Kollwitz, to give more depth (1)."

**2.** Analyse the mood of a work you have studied using form. [4]

*Model:* In *The Starry Night* the swirling, curved brushstrokes (1) in blue and yellow complementary colours (1) create a sense of energy and anxiety (1) because the movement in the sky contrasts with the stillness of the village (1).

**3.** What is wrong with: "I like this because it looks good and it's really detailed"? Rewrite it. [3]

*Model:* It is vague: no evidence, no analysis, no link (1). Rewrite: "The fine hatching in the eye creates a realistic curved surface (1), which I will use to show depth in my portrait (1)."

## Common mistakes

- Describing only: "It is a painting of a woman" with no analysis of how it is made or its effect.
- Vague words: "nice", "good", "interesting", "detailed".
- Long paragraphs hidden at the bottom of the page, far from the work.
- Writing a biography instead of analysis.
- Saying everything worked: no evaluation or problems = no insight.
- Not saying what you will do next.
- Wrong facts: check artist names, dates and titles.
