# Developing and refining ideas (AO2)

> **In the exam:** There is no written paper. AO2 is marked in **both components**, 18 of the 72 marks each (12.5% of the qualification per component). Examiners read your three sheets of supporting studies and final outcome to see whether you **explored, experimented, reviewed and refined**, rather than settling on the first idea.

## What you need to know

- **AO2:** "Refine work by exploring ideas, selecting and experimenting with appropriate media, materials, techniques and processes."
- The grid judges: refining work **driven by insights gained through exploration of ideas and reflection**; exploring ideas through **experimentation and review**; and selecting and experimenting with media, materials, techniques and processes **appropriate to personal intentions**.
- The lowest band describes ideas that are "**consolidated too early** and not fully realised".
- The spec requires you to "develop and realise ideas and outcomes, exploring and reviewing possible solutions and modifying work appropriately".
- Preparatory work can be sketchbooks, design sheets, rough studies of different scales, samples, test pieces, maquettes and digital material.

## Notes

### The development cycle
**Explore → Experiment → Review → Refine → Repeat.**
1. **Explore** an idea from several angles (viewpoint, subject, meaning).
2. **Experiment** with materials, techniques and compositions.
3. **Review** each result honestly: what works, what doesn't, why.
4. **Refine**: select the strongest, change one thing, and try again.
5. **Repeat** until your idea is strong enough to realise.

Marks are for the **journey**, not just a good result. A failed experiment that you analyse and learn from is better evidence than a perfect first attempt with no development.

### Starting points: generating ideas
| Method | How |
|---|---|
| **Mind map** | Theme in the centre; branches for subjects, places, people, feelings, materials, artists |
| **Mood board** | Images, colours, textures that capture the atmosphere |
| **Brainstorm lists** | 20 possible subjects in 5 minutes; pick 3 |
| **Thumbnails** | 6–12 tiny compositions (2 min each) to test viewpoints and layouts |
| **Primary research** | Photos and drawings from life; visit a place |
| **Artist links** | Techniques from artists you have researched |

Move from **broad** (many ideas) to **focused** (your chosen idea).

### What to experiment with
| Area | Ideas to test |
|---|---|
| **Composition** | Viewpoint, crop, scale, symmetry, focal point, negative space |
| **Colour** | Limited palette vs full; warm/cool; complementary; monochrome |
| **Media** | Pencil, charcoal, ink, watercolour, acrylic, pastel, collage, print, digital edit |
| **Techniques** | Washes, layering, scraping, resist, hatching, stencil, mono-printing |
| **Combination** | Mixed media: e.g. ink over watercolour; printed paper in a collage |
| **Surface** | Paper type, coloured ground, tracing paper, fabric, card |
| **Scale** | Small rough vs A3 trial; maquette for 3D |
| **Process** | Digital: layers, filters, cropping; Textiles: samples of stitch, dye, fabrics |

**Rule:** change **one variable** at a time, so you know what caused the result.

### Review and refine: how to show it
- Place experiments **side by side** so that comparison is clear.
- Annotate with **decisions**: "I chose A over B because…"
- Mark the **next step** with an arrow or sticky label.
- Show **progress**: later pages should look more resolved than earlier ones.
- Link back to your **intentions** and **artists**: "Hockney's flat colour helps me simplify this."

### From sketch to final design
1. **Thumbnails** → choose 2–3.
2. **Larger composition studies** (A4/A3) → test colour and tone.
3. **Material tests** → on the same paper/surface as the final.
4. **Mock-up or maquette** → check layout and scale.
5. **Final plan** → a clear annotated sheet: composition, palette, materials, timing.
The final design should feel like the **natural result** of your decisions.

### Avoiding "consolidated too early"
Warning signs: only one composition; same technique on every page; no change between experiments; no rejected ideas. Fix it: do **three different** versions of your key image (different viewpoint, different medium, different colour mood), then select.

### Appropriate materials
"Appropriate to personal intentions" means the materials suit your **idea**: a fragile, nostalgic theme might suit soft watercolour and tracing paper; a bold protest theme might suit stencil and high-contrast print. Explain **why** you chose them.

### Endorsed titles: examples of development
- **Fine Art:** colour and composition trials, media tests, artist-based studies.
- **Graphic Communication:** typography and layout roughs, logo variations, colour palettes.
- **Photography:** contact-sheet selection, edits and crops, lighting tests.
- **Textile Design:** samples of fabric, stitch, print and dye; design sheets.
- **Three-dimensional Design:** maquettes and models, material tests, scale drawings.

## Key terms and definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Develop | Extend and improve ideas through exploration and investigation |
| Refine | Improve and make an idea more precise and resolved through review |
| Experiment | Try different materials, techniques or compositions to see what works |
| Review | Look critically at your work to judge its success |
| Thumbnail | A small, quick sketch used to plan or test a composition |
| Maquette | A small preliminary model for a 3D work |
| Composition | The arrangement of elements in an artwork |
| Intentions | What you are trying to express or achieve |
| Realise | Bring an idea to its final outcome |

*Model:* Present several media experiments side by side (1) with annotation explaining which worked and why (1). Show a refined second version that changes something based on that review (1) and explain the choice of materials in relation to the intended mood (1).

*Model:* The idea is "consolidated too early" (1), so there is no evidence of exploration or review (1). They should produce several thumbnails (1) and test colour/material options, then select and justify a design (1).

**3.** Annotate this: you tested ink wash and oil pastel on grey paper. [3]

*Model:* "Ink wash gave soft shadow but the pastel highlights looked bright and dramatic on grey paper (1). The pastel lines were too heavy over the wash (1), so next I will reduce pressure and use the wash for background only (1)."

## Common mistakes

- Settling on the first idea.
- Repeating the same technique across pages with no change.
- Changing too many things at once.
- No annotation, so the examiner cannot see your reasoning.
- Experiments that don't relate to the theme or artists.
- Throwing out "failed" tests; show them with comments.
- Final piece that looks unrelated to development work.

## Plan for this hour

- **10 min** – Read the notes; check your sketchbook for signs of "consolidated too early".
- **15 min** – Draw 8 thumbnails of one idea (2 min each, different viewpoints and crops).
- **20 min** – Choose 2; test two materials on each (small samples), changing one variable.
- **10 min** – Annotate each test: what worked, what didn't, next step.
- **5 min** – Write the decision you will take forward and why.
