# Exploring media and developing ideas (AO2)

> **In the exam:** There is no written paper. In Cambridge IGCSE Art & Design 0400 (Component 1 Coursework and Component 2 Externally Set Assignment, each 100 marks and 50%), **AO2 Explore** is "Explore and select appropriate resources, media, materials, techniques and processes" (25 marks). It works closely with **AO3 Develop** ("Develop ideas through investigation, demonstrating critical understanding", 25 marks). Examiners look for a visible journey from first ideas, through experiments, to a refined final outcome.

## What you need to know

- The syllabus says candidates "explore and experiment with a wide range of different media (including recycled materials), techniques and processes".
- You must "select, review and refine" work throughout the whole process to produce "a personal and resolved response".
- You must "evaluate their own work, review and edit" and "reflect, refine and adapt".
- Work in any areas of study: painting and related media, graphic communication, three-dimensional design, textiles and fashion, photography. You do not have to work in all of them.
- You can work **entirely in digital media, entirely non-digital, or both**, provided the assessment objectives are met.
- Final outcomes can be any size or medium, but everything is photographed or scanned into a **digital portfolio**.

## Notes

### 1. What the AO2 marks reward
The Cambridge grid describes AO2 in levels (out of 25).

| Level | Wording in the grid (shortened) | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 21–25 | Excellent exploration of media, materials, techniques and processes, effective selection of relevant resources | Many varied, purposeful tests; clear reasons for choices |
| 16–20 | Confident exploration, consistently selecting relevant resources | Several tests, mostly linked to the idea |
| 11–15 | Competent exploration | Some good tests, but limited reasoning |
| 6–10 | Satisfactory exploration, some selection | A few unlinked experiments |
| 1–5 | Limited exploration, basic selection | One or two materials only |

Key words: **explore** (try several things) and **select** (choose the best, with a reason).

### 2. Experiment with a purpose
A purposeful experiment answers a **question** about your idea.

| Question | Experiment |
|---|---|
| How can I show the texture of old walls? | Wax resist with ink wash; scraped acrylic; collage of torn paper |
| How can I make this portrait feel tense? | Charcoal with heavy contrast; ink with a loose brush; cut-paper shapes |
| How can I show movement? | Repeated lines; blurred long-exposure photo; smudged pastel |
| Which colour scheme feels calm? | Three small painted swatches: analogous blues, complementary orange/blue, monochrome |

Try **at least three** versions for each key decision. Keep **failed** tests; with a short note they prove your thinking.

### 3. Ways to experiment
- **Change the media:** pencil, charcoal, ink, pastel, watercolour, gouache, acrylic, oil pastel.
- **Change the surface:** coloured paper, cardboard, tracing paper, fabric, canvas, found or recycled materials.
- **Change the process:** drawing, printmaking (lino, monoprint, collagraph), collage, stitching, relief sculpture, clay or wire.
- **Change the scale:** thumbnail, A3, enlarged crop.
- **Change the composition:** zoom in, crop, rotate, shift the viewpoint.
- **Mix media:** ink over wax; collage plus paint; photo printed then drawn onto.
- **Use digital tools:** edit contrast and crop in Photoshop-type software, make a digital drawing on a tablet. Remember digital media is a tool and a process; formal elements still apply.

### 4. Developing ideas: from sketch to final design
1. **Starting point:** theme, mind map, collected images.
2. **Primary recording:** drawings and photographs (AO1).
3. **Research:** artists and designers (AO3).
4. **Experiments:** media and techniques (AO2).
5. **Idea development:** thumbnails, composition sketches, trials from different viewpoints.
6. **Review:** compare ideas and choose one, with reasons.
7. **Refinement:** improve the chosen design (a scaled drawing, a colour plan, a maquette).
8. **Final outcome:** realised piece (AO4).

Think of the final piece as the end of a **chain**. Each step should clearly lead to the next.

### 5. Idea development techniques
- **Thumbnail sketches:** at least 6, quick, 5 minutes each.
- **Combine ideas:** put the composition from sketch 2 with the colours of test 5.
- **Change one thing:** the same drawing in a different media, viewpoint or colour scheme.
- **Crop and zoom:** a viewfinder card to find the strongest area.
- **Cut and shift:** cut a print in strips and rearrange them.
- **Respond to artists:** take a method from your research and apply it to your own subject.
- **Maquettes (for 3D):** small models in card, clay, wire or foam to test form and structure.
- **Design sheets (graphic):** layout, lettering and colour trials.

### 6. Reviewing and selecting
After each stage, write a short **review** (see topic 8).
- "Which experiment best suited my idea, and why?"
- "What do I need to change?"
- "What will I do next?"

Example: "Test 2 (ink over wax) gave me the peeling texture I wanted. Test 1 (pencil) was too neat. I will use ink and wax for the background, and add collage for the figures."

Examiners reward a clear **decision**.

### 7. Planning the final piece
Before you make the final outcome, prepare:
- A **design drawing** at final size (or a grid scaled plan).
- A **colour plan** (swatches).
- A **materials list** and a **time plan**.
- A **risk check**: what could go wrong, and how will you fix it?

This is practice for the supervised test in topic 12.

### 8. Presenting your development
- **Layout:** group related tests together on a page; order the pages in the sequence they happened.
- **Label** each experiment briefly ("Test 3: acrylic, palette knife").
- **Use arrows or numbers** to show development.
- **Photograph or scan** each page evenly lit, in focus, with no shadows. Cambridge assesses the digital portfolio, so poor images lose marks.
- **Keep variety**: do not show ten versions of the same test.

## Key terms and definitions

- **Explore:** try out different resources, media, materials, techniques and processes to find what works for your ideas.
- **Experiment:** a trial made to see what a material or technique does.
- **Medium (media):** the material or tool used to make art, for example watercolour or charcoal.
- **Technique:** the way a material is used, such as glazing or hatching.
- **Process:** the stages of making, such as printing or constructing.
- **Develop:** to improve and extend an idea through investigation and testing.
- **Refine:** to improve a design by making careful changes.
- **Thumbnail sketch:** a small, quick drawing of an idea.
- **Maquette:** a small preliminary model of a sculpture or 3D design.
- **Resolved:** finished and well thought through, where the parts work together.
- **Review:** looking back at your work, judging it, and deciding what to do next.

## Worked examples

**Q1. A student's page shows five watercolour sketches of the same still life. What advice would help them to gain AO2 marks? [4]**

*Model answer:* "Experiment with other media such as ink, charcoal, oil pastel or collage, not only watercolour (1). Try different surfaces or scales (1). Annotate each trial to say what worked or failed (1). Select one and explain why it best suits the idea (1)."

**Q2. Explain how you would develop one drawing into a final design. [4]**

*Model answer:* "I would make thumbnail variations changing composition and viewpoint (1), test two or three media on small samples (1) and review them in a short note, choosing the strongest with a reason (1). Then I would make a scaled design with a colour plan for the final piece (1)."

**Q3. Give two reasons why a failed experiment can still earn marks. [2]**

*Model answer:* "It shows I explored a range of techniques (1) and, with a note on why it failed and what I changed, it shows reflection and refinement (1)."

## Common mistakes

- Using only one or two familiar materials.
- Experiments that are **not linked** to the idea.
- Going straight from first sketch to final piece with no development.
- Throwing away "failed" tests.
- No review: pages of work with no decisions visible.
- Presenting the final piece as something unrelated to the research.
- Poor photographs of work.
- Leaving the final plan until the day of the test.

## Plan for this hour

- **10 min:** Read the notes. Memorise the AO2 wording and the 8-step chain.
- **25 min:** Choose a small subject (one object). Make three media experiments (for example pencil, ink and wax, collage), each 6–8 minutes, with a one-line label.
- **10 min:** Make six thumbnails and circle the best one.
- **10 min:** Write a review paragraph: what worked, what didn't, what you chose.
- **5 min:** Do the quiz.
