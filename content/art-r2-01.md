# Mock prep: plan your final piece

> **Goal:** Turn your research and experiments into a clear plan for a final piece, so that in the two timed practical sessions you can spend your time making, not deciding. This mirrors the planning you will do for Component 2 (Externally Set Assignment), where the final outcome is made in a supervised test of 8 hours in total.

## Before you start

- **What to use:** the starting point for your mock. Best option: a real Cambridge IGCSE Art & Design 0400 Component 2 question paper from a recent series (your teacher can give you the early paper; past ESA papers are on the Cambridge school support hub). If your school has set its own mock theme, use that instead.
- **Have ready:** your sketchbook or portfolio pages so far (recording, artist research, experiments), a pencil, a ruler, plain paper for design drawings, coloured pencils or paint swatches, and your phone to photograph your plan.
- **Timer:** 60 minutes, split as in "How to do it".
- **Materials list in front of you:** what you actually own or can use at school. Do not plan for materials you cannot get.
- Check how long your school's mock practical is (sessions 2 and 3 together total your mock time). Use that to scale the plan. If your school gives a different total from 8 hours, divide the plan in proportion.

## Exam format reminder

- **Component 2, Externally Set Assignment:** 100 marks, 50% of the qualification. One starting point from the paper. **Supporting studies** are made in the preparation period; the **final outcome** is made in a **supervised test of 8 hours' total duration**. The supporting studies must be available during the test.
- Marked together against **AO1 Record, AO2 Explore, AO3 Develop, AO4 Present**: 25 marks each.
- **AO4 Present:** "Present a personal and coherent response that realises intentions and demonstrates an understanding of visual language."
- Work in any area of study; digital, non-digital or mixed. Everything is photographed or scanned into a digital portfolio.
- Component 2 must not repeat Component 1 work or theme.

## How to do it

| Time | What to do |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Read the starting point (or mock theme). Circle three key words. Write your **intention** in one sentence: "I want to show… using…" |
| 0:05–0:15 | Look back through your research and experiments. Choose **one** idea and **one** artist influence. Write down why (two lines). |
| 0:15–0:30 | **Design drawing:** draw the final composition at a smaller scale with a pencil box showing the same proportions as the real piece. Mark the **focal point**, the **light and dark areas** and the main shapes. |
| 0:30–0:38 | **Colour and materials plan:** paint or colour three small swatches. List every material you will need and tick the ones you have. Name your back-up material. |
| 0:38–0:50 | **Time plan:** divide your practical time into stages (set-up, blocking in, building up, refining, final checks) with minutes beside each. Write what you will have finished at the half-way point. |
| 0:50–0:57 | **Risk check:** write three things that could go wrong (colours drying too dark, running out of time, a new technique failing) and a fix for each. |
| 0:57–1:00 | Photograph the plan. Pack your materials and equipment so that the next session starts immediately. |

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Choose a starting point you can see and touch.** If you have first-hand drawings and photos of something, use it. Avoid a concept for which you have nothing recorded.
- **Plan around your strongest experiments.** Use media and techniques that you have already tested; the final piece should show that your experiments led somewhere (AO2/AO3).
- **Keep the idea simple.** A clear, well-made outcome beats an over-ambitious unfinished one (AO4: realises intentions).
- **Make the focal point obvious.** Decide where the viewer's eye will go first, and use contrast (light/dark, bright/dull colour, sharp/soft edges) to guide it.
- **Link back to your research.** Name the artist or movement whose technique you will use and where it will appear in the piece.
- **Don't change the idea mid-test.** If something is not working, adjust a detail; do not restart.
- **Avoid repeating Component 1.** Your mock should respond to its own starting point.
- **Plan for the photo.** Think how the piece will be photographed at the end: flat, evenly lit, in focus.

## Checklist while marking

Mark your plan yourself against the AOs (out of 5 each, quick judgement):
- **AO1 Record:** does the plan use my own drawings and photos as its base?
- **AO2 Explore:** does it use materials and techniques I have tested, and have I a back-up?
- **AO3 Develop:** is the idea clearly developed from research and artist study, and can I say why I chose it?
- **AO4 Present:** will the design realise my stated intention, with a clear focal point and coherent colour?
- Is the **time plan** realistic and in minutes, with a half-way checkpoint?
- Do I have **all materials** ready?
- Could a classmate understand my plan without me explaining it?

## Quick-fire revision

- AO1 Record: "Record ideas, observations and insights relevant to intentions as work progresses."
- AO2 Explore: "Explore and select appropriate resources, media, materials, techniques and processes."
- AO3 Develop: "Develop ideas through investigation, demonstrating critical understanding."
- AO4 Present: "Present a personal and coherent response that realises intentions and demonstrates an understanding of visual language."
- Each AO is 25 marks, 25% of each component; each component is 100 marks and 50%.
- Component 2: one starting point; supporting studies then a supervised test of 8 hours in total.
- Formal elements: line, tone, colour, texture, shape, form (also pattern, space).
- Focal point and contrast guide the eye.
- Annotation is optional, but if used must be relevant, purposeful and in English.
- Content, form, process, mood: the four-word way to analyse art.
- Tenebrism (Caravaggio) is dark shadow and spotlight; sfumato (Leonardo) is soft blending.
- Cubism (Picasso, Braque) shows several viewpoints; Pop Art (Warhol, Lichtenstein) uses popular culture.
- Work must be photographed evenly lit and in focus for the digital portfolio.
