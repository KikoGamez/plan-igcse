# Mock prep: plan your final piece

> **Goal:** Turn your research and experiments into a clear, annotated plan for your mock final piece (design, materials and a timing plan), so that you can spend the timed practical making, not deciding.

## Before you start

- **Material:** your sketchbook or three A2 supporting-study sheets; this year's or a previous Pearson **Externally-set Assignment** paper if your teacher has it (ask your teacher; the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Art and Design SAMs include an example assignment); your teacher's mock theme and rules.
- Have ready: a pencil, rubber, A3 paper for a final plan, ruler, the materials you intend to use, a phone camera for photographing your design, and a **timer**.
- Find out from your teacher: the **length of your mock timed practical** (the real exam is 10 hours over up to four sessions; mocks are often shorter), the paper size, and what you can bring.
- Choose an area of the exam: use the **same endorsed title** (e.g. Fine Art) as your portfolio.

## Exam format reminder

- **Component 2:** Externally-set Assignment, 72 marks, 50% of the qualification. A broad theme is released by Pearson on 2 January each year.
- Preparatory studies, then a **10-hour sustained focus period** under exam conditions, unaided, in up to four sessions within three consecutive weeks.
- Submit **three A2 sheets of supporting studies and one A2 sheet of final outcome(s)** (3D/large work is photographed).
- Marked with the assessment grid: **18 marks per AO** (AO1 to AO4), total 72.
- AO4: "Present a personal and meaningful response that realises intentions and demonstrates understanding of visual language."

## How to do it

1. **0–5 min** – Re-read the theme and your intention. Write one sentence: "My final piece will show ___ using ___ to create a ___ mood."
2. **5–20 min** – **Choose your design.** Lay out your thumbnails and composition studies; pick the strongest. Check it: focal point? contrast? links to my artist(s)? Can it be finished in the mock time?
3. **20–35 min** – **Draw a final design** on A3: lightly drawn composition with a few colour swatches, key tones and notes on materials. Photograph it.
4. **35–45 min** – **Materials and test.** List each material, paper type and size. Test any risky technique or colour mix on a scrap (5 minutes). Note any prep needed (stretching, priming, cutting stencils).
5. **45–55 min** – **Timing plan.** Split your mock time into stages (see the example). Write minutes next to each stage and a **fallback** for each (the simpler option if you run late).
6. **55–60 min** – **Checklist and pack.** Tick off the checklist below. Write one sentence on your plan's biggest risk.

### Example timing plan (scale to your mock length, e.g. 2 hours)
| Stage | Share of time | If a 10-hour piece |
|---|---|---|
| Set up and composition | 10% | 1 h |
| Base layers / underpainting | 20% | 2 h |
| Main development (focal point first) | 30% | 3 h |
| Refining detail and contrast | 20% | 2 h |
| Finishing | 10% | 1 h |
| Review and adjust | 10% | 1 h |

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Plan for the time you have.** An ambitious design that is unfinished scores lower for AO4 than a simpler design done well.
- Your outcome should be a **clear development of your preparatory work**, not a new idea.
- Show **intentions**: annotate your plan with what you want the viewer to feel and which elements create it.
- Show **visual language**: choose palette, tone and composition deliberately.
- Include a **fallback**: what you will drop or simplify if you are late.
- Do the **focal point first**; leave edges and background simplification for later.
- Prepare surfaces and tools beforehand; setup time eats the clock.

## Checklist while marking

- [ ] My design uses ideas from my research and experiments (AO1, AO2).
- [ ] It records or builds on my first-hand observations (AO3).
- [ ] It has a clear focal point, balanced composition and a controlled palette (AO4).
- [ ] The materials are appropriate to my intentions and I tested them.
- [ ] I have a timing plan with minutes for each stage and 10% for review.
- [ ] I have written my intention in one sentence.
- [ ] I have a fallback for the riskiest part.
- [ ] Everything I need is packed and labelled.

## Quick-fire revision

- AO1: develop ideas through investigations, demonstrating critical understanding of sources.
- AO2: refine work by exploring ideas, selecting and experimenting with appropriate media, materials, techniques and processes.
- AO3: record ideas, observations and insights relevant to intentions as work progresses.
- AO4: present a personal and meaningful response that realises intentions and demonstrates understanding of visual language.
- Each AO is 18 of the 72 marks in the component.
- Component 2: released 2 January; 10 hours sustained focus; up to four sessions in three weeks.
- Submit three A2 sheets of supporting studies and one A2 sheet of final outcome(s).
- Preparatory studies must stop when the 10 hours begin.
- Photograph 3D/large work from about four angles and mount on A2.
- Formal elements: line, tone, colour, texture, shape, form (and pattern, composition).
- CFPM: content, form, process, mood.
- Time plan rule: focal point first; 10% for review.
