# Mock: timed practical (2/2)

> **Goal:** Finish your final piece in this second timed session: develop the focal point, refine details, check it against your intention, and photograph it properly for the digital portfolio.

## Before you start

- **What to use:** your work from the previous session (timed practical 1/2), your final plan and your supporting studies. Re-read your three bullet notes from last time.
- **Materials:** the same list as before, plus tape or a clean backing sheet and a plain surface for photographing the finished work.
- **Timer:** one hour (about 50 minutes making, 10 minutes for review and photography).
- **Conditions:** same as before: work alone, silently and unaided, with no phone until the final photograph. In the real Cambridge IGCSE Art & Design 0400 Component 2 test, the final outcome is made during the supervised test of 8 hours in total, with the supporting studies available.
- Take a photo of the work at the start to compare with the end.

## Exam format reminder

- **Component 2, Externally Set Assignment:** 100 marks, 50%. Supporting studies plus final outcome are marked together.
- AO1 Record, AO2 Explore, AO3 Develop, AO4 Present: 25 marks each.
- **AO4 Present** is the focus today: "Present a personal and coherent response that realises intentions and demonstrates an understanding of visual language."
- The top descriptor for AO4 is "Excellent realisation of intentions, demonstrating effective understanding of visual language."
- Work is submitted as a digital portfolio, so photographing it well matters.

## How to do it

| Time | What to do |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Set up. Look at the work from a distance and read your notes. Choose the **three most important jobs** to finish. |
| 0:05–0:30 | **Develop the main areas:** complete the focal point first, then the supporting areas. Build colour, tone and texture. Keep checking the plan. |
| 0:30–0:42 | **Refine details:** sharpen edges, add highlights and darkest darks, correct proportions and balance the composition. |
| 0:42–0:50 | **Step back and evaluate.** Ask: Does it show my intention? Is the focal point clear? Do the colours work together? Make final adjustments. Stop adding detail when it is working. |
| 0:50–0:55 | **Finish and tidy.** Check the edges, sign it if suitable, and remove any marks or tape that should not be there. |
| 0:55–1:00 | **Photograph** the piece flat (or the best 3D views), evenly lit, in focus, with no shadows or glare. Take a detail shot of the best area. |

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Finish the focal point first.** If time runs out, the most important area should already be strong.
- **Stop in time.** Overworking can ruin a good piece. Leave 10 minutes for review and photographs.
- **Refine, don't restart.** Small, controlled corrections work better than big changes.
- **Strengthen contrast and unity.** Repeat a colour or texture across the piece so that it feels coherent.
- **Use visual language on purpose.** Line, tone, colour, texture, shape, form and composition should each have a job.
- **Be honest in your evaluation.** The best self-review names what worked and what you would change; this feeds the sketchbook review in the next session.
- **Photograph well.** Use daylight near a window, shoot square-on, avoid flash, and crop neatly. A dull photo hides a good piece.
- **Annotation is optional,** but a short note about your intention and what you would improve can help the examiner (relevant, purposeful, in English).

## Checklist while marking

Mark your own finished piece out of 5 for each (do it after a break):
- **AO1:** can you see the link back to my own drawings and photographs?
- **AO2:** are the media and techniques controlled and clearly chosen from my experiments?
- **AO3:** is there a visible link to research and development in my supporting studies?
- **AO4:** does it do what I said I would do? Is it personal, coherent and does it use visual language deliberately?
- Is the **focal point** strong?
- Are the **edges, details and finish** tidy?
- Is there a good **photograph** (flat, lit, sharp)?
- **Time:** did I finish on time? Where did I lose minutes?

## Quick-fire revision

- Each AO is worth 25 marks; Component 2 is 100 marks, 50% of the IGCSE.
- AO4 key words: personal, coherent, realises intentions, visual language.
- The supporting studies must be available during the test.
- Component 2 must not be the same as Component 1.
- Focal point plus contrast guides the eye.
- Stop adding detail when it works; step back often.
- Evaluate with: what did I intend, what happened, why, what next.
- Content, form, process, mood is the framework for describing work.
- Impressionism: broken brushstrokes, light and moment (Monet).
- Cubism: several viewpoints (Picasso, Braque).
- Surrealism: dream-like scenes (Dalí, Magritte).
- Photograph work flat, evenly lit, in focus; crop neatly.
