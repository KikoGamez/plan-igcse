# Characteristics of living organisms and cells

> **In the exam:** In Co-ordinated Sciences 0654, Biology questions appear alongside Chemistry and Physics in the same papers. **Paper 2** (Multiple Choice, Extended: 45 min, 40 four-option questions across all three sciences, 30 %) is likely to include items on the characteristics of living organisms, identifying cell structures or specialised cells, and a magnification calculation. **Paper 4** (Theory, Extended: 2 h, 120 marks, 50 %; in the 2025 specimen Questions 1–4 are Biology, about 40 marks) often uses a cell diagram, a micrograph or a magnification calculation as one part of a mixed-topic Biology question. **Paper 6** (Alternative to Practical: 1 h 30 min, 60 marks, 20 %, all three sciences) can ask you to draw a specimen, measure an image and calculate magnification or actual size.

## What you need to know

**B1.1 Characteristics of living organisms (Core)**
- Describe the characteristics of living organisms by defining: movement, respiration, sensitivity, growth, reproduction, excretion and nutrition.

**B2.1 Cell structure (Core)**
- Describe and compare the structure of a plant cell with an animal cell, limited to: cell wall, cell membrane, nucleus, cytoplasm, chloroplasts, ribosomes, mitochondria, vacuoles.
- Describe the structure of a bacterial cell, limited to: cell wall, cell membrane, cytoplasm, ribosomes, circular DNA, plasmids.
- Identify these cell structures in diagrams and images of plant, animal and bacterial cells.
- Describe the functions of these structures in plant, animal and bacterial cells.
- State that new cells are produced by division of existing cells.
- State that specialised cells have specific functions, limited to: ciliated cells, root hair cells, palisade mesophyll cells, neurones, red blood cells, sperm and egg cells (gametes).
- Describe the meaning of the terms: cell, tissue, organ, organ system and organism as illustrated by examples given in the syllabus.

**B2.2 Size of specimens**
- State and use the formula: magnification = image size ÷ actual size (Core).
- Calculate magnification and size of biological specimens using millimetres as units (Core).
- Convert measurements between millimetres (mm) and micrometres (µm) (Supplement).

## Notes

*Note: classification (keys, kingdoms, vertebrate and arthropod groups, binomial names) is **not** in the 0654 syllabus. This session covers B1 and B2 only. Viruses are covered with Diseases and immunity (B10); the definition of a species is in Reproduction (B15).*

### Characteristics of living organisms (MRS GREN)

| Characteristic | Syllabus definition |
|---|---|
| Movement | an action by an organism or part of an organism causing a change of position or place |
| Respiration | the chemical reactions in cells that break down nutrient molecules and release energy for metabolism |
| Sensitivity | the ability to detect and respond to changes in the internal or external environment |
| Growth | a permanent increase in size and dry mass |
| Reproduction | the processes that make more of the same kind of organism |
| Excretion | the removal of waste products of metabolism and substances in excess of requirements |
| Nutrition | the taking in of materials for energy, growth and development |

Note "dry mass" in growth and "release energy" (never "produce/make energy") in respiration. A Paper 2 item may give a list of features and ask which shows that something is alive: it must show **all seven** characteristics (a car moves and "uses fuel", but does not grow or reproduce).

### Cell structure

| Part | Animal | Plant | Bacterium | Function |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cell membrane | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | controls what enters and leaves the cell |
| Cytoplasm | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | where most chemical reactions take place |
| Nucleus | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ | contains the genetic material (chromosomes / DNA) that controls the cell's activities |
| Ribosomes | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | protein synthesis |
| Mitochondria | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ | aerobic respiration (releases energy) |
| Cell wall | ✗ | ✓ (cellulose) | ✓ (not cellulose) | strength, keeps the shape of the cell, stops it bursting |
| Chloroplasts | ✗ | ✓ (some cells) | ✗ | photosynthesis (contain chlorophyll) |
| Vacuole | small, temporary if present | large, permanent, contains cell sap | ✗ | stores cell sap; keeps the plant cell turgid |
| Circular DNA | ✗ | ✗ | ✓ | genetic material controlling the cell |
| Plasmids | ✗ | ✗ | ✓ | small rings of DNA (extra genes) |

- Plant cells **do** have mitochondria. Root cells have **no chloroplasts** (no light underground).
- **New cells are produced by division of existing cells.**

How to recognise cells in images:
- Regular, box-like shape, thick outer line, green discs → **plant cell**.
- Irregular shape, no wall, central nucleus → **animal cell**.
- Very small, no nucleus, loop of DNA and small rings → **bacterial cell**.

### Levels of organisation

| Term | Meaning | Examples from the syllabus |
|---|---|---|
| Cell | the basic unit of living organisms | red blood cell, root hair cell |
| Tissue | a group of similar cells working together to perform a shared function | xylem, phloem, palisade mesophyll, muscle |
| Organ | a structure made of a group of tissues working together to perform specific functions | heart, leaf, stomach, lung |
| Organ system | a group of organs with related functions working together | digestive system, circulatory system |
| Organism | a living thing made of organ systems working together (or a single cell) | human, flowering plant, bacterium |

### Specialised cells

| Cell | Function (syllabus) | How it is suited to its function |
|---|---|---|
| Ciliated cell | movement of mucus in the trachea and bronchi | cilia on the surface beat to sweep mucus (with trapped dust and pathogens) upwards |
| Root hair cell | absorption (water and mineral ions) | long extension gives a large surface area |
| Palisade mesophyll cell | photosynthesis | many chloroplasts; near the upper surface of the leaf |
| Neurone | conduction of electrical impulses | long fibre carries impulses over long distances |
| Red blood cell | transport of oxygen | contains haemoglobin; no nucleus; biconcave shape |
| Sperm and egg cells (gametes) | reproduction | sperm can swim (flagellum); egg has energy stores (details in Reproduction) |

### Magnification

**magnification = image size ÷ actual size** (no units — write "×", e.g. ×400).

- Rearranged: **actual size = image size ÷ magnification**; image size = actual size × magnification.
- Core: work in **mm**. Measure the image with a ruler in mm, edge to edge along the line shown.
- Supplement: **1 mm = 1000 µm**. mm → µm: × 1000. µm → mm: ÷ 1000. Both sizes must be in the **same unit** before dividing.

Formula triangle check: if magnification is large, the actual size must be much smaller than the image.

### Paper 6 skills on this topic

- **Biological drawings**: sharp pencil; single clear continuous lines; no shading; drawing larger than the specimen and using most of the space; correct proportions; label lines drawn with a ruler that touch the structure and do not cross.
- **Measuring** a line on a photograph or on your drawing to the nearest mm, then calculating: magnification of drawing = length on drawing ÷ real length (same units).
- Calculating **actual size** from an image and a stated magnification.
- Observing familiar and unfamiliar specimens and recording features you can see (e.g. a stained onion epidermis or cheek cell image). Stains such as iodine can mark skin and eyes — wear eye protection.

## Worked examples

**1.** A student draws a cheek cell. The drawing is 45 mm across. The real cell is 15 µm across. Calculate the magnification of the drawing. Show your working. [2]

- Convert: 45 mm = 45 000 µm (or 15 µm = 0.015 mm) (1)
- 45 000 ÷ 15 = **×3000** (1)

**2.** A photomicrograph has magnification ×400. A chloroplast in it measures 2 mm. Calculate the actual length in µm. [2]

- actual = 2 ÷ 400 = 0.005 mm (1)
- 0.005 × 1000 = **5 µm** (1)

**3.** State **three** differences between the structure of a bacterial cell and a plant cell. [3]

Any three (1 each):
- bacterium has no nucleus / plant cell has a nucleus
- bacterium has circular DNA (and plasmids); plant has DNA in chromosomes in the nucleus
- bacterial cell wall is not made of cellulose
- bacterium has no chloroplasts / no mitochondria / no large permanent vacuole

**4.** Describe how a red blood cell is adapted to its function. [3]

- contains haemoglobin to carry oxygen (1)
- no nucleus, so more room for haemoglobin (1)
- biconcave shape, giving a large surface area for diffusion of oxygen (1)

## Common mistakes

- Writing "respiration makes energy" — energy is **released**, never made.
- Defining growth as "getting bigger" — you must say **permanent** increase in **size and dry mass**.
- Forgetting to convert units before calculating magnification, or giving magnification a unit (it has none).
- Saying bacteria have a nucleus or mitochondria, or that plant cells have no mitochondria (they do).
- Confusing tissue and organ: xylem and palisade mesophyll are tissues; the heart and the leaf are organs.
- Labelling the cell membrane of a plant cell as the cell wall — in diagrams the membrane is the thin line just inside the wall.
- Drawing with shading, double lines or label lines with arrowheads in Paper 6.

## Practice questions

1. State **three** structures found in a plant cell but not in an animal cell. [3]
2. A red blood cell is 8 µm in diameter. In a textbook diagram it is 24 mm across. Calculate the magnification. [2]
3. State the function of the ribosomes and of the mitochondria. [2]
4. A leaf is an organ. Explain what this means. [2]
5. A drawing of a seed is 60 mm long. The real seed is 12 mm long. Calculate the magnification of the drawing. [1]
6. A bacterium is shown at ×20 000. On the image it measures 30 mm. Calculate its actual length in µm. [2]
7. Describe how a root hair cell is adapted for absorption. [2]
8. Which characteristic of living organisms is defined as "the taking in of materials for energy, growth and development"? [1]

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Cell wall; chloroplasts; large permanent vacuole (containing cell sap).
2. 24 mm = 24 000 µm (1); 24 000 ÷ 8 = ×3000 (1).
3. Ribosomes: protein synthesis (1); mitochondria: aerobic respiration / release energy (1).
4. Made of a group of (different) tissues (1); working together to perform specific functions (e.g. photosynthesis) (1).
5. 60 ÷ 12 = ×5.
6. 30 ÷ 20 000 = 0.0015 mm (1); × 1000 = 1.5 µm (1).
7. Long extension / hair gives a large surface area (1); for uptake of water and mineral ions (1).
8. Nutrition.
