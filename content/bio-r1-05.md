## Notes

### Balanced diet

A **balanced diet** contains all the nutrients (carbohydrates, fats and oils, proteins, vitamins, mineral ions, fibre and water) in the correct proportions, and provides the right amount of energy for a person's needs.

| Nutrient | Principal sources | Importance |
|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrates | bread, rice, pasta, potatoes | main source of energy (for respiration) |
| Fats and oils | butter, vegetable oil, nuts | energy store; insulation; part of cell membranes |
| Proteins | meat, fish, eggs, beans, lentils | growth and repair of cells; making enzymes, antibodies |
| Vitamin C | citrus fruit, fresh vegetables | healthy skin and gums; lack causes **scurvy** (bleeding gums, poor wound healing) |
| Vitamin D | oily fish, egg yolk; made in skin in sunlight | helps absorption of calcium for bones and teeth; lack causes **rickets** (soft, weak, bent bones) |
| Calcium | milk, cheese | strong bones and teeth; lack causes **rickets** |
| Iron | red meat, liver, spinach | making haemoglobin in red blood cells (to carry oxygen) |
| Fibre (roughage) | wholemeal bread, vegetables, fruit | adds bulk so food moves easily through the gut (peristalsis); prevents constipation |
| Water | drinks, food | solvent for chemical reactions; transport in blood; removing waste; cooling by sweating |

**Causes of deficiency diseases**: scurvy — lack of vitamin C; rickets — lack of vitamin D or calcium.

### The digestive system

Alimentary canal: mouth → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine (**duodenum**, then **ileum**) → large intestine (**colon**, **rectum**, **anus**). Associated organs: **salivary glands**, **pancreas**, **liver**, **gall bladder**.

| Organ | Main function(s) |
|---|---|
| Mouth | ingestion; chewing (physical digestion); saliva contains amylase |
| Salivary glands | secrete saliva containing amylase |
| Oesophagus | carries food from the mouth to the stomach |
| Stomach | churns food (physical digestion); secretes gastric juice with protease and hydrochloric acid |
| Pancreas | secretes amylase, protease and lipase into the duodenum |
| Liver | makes bile; assimilation (nutrients used by cells, e.g. to make proteins) |
| Gall bladder | stores bile and releases it into the duodenum |
| Small intestine (duodenum, ileum) | chemical digestion is completed; **absorption** of nutrients into the blood (mainly the ileum) |
| Large intestine (colon) | absorbs water |
| Rectum | stores faeces |
| Anus | egestion of faeces |

### The five processes (syllabus wording)

- **Ingestion** – the taking of substances, e.g. food and drink, into the body.
- **Digestion** – the breakdown of food.
- **Absorption** – the movement of nutrients from the intestines into the blood.
- **Assimilation** – uptake and use of nutrients by cells.
- **Egestion** – the removal of undigested food from the body as faeces.

### Physical and chemical digestion

- **Physical digestion**: the breakdown of food into smaller pieces without chemical change to the food molecules (chewing in the mouth, churning in the stomach). It **increases the surface area** of food for enzymes.
- **Chemical digestion**: the breakdown of large insoluble molecules into small soluble molecules, by enzymes. Only small soluble molecules can be **absorbed** into the blood.

### Enzymes in digestion (Supplement)

| Enzyme | Secreted by | Acts in | Substrate → products |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amylase | salivary glands; pancreas | mouth; small intestine (duodenum) | starch → simple reducing sugars |
| Protease | stomach; pancreas | stomach; small intestine (duodenum) | protein → amino acids |
| Lipase | pancreas | small intestine (duodenum) | fats and oils → fatty acids and glycerol |

### Hydrochloric acid and bile (Supplement)

**Hydrochloric acid** in gastric juice:
1. kills harmful microorganisms in food;
2. provides an acidic pH for the optimum activity of the **proteases in the stomach**.

**Bile** — made in the liver, stored in the gall bladder, released into the duodenum:
1. it is **alkaline** and **neutralises** the acidic mixture of food and gastric juice entering the duodenum from the stomach, giving a suitable pH for the enzymes in the small intestine (which work best at a neutral to slightly alkaline pH);
2. it **emulsifies** fats and oils — breaks large droplets into many small droplets — increasing the **surface area** for lipase (chemical digestion).

Emulsification is **not** chemical digestion: no bonds in the fat molecules are broken. Bile contains no enzymes.

## Worked examples

**1.** Explain how bile helps the digestion of fats in the small intestine. [4]

- bile emulsifies fats / oils (1)
- breaks large droplets into small droplets (1)
- increases surface area for lipase (1)
- bile is alkaline, neutralises stomach acid, giving a suitable pH for lipase (1)

**2.** Describe the functions of hydrochloric acid in the stomach. [2]

- kills harmful microorganisms in food (1)
- provides an acidic pH for optimum activity of proteases (in the stomach) (1)

**3.** Explain why physical digestion speeds up chemical digestion. [2]

- smaller pieces of food have a larger surface area (1)
- more area for enzymes to act on (1)

**4.** A teenager eats very little fresh fruit or vegetables and rarely goes outside. Suggest **two** deficiency diseases they may develop and give the cause of each. [4]

- scurvy (1); lack of vitamin C (1)
- rickets (1); lack of vitamin D (made in skin in sunlight) / calcium (1)

## Common mistakes

- Defining absorption as "food going into the body" — it is nutrients moving from the **intestines into the blood**.
- Confusing **egestion** (removal of undigested food as faeces) with **excretion** (removal of waste products of metabolism).
- Saying bile is an enzyme or that it "digests" fat — bile **emulsifies**; lipase digests.
- Saying bile is made in the gall bladder — it is made in the **liver** and stored in the gall bladder.
- Writing that the acid in the stomach "digests food" — it kills microorganisms and gives the optimum pH for protease.
- Forgetting that protease acts in **two** places (stomach and small intestine) and amylase in two places (mouth and small intestine).
- Writing "lipase breaks down fat into fatty acids" without **glycerol**.
