# Human nutrition

> **In the exam:** Paper 2 has 3–5 questions (organ labelling, which enzyme acts where, deficiency diseases). Paper 4 often has a full question (8–14 marks) on the alimentary canal, enzymes, bile or villi, sometimes linked to cholera. Paper 6 links here through food tests and enzyme experiments (see the topics on biological molecules and enzymes).

## What you need to know

- Describe a balanced diet; list the principal sources and the importance of carbohydrates, fats, proteins, vitamins C and D, mineral ions calcium and iron, fibre (roughage) and water.
- Explain the causes of scurvy and rickets (deficiency of vitamin C, and vitamin D / calcium).
- Identify the main organs of the digestive system and describe their functions.
- Define ingestion, digestion (mechanical and chemical), absorption, assimilation, egestion.
- Identify the types of human teeth and describe their functions in mechanical digestion.
- Describe chemical digestion: amylase, protease, lipase — where they are made, where they act, substrates and products.
- Describe the functions of hydrochloric acid in the stomach and of bile.
- Extended: maltase; pepsin and trypsin; bile emulsifies fats; explain the significance of villi and microvilli; describe the structure of a villus (capillaries and lacteal).
- Extended: describe how cholera causes diarrhoea and explain the use of oral rehydration therapy.
- State that the small intestine is where nutrients and most water are absorbed.

## Notes

### Balanced diet

| Nutrient | Good sources | Importance |
|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrates | bread, rice, pasta, potatoes | energy |
| Fats and oils | butter, oil, nuts | energy store, insulation, cell membranes |
| Proteins | meat, fish, eggs, beans | growth and repair; enzymes, antibodies |
| Vitamin C | citrus fruit, fresh vegetables | needed for healthy skin and gums (collagen); deficiency → **scurvy** (bleeding gums) |
| Vitamin D | oily fish, egg yolk; made in skin in sunlight | absorption of calcium for bones and teeth; deficiency → **rickets** (soft, bent bones) |
| Calcium | milk, cheese | bones and teeth; blood clotting; deficiency → rickets |
| Iron | red meat, liver, spinach | making haemoglobin; deficiency → anaemia (tiredness) |
| Fibre (roughage) | wholemeal bread, vegetables, fruit | adds bulk so food moves along by peristalsis; prevents constipation |
| Water | drinks, food | solvent, transport, removing waste, temperature control |

A **balanced diet** contains all nutrients in the right proportions for a person's age, sex and activity level.

### The digestive system

Mouth → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine (**duodenum**, then **ileum**) → large intestine (**colon**, **rectum**) → anus. Associated organs: **salivary glands**, **pancreas**, **liver**, **gall bladder**.

| Organ | Function |
|---|---|
| Mouth | ingestion; teeth chew (mechanical digestion); salivary amylase starts starch digestion |
| Oesophagus | carries food to the stomach by peristalsis |
| Stomach | churns food (mechanical); protease (pepsin) digests protein; hydrochloric acid |
| Pancreas | makes amylase, protease (trypsin) and lipase, secreted into the duodenum |
| Liver | makes bile; also assimilation (e.g. amino acids → proteins), deamination |
| Gall bladder | stores bile |
| Small intestine | completes digestion; **absorbs nutrients and most water** |
| Large intestine (colon) | absorbs some more water |
| Rectum / anus | stores faeces / egestion |

### Key processes

- **Ingestion**: the taking of substances, e.g. food and drink, into the body.
- **Digestion**: the breakdown of food.
  - **Mechanical digestion**: the breakdown of food into smaller pieces without chemical change to the food molecules (teeth, churning in the stomach). It increases the surface area for enzymes.
  - **Chemical digestion**: the breakdown of large insoluble molecules into small soluble molecules (by enzymes), so they can be absorbed.
- **Absorption**: the movement of nutrients from the intestines into the blood.
- **Assimilation**: uptake and use of nutrients by cells.
- **Egestion**: the removal of undigested food from the body as faeces.

### Teeth

- **Incisors**: chisel-shaped, cutting/biting.
- **Canines**: pointed, gripping and tearing.
- **Premolars and molars**: broad, ridged, chewing and grinding.
- Structure: enamel (hard outer layer), dentine, pulp cavity with nerves and blood vessels, cement holding the root in the jaw bone, gum.

### Chemical digestion

| Enzyme | Made in | Acts in | Substrate → product |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amylase | salivary glands, pancreas | mouth, small intestine | starch → simple reducing sugars (maltose) |
| Maltase (Extended) | membranes of epithelium of small intestine | small intestine | maltose → glucose |
| Protease: pepsin (Extended) | stomach | stomach | protein → amino acids (shorter chains) |
| Protease: trypsin (Extended) | pancreas | small intestine | protein → amino acids |
| Lipase | pancreas | small intestine | fats and oils → fatty acids and glycerol |

**Hydrochloric acid** in the stomach: kills harmful microorganisms in food; (Extended) gives the acid pH that is the optimum for pepsin.

**Bile**: made in the liver, stored in the gall bladder, released into the duodenum.
- It is **alkaline** and **neutralises** the acidic mixture leaving the stomach, giving a suitable pH for enzymes in the small intestine.
- Extended: it **emulsifies** fats and oils, breaking large droplets into small droplets, increasing the **surface area** for the action of lipase. This is not chemical digestion — no bonds broken.

### Absorption: villi (Extended)

- The small intestine is long and its lining is folded into **villi**; the cells on villi have **microvilli**. Both give a very **large surface area** for absorption.
- Each villus has:
  - a wall one cell thick (short diffusion distance);
  - a network of **capillaries** — absorb glucose, amino acids, water, ions; blood goes to the liver via the hepatic portal vein;
  - a **lacteal** (lymph vessel) — absorbs fatty acids and glycerol;
  - many mitochondria in epithelial cells for active transport.

### Cholera (Extended)

1. The cholera **bacterium** produces a **toxin**.
2. The toxin causes secretion of **chloride ions** into the small intestine.
3. This lowers the water potential of the gut contents, so **water moves out of the blood/cells into the gut by osmosis**.
4. Result: severe **diarrhoea**, loss of water and ions from the blood → dehydration.
- **Oral rehydration therapy**: a drink of water containing salts (ions) and glucose, which replaces lost water and ions.
- Cholera spreads through water contaminated with faeces — prevented by clean water supplies and sewage treatment.

## Key terms and definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Ingestion | the taking of substances, e.g. food and drink, into the body |

## Worked examples

**1.** Extended: explain how bile helps the digestion of fats. [3]

- bile emulsifies fats (1)
- breaks large drops into small droplets (1)
- larger surface area for lipase to act on (1)
- (also credited) neutralises stomach acid giving optimum pH for lipase

**2.** State the enzyme and the products of protein digestion in the stomach, and state why the stomach contains acid. [3]

- protease / pepsin (1)
- amino acids (1)
- kills (harmful) microorganisms / gives optimum pH for pepsin (1)

**3.** Extended: explain why a person with cholera becomes dehydrated. [4]

- bacterium releases a toxin (1)
- causes chloride ions to be secreted into the small intestine (1)
- water moves out of blood / cells into the intestine by osmosis (1)
- lost as diarrhoea (1)

## Common mistakes

- Defining absorption as "food going into the body" — it is nutrients moving from the intestines **into the blood**.
- Confusing **egestion** (faeces) with **excretion** (metabolic waste).
- Saying bile is an enzyme or that it "digests" fat — it **emulsifies**.
- Saying bile is made in the gall bladder — made in the **liver**, stored in the gall bladder.
- Saying "amylase breaks down starch into glucose" — salivary/pancreatic amylase gives **maltose**; maltase gives glucose.
- Forgetting the lacteal absorbs fatty acids and glycerol.

## Practice questions

2. Name the organ that stores bile and the organ that makes it. [2]
3. Describe the role of fibre in the diet. [2]
4. Copy and complete: lipase breaks down ______ into ______ and ______. [2]
5. Extended: explain **three** ways in which a villus is adapted for absorption. [3]
6. Explain why mechanical digestion makes chemical digestion faster. [2]
7. Extended: suggest why oral rehydration solution contains salts and glucose, not just water. [2]

1. Lack of vitamin C (1); bleeding gums / poor wound healing (1).

4. Fats (and oils) (1); fatty acids and glycerol (1).
5. Any three: microvilli give large surface area; wall one cell thick / short diffusion distance; capillary network takes absorbed nutrients away maintaining concentration gradient; lacteal absorbs fatty acids and glycerol; mitochondria for active transport.
6. Smaller pieces have a larger surface area (1) for enzymes to act on (1).
7. Replace ions lost in diarrhoea (1); glucose provides energy / helps absorption of ions and water (1).
