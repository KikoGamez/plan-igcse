# Transport in animals

> **In the exam:** Paper 2 has 3–5 questions (heart diagram, vessel structure, blood components). Paper 4 almost always includes heart or blood (8–14 marks): labelling the heart, explaining valves, coronary heart disease, double circulation, vessel adaptations. Paper 6 may ask you to plan an investigation into the effect of exercise on pulse rate, or to plot heart-rate data.

## What you need to know

- Describe the circulatory system as heart, blood vessels and valves; describe the single circulation of a fish and the double circulation of a mammal; Extended: explain the advantages of a double circulation.
- Identify the structures of the mammalian heart; state that blood is pumped away from the heart in arteries and returns in veins.
- Describe how the heart is monitored (ECG, pulse rate, listening to valves) and the effect of physical activity on heart rate; Extended: explain the effect.
- Describe coronary heart disease (CHD) and its risk factors; Extended: discuss prevention.
- Extended: explain the relative thickness of the walls of the chambers; explain how the heart works (contraction of muscles, valves).
- Describe the structure of arteries, veins and capillaries and explain how they are adapted to their functions.
- Identify the main blood vessels of the heart, lungs, (Extended) liver and kidneys.
- List the components of blood and their functions; identify red and white blood cells (phagocytes and lymphocytes) from images.
- State the roles of blood clotting; Extended: describe the process (fibrinogen to fibrin).

## Notes

### Single and double circulation

- **Fish**: single circulation — heart → gills → body → heart (blood passes through the heart **once** per complete circuit).
- **Mammals**: double circulation — blood passes through the heart **twice** per circuit: **pulmonary** circulation (heart ↔ lungs) and **systemic** circulation (heart ↔ body).
- Extended advantages: blood returns to the heart after the lungs, so it can be pumped at **high pressure** to the body → faster delivery of oxygen and glucose; **lower pressure** in the lungs avoids damage to the delicate capillaries; oxygenated and deoxygenated blood are kept separate.

### The heart

Order of blood flow: **vena cava → right atrium → (atrioventricular valve) → right ventricle → (semilunar valve) → pulmonary artery → lungs → pulmonary vein → left atrium → (atrioventricular valve) → left ventricle → (semilunar valve) → aorta → body.**

- **Septum**: separates the left and right sides — stops oxygenated and deoxygenated blood mixing.
- **Valves**: prevent backflow.
  - **Atrioventricular valves** (between atria and ventricles).
  - **Semilunar valves** (at the base of the aorta and pulmonary artery).
- **Coronary arteries** supply the heart muscle itself with oxygen and glucose.
- The diagram shows the heart as if facing you: the **left side is on the right** of the diagram.

Extended:
- **Walls**: atria thin (pump only to ventricles); ventricles thicker; **left ventricle thickest** — pumps blood all round the body at high pressure; right ventricle pumps only to the nearby lungs.
- **How it works**: atria contract, pushing blood into ventricles. Ventricles contract → pressure rises → atrioventricular valves close (stop backflow into atria) → semilunar valves open → blood leaves through arteries. Ventricles relax → semilunar valves close (stop backflow into ventricles).

### Monitoring and exercise

- Monitor by: **ECG**, **pulse rate**, listening to the **sounds of valves closing** with a stethoscope.
- Physical activity **increases heart rate**.
- Extended explanation: muscles respire faster → need more **oxygen and glucose** delivered and more **CO₂ removed** (and lactic acid when anaerobic). Heart beats faster (and more strongly) to increase blood flow.

### Coronary heart disease (CHD)

- **Coronary arteries** become blocked by fatty deposits (and cholesterol); a blood clot may form.
- Heart muscle gets less oxygen and glucose → cannot respire aerobically → may die → **heart attack**.
- **Risk factors**: diet high in saturated fat; lack of exercise; stress; smoking; genetic predisposition; age; sex (more common in males).
- Extended prevention: diet low in saturated fat and salt; regular exercise; not smoking; reduce stress. (Treatments: aspirin to reduce clotting, stents, by-pass surgery — only if a question gives them.)

### Blood vessels

| Vessel | Function | Structure → adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| Artery | carries blood **away** from heart at **high pressure** | thick muscular and elastic wall withstands pressure, elastic recoil evens out flow; narrow lumen keeps pressure high |
| Vein | carries blood **back** to heart at **low pressure** | thin wall, wide lumen (less resistance); **valves** prevent backflow |
| Capillary | exchange of substances with tissues | wall **one cell thick** (short diffusion distance); narrow; many, so large surface area; leaky |

Extended: **arterioles** and **venules** link arteries/capillaries and capillaries/veins; capillaries make a network close to every cell.

Main vessels:

| Organ | In (to the organ) | Out (from the organ) |
|---|---|---|
| Heart | vena cava, pulmonary vein | aorta, pulmonary artery |
| Lungs | pulmonary artery | pulmonary vein |
| Kidneys (Extended) | renal artery | renal vein |
| Liver (Extended) | hepatic artery; **hepatic portal vein** (from the gut) | hepatic vein |

Note: the pulmonary artery carries **deoxygenated** blood; the pulmonary vein carries **oxygenated** blood.

### Blood

| Component | Function |
|---|---|
| Red blood cells | transport oxygen (haemoglobin) |
| White blood cells: **phagocytes** | engulf and digest pathogens (**phagocytosis**); lobed nucleus |
| White blood cells: **lymphocytes** | produce **antibodies**; large round nucleus filling most of the cell |
| Platelets | blood clotting (cell fragments) |
| Plasma | transports blood cells, ions, nutrients (glucose, amino acids), urea, hormones, carbon dioxide |

- **Clotting**: prevents blood loss and the entry of pathogens.
- Extended: soluble **fibrinogen** is converted to insoluble **fibrin**, which forms a mesh of fibres that traps red blood cells, forming a clot.

### Paper 6 skills on this topic

- **Exercise and pulse rate**: independent variable — intensity/duration of exercise (e.g. step-ups per minute); dependent — pulse (beats per minute) measured at the wrist for 15 s × 4 or for 60 s.
- Controlled: same person (or same age/fitness/sex), same type of exercise, same duration, rest until resting pulse returns before each trial; repeat and calculate mean.
- Risk: stop if dizzy; check participants have no health conditions.
- Table: time after exercise / min | pulse rate / beats per min (trial 1, 2, 3, mean).
- Recovery time = time to return to resting rate — fitter people recover faster.
- Heart dissection (if done): sharp scalpel, wash hands, gloves.

## Key terms and definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Double circulation | blood passes through the heart twice for each complete circuit of the body |
| Phagocytosis | engulfing and digestion of pathogens by phagocytes |
| Coronary heart disease | blockage of the coronary arteries (by fatty deposits) reducing the supply of oxygen to heart muscle |
| Artery | vessel carrying blood away from the heart at high pressure |
| Vein | vessel carrying blood back to the heart at low pressure |

## Worked examples

**1.** Explain why the wall of the left ventricle is thicker than the wall of the right ventricle. [3]

- left ventricle pumps blood to the whole body / further (1)
- needs higher pressure (1)
- more muscle to contract with greater force; right only pumps to the lungs (1)

**2.** Describe the role of the valves in the heart when the ventricles contract. [3]

- atrioventricular valves close (1)
- preventing backflow of blood into the atria (1)
- semilunar valves open so blood flows into the aorta and pulmonary artery (1)

**3.** A student's pulse was 18 beats in 15 seconds at rest and 39 beats in 15 seconds after exercise. Calculate the percentage increase in heart rate. [3]

- rates: 72 and 156 beats per minute (1)
- increase = 84 (1)
- 84 ÷ 72 × 100 = **116.7 %** (accept 117 %) (1)

**4.** Explain how veins are adapted to return blood to the heart. [3]

- wide lumen, low resistance to flow (1)
- valves prevent backflow (1)
- thin walls, can be squeezed by surrounding muscles (1)

## Common mistakes

- Saying arteries carry oxygenated blood and veins deoxygenated — the definition is **direction** (away from / to the heart); pulmonary vessels are the exceptions.
- Mixing up left and right on heart diagrams.
- Saying capillaries have "thin walls" without "**one cell thick**".
- Saying the heart beats faster to "get more energy" — say it delivers more **oxygen and glucose** for **respiration** and removes **CO₂**.
- Writing that the septum "pumps blood" or that valves "push blood".
- Confusing phagocytes (engulf) with lymphocytes (antibodies).
- Forgetting "fibrinogen → fibrin" (soluble → insoluble) in clotting.

7. Plan an investigation into whether fitness affects the time for pulse rate to return to normal after exercise. [6]
