## Notes

### The circulatory system

A system of **blood vessels** with a **pump** (the heart) and **valves** to ensure **one-way flow** of blood.

**Supplement:**
- **Fish – single circulation**: heart → gills → body → heart. Blood passes through the heart **once** for each complete circuit; pressure falls a lot after the gills.
- **Mammal – double circulation**: blood passes through the heart **twice** for each complete circuit:
  - **pulmonary circulation**: right side of heart → lungs → left side of heart;
  - **systemic circulation**: left side of heart → body → right side of heart.
- **Advantages of a double circulation**: blood returns to the heart after the lungs and is pumped again, so it reaches the body at **high pressure** → faster delivery of oxygen and glucose to respiring tissues; blood goes to the lungs at **lower pressure**, so delicate lung capillaries are not damaged; oxygenated and deoxygenated blood are kept separate.

### The heart

Structures to identify: **muscular wall**, **septum**, **left and right atria**, **left and right ventricles**, **one-way valves**, **coronary arteries**.

- Blood is pumped **away** from the heart in **arteries** and returns **to** the heart in **veins**.
- **Septum**: wall separating left and right sides — stops oxygenated and deoxygenated blood mixing.
- **Valves**: one-way; prevent backflow. (Valves between atria and ventricles are atrioventricular valves; valves at the base of the aorta and pulmonary artery are semilunar valves — useful names, but "one-way valves" is the syllabus term.)
- **Coronary arteries**: on the surface of the heart; supply the heart muscle with oxygen and glucose.
- In diagrams the heart is drawn as if facing you, so the **left side is on the right** of the diagram. The ventricle walls are thicker than the atrial walls, and the left ventricle wall is the thickest.

Blood flow: vena cava → right atrium → right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs → pulmonary vein → left atrium → left ventricle → aorta → body.

**Supplement – how the heart functions:**
1. Muscles of the **atria contract** → blood is pushed into the ventricles (valves between atria and ventricles are open).
2. Muscles of the **ventricles contract** → pressure in ventricles rises → valves between atria and ventricles **close** (preventing backflow into the atria) → valves at the base of the arteries **open** → blood leaves in the aorta and pulmonary artery.
3. Ventricles **relax** → valves at the base of the arteries **close**, preventing backflow into the ventricles. The atria refill from the veins.

The "lub-dub" sounds heard with a stethoscope are the **valves closing**.

### Monitoring the heart and physical activity

- Heart activity can be monitored by: **ECG** (electrocardiogram), **pulse rate**, listening to the **sounds of valves closing**.
- Physical activity **increases heart rate**; after exercise it gradually returns to the resting rate.
- **Supplement – explanation**: muscles contract more → respire faster → need more **oxygen and glucose** → and produce more **carbon dioxide** to remove. The heart beats faster (and more strongly) so blood flows faster to and from the muscles. (Lactic acid from anaerobic respiration is carried to the liver — see *Gas exchange and respiration*.)

### Coronary heart disease (CHD)

- The **coronary arteries become blocked** (by fatty deposits; a clot may form).
- Less blood reaches part of the heart muscle → less oxygen and glucose → muscle cannot respire → it may die (heart attack).
- **Risk factors**: diet (e.g. high in saturated fat or salt), lack of exercise, stress, smoking, genetic predisposition, age, sex (more common in males).
- **Diet and exercise to reduce risk**: eat less saturated fat (fewer fatty deposits in arteries) and less salt; avoid excess energy intake (obesity); regular exercise strengthens the heart, helps control body mass and lowers blood pressure.

### Blood vessels

| Vessel | Wall | Lumen | Valves | Supplement: link to pressure / function |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Artery | thick, muscular and elastic | narrow | no (except at base of aorta and pulmonary artery) | carries blood at **high pressure**; thick wall withstands pressure; elastic tissue stretches and recoils |
| Vein | thin | wide | yes | carries blood at **low pressure**; wide lumen reduces resistance; valves prevent backflow |
| Capillary | **one cell thick** | very narrow | no | exchange of substances with tissues |

**Functions of capillaries**: exchange of substances (oxygen, glucose, carbon dioxide, urea) between blood and tissues.

**Supplement – capillary structure and function**: wall one cell thick → **short diffusion distance**; very many capillaries close to every cell → **large surface area**; narrow lumen → blood flows slowly, more time for exchange; walls are leaky so fluid can pass to the tissues.

**Supplement – main vessels:**

| Organ | Vessels carrying blood **to** it | Vessels carrying blood **away** |
|---|---|---|
| Heart | vena cava (from body), pulmonary vein (from lungs) | aorta (to body), pulmonary artery (to lungs) |
| Lungs | pulmonary artery | pulmonary vein |

The pulmonary artery carries **deoxygenated** blood; the pulmonary vein carries **oxygenated** blood.

### Blood

| Component | Function | How to recognise it |
|---|---|---|
| Red blood cells | transport oxygen; contain **haemoglobin**, which combines with oxygen | biconcave discs, no nucleus |
| White blood cells | phagocytosis and antibody production | have a nucleus; fewer, larger than red cells |
| – Phagocytes (Supp.) | engulfing pathogens by **phagocytosis** | lobed nucleus, granular cytoplasm |
| – Lymphocytes (Supp.) | **antibody production** | large round nucleus filling most of the cell |
| Platelets | blood clotting | small cell fragments |
| Plasma | transport of blood cells, ions, nutrients, urea, hormones and carbon dioxide | liquid part |

**Supplement**: blood clotting prevents **blood loss** and the **entry of pathogens** (details of the mechanism are not required).

### Paper 6 skills on this topic

- **Exercise and heart rate**: independent variable — e.g. duration of step-ups; dependent — pulse rate in beats per minute (count for 15 s and × 4, or for 60 s).
- Control: same person or matched age/sex/fitness, same type and rate of exercise, rest until pulse returns to resting value before the next trial; repeat and calculate a mean.
- Table headings with units: *time after exercise / min* | *pulse rate / beats per minute*. Plot a line graph; recovery time = time to return to resting rate.
- Safety: stop if dizzy; do not involve people with heart or breathing conditions.

## Key terms and definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Circulatory system | a system of blood vessels with a pump and valves to ensure one-way flow of blood |
| Double circulation | blood passes through the heart twice for each complete circuit of the body |
| Coronary heart disease | blockage of the coronary arteries, reducing the supply of oxygen to the heart muscle |
| Artery | blood vessel carrying blood away from the heart |
| Vein | blood vessel carrying blood back to the heart |
| Phagocytosis | engulfing (and digesting) pathogens, carried out by phagocytes |

## Worked examples

**1.** Describe the role of the valves in the heart when the ventricles contract. [3]

- valves between the atria and ventricles close (1)
- preventing backflow of blood into the atria (1)
- valves at the base of the aorta / pulmonary artery open, so blood flows into the arteries (1)

**2.** Explain **two** advantages of a double circulation. [2]

- blood returns to the heart after the lungs, so it is pumped to the body at high pressure → faster delivery of oxygen / glucose (1)
- low pressure in the lungs avoids damage to (lung) capillaries / oxygenated and deoxygenated blood do not mix (1)

**3.** A student's pulse was 18 beats in 15 seconds at rest and 39 beats in 15 seconds after exercise. Calculate the percentage increase in heart rate. [3]

- rates: 72 and 156 beats per minute (1)
- increase = 156 − 72 = 84 (1)
- 84 ÷ 72 × 100 = **116.7 %** (accept 117 %) (1)

**4.** Explain how the structure of an artery is related to the pressure of the blood it carries. [3]

- blood in arteries is at high pressure (1)
- thick (muscular) wall withstands the pressure / does not burst (1)
- elastic tissue stretches and recoils, keeping blood moving / evening out pressure (1)

## Common mistakes

- Saying arteries carry oxygenated blood and veins deoxygenated blood — the definition is **direction** (away from / back to the heart); the pulmonary artery and vein are the exceptions.
- Mixing up left and right on heart diagrams.
- Saying capillaries have "thin walls" without "**one cell thick**".
- Saying the heart beats faster to "make more energy" — it delivers more **oxygen and glucose** for **respiration** and removes **carbon dioxide** faster.
- Writing that valves "pump" or "push" blood — valves only **prevent backflow**.
- Confusing phagocytes (engulf pathogens) with lymphocytes (produce antibodies).
- Listing CHD risk factors that are not on the syllabus list while missing easy ones (genetic predisposition, age, sex).
