# Gas exchange and respiration

> **In the exam:** Co-ordinated Sciences papers are combined, so Biology questions appear alongside Chemistry and Physics in every paper. **Paper 2** (Multiple Choice, Extended: 45 min, 40 four-option questions, 30 %) can include items on inspired and expired air, parts of the breathing system and respiration equations. **Paper 4** (Theory, Extended: 2 h, 120 marks, 50 %; in the 2025 specimen Questions 1–4 are Biology, about 40 marks) often has a respiration part — the 2025 specimen asks for the balanced symbol equation for aerobic respiration — and parts on gas exchange surfaces, breathing during exercise or oxygen debt. **Paper 6** (Alternative to Practical: 1 h 30 min, 60 marks, 20 %) lists breathing rate and respiration as practical contexts: limewater with inspired/expired air, breathing rate before and after exercise, germinating seeds with hydrogencarbonate indicator.

## Notes

### The breathing system

Air pathway: nose/mouth → **larynx** → **trachea** → **bronchi** (one to each lung) → **bronchioles** → **alveoli**, each surrounded by **capillaries**.
The lungs sit inside the chest, protected by the **ribs**; the **intercostal muscles** lie between the ribs; the **diaphragm** is the sheet of muscle under the lungs. Practise labelling all of these on a diagram.

### Gas exchange surfaces (Supplement)

| Feature | Why it helps diffusion |
|---|---|
| Large surface area (millions of alveoli) | more oxygen and carbon dioxide can diffuse at the same time |
| Thin surface (alveolus wall and capillary wall each one cell thick) | short diffusion distance |
| Good blood supply (dense capillary network) | blood carries oxygen away and brings carbon dioxide, keeping a steep concentration gradient |
| Good ventilation with air | keeps oxygen concentration high and carbon dioxide low in the alveoli, maintaining the gradient |

### Inspired and expired air

| Gas | Inspired air | Expired air | Supplement: explanation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oxygen | about 21 % | about 16 % | oxygen diffuses from alveoli into the blood and is used in aerobic respiration |
| Carbon dioxide | about 0.04 % | about 4 % | carbon dioxide made in respiration diffuses from the blood into the alveoli |
| Water vapour | lower (variable) | higher (saturated) | water evaporates from the moist lining of the alveoli |

**Test for carbon dioxide**: **limewater** turns from colourless to **milky / cloudy**. Expired air turns limewater milky much faster than inspired air.

### Physical activity and breathing

- Core: during physical activity the **rate** (breaths per minute) and **depth** (volume per breath) of breathing **increase**; they gradually return to normal afterwards.
- Supplement: muscles respire faster → more **carbon dioxide** produced → **carbon dioxide concentration in the blood increases** → this is **detected by the brain** → the brain causes an **increased rate and greater depth of breathing** → more carbon dioxide removed and more oxygen taken in.

### Respiration

Respiration happens in **all living cells** — it is not the same as breathing.

**Uses of energy**: muscle contraction, protein synthesis, cell division, growth, the passage of nerve impulses, the maintenance of a constant body temperature.

**Aerobic respiration**: the chemical reactions in cells that use oxygen to break down nutrient molecules to release energy.
- glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water
- Supplement: **C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O**

**Anaerobic respiration (Supplement)**: the chemical reactions in cells that break down nutrient molecules to release energy without using oxygen.
- In muscles during vigorous exercise: **glucose → lactic acid**
- It releases **much less energy** per glucose molecule than aerobic respiration.

| | Aerobic | Anaerobic (muscles) |
|---|---|---|
| Oxygen used? | yes | no |
| Products | carbon dioxide and water | lactic acid |
| Energy released per glucose | much more | much less |

### Oxygen debt (Supplement)

- During vigorous exercise, the muscles cannot be supplied with oxygen fast enough, so some respiration is **anaerobic**.
- **Lactic acid builds up in muscles and blood**, causing an **oxygen debt**.
- After exercise, the oxygen debt is removed by:
  1. **continuation of fast heart rate** to transport lactic acid in the blood from the muscles to the **liver**;
  2. **continuation of deeper and faster breathing** to supply oxygen for aerobic respiration of lactic acid;
  3. **aerobic respiration of lactic acid in the liver**.

### Paper 6 skills on this topic

- **Inspired vs expired air**: two tubes of limewater; breathe in through one and out through the other; compare the time for each to turn milky. Safety: never suck limewater into the mouth.
- **Breathing rate and exercise**: count breaths per minute at rest and after exercise; same person / same exercise for the same time; repeat and calculate a mean; plot a bar chart or line graph.
- **Respiration in germinating seeds**: hydrogencarbonate indicator is **red/orange** at atmospheric CO₂, turns **yellow** when CO₂ increases (respiration), **purple** when CO₂ decreases. Germinating seeds in a sealed tube with the indicator turn it yellow.
  - Control: boiled (dead) seeds, cooled, or glass beads of the same volume — shows the change is caused by living, respiring seeds.
  - Keep in the dark if green tissue is present, so photosynthesis does not use CO₂.
- Paper 6 may also describe an unfamiliar respiration set-up (e.g. counting gas bubbles from a respiring organism at different temperatures): read the method, identify variables, and process the data.

## Key terms and definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Respiration | the chemical reactions in cells that break down nutrient molecules and release energy for metabolism |
| Aerobic respiration | the chemical reactions in cells that use oxygen to break down nutrient molecules to release energy |
| Anaerobic respiration | the chemical reactions in cells that break down nutrient molecules to release energy without using oxygen |
| Oxygen debt | caused by the build-up of lactic acid in muscles and blood during vigorous exercise; repaid by supplying oxygen for the aerobic respiration of lactic acid |

## Worked examples

**1.** State the balanced symbol equation for aerobic respiration. [2]

- C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ (1)
- → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O (1)

**2.** Explain why a runner continues to breathe deeply and quickly after a race. [3]

- lactic acid built up during anaerobic respiration / oxygen debt (1)
- extra oxygen needed for aerobic respiration of lactic acid (1)
- in the liver (1)

**3.** A student counted breaths: 54 breaths in 3 minutes at rest and 75 breaths in 3 minutes after exercise. Calculate the breathing rate at rest and the percentage increase after exercise. [3]

- at rest: 54 ÷ 3 = 18 breaths per minute (1)
- after exercise: 75 ÷ 3 = 25 breaths per minute (1)
- (25 − 18) ÷ 18 × 100 = **38.9 %** (accept 39 %) (1)

**4.** Explain why expired air contains less oxygen and more carbon dioxide than inspired air. [3]

- oxygen diffuses from the alveoli into the blood (1)
- oxygen is used in (aerobic) respiration in cells (1)
- carbon dioxide produced by respiration diffuses from the blood into the alveoli (1)

## Common mistakes

- Saying "respiration is breathing" — respiration is a set of **chemical reactions in cells**.
- Saying energy is "produced" or "made" — it is **released**.
- Writing "lactic acid + carbon dioxide" for muscles — anaerobic respiration in muscles makes **lactic acid only**.
- Saying alveoli have "thin walls" without "**one cell thick**", or "large surface" without "**area**".
- Saying the brain detects "lack of oxygen" — the syllabus explanation is an increased **carbon dioxide concentration in the blood** detected by the brain.
- Saying "expired air contains no oxygen" — it is still about **16 %**.
- Forgetting the **liver** in oxygen debt answers.
