# Gas exchange and respiration

> **In the exam:** Paper 2 has 3–4 questions (inspired vs expired air, respiration equations, anaerobic products). Paper 4 often has a question (8–12 marks) on breathing mechanics, features of gas exchange surfaces, oxygen debt or yeast respiration. Paper 6 frequently uses respiration experiments: germinating seeds with indicator, yeast producing CO₂ at different temperatures, or limewater comparing inspired and expired air.

## What you need to know

- Describe the features of gas exchange surfaces in humans: large surface area, thin surface, good blood supply, good ventilation with air.
- Identify the parts of the breathing system: lungs, diaphragm, ribs, intercostal muscles, larynx, trachea, bronchi, bronchioles, alveoli, associated capillaries.
- Investigate and describe the differences in composition between inspired and expired air (limewater test for CO₂).
- Investigate and describe the effects of physical activity on the rate and depth of breathing.
- Extended: explain the role of the ribs, internal and external intercostal muscles and diaphragm in ventilation; explain differences between inspired and expired air; explain the link between physical activity and breathing rate (CO₂ concentration detected by the brain); explain how goblet cells, mucus and ciliated cells protect the gas exchange system.
- State the uses of energy in humans; define aerobic and anaerobic respiration; state the word equations (Extended: balanced symbol equation for aerobic respiration and for anaerobic respiration in yeast).
- Investigate the uptake of oxygen by respiring organisms and the effect of temperature on respiration in yeast.
- State that anaerobic respiration releases much less energy per glucose molecule than aerobic respiration.
- Extended: explain oxygen debt and how it is removed.

## Notes

### Gas exchange surfaces (alveoli)

| Feature | Why it helps |
|---|---|
| Large surface area (millions of alveoli) | more diffusion at once |
| Thin surface (alveolus wall and capillary wall each one cell thick) | short diffusion distance |
| Good blood supply (capillary network) | carries O₂ away / brings CO₂, keeping a steep concentration gradient |
| Good ventilation with air | keeps O₂ high and CO₂ low in alveoli, maintaining the gradient |

Air pathway: nose/mouth → larynx → **trachea** → **bronchi** → **bronchioles** → **alveoli**. C-shaped rings of **cartilage** keep the trachea and bronchi open.

### Inspired vs expired air

| Gas | Inspired | Expired | Extended reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oxygen | 21 % | 16 % | O₂ diffuses into blood and is used in respiration |
| Carbon dioxide | 0.04 % | 4 % | CO₂ produced by respiration diffuses into alveoli |
| Water vapour | variable/less | more (saturated) | evaporation from the moist lining of the alveoli |
| Nitrogen | 78 % | 78 % | not used |

Test for CO₂: **limewater** turns from colourless (clear) to **milky / cloudy**; expired air turns it milky faster.

### Ventilation (Extended)

| | Inspiration (breathing in) | Expiration (breathing out, at rest) |
|---|---|---|
| External intercostal muscles | contract | relax |
| Internal intercostal muscles | relax | contract (forced breathing out) |
| Ribs | move up and out | move down and in |
| Diaphragm | contracts, flattens (moves down) | relaxes, domes up |
| Volume of thorax | increases | decreases |
| Pressure in thorax | decreases (below atmospheric) | increases (above atmospheric) |
| Air | flows in | flows out |

### Exercise and breathing

- During physical activity, the **rate and depth** of breathing increase.
- Extended: muscles respire faster → more **CO₂** produced → CO₂ concentration in the blood rises → detected by the **brain** → brain sends impulses to increase rate and depth of breathing → more CO₂ removed and more O₂ taken in.

### Protecting the airways (Extended)

- **Goblet cells** secrete **mucus**, which traps pathogens and particles.
- **Ciliated cells** have cilia that beat to move mucus up to the throat, where it is swallowed.

### Respiration

- Energy released by respiration is used for: **muscle contraction, protein synthesis, cell division, active transport, growth, the passage of nerve impulses, the maintenance of a constant body temperature**.
- **Aerobic respiration**: the chemical reactions in cells that use oxygen to break down nutrient molecules to release energy.
  - glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water
  - Extended: **C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O**
- **Anaerobic respiration**: the chemical reactions in cells that break down nutrient molecules to release energy without using oxygen.
  - In **muscles**: glucose → lactic acid
  - In **yeast**: glucose → alcohol (ethanol) + carbon dioxide; Extended: **C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂**
- Anaerobic respiration releases **much less energy** per glucose molecule than aerobic respiration.
- Respiration is controlled by **enzymes**, so it is affected by temperature.

### Oxygen debt (Extended)

- During vigorous exercise, muscles cannot get enough oxygen, so they also respire **anaerobically**. **Lactic acid** builds up in muscles and blood, causing an **oxygen debt**.
- Removed during recovery by:
  - **continuation of fast heart rate** to transport lactic acid in the blood from the muscles to the **liver**;
  - **continuation of deeper and faster breathing** to supply oxygen for aerobic respiration of lactic acid;
  - **aerobic respiration of lactic acid in the liver**.

### Paper 6 skills on this topic

- **Germinating seeds** in a sealed tube with hydrogencarbonate indicator or with soda lime and a coloured liquid (respirometer). The liquid moves towards the seeds as O₂ is used (CO₂ is absorbed by soda lime).
- **Control**: boiled (dead) seeds, cooled and disinfected (to kill microorganisms), or glass beads of the same volume — shows changes are due to living seeds.
- **Yeast and temperature**: yeast + glucose solution in water baths at different temperatures; count bubbles of CO₂ or measure volume of gas per minute. Controlled: concentration and volume of yeast and glucose, time allowed to equilibrate. Add a layer of oil to keep the mixture anaerobic if required.
- **Inspired/expired air**: breathe in through one tube and out through another; compare time for limewater to go cloudy. Risk: do not suck limewater into mouth.
- **Breathing rate**: count breaths per minute before and after exercise; repeat and mean.
- Risk with soda lime: corrosive — handle with forceps, eye protection.

## Key terms and definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Aerobic respiration | the chemical reactions in cells that use oxygen to break down nutrient molecules to release energy |
| Anaerobic respiration | the chemical reactions in cells that break down nutrient molecules to release energy without using oxygen |
| Respiration | the chemical reactions in cells that break down nutrient molecules and release energy for metabolism |
| Oxygen debt | the oxygen needed after exercise to break down the lactic acid that built up during anaerobic respiration |

## Worked examples

**1.** Extended: explain how air is drawn into the lungs. [4]

- external intercostal muscles contract, ribs move up and out (1)
- diaphragm contracts and flattens (1)
- volume of thorax increases (1)
- pressure decreases below atmospheric, so air moves in (1)

**2.** Extended: explain why a runner continues to breathe deeply and quickly after a race. [3]

- lactic acid built up from anaerobic respiration / oxygen debt (1)
- extra oxygen needed for aerobic respiration of lactic acid (1)
- in the liver (1)

**3.** A student measured CO₂ released by yeast. At 25 °C, 18 bubbles were counted in 3 minutes; at 35 °C, 42 bubbles in 3 minutes. Calculate the rate at each temperature and the ratio of the two rates. [3]

- 25 °C: 18 ÷ 3 = 6 bubbles per minute (1)
- 35 °C: 42 ÷ 3 = 14 bubbles per minute (1)
- ratio 14 ÷ 6 = **2.3** (1)

## Common mistakes

- Saying "respiration is breathing" — breathing is **ventilation**; respiration is a chemical reaction in cells.
- Saying energy is "produced" or "made".
- Writing "lactic acid + CO₂" for muscles — anaerobic respiration in muscles makes **lactic acid only**.
- Saying alveoli have "thin walls" without saying "**one cell thick**", or "large surface" without "**area**".
- Saying the lungs "suck in" air — explain volume and **pressure** changes.
- Confusing internal and external intercostal muscles.
- Saying "oxygen in expired air is 0 %" — it is about **16 %**.

## Practice questions

1. State **four** uses of energy in the human body. [4]
2. State **two** differences between aerobic and anaerobic respiration in humans. [2]
3. Give the balanced equation for anaerobic respiration in yeast. [2]
4. Explain why expired air contains more water vapour than inspired air. [1]
5. Extended: explain how mucus and ciliated cells protect the lungs. [3]
6. Describe a suitable control for an experiment using germinating peas to show that respiration uses oxygen. [2]
7. Extended: explain why breathing rate increases during exercise. [3]

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Any four: muscle contraction; protein synthesis; cell division; active transport; growth; passage of nerve impulses; maintenance of constant body temperature.
2. Any two: aerobic uses oxygen, anaerobic does not; aerobic releases much more energy per glucose; aerobic products CO₂ and water, anaerobic product lactic acid.
3. C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂ (correct formulae 1; balanced 1).
4. Water evaporates from the moist surfaces of the alveoli.
5. Goblet cells make mucus (1); mucus traps pathogens/dust (1); cilia beat and move mucus up to the throat (1).
6. Boiled (dead) peas / glass beads (1) of the same mass/volume, otherwise identical set-up (1).
7. More CO₂ produced by faster respiration in muscles (1); higher CO₂ concentration in blood detected by brain (1); brain increases rate and depth of breathing to remove CO₂ / supply more O₂ (1).
