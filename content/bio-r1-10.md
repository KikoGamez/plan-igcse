# Coordination and response

> **In the exam:** Paper 2 has 3–5 questions (reflex arc order, parts of the eye, hormones, tropisms). Paper 4 usually has a full question (8–14 marks): synapses, accommodation or the pupil reflex, nervous vs hormonal control, or auxin and phototropism. Paper 6 often tests reaction-time experiments (ruler drop) and tropism experiments with seedlings (variables, controls, drawing results).

## What you need to know

- Describe the nervous system: central nervous system (brain and spinal cord) and peripheral nervous system; describe its role in coordination and regulation.
- Identify sensory, relay and motor neurones; describe a simple reflex arc; define reflex action.
- Define synapse; Extended: describe the structure and function of a synapse; state that synapses ensure impulses travel in one direction.
- Define sense organs; identify the structures of the eye and describe their functions.
- Explain the pupil reflex; Extended: explain accommodation; describe the distribution and function of rods and cones (three types of cone, fovea).
- Define hormone; identify endocrine glands and their hormones (adrenaline, insulin, testosterone, oestrogen; Extended: glucagon); describe the effects of adrenaline; compare nervous and hormonal control.
- Define gravitropism and phototropism; investigate them; Extended: explain the role of auxin in controlling shoot growth.

## Notes

### The nervous system

- **CNS**: brain and spinal cord. **PNS**: nerves connecting the CNS to the rest of the body.
- Role: coordination and regulation of body functions.
- **Nerve impulse**: an electrical signal that passes along nerve cells called neurones.

| Neurone | Job | Features |
|---|---|---|
| Sensory | carries impulses from receptors to the CNS | cell body part-way along, outside the CNS |
| Relay | connects sensory and motor neurones in the CNS | short, many connections |
| Motor | carries impulses from the CNS to effectors | cell body in the CNS, long axon to muscle/gland |

### Reflex action

- **Reflex action**: a means of automatically and rapidly integrating and coordinating stimuli with the responses of effectors (muscles and glands).
- **Reflex arc**: stimulus → **receptor** → **sensory neurone** → **relay neurone** (in spinal cord/brain) → **motor neurone** → **effector** (muscle or gland) → response.
- Example: hand on hot object → pain/temperature receptors → … → biceps contracts, hand withdrawn.
- Reflexes are fast and **involuntary** — they protect the body.

### Synapses

- **Synapse**: a junction between two neurones.
- Extended — how it works:
  1. An impulse arrives at the end of the first neurone.
  2. **Vesicles** containing **neurotransmitter** move to and fuse with the membrane, releasing neurotransmitter into the **synaptic gap**.
  3. Neurotransmitter **diffuses** across the gap.
  4. It **binds to receptor proteins** on the membrane of the next neurone.
  5. An impulse is stimulated in the next neurone.
- Synapses ensure impulses travel in **one direction only** (vesicles only on one side, receptors only on the other).

### Sense organs and the eye

- **Sense organs**: groups of receptor cells responding to specific stimuli: light, sound, touch, temperature and chemicals.

| Structure | Function |
|---|---|
| Cornea | refracts (bends) light, the most refraction |
| Iris | controls how much light enters the pupil |
| Pupil | hole that lets light in |
| Lens | focuses light onto the retina (fine adjustment) |
| Retina | contains light receptors (rods and cones) |
| Optic nerve | carries impulses to the brain |
| Blind spot | where the optic nerve leaves; no receptors |
| Extended: ciliary muscles, suspensory ligaments | change the shape of the lens |
| Extended: fovea | many cones; sharpest, colour vision |

**Pupil reflex**:

| Bright light | Dim light |
|---|---|
| circular muscles of iris **contract**, radial muscles **relax** | circular muscles **relax**, radial muscles **contract** |
| pupil becomes **narrower** | pupil becomes **wider** |
| less light enters, protects retina | more light enters, so we can see |

**Accommodation** (Extended):

| Near object | Distant object |
|---|---|
| ciliary muscles **contract** | ciliary muscles **relax** |
| suspensory ligaments **slacken** | suspensory ligaments **tighten / are pulled tight** |
| lens becomes **fatter / more convex** | lens becomes **thinner / less convex** |
| more refraction of light | less refraction |

**Rods and cones** (Extended):
- **Rods**: all over the retina except the fovea; very sensitive — **night vision** / dim light; black and white.
- **Cones**: concentrated in the **fovea**; three types sensitive to different colours of light; **colour vision**; need bright light.

### Hormones

- **Hormone**: a chemical substance, produced by a gland and carried by the blood, which alters the activity of one or more specific target organs.

| Gland | Hormone |
|---|---|
| Adrenal glands | adrenaline |
| Pancreas | insulin (Extended: and glucagon) |
| Testes | testosterone |
| Ovaries | oestrogen (and progesterone) |
| Pituitary gland | FSH, LH (and others) |

- **Adrenaline** is released in "fight or flight" situations: increases **breathing rate**, **heart rate** and **pupil diameter**. Extended: increases blood glucose concentration (more glucose for muscle respiration).

**Nervous vs hormonal control**

| | Nervous | Hormonal |
|---|---|---|
| Signal | electrical impulses | chemicals (hormones) |
| Carried by | neurones | blood |
| Speed | very fast | slower |
| Duration | short-lived | longer-lasting |
| Target | very precise area | can affect several organs |

### Plant tropisms

- **Phototropism**: a response in which parts of a plant grow towards or away from the direction from which light is coming. Shoots: positively phototropic.
- **Gravitropism**: a response in which parts of a plant grow towards or away from gravity. Roots: positively gravitropic; shoots: negatively gravitropic.
- Extended — **auxin** and shoot growth:
  - auxin is made in the **shoot tip** only and spreads (diffuses) through the plant from the tip;
  - it becomes **unequally distributed** in response to light and gravity;
  - auxin **stimulates cell elongation**.
  - Phototropism: auxin moves to the **shaded side**, cells there elongate more, so the shoot bends **towards the light**.
  - Gravitropism in a horizontal shoot: auxin collects on the **lower side**, cells there elongate more, so the shoot grows **upwards**.

### Paper 6 skills on this topic

- **Tropism experiments**: seedlings in a box with light from one side vs a **clinostat** (rotating, so light/gravity act evenly) as a control; or seedlings with tips covered by foil caps. Measure angle of curvature with a protractor or length with a ruler; repeat with several seedlings and take a mean.
- Controlled variables: same species, age, number of seedlings, water, temperature, light intensity, time.
- **Reaction time (ruler drop)**: catch a dropped ruler; record distance; repeat several times; convert to time with a table given. Controlled: same hand, same starting height, same person dropping, no warning. Practice trials improve results — ignore the first or use a mean of later results.
- **Drawings** of seedlings: clear outline, no shading, show the direction of bending.

## Worked examples

**1.** Extended: explain how the eye focuses on a book after looking at a distant tree. [3]

- ciliary muscles contract (1)
- suspensory ligaments slacken (1)
- lens becomes fatter / more convex, refracting light more (1)

**2.** Extended: describe how an impulse passes across a synapse. [4]

- vesicles release neurotransmitter (into gap) (1)
- neurotransmitter diffuses across the synaptic gap (1)
- binds to receptor proteins on the next neurone (1)
- stimulates an impulse in the next neurone (1)

**3.** Extended: a shoot is lit from the left. Explain why it bends to the left. [4]

- auxin made in shoot tip (1)
- auxin moves to the shaded (right) side (1)
- stimulates cell elongation on the right side (1)
- right side grows more, so shoot bends to the left / towards light (1)

**4.** A ruler-drop reaction distance of 18 cm, 15 cm, 14 cm, 13 cm was recorded. Calculate the mean. [1]

- (18 + 15 + 14 + 13) ÷ 4 = **15 cm** (1)

## Common mistakes

- Saying "auxin moves away from light because it is destroyed" — just say it moves to / accumulates on the **shaded side**.
- Saying auxin "makes cells divide" — it stimulates **cell elongation**.
- Saying the "iris muscles" without saying which (circular/radial) contract.
- Mixing up accommodation: near object = ciliary muscles **contract**, ligaments **slacken**.
- Saying hormones travel along nerves — they are carried by the **blood**.
- Writing "message" or "signal" instead of "**impulse**".
- Forgetting that the effector in a reflex can be a muscle **or a gland**.

## Practice questions

1. Put in order: motor neurone, receptor, effector, relay neurone, sensory neurone. [1]
2. State the function of the cornea and of the retina. [2]
3. Describe the pupil reflex when a person walks into a dark room. [3]
4. Extended: state where cones are concentrated and their function. [2]
5. State **three** effects of adrenaline. [3]
6. Give **two** differences between nervous and hormonal control. [2]
7. Describe a control for an experiment to show that shoots grow towards light, and explain why it is needed. [2]

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Receptor → sensory neurone → relay neurone → motor neurone → effector.
2. Cornea refracts / bends light (1); retina contains light receptors / rods and cones (1).
3. Radial muscles contract (1); circular muscles relax (1); pupil widens, more light enters (1).
4. Fovea (1); colour vision / detect different colours in bright light (1).
5. Increased heart rate; increased breathing rate; increased pupil diameter (Extended alternative: increased blood glucose).
6. Any two: impulses vs chemicals; neurones vs blood; faster vs slower; short-lived vs longer-lasting; precise vs widespread.
7. Seedlings on a clinostat / lit evenly from all sides / in darkness (1); shows the bending is caused by one-sided light (1).
