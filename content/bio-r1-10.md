# Coordination and response

> **In the exam:** In Co-ordinated Sciences 0654 this topic (B13.1 nervous system, B13.2 hormones) is mostly **Core** and is shorter than in Biology 0610: there are **no synapses, no eye structure, no pupil reflex or accommodation, and no plant tropisms or auxin**. **Paper 2** (Multiple Choice, 45 min, 40 four-option questions across Biology, Chemistry and Physics, 30 %) may ask for the order of a reflex arc, a neurone type, or a gland and its hormone. **Paper 4** (Theory, 2 h, 120 marks, 50 %): Biology questions appear alongside Chemistry and Physics (Q1–4 are Biology in the 2025 specimen, about 9–11 marks each, often mixing topics); expect short parts (1–4 marks) such as labelling neurones, describing a reflex arc, or the effects of adrenaline. **Paper 6** (Alternative to Practical, 1 h 30 min, 60 marks, 20 %) does not list reflexes as a context, but reaction-time data can appear as an unfamiliar procedure.

## What you need to know

**Core (B13.1 Coordination and response)**
- State that electrical impulses travel along neurones.
- Describe the mammalian nervous system in terms of: the central nervous system (CNS) consisting of the brain and spinal cord; the peripheral nervous system (PNS) consisting of the nerves outside of the brain and spinal cord.
- Describe the role of the nervous system as coordination and regulation of body functions.
- Identify in diagrams and images sensory, relay and motor neurones.
- Describe a simple reflex arc in terms of: receptor, sensory neurone, relay neurone, motor neurone and effector.
- Describe a reflex action as a means of automatically and rapidly integrating and coordinating stimuli with the responses of effectors (muscles and glands).
- Describe sense organs as groups of receptor cells responding to specific stimuli: light, sound, touch, temperature and chemicals.

**Core (B13.2 Hormones)**
- Describe a hormone as a chemical substance, produced by a gland and carried by the blood, which alters the activity of one or more specific target organs.
- Identify in diagrams and images specific endocrine glands and state the hormones they secrete, limited to: adrenal glands and adrenaline; pancreas and insulin; testes and testosterone; ovaries and oestrogen.
- Describe adrenaline as the hormone secreted in "fight or flight" situations and its effects, limited to: increased breathing rate, increased heart rate, increased pupil diameter.

**Supplement**
- State that glucagon is secreted by the pancreas.

## Notes

### The nervous system

- **Electrical impulses** travel along **neurones** (nerve cells).
- **CNS** (central nervous system): the **brain** and **spinal cord**.
- **PNS** (peripheral nervous system): the **nerves outside** the brain and spinal cord.
- Role of the nervous system: **coordination and regulation of body functions**.

| Neurone | Carries impulses | How to recognise it on a diagram |
|---|---|---|
| **Sensory** | from a **receptor** to the CNS | cell body part-way along the fibre, on a short branch (outside the CNS) |
| **Relay** | from the sensory neurone to the motor neurone, **inside the CNS** | short, many branched connections |
| **Motor** | from the CNS to an **effector** | cell body at one end (in the CNS), with a long fibre to a muscle or gland |

### Reflex arc and reflex action

**Reflex arc**: stimulus → **receptor** → **sensory neurone** → **relay neurone** (in the spinal cord or brain) → **motor neurone** → **effector** → response.

- **Effectors** are **muscles** (which contract) or **glands** (which secrete).
- Example: touching a hot object → temperature/pain receptors in the skin → sensory neurone → relay neurone in spinal cord → motor neurone → arm muscle contracts → hand is pulled away.
- **Reflex action**: a means of automatically and rapidly integrating and coordinating stimuli with the responses of effectors (muscles and glands).
- Reflexes are **automatic** (no conscious thought) and **rapid**, so they protect the body from damage.

### Sense organs

- **Sense organs**: groups of receptor cells responding to specific stimuli: light, sound, touch, temperature and chemicals.

| Stimulus | Sense organ |
|---|---|
| light | eye |
| sound | ear |
| touch, temperature | skin |
| chemicals | tongue (taste) and nose (smell) |

(The structure of the eye is not required for 0654.)

### Hormones

- **Hormone**: a chemical substance, produced by a gland and carried by the blood, which alters the activity of one or more specific target organs.
- Hormones are secreted by **endocrine glands** directly into the **blood**.

| Endocrine gland | Hormone secreted |
|---|---|
| Adrenal glands (on top of the kidneys) | adrenaline |
| Pancreas | insulin (Supplement: and glucagon) |
| Testes | testosterone |
| Ovaries | oestrogen |

Be able to point to each gland on an outline of the human body: adrenal glands just above the kidneys; pancreas just below the stomach; testes in the scrotum; ovaries in the lower abdomen either side of the uterus.

### Adrenaline — "fight or flight"

Adrenaline is secreted in "fight or flight" situations (fear, danger, excitement). Its effects (the only three you need):
- **increased breathing rate**
- **increased heart rate**
- **increased pupil diameter** (pupils widen)

Linked reasoning examiners credit: faster breathing and heart rate supply more oxygen (and glucose) to muscles for respiration, releasing more energy for action.

### Links to other topics

- Insulin and glucagon control blood glucose (homeostasis, Supplement — see the homeostasis page).
- Testosterone and oestrogen control secondary sexual characteristics at puberty (reproduction page).

### Paper 6 skills on this topic

- **Ruler-drop reaction time** (unfamiliar-procedure style): one person drops a ruler, the other catches it; record the distance; repeat and calculate a mean. Controlled variables: same hand, same starting position of fingers, same person dropping, no warning. Identify anomalous results and exclude them from the mean.
- **Heart rate and breathing rate** are listed practical contexts: count pulse or breaths for a set time (e.g. 15 s × 4 = per minute), at rest and after exercise.

## Worked examples

**1.** A person steps on a sharp pin and lifts the foot. Describe the pathway of the reflex arc involved. [4]

- pain/touch receptor in the skin detects the stimulus (1)
- impulse along sensory neurone to the spinal cord / CNS (1)
- relay neurone passes impulse to motor neurone (1)
- motor neurone carries impulse to the effector / leg muscle, which contracts (1)

**2.** Describe adrenaline and state its effects. [4]

- hormone secreted by the adrenal glands (1)
- in "fight or flight" situations (1)
- any two effects: increased breathing rate; increased heart rate; increased pupil diameter (2)

**3.** A ruler-drop test gave catch distances of 18 cm, 15 cm, 14 cm and 13 cm. Calculate the mean distance. [1]

- (18 + 15 + 14 + 13) ÷ 4 = **15 cm** (1)

**4.** State the difference between the central nervous system and the peripheral nervous system. [2]

- CNS is the brain and spinal cord (1)
- PNS is the nerves outside the brain and spinal cord (1)

## Common mistakes

- Writing "message" or "signal" instead of **impulse**.
- Saying hormones travel along nerves — they are carried by the **blood**.
- Forgetting that an effector can be a **gland** as well as a muscle.
- Putting the relay neurone outside the CNS — it is **in the brain or spinal cord**.
- Giving extra effects of adrenaline not in the syllabus instead of the three listed ones (breathing rate, heart rate, pupil diameter).
- Mixing up which gland makes which hormone (adrenal → adrenaline; pancreas → insulin and glucagon).
