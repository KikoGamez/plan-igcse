# Reproduction

> **In the exam:** a big topic in Co-ordinated Sciences 0654 (B15), but narrower than Biology 0610: **no** pollen tube, fruit and seed formation, self-/cross-pollination, placenta and fetal development, menstrual-cycle hormones (FSH, LH, progesterone), or contraception. **Paper 2** (Multiple Choice, 45 min, 40 four-option questions across Biology, Chemistry and Physics, 30 %) may ask about flower parts, pollination, gametes or HIV. **Paper 4** (Theory, 2 h, 120 marks, 50 %): Biology questions appear alongside Chemistry and Physics (Q1–4 are Biology in the 2025 specimen, about 9–11 marks each, often mixing topics); typical parts are labelling a flower or reproductive system, comparing gametes, explaining adaptations, or advantages of sexual/asexual reproduction. **Paper 6** (Alternative to Practical, 1 h 30 min, 60 marks, 20 %) lists **observation and dissection of seeds and flowers** and **germination** as practical contexts — drawing a flower and planning a germination experiment are likely skills.

## What you need to know

**Core**
- Describe asexual reproduction as a process resulting in the production of genetically identical offspring from one parent; identify examples of asexual reproduction in diagrams, images and information provided.
- Describe sexual reproduction as a process involving the fusion of the nuclei of two gametes to form a zygote and the production of offspring that are genetically different from each other.
- Describe a species as a group of organisms that can reproduce to produce fertile offspring.
- Identify in diagrams and images and draw the following parts of an insect-pollinated flower: sepals, petals, stamens, filaments, anthers, carpels, style, stigma, ovary and ovules; state their functions.
- Describe pollination as the transfer of pollen grains from an anther to a stigma.
- State that fertilisation occurs when a pollen nucleus fuses with a nucleus in an ovule.
- Describe the structural adaptations of insect-pollinated and wind-pollinated flowers.
- Investigate and describe the environmental conditions that affect germination of seeds, limited to the requirement for: water, oxygen and a suitable temperature.
- Identify on diagrams and state the functions of: testes, scrotum, sperm ducts, prostate gland, urethra and penis; ovaries, oviducts, uterus, cervix and vagina.
- Describe fertilisation as the fusion of the nuclei from a male gamete (sperm) and a female gamete (egg cell).
- Describe the roles of testosterone and oestrogen in the development and regulation of secondary sexual characteristics during puberty.
- Describe the menstrual cycle in terms of changes in the ovaries and in the lining of the uterus (knowledge of sex hormones is not required).
- Describe a sexually transmitted infection (STI) as an infection that is transmitted through sexual contact; state that HIV is a pathogen that causes an STI and that HIV infection may lead to AIDS; describe the methods of transmission of HIV; explain how the spread of STIs is controlled.

**Supplement**
- Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of asexual reproduction and of sexual reproduction to a population of a species in the wild.
- State that nuclei of gametes are haploid and that the nucleus of a zygote is diploid.
- Identify in diagrams and images and describe the anthers and stigmas of a wind-pollinated flower.
- Explain the adaptive features of sperm, limited to: flagellum, mitochondria and the presence of enzymes in the acrosome.
- Explain the adaptive features of egg cells, limited to: energy stores and the jelly coat that changes at fertilisation.
- Compare male and female gametes in terms of: size, structure, motility and numbers.

## Notes

### Asexual and sexual reproduction

- **Asexual reproduction**: a process resulting in the production of genetically identical offspring from one parent. Examples: bacteria dividing; potato tubers; strawberry runners; bulbs; yeast budding.
- **Sexual reproduction**: a process involving the fusion of the nuclei of two gametes to form a zygote and the production of offspring that are genetically different from each other.
- **Species**: a group of organisms that can reproduce to produce fertile offspring.
- Supplement: gamete nuclei are **haploid** (one set of chromosomes); the zygote nucleus is **diploid** (two sets).

Supplement — to a population in the wild:

| | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Asexual | only one parent needed; fast, many offspring; offspring keep features suited to that environment | no genetic variation, so a change in the environment or a new disease could kill the whole population; offspring close together compete |
| Sexual | genetic variation, so some individuals may survive environmental change or disease; allows natural selection | needs two parents (or pollinators); slower; more energy used finding mates/making flowers |

### Flowers

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| Sepals | protect the flower while it is a bud |
| Petals | large, brightly coloured (often scented) to attract insects |
| Stamen (= anther + filament) | male part |
| Anther | produces pollen |
| Filament | supports / holds up the anther |
| Carpel (= stigma + style + ovary) | female part |
| Stigma | receives pollen during pollination |
| Style | supports the stigma; connects it to the ovary |
| Ovary | contains the ovules |
| Ovules | contain the female nucleus (female gamete) |

- **Pollination**: the transfer of pollen grains from an anther to a stigma.
- **Fertilisation** in plants: a **pollen nucleus fuses with a nucleus in an ovule**.

| Insect-pollinated | Wind-pollinated |
|---|---|
| large, brightly coloured, scented petals; nectar | small, dull/green petals or none; no scent or nectar |
| anthers inside the flower, so insects brush against them | Supplement: **anthers hang outside** the flower on long filaments, so pollen is blown away |
| stigma inside the flower, sticky | Supplement: **large, feathery stigmas outside** the flower, to catch pollen from the air |
| fewer, larger, sticky or spiky pollen grains | large amounts of small, light, smooth pollen |

### Germination

Seeds need three conditions:
- **water** — makes the seed swell and activates enzymes;
- **oxygen** — for aerobic respiration to release energy for growth;
- **a suitable temperature** — for enzyme activity.

Light is **not** needed for germination.

### Human reproductive systems

| Male | Function |
|---|---|
| Testes | produce sperm and testosterone |
| Scrotum | holds the testes outside the body, where it is cooler |
| Sperm ducts | carry sperm from the testes to the urethra |
| Prostate gland | adds fluid to the sperm (making semen) |
| Urethra | carries semen (and urine) out of the body |
| Penis | passes semen into the vagina |

| Female | Function |
|---|---|
| Ovaries | produce egg cells (and oestrogen) |
| Oviducts | carry the egg to the uterus; fertilisation happens here |
| Uterus | where the embryo develops |
| Cervix | ring of muscle at the base of the uterus |
| Vagina | receives the penis and sperm during intercourse; birth canal |

- **Fertilisation**: the fusion of the nuclei from a male gamete (sperm) and a female gamete (egg cell).

### Gametes (Supplement)

| | Sperm | Egg cell |
|---|---|---|
| Size | very small | much larger |
| Structure | head with nucleus, acrosome, mid-piece, flagellum | large cytoplasm with energy stores, jelly coat |
| Motility | swims using a flagellum | cannot move itself (moved along the oviduct) |
| Numbers | millions released | one released per cycle |

Adaptive features:
- **Flagellum** — lets the sperm swim to the egg.
- **Mitochondria** (many in the mid-piece) — aerobic respiration releases energy for swimming.
- **Enzymes in the acrosome** — digest the jelly coat so the sperm can enter the egg.
- **Energy stores** in the egg cytoplasm — supply the zygote/embryo after fertilisation.
- **Jelly coat that changes at fertilisation** — stops more than one sperm entering.

### Puberty and the menstrual cycle

- **Testosterone** (testes): male secondary sexual characteristics — growth of facial and body hair, voice deepens, muscles develop, sperm production starts.
- **Oestrogen** (ovaries): female secondary sexual characteristics — breasts develop, hips widen, body hair grows, menstrual cycle starts.

Menstrual cycle (about 28 days), changes only — no hormones needed:

| Days (approx.) | Uterus lining | Ovary |
|---|---|---|
| 1–5 | lining breaks down and is lost (menstruation) | an egg starts to develop |
| 6–13 | lining thickens again (repairs, builds blood vessels) | egg matures |
| about 14 | lining thick | **ovulation**: egg released into the oviduct |
| 15–28 | lining stays thick, ready for an embryo | if the egg is not fertilised, the lining breaks down at the end of the cycle |

### STIs and HIV

- **STI**: an infection that is transmitted through sexual contact.
- **HIV** (human immunodeficiency virus) is a pathogen that causes an STI; HIV infection may lead to **AIDS**.
- Transmission of HIV: unprotected sexual intercourse; blood-to-blood contact (shared needles, unscreened blood transfusions); from mother to baby (during pregnancy, at birth, in breast milk).
- Controlling the spread of STIs: using condoms; limiting the number of sexual partners; testing and treating people (and their partners); not sharing needles; screening donated blood; education.

### Paper 6 skills on this topic

- **Germination experiment**: four tubes of 20 seeds — control (water, air, 20 °C); no water (dry); no oxygen (boiled, cooled water with a layer of oil); cold (water, air, 4 °C in a fridge). Count germinated seeds after a few days.
- % germination = number germinated ÷ total × 100.
- Controlled variables: same species, number and size of seeds, same time. Many seeds per tube make the percentage more reliable.
- **Flower/seed dissection**: draw with clear single lines, no shading, ruled label lines; measure and calculate magnification = image size ÷ actual size.

## Worked examples

**1.** Explain how the anthers and stigmas of a wind-pollinated flower are adapted. [3]

- anthers hang outside the flower / on long filaments (1)
- so pollen is easily blown away by the wind (1)
- large, feathery stigmas outside the flower catch pollen from the air (1)

**2.** 20 seeds were placed in each tube. In the tube kept at 4 °C, 3 germinated. Calculate the percentage germination and explain the result. [3]

- 3 ÷ 20 × 100 = **15 %** (1)
- temperature too low (1)
- enzymes work slowly / have little kinetic energy (1)

**3.** State **two** adaptations of a sperm cell and explain each. [4]

- flagellum (1) to swim to the egg (1)
- many mitochondria (1) to release energy (by respiration) for swimming (1)
- (or) acrosome containing enzymes (1) to digest the jelly coat (1)

**4.** Discuss one advantage and one disadvantage of asexual reproduction to a population of plants in the wild. [2]

- advantage: only one parent needed / rapid, so the population increases quickly (1)
- disadvantage: no variation, so a new disease or environmental change could kill all of them (1)

## Common mistakes

- Confusing **pollination** (pollen moves from anther to stigma) with **fertilisation** (nuclei fuse).
- Saying fertilisation happens in the uterus — in humans it is in the **oviduct**.
- Forgetting that **light is not needed** for germination.
- Saying the egg "swims" — only sperm have a flagellum.
- Writing "HIV is AIDS" — HIV is the virus (pathogen); AIDS is a condition it may lead to.
- Answering menstrual-cycle questions with hormone names; for 0654 describe the **changes** in the ovary and uterus lining.
- In "compare" questions, giving only one gamete — write both sides ("sperm are small, whereas eggs are large").

8. Boiling removes dissolved oxygen (1); oil stops oxygen from the air dissolving back into the water (1).
