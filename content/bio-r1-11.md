# Reproduction

> **In the exam:** a large topic. Paper 2 has 3–5 questions (flower parts, pollination, gametes, placenta, hormones). Paper 4 usually has a full question (10–15 marks) on either flowers/pollination/germination or human reproduction (menstrual cycle hormones, placenta, sperm/egg adaptations, HIV). Paper 6 often tests germination experiments (variables: water, oxygen, temperature) and drawing a flower or seed.

## What you need to know

- Define asexual and sexual reproduction; Extended: discuss their advantages and disadvantages.
- Define fertilisation; state that gametes are haploid and the zygote diploid.
- Identify the parts of insect-pollinated and wind-pollinated flowers and state their functions; define pollination, self-pollination and cross-pollination; Extended: discuss their implications for a species.
- Describe the growth of the pollen tube and fertilisation; investigate the conditions needed for germination (water, oxygen, suitable temperature).
- Identify the male and female reproductive systems and their functions; describe fertilisation; compare sperm and egg cells (Extended: adaptive features).
- Describe early development: zygote → embryo → fetus; implantation; the functions of the amniotic sac, amniotic fluid, placenta and umbilical cord (Extended: exchange across the placenta; some pathogens and toxins can pass).
- Describe the roles of testosterone and oestrogen in secondary sexual characteristics; describe the menstrual cycle; Extended: the roles of FSH, LH, oestrogen and progesterone, and the sites of production of oestrogen and progesterone.
- Describe how HIV is transmitted and how its spread can be controlled; Extended: explain how HIV affects the immune system.

## Notes

### Asexual and sexual reproduction

- **Asexual reproduction**: a process resulting in the production of genetically identical offspring from one parent.
- **Sexual reproduction**: a process involving the fusion of the nuclei of two gametes to form a zygote and the production of offspring that are genetically different from each other.
- **Fertilisation**: the fusion of the nuclei of gametes. Gametes are **haploid**; the zygote is **diploid**.

Extended:

| | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Asexual | one parent only; fast; good features kept; no need for pollinators/mates | no variation, so a change in environment or a disease may kill all; competition and overcrowding |
| Sexual | variation → some may survive environmental change; natural selection can occur; seeds dispersed | needs two parents/pollinators; slower; more energy |

### Flowers

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| Sepals | protect the flower in bud |
| Petals | (insect-pollinated) brightly coloured, scented, to attract insects |
| Stamen = anther + filament | anther makes pollen (male gametes); filament holds the anther |
| Carpel = stigma + style + ovary | stigma receives pollen; style supports the stigma; ovary contains ovules (female gametes) |
| Nectary | makes nectar to attract insects |

| Insect-pollinated | Wind-pollinated |
|---|---|
| large, coloured, scented petals; nectar | small, green/dull petals or none; no nectar |
| anthers inside the flower | anthers hang outside on long filaments |
| stigma inside, sticky | large, feathery stigma outside the flower |
| pollen larger, sticky/spiky, less of it | pollen small, light, smooth, large amounts |

- **Pollination**: the transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma.
- **Self-pollination**: the transfer of pollen grains from the anther of a flower to the stigma of the same flower or a different flower on the same plant.
- **Cross-pollination**: the transfer of pollen grains from the anther of a flower to the stigma of a flower on a different plant of the same species.
- Extended implications: self-pollination gives **less variation**, so the species is less able to adapt to change; cross-pollination gives **more variation** but needs pollinators (risk if pollinators decline).

**Fertilisation in a flower**: a **pollen tube** grows from the pollen grain down through the **style** to the **ovule** (through the micropyle); the male nucleus passes down and fuses with the female nucleus in the ovule. The ovule becomes a **seed**; the ovary becomes the **fruit**.

**Germination** needs **water**, **oxygen** and a **suitable temperature** (light is not needed).
- Water: activates enzymes, makes the seed swell, transport of food stores.
- Oxygen: for aerobic respiration.
- Temperature: for enzyme activity.

### Human reproductive systems

| Male | Function |
|---|---|
| Testes | produce sperm and testosterone |
| Scrotum | holds testes outside the body (cooler) |
| Sperm duct | carries sperm to the urethra |
| Prostate gland | adds fluid (semen) giving nutrients for sperm |
| Urethra | carries semen (and urine) out |
| Penis | deposits semen in the vagina |

| Female | Function |
|---|---|
| Ovaries | produce eggs, oestrogen and progesterone |
| Oviduct | carries egg to uterus; **site of fertilisation** |
| Uterus | where the embryo implants and develops |
| Cervix | ring of muscle at the base of the uterus |
| Vagina | receives sperm; birth canal |

### Gametes

| | Sperm | Egg |
|---|---|---|
| Size | very small | much larger |
| Number | very many | one per month |
| Movement | swims with a **flagellum** | moved by cilia in oviduct |
| Extended adaptations | **many mitochondria** (energy for swimming); **acrosome** with enzymes to digest the jelly coat; haploid nucleus | **energy stores** in cytoplasm; **jelly coat** that changes after fertilisation so only one sperm enters; haploid nucleus |

### Pregnancy

- Fertilisation in the oviduct → **zygote** divides → **embryo** (ball of cells) → **implantation** in the uterus lining → **fetus** (when organs form).
- **Amniotic sac** contains **amniotic fluid**, which protects the fetus from mechanical shock.
- **Umbilical cord**: contains blood vessels joining the fetus to the placenta.
- **Placenta** (Extended): exchange between maternal and fetal blood (they do not mix):
  - to fetus: **glucose, amino acids, fatty acids, oxygen, water, ions**;
  - from fetus: **carbon dioxide, urea**;
  - some **pathogens** (e.g. rubella virus) and **toxins** (e.g. nicotine) can pass across.

### Puberty hormones and the menstrual cycle

- **Testosterone** (testes): male secondary sexual characteristics — growth of facial and body hair, deepening voice, muscle development, sperm production.
- **Oestrogen** (ovaries): female secondary sexual characteristics — breast growth, widening hips, menstrual cycle starts, body hair.
- Menstrual cycle (about 28 days): days 1–5 menstruation (lining shed); lining rebuilds; **ovulation** around day 14; lining maintained; if no fertilisation, lining breaks down.

Extended — hormones:

| Hormone | Made in | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FSH | pituitary | stimulates development of a follicle (egg) in the ovary and oestrogen production |
| Oestrogen | ovaries (follicle) | thickens/repairs the uterus lining; (high levels) trigger LH release |
| LH | pituitary | triggers **ovulation** |
| Progesterone | ovaries (corpus luteum); **placenta** in pregnancy | maintains the uterus lining |

- If no pregnancy, progesterone falls → lining breaks down (menstruation). In pregnancy, the **placenta** makes progesterone and oestrogen.

### HIV

- Transmitted by **sexual intercourse**, **blood** (shared needles, transfusions of unscreened blood), mother to baby (across placenta, at birth, in breast milk).
- Control: condoms; not sharing needles; screening blood; antiretroviral drugs; testing; education.
- Extended: HIV infects and destroys **lymphocytes**, so fewer antibodies are made; the person cannot fight infections → **AIDS**.

### Paper 6 skills on this topic

- **Germination**: tubes with seeds — (A) water, air, 20 °C (control); (B) dry; (C) water, no oxygen (boiled water with oil layer, or pyrogallol); (D) water, air, 4 °C (fridge). Count % germinated after a few days.
- Controlled: same species, same number of seeds, same time. Use many seeds (e.g. 20 per tube) for reliable percentages.
- % germination = number germinated ÷ total × 100.
- **Flower dissection / drawing**: label sepals, petals, stamen, carpel; measure and calculate magnification.

## Worked examples

**1.** Explain how a wind-pollinated flower is adapted. [3]

- anthers outside flower / on long filaments so pollen blown away (1)
- large, feathery stigma to catch pollen (1)
- large amounts of small, light pollen (1)

**2.** Extended: explain the role of progesterone in the menstrual cycle and in pregnancy. [3]

- made by the corpus luteum / ovary (1)
- maintains the lining of the uterus (1)
- in pregnancy, made by the placenta so lining is kept and no menstruation (1)

**3.** 20 seeds were placed in each tube. In the tube kept at 4 °C, 3 germinated. Calculate the percentage germination and explain the result. [3]

- 3 ÷ 20 × 100 = **15 %** (1)
- low temperature (1)
- enzymes have little kinetic energy / work slowly (1)

**4.** Extended: state **two** adaptations of a sperm cell and explain each. [4]

- flagellum (1) to swim to the egg (1)
- many mitochondria (1) release energy for swimming (1)
- (or) acrosome with enzymes (1) to digest the jelly coat / enter the egg (1)

## Common mistakes

- Confusing **pollination** (pollen to stigma) with **fertilisation** (fusion of nuclei).
- Saying the pollen grain travels down the style — the **pollen tube** grows; the **nucleus** travels.
- Saying fertilisation happens in the uterus — it is in the **oviduct**.
- Saying maternal and fetal blood mix in the placenta.
- Forgetting **light is not needed** for germination.
- Mixing up which hormone triggers ovulation (**LH**) and which maintains the lining (**progesterone**).
- Writing "HIV is AIDS" — HIV is the virus; AIDS is the condition.

6. FSH.
