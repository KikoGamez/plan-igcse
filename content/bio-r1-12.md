# Inheritance, variation and selection

> **In the exam:** In Co-ordinated Sciences 0654 (B16, B17) this topic is narrower than in Biology 0610: **no** protein synthesis/mRNA, codominance, blood-group crosses, sex linkage, test crosses, stem cells, sickle-cell anaemia, xerophytes/hydrophytes or causes of mutation. Genetic crosses are limited to **1 : 1 and 3 : 1** ratios. **Paper 2** (Multiple Choice, 45 min, 40 four-option questions across Biology, Chemistry and Physics, 30 %) often has a definition or a ratio question. **Paper 4** (Theory, 2 h, 120 marks, 50 %): Biology questions appear alongside Chemistry and Physics (Q1–4 are Biology in the 2025 specimen, about 9–11 marks each, often mixing topics); a genetic diagram or pedigree (3–5 marks) and a natural selection or selective breeding explanation (3–5 marks) are likely parts. **Paper 6** (Alternative to Practical, 1 h 30 min, 60 marks, 20 %) lists **continuous and discontinuous variation** as a practical context: measuring a sample, tallying, drawing histograms or bar charts.

## What you need to know

**Core**
- State that chromosomes are made of DNA, which contains genetic information in the form of genes.
- Define a gene as a length of DNA that codes for a protein; define an allele as an alternative form of a gene.
- Describe the inheritance of sex in humans with reference to XX and XY chromosomes.
- Describe inheritance as the transmission of genetic information from generation to generation.
- Describe genotype as the genetic make-up of an organism and in terms of the alleles present; phenotype as the observable features of an organism.
- Describe homozygous as having two identical alleles of a particular gene; state that two identical homozygous individuals that breed together will be pure-breeding.
- Describe heterozygous as having two different alleles of a particular gene; state that a heterozygous individual will not be pure-breeding.
- Describe a dominant allele as an allele that is expressed if it is present in the genotype; a recessive allele as an allele that is only expressed when there is no dominant allele of the gene present in the genotype.
- Interpret pedigree diagrams; use genetic diagrams to predict the results of monohybrid crosses and calculate phenotypic ratios, limited to 1 : 1 and 3 : 1; use Punnett squares in crosses which result in more than one genotype.
- Describe variation as differences between individuals of the same species; continuous variation (a range of phenotypes between two extremes, e.g. body length) and discontinuous variation (a limited number of phenotypes with no intermediates, e.g. ABO blood groups).
- Describe mutation as a genetic change; state that mutation is the way in which new alleles are formed.
- Describe natural selection and selective breeding (see notes); outline how selective breeding by artificial selection is carried out over many generations to improve crop plants and domesticated animals, and apply this to given contexts.

**Supplement**
- Describe a haploid nucleus as a nucleus containing a single set of chromosomes; a diploid nucleus as a nucleus containing two sets of chromosomes; state that in a diploid cell there is a pair of each type of chromosome and in a human diploid cell there are 23 pairs.
- Describe mitosis as nuclear division giving rise to genetically identical cells; state its role in growth, repair of damaged tissues, replacement of cells and asexual reproduction; state that the exact replication of chromosomes occurs before mitosis and that during mitosis the copies separate, maintaining the chromosome number.
- State that meiosis is involved in the production of gametes; describe meiosis as reduction division in which the chromosome number is halved from diploid to haploid resulting in genetically different cells.
- Describe the development of strains of antibiotic-resistant bacteria as an example of natural selection.

## Notes

### Chromosomes, genes and alleles

- **Chromosomes** are made of **DNA**, which contains genetic information in the form of **genes**.
- **Gene**: a length of DNA that codes for a protein.
- **Allele**: an alternative form of a gene (e.g. the gene for flower colour has a purple allele and a white allele).
- **Sex inheritance**: females **XX**, males **XY**. Every egg carries an X; sperm carry X or Y, so each child has a 50 % (1 : 1) chance of being male or female.

| | X (mother) | X (mother) |
|---|---|---|
| **X** (father) | XX female | XX female |
| **Y** (father) | XY male | XY male |

Supplement:
- **Haploid nucleus**: a nucleus containing a single set of chromosomes (human gametes: 23 chromosomes).
- **Diploid nucleus**: a nucleus containing two sets of chromosomes; there is a pair of each type of chromosome — **23 pairs** in a human diploid cell.

### Cell division (Supplement)

- **Mitosis**: nuclear division giving rise to genetically identical cells (details of the stages not required).
  - Roles: **growth, repair of damaged tissues, replacement of cells, asexual reproduction**.
  - The **exact replication of chromosomes** occurs before mitosis; during mitosis the **copies separate**, so each daughter cell keeps the same chromosome number.
- **Meiosis**: reduction division in which the chromosome number is halved from diploid to haploid, resulting in genetically different cells. Involved in the production of **gametes**.

| | Mitosis | Meiosis |
|---|---|---|
| Daughter cells | genetically identical | genetically different |
| Chromosome number | kept the same (diploid → diploid) | halved (diploid → haploid) |
| Used for | growth, repair, replacement, asexual reproduction | production of gametes |

### Monohybrid inheritance

- **Inheritance**: the transmission of genetic information from generation to generation.
- **Genotype**: the genetic make-up of an organism in terms of the alleles present (e.g. Tt).
- **Phenotype**: the observable features of an organism (e.g. tall).
- **Homozygous**: having two identical alleles of a particular gene (TT or tt). Two identical homozygous individuals that breed together will be **pure-breeding**.
- **Heterozygous**: having two different alleles of a particular gene (Tt). A heterozygous individual will **not** be pure-breeding.
- **Dominant allele**: an allele that is expressed if it is present in the genotype (capital letter).
- **Recessive allele**: an allele that is only expressed when there is no dominant allele of the gene present in the genotype (lower-case letter).

**Laying out a genetic diagram** (a mark is often given for each stage):
1. Parental phenotypes
2. Parental genotypes
3. Gametes (one allele each, circled)
4. Punnett square / offspring genotypes
5. Offspring phenotypes
6. Ratio

Tall (T) is dominant to short (t). **Tt × Tt**:

| | T | t |
|---|---|---|
| **T** | TT | Tt |
| **t** | Tt | tt |

Genotypes 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt → phenotypes **3 tall : 1 short**.

**Tt × tt**:

| | T | t |
|---|---|---|
| **t** | Tt | tt |
| **t** | Tt | tt |

→ **1 tall : 1 short**.

Also: TT × tt → all Tt (all tall); TT × TT or tt × tt → all the same (pure-breeding).

**Pedigree diagrams** (squares = males, circles = females, shaded = affected):
- If two **unaffected** parents have an **affected** child, the condition is caused by a **recessive** allele and both parents must be **heterozygous**.
- An affected (recessive) person must be homozygous recessive (e.g. aa).

### Variation

- **Variation**: differences between individuals of the same species.
- **Continuous variation**: a range of phenotypes between two extremes, e.g. **body length**, height, mass → plot a **histogram** (bars touching).
- **Discontinuous variation**: a limited number of phenotypes with no intermediates, e.g. **ABO blood groups** (A, B, AB, O) → plot a **bar chart** (bars separate).
- **Mutation**: a genetic change. Mutation is the way in which **new alleles** are formed.

### Natural selection

Learn these five points in order (syllabus list):
1. **Genetic variation** within populations.
2. **Production of many offspring**.
3. **Struggle for survival**, including **competition for resources** (food, water, space, mates).
4. A **greater chance of reproduction** by individuals that are **better adapted** to the environment than others.
5. These individuals **pass on their alleles** to the next generation.

Over many generations the population changes, with more individuals carrying the useful alleles.

**Supplement — antibiotic-resistant bacteria**:
- A random **mutation** gives some bacteria resistance to an antibiotic.
- When the antibiotic is used, non-resistant bacteria are killed; resistant bacteria survive.
- Resistant bacteria **reproduce** (asexually, quickly) and pass on the **resistance allele**.
- A **strain** of resistant bacteria develops (e.g. MRSA). This is why antibiotics should only be used when essential.

### Selective breeding

Described with reference to:
1. **Selection by humans** of individuals with **desirable features**;
2. **crossing** these individuals to produce the next generation;
3. **selection of offspring** showing the desirable features;
4. repeated over **many generations**.

Examples: crop plants with high yield, disease resistance or drought tolerance; cows with high milk yield; sheep with more wool; dogs with a calm temperament.
Key contrast: in **selective breeding humans** choose which individuals breed; in **natural selection** the **environment** determines which individuals survive and reproduce.

### Paper 6 skills on this topic

- Measure a feature (e.g. leaf length or hand span) in a sample of 20–30; choose **equal class widths** (e.g. 30–39 mm, 40–49 mm), tally, draw a **histogram** with no gaps for continuous data.
- Discontinuous data (e.g. tongue rolling: yes/no; blood group) → **bar chart** with gaps.
- Larger, random samples are more representative; state units in column headings.

## Worked examples

**1.** In pea plants, purple flowers (P) are dominant to white (p). Two heterozygous plants are crossed. Use a genetic diagram to show the expected ratio of offspring phenotypes. [4]

- parental genotypes Pp × Pp (1)
- gametes P, p and P, p (1)
- offspring genotypes PP, Pp, Pp, pp (1)
- 3 purple : 1 white (1)

**2.** A heterozygous purple plant is crossed with a white plant. 120 seeds grow. Predict how many will have white flowers. [2]

- Pp × pp gives 1 purple : 1 white (1)
- 120 ÷ 2 = **60 white** (1)

**3.** Explain how a population of insects becomes resistant to an insecticide. [4]

- genetic variation: a mutation gives some insects resistance (1)
- insecticide kills non-resistant insects (1)
- resistant insects survive and reproduce (1)
- pass on the resistance allele to the next generation; over many generations most are resistant (1)

**4.** Supplement: state **two** differences between mitosis and meiosis. [2]

- mitosis gives genetically identical cells; meiosis gives genetically different cells (1)
- mitosis keeps the chromosome number; meiosis halves it (diploid to haploid) / meiosis produces gametes (1)

## Common mistakes

- Confusing **gene** and **allele**.
- Writing gametes with two letters — each gamete carries **one** allele.
- Leaving out the **phenotypes and ratio** at the end of a genetic diagram.
- Saying organisms "adapt because they need to" — mutation is **random**; the environment then selects.
- Saying mitosis produces gametes (it is **meiosis**).
- Drawing a bar chart for continuous data (it needs a **histogram**).
- Writing "3 : 1" when the question asks for numbers — convert the ratio into numbers of offspring.
