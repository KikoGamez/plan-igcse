# Co-ordinated Sciences · Paper 4 timed (1/2)

> **Goal:** sit the first hour of a complete Co-ordinated Sciences Paper 4 (Extended theory) under exam conditions, stopping at the planned point so you can finish it in the next session.

## Before you start

- **Paper**: one Cambridge IGCSE Co-ordinated Sciences **0654 Paper 4** (Theory, Extended — components 41, 42 or 43) that you have **not** done. Best choice: May/June 2025 or Oct/Nov 2025, the first series on the current 2025–2027 syllabus. If you have done those, use 2024 or 2023: the format (2 hours, 120 marks) is the same, but a few questions may test topics that were removed in 2025 (for Biology, e.g. kidney, eye, tropisms, genetic modification). Skip those parts and note them.
- **Not** the Biology 0610 paper: your exam is the combined paper with Biology, Chemistry **and** Physics questions.
- Do **not** open the mark scheme or examiner report — they are for the "Mark Paper 4" session.
- **Timer** set to 60 minutes.
- Black or blue pen, HB pencil (graphs and diagrams), ruler, eraser, **calculator** (allowed in Paper 4), protractor. The paper includes the Periodic Table, and the front cover tells you to take the weight of 1.0 kg as 9.8 N (g = 9.8 m/s²); learn the physics equations, as you should not expect them to be given.
- Quiet room, phone in another room. Print the paper if you can and write in the answer spaces.

## Exam format reminder

- **Paper 4 Theory (Extended): 2 hours, 120 marks, 50 % of the IGCSE** (the Double Award grade, A*A* to GG).
- Short-answer and structured questions, all compulsory, on the Core **and** Supplement content of all three sciences.
- In the Cambridge specimen paper there are **12 questions of about 7–14 marks each**: Questions **1–4 Biology**, **5–8 Chemistry**, **9–12 Physics** — roughly 40 marks per science. Real papers follow a similar pattern, but check the order on your paper.
- Pace: **1 mark per minute**. 120 marks in 120 minutes leaves almost no spare time, so you cannot afford to get stuck.

### Splitting the paper across two sessions

The paper is 2 hours, so it fits exactly into two 60-minute sessions:

| Session | Time on the clock | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **This session (1/2)** | 0:00 – 1:00 | about 60 marks: all of Biology (Q1–4, ~40 marks) and roughly the first half of Chemistry (Q5–6) |
| Next session (2/2) | 1:00 – 2:00 | the rest: Chemistry Q7–8 and all of Physics (Q9–12), then checking |

**Where to stop:** stop when the timer reaches 60:00, **wherever you are**, even mid-question. Draw a line under your last answer and write the time and the question number (e.g. "60:00 — stopped at 6(b)(ii)"). Do not "just finish this part": the split is only realistic if the two halves add up to exactly 2 hours. If you can find a free 2-hour block before the mock, do the whole paper in one sitting instead — that is the best practice of all.

## How to do it

1. **0–2 min**: flick through the whole paper. Find where Biology ends and Chemistry starts, and add up the marks in Q1–6 so you know your 60-mark target.
2. **2–42 min**: Biology, Q1–4 (about 40 marks → about 40 minutes). Look at the clock after each question: after Q2 you should be at about 22 minutes.
3. **42–60 min**: Chemistry, starting with Q5. Keep going at a mark a minute.
4. If you are stuck for more than a minute, write something relevant (a key term may earn a mark), put an asterisk in the margin and move on. You will come back in session 2 only if time allows.
5. **At 60:00**: stop, draw the line, record the time and question. Put the paper in a folder unmarked and unchanged — do not look up answers.
6. If you finished your target early, **do not** start Physics: use the spare minutes to check your Biology answers (units, ratios, labels).

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Count the marks**: a 3-mark "explain" needs three separate, relevant points. The answer space is a guide to length.
- **Command words** (Cambridge list):
  - *State / Identify / Give*: short answer, no explanation.
  - *Describe*: say what happens or what the data show — trend plus quoted values with units.
  - *Explain*: give reasons — "because…", linked to science (diffusion, enzymes, water potential).
  - *Suggest*: apply knowledge to an unfamiliar context; any sensible scientific answer may score.
  - *Calculate / Determine*: show working, give the unit, use the significant figures asked for.
  - *Compare*: make comparative points about both things ("A is …, whereas B is …").
- **Biology questions in this paper** are usually context-based and mix topics (e.g. a photosynthesis experiment followed by translocation and uses of carbohydrates; a disease question moving to vaccination and antibodies). Expect a table or graph to read, a balanced equation (photosynthesis or aerobic respiration), a genetic diagram or Punnett square, and definitions in syllabus wording.
- **Graph and data parts**: quote two values with units, state the trend and any peak or plateau.
- **Genetic diagrams**: parental phenotypes and genotypes, gametes (circled, one allele each), offspring genotypes, phenotypes and ratio (1 : 1 or 3 : 1).
- **Chemistry part**: balanced symbol equations with state symbols when asked; moles calculations step by step.
- Examiners' reports regularly mention vague answers ("it helps the body"), missing units, confusing similar terms (glucose / glycogen / glucagon; xylem / phloem) and describing when asked to explain.

## Checklist while marking

You will mark in the "Mark Paper 4" session, after finishing the paper. For now:
- Keep the paper exactly as written — no corrections in pen.
- On a sticky note, list the parts you guessed or left blank and why (didn't know / ran out of time / misread). This lets you tell knowledge gaps from timing problems when you mark.
- Note your time at the end of Q4 (end of Biology): if it was well over 45 minutes, Biology is costing you time.

## Quick-fire revision

Read once before you start the timer:
- Osmosis (Supplement wording): net movement of water molecules from higher water potential (dilute solution) to lower water potential (concentrated solution) through a partially permeable membrane.
- Photosynthesis: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂. Aerobic respiration: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O. Anaerobic in muscles: glucose → lactic acid.
- Enzymes and temperature: kinetic energy → more effective collisions → optimum → active site changes shape → substrate no longer fits → denatured.
- Transpiration: evaporation from mesophyll cell surfaces into air spaces, diffusion of water vapour out through stomata; faster with higher temperature and wind speed, slower with higher humidity.
- Double circulation: blood passes through the heart twice per circuit; higher pressure to the body.
- Vaccination: weakened pathogens or antigens → lymphocytes make antibodies → memory cells → long-term immunity.
- Oxygen debt: fast heart rate carries lactic acid to the liver; deeper, faster breathing supplies oxygen; lactic acid respired aerobically in the liver.
- Blood glucose: high → insulin → liver converts glucose to glycogen; low → glucagon → glycogen to glucose.
- Magnification = image size ÷ actual size; 1 mm = 1000 µm.
