# Past paper · Paper 4 timed

> **Goal:** sit a complete Paper 4 (Extended theory) in exactly 1 hour 15 minutes under exam conditions, so you can mark it carefully in the next session.

## Before you start

- **Paper**: a Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 Paper 4 (Extended theory, components 41/42/43) from a recent series you have **not** done — e.g. May/June 2025 or Oct/Nov 2025; otherwise 2024. Papers from 2023 onwards match the current syllabus content most closely.
- Do **not** look at the mark scheme or examiner report yet — they are for the next session ("Mark Paper 4").
- **Timer** set to 1 h 15 min.
- Black/blue pen, pencil (for graphs and diagrams), ruler, eraser, calculator.
- Quiet room, phone away. Print the paper if possible; writing in the answer spaces trains you to match the space to the marks.

## Exam format reminder

- **1 hour 15 minutes, 80 marks, 50 %** of the IGCSE — the most important paper.
- Usually **6 structured questions**, each 10–17 marks, made of short parts (1–5 marks each).
- Covers the whole Extended syllabus; expect diagrams to label, data/graph analysis, calculations, definitions, "explain" questions and at least one longer extended answer.
- Pace: about **1 mark per minute** (80 marks in 75 minutes) — e.g. a 14-mark question should take about 13 minutes.

## How to do it

1. **0–2 min**: flick through the paper. Note the total marks per question and where the long answers are.
2. **2–72 min**: answer in order (or start with your strongest question). Keep checking the time at the end of each question: after question 3 you should be at about 37 minutes.
3. If stuck for more than 1 minute, write something relevant (a key term may score), mark it with an asterisk and move on.
4. **72–75 min**: return to asterisked parts; check every calculation has a unit and every graph part is complete.
5. Stop at exactly 1 h 15 min. Write your finishing time and any unanswered parts on the front.
6. Remaining minutes of the hour: write a few lines on how it went — which questions felt hard, where you ran short of time. Put the paper away unmarked.

(The paper takes 75 minutes, which is more than this 60-minute slot — plan the session so you have 80 minutes free, or start 15 minutes early.)

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Count the marks**: a 3-mark "explain" needs three separate, relevant points.
- **Command words**:
  - *State / name*: a short answer, no explanation.
  - *Describe*: say what happens / what the data show (trends with data quotes), no reasons.
  - *Explain*: give reasons — use "because", link to biology (e.g. enzymes, diffusion, osmosis).
  - *Suggest*: apply knowledge to a new situation; any sensible biological answer may score.
  - *Calculate*: show working, give units, use the decimal places requested.
  - *Compare*: give similarities and differences with both sides in each point ("A is…, whereas B is…").
- **Data/graph questions**: describe the trend, quote values **with units** from the graph (e.g. "rises from 2 to 14 arbitrary units between 10 and 35 °C"), mention any plateau or peak.
- **Definitions**: learn the syllabus wording — mark schemes reward it almost word for word.
- **Use the key terms** examiners look for: water potential, partially permeable membrane, active site, complementary, denatured, kinetic energy, concentration gradient, one cell thick, surface area, effective collisions, memory cells.
- **Genetic diagrams**: always set out parents, gametes, offspring genotypes, phenotypes and ratio.
- **Extended answers**: write in logical order; one idea per sentence; don't repeat the question.
- Examiner reports repeatedly note: answers that are too vague ("it helps the body"), confusing terms (glycogen/glucagon, xylem/phloem), forgetting units, and describing when asked to explain.

## Checklist while marking

You will mark this paper in the next session. Before then:
- Keep the paper as you wrote it — do not correct it in pen.
- Note any questions where you ran out of time or guessed, so you can tell knowledge gaps from timing problems.
- When marking, use a different colour; record each lost mark in the error log with topic, reason and correct wording.

## Quick-fire revision

- Turgid / flaccid / plasmolysis; water potential.
- Enzyme graphs: kinetic energy → collisions → enzyme–substrate complexes; denaturation → active site shape changes.
- Limiting factors: whichever factor rises makes the rate rise.
- Transpiration: evaporation from mesophyll surfaces, diffusion of water vapour through stomata; humidity decreases rate.
- Double circulation: high pressure to body, low to lungs.
- Vaccination: antigens → lymphocytes → antibodies + memory cells.
- Oxygen debt: fast heart rate (lactic acid to liver), deep fast breathing (oxygen), aerobic respiration of lactic acid in liver.
- Nephron: filtration at glomerulus; all glucose reabsorbed in proximal convoluted tubule; water reabsorbed in loop of Henle and collecting duct.
- Synapse: vesicles → neurotransmitter diffuses → binds receptors → impulse.
- Menstrual cycle: FSH (follicle), LH (ovulation), oestrogen (builds lining), progesterone (maintains lining).
- Natural selection: variation → competition → survival of best adapted → reproduce → pass on alleles.
- GM: restriction enzymes, sticky ends, plasmid, ligase, recombinant plasmid, bacteria, expression.
