# Co-ordinated Sciences · Paper 4 timed (2/2)

> **Goal:** finish the Co-ordinated Sciences Paper 4 you started last session, in exactly the second hour of the 2-hour paper, so the whole paper has been done in real exam time.

## Before you start

- The **same paper** you started in session 1/2, with your line and note showing where you stopped (e.g. "60:00 — stopped at 6(b)(ii)").
- Still **no** mark scheme or examiner report — you mark everything in the next session ("Mark Paper 4").
- **Timer** set to 60 minutes. This is the second half of the 2-hour paper: when it reaches 60:00 the "exam" is over.
- Pen, HB pencil, ruler, eraser, calculator, protractor. Quiet room, phone away.
- Do **not** go back and revise the Biology you answered last time. In the real exam you could, but you would be using time you no longer have. The only exception is step 4 below.

## Exam format reminder

- **Paper 4 Theory (Extended): 2 hours, 120 marks, 50 % of the IGCSE.** Short-answer and structured questions on Core and Supplement content of Biology, Chemistry and Physics.
- In the Cambridge specimen paper: Q1–4 Biology, Q5–8 Chemistry, Q9–12 Physics, each question about 7–14 marks (about 40 marks per science).
- You should have about **60 marks left**: roughly Chemistry Q7–8 and Physics Q9–12. If you stopped earlier than planned, you are behind: aim to make up the time on the questions you find easiest, and accept that some parts may stay blank.

## How to do it

1. **0–1 min**: find your stopping point. Count the marks left and work out your pace: marks left ÷ 59 minutes. If it is more than 1 mark per minute, decide now which questions you will do first (your strongest science or the highest-mark questions).
2. **1–20 min**: finish Chemistry (from where you stopped to the end of Q8).
3. **20–58 min**: Physics, Q9–12. Check the clock after each question: you should finish Q10 by about 39 minutes.
4. **58–60 min**: return to any asterisked parts from either session. Check every calculation has a unit, every graph has a line, and every "compare" has both sides.
5. **At 60:00**: stop. Write on the front: total time used (should be 2:00), parts left blank, parts guessed. Put the paper away unmarked.
6. If anything is left over in the hour, write three lines: Which science cost the most time? Which questions felt unfamiliar? Did you run out of time anywhere?

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Keep the pace honest**: 1 mark per minute. A 2-mark part should not take 5 minutes. If you cannot see how to start within a minute, write the relevant equation or key term and move on.
- **Physics calculations**: write the equation in symbols, substitute with units, give the answer with a unit and sensible significant figures. A correct method can earn marks even if the final answer is wrong.
- **Chemistry**: balanced equations (check atoms on both sides), state symbols when asked, moles method shown line by line.
- **Mixed science contexts**: a question can bring in ideas from another science (e.g. a Biology question asking for an energy transfer, or a Chemistry question on carbon dioxide and the carbon cycle). Use the right vocabulary for the science being tested.
- **Command words**: describe = what; explain = why; suggest = apply to a new context; calculate = working plus unit; state = brief.
- **Extended prose** parts: one idea per sentence, in logical order, using key terms. Do not repeat the question.
- Leave nothing blank in the last minutes — a sensible attempt at a 1-mark "state" part is more likely to score than nothing.

## Checklist while marking

Marking happens next session, with the mark scheme and the examiner report for **all three sciences**. Before you put the paper away:
- Note any part where you guessed, so you can separate lucky marks from secure ones.
- Note where the time went: end time of Biology (Q4), Chemistry (Q8) and Physics (Q12).
- Bring the paper, a different-coloured pen and your error log to the marking session.

## Quick-fire revision

Last check before starting the timer (mostly the sciences still to come, plus Biology links that appear in mixed questions):
- Physics: speed = distance ÷ time; F = ma; W = mg; work done = F × d; P = W ÷ t; V = IR; P = IV; ρ = m ÷ V; v = fλ; efficiency = useful output ÷ total input × 100 %.
- Chemistry: amount of substance (mol) = mass (g) ÷ molar mass (g/mol); 1 mol of any gas = 24 dm³ at r.t.p. (1 dm³ = 1000 cm³); concentration can be measured in g/dm³.
- Carbon cycle links: photosynthesis removes CO₂; respiration, decomposition and combustion return it.
- Energy transfer in food chains is inefficient: energy lost as heat from respiration, in uneaten parts, in egestion.
- Antibiotics kill bacteria but do not affect viruses; antibiotic resistance develops by natural selection.
