# Mark Paper 4

> **Goal:** mark the Paper 4 you sat last session exactly as an examiner would, using the mark scheme and the examiner report, and turn every lost mark into an error-log entry.

## Before you start

- Your completed Paper 4 (unmarked) from the previous session.
- The **mark scheme** for the same component and series (e.g. 0610/42 May/June 2025).
- The **examiner report** (Principal Examiner Report for Teachers) for that series — it covers all variants; find the section for your component.
- A different-coloured pen (green or red), highlighter, error log.
- No timer pressure, but keep to the plan below.

## Exam format reminder

- Paper 4: **1 h 15 min, 80 marks, 50 %** of the IGCSE; about 6 structured questions on the whole Extended syllabus.
- Marks are given for **specific points** listed in the mark scheme; each part has a maximum.

## How to do it

1. **0–5 min**: read the first pages of the mark scheme — the guidance on symbols and abbreviations.
2. **5–40 min**: mark question by question. For each part, tick each creditable point and write the mark in the margin. Be strict: if you are unsure whether your wording counts, it probably doesn't.
3. **40–50 min**: read the examiner report for each question where you lost 2 or more marks. Note what "most candidates" got wrong and the expected answer.
4. **50–60 min**: total your marks, work out a percentage, and fill in the error log.

## Exam technique for this paper

How to read a Cambridge biology mark scheme:
- **;** separates marking points — each is usually one mark.
- **/** separates alternative answers that are equally acceptable.
- **ORA** = or reverse argument; **AW** = alternative wording (same meaning accepted); **AVP** = any other valid point.
- **underlined** words must be present for the mark (e.g. "partially permeable membrane").
- **( )** brackets: words not needed for the mark.
- **R** = reject (this answer gets no mark); **I** = ignore (does not gain or lose); **A** = accept.
- **max** = there are more points than marks; you can only get the maximum.
- **ecf** = error carried forward: in calculations, if an earlier number was wrong but you used it correctly, you still get the later marks.

What examiner reports repeatedly say:
- Vague answers lose marks: "it is healthier", "helps the body", "more energy".
- Contradictions cancel marks (e.g. writing both "diffusion" and "active transport" for the same process).
- Data questions need **quoted figures with units**; "it increases" alone often gets 0.
- Units missing or wrong in calculations; rounding too early.
- Definitions not in syllabus wording.
- Describe vs explain confusion.
- Genetic diagrams without gametes, or with gametes containing two alleles.

## Checklist while marking

- [ ] Did I use the **key term** the mark scheme requires (underlined words)?
- [ ] Did I give **enough separate points** for the marks?
- [ ] Did I **quote data** and units where asked?
- [ ] Did I show **working** in calculations, with units and correct decimal places?
- [ ] Graph: correct axes orientation, labels with units, scale using over half the grid, points plotted accurately (± half a small square), suitable line?
- [ ] Did I answer the **command word** (describe vs explain vs suggest)?

For each lost mark, record in the error log:

| Q | Topic | Marks lost | Type of error | Correct answer / wording |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3(b)(ii) | Enzymes | 2 | missing key terms | "active site changes shape; substrate no longer complementary" |

Types of error: **K** knowledge gap · **T** missing key term/wording · **C** command word not answered · **D** data not quoted · **M** maths slip · **R** misread question · **S** ran out of time.

Finish by writing your three most frequent error types and the topics with most marks lost. These go to the weak-topics session.

## Quick-fire revision

Model wording that scores (learn these phrases):
- Osmosis: "water moves from a higher water potential to a lower water potential through a partially permeable membrane".
- Enzymes and heat: "active site changes shape; substrate no longer fits / complementary; enzyme is denatured".
- Diffusion surfaces: "large surface area; thin / one cell thick; short diffusion distance; steep concentration gradient maintained".
- Vaccination: "antigens stimulate lymphocytes to produce antibodies; memory cells formed; faster, larger secondary response".
- Transpiration: "water evaporates from mesophyll cell surfaces; water vapour diffuses out through stomata".
- Natural selection: "variation; competition; better adapted survive and reproduce; pass on alleles".
- Eutrophication: "decomposers / bacteria respire aerobically; dissolved oxygen decreases".
- Temperature control: "arterioles (supplying skin capillaries) dilate / constrict".
