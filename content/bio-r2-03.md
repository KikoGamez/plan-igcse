# Mark Paper 4

> **Goal:** mark the whole Co-ordinated Sciences Paper 4 you sat over the last two sessions — Biology, Chemistry and Physics — exactly as an examiner would, and turn every lost mark into an error-log entry.

## Before you start

- Your completed Paper 4 (unmarked), with your notes on guessed parts and timings.
- The **mark scheme** for exactly the same component and series (e.g. 0654/42 May/June 2025 — the variant number must match).
- The **examiner report** (Principal Examiner Report for Teachers) for that series: one document covers all components and variants of 0654; find the section for Paper 4 (it usually has comments on each question, so it covers all three sciences).
- A different-coloured pen (green or red), highlighter, calculator, error log.
- The time is tight for 120 marks: mark quickly where you are clearly right or clearly wrong, and spend your time on the borderline answers.

## Exam format reminder

- **Paper 4 Theory (Extended): 2 hours, 120 marks, 50 %** of the IGCSE Co-ordinated Sciences (Double Award).
- Short-answer and structured questions on Biology, Chemistry and Physics (in the specimen: Q1–4 Biology, Q5–8 Chemistry, Q9–12 Physics, about 40 marks each).
- Marks are awarded for **specific points** in the mark scheme; each part has a maximum.

## How to do it

1. **0–3 min**: read the mark scheme's general guidance and its list of abbreviations.
2. **3–18 min — Biology (Q1–4)**: mark each part, tick each creditable point, write the mark in the margin. Be strict: if you are not sure your wording counts, it probably does not.
3. **18–31 min — Chemistry (Q5–8)**: check equations (formulae, balancing, state symbols if asked) and calculation working.
4. **31–44 min — Physics (Q9–12)**: check equation, substitution, answer and unit separately — they are often separate marks.
5. **44–52 min — examiner report**: read the comments for every question where you lost 2 or more marks. Note the common errors and the answer the examiners expected.
6. **52–60 min**: total each science out of its marks and the whole paper out of 120; work out percentages; fill in the error log (one line per lost mark or group of marks).

## Exam technique for this paper

How to read a Cambridge science mark scheme:
- **;** separates marking points — usually one mark each.
- **/** separates alternatives that are equally acceptable; **OR** separates alternative whole answers.
- **A** = accept; **R** = reject; **I** = ignore (does not gain or lose); **AW / AWRT** = alternative wording / answer which rounds to.
- **underlined** words must be present for the mark (e.g. "partially permeable").
- **( )** words in brackets are not needed for the mark.
- **max** = more points listed than marks available.
- **ecf** = error carried forward: in a calculation, a wrong earlier value used correctly still earns later marks.
- **ORA** = or reverse argument.

What examiner reports for science papers repeatedly say:
- Vague answers lose marks: "it is healthier", "helps the body", "more energy".
- Contradictions cancel marks (e.g. giving both "xylem" and "phloem").
- Data questions need **quoted figures with units**; "it increases" alone often gets 0.
- Units missing or wrong; rounding too early; working not shown.
- Biology definitions not in syllabus wording (e.g. growth without "dry mass", respiration that "makes" energy).
- Confusing similar terms: glucose / glycogen / glucagon; xylem / phloem; antigen / antibody; mitosis / meiosis.
- Genetic diagrams without gametes, or gametes containing two alleles.
- Chemistry: unbalanced equations, wrong formulae for ions; Physics: confusing mass and weight, energy and power.

## Checklist while marking

- [ ] Did I use the **key term** the mark scheme requires?
- [ ] Did I give **enough separate points** for the marks?
- [ ] Did I **quote data** with units where asked?
- [ ] Calculations: **working**, **unit**, correct significant figures?
- [ ] Graphs: axes labelled with units, sensible scale, points accurate to half a small square, suitable line or curve?
- [ ] Did I answer the **command word** (describe vs explain vs suggest)?

Record each lost mark in the error log:

| Q | Science / topic | Marks lost | Error type | Correct answer / wording |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2(c) | Biology – immunity | 2 | T | "memory cells produced; long-term immunity" |
| 7(b) | Chemistry – moles | 1 | M | Mᵣ of CO₂ = 44, not 28 |

Error types: **K** knowledge gap · **T** missing key term/wording · **C** command word not answered · **D** data not quoted · **M** maths slip · **R** misread question · **S** ran out of time.

Finish by writing: your score for each science (e.g. Biology 29/40), your three most frequent error types, and the Biology topics with most marks lost. Chemistry and Physics errors go to the error logs for those subjects; Biology errors go to the next session ("Biology: weak topics and definitions").

## Quick-fire revision

Biology model wording that scores (0654 syllabus):
- Osmosis: "net movement of water molecules from a region of higher water potential to a region of lower water potential through a partially permeable membrane".
- Enzymes and heat: "active site changes shape; substrate no longer fits / not complementary; enzyme denatured".
- Gas exchange surfaces: "large surface area; thin surface; good blood supply; good ventilation with air".
- Vaccination: "weakened pathogens or antigens; lymphocytes produce antibodies; memory cells give long-term immunity".
- Transpiration: "water evaporates from the surfaces of mesophyll cells into air spaces; water vapour diffuses out through stomata".
- Natural selection: "variation; many offspring; struggle for survival / competition; better adapted more likely to reproduce; pass on alleles".
- Temperature control: "vasodilation / vasoconstriction of arterioles supplying skin surface capillaries".
- Food chains: "energy lost at each trophic level (heat from respiration, uneaten parts, egestion), so fewer than five trophic levels".
