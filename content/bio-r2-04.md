# Past paper · Paper 6

> **Goal:** sit a full Alternative to Practical paper in 1 hour, then mark it with the mark scheme, focusing on the practical skills that repeat every year.

## Before you start

- **Paper**: a Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 Paper 6 (Alternative to Practical, components 61/62/63) from a recent series you have **not** done — e.g. May/June 2025 or Oct/Nov 2025; otherwise 2024. Papers from 2023 onwards match the current syllabus most closely.
- **Mark scheme** for the same component, out of sight.
- **Timer** set to 1 hour.
- Sharp **HB pencil** (for drawings and graphs), eraser, **30 cm ruler** (mm scale), calculator, black/blue pen.
- This session needs about **75–80 minutes** (60 min paper + 15–20 min marking). If you only have 60 minutes, sit the paper today and mark at the start of the next session.

## Exam format reminder

- **1 hour, 40 marks, 20 %** of the IGCSE.
- Usually **2–3 structured questions**, each based on an experiment described in the paper (often one long question of around 20 marks and one or two shorter ones).
- Tests planning, recording, analysing and evaluating — **not** recall of lots of facts, but you need practical knowledge (food tests, enzyme experiments, osmosis, photosynthesis, respiration, drawing specimens).
- Pace: about **1.5 minutes per mark**. Leave about 10 minutes for the planning question if there is one.

## How to do it

1. **0–60 min**: sit the paper. Read each experiment description carefully before answering — underline the variables.
2. **60–75 min**: mark with the mark scheme (strictly). Graph and drawing marks are often lost — check each criterion separately.
3. **75–80 min**: error log — list lost marks by skill (table, graph, drawing, variables, planning, evaluation, calculation).

## Exam technique for this paper

**Variables**
- Independent: what you change. Dependent: what you measure. Controlled: what you keep the same — always name a specific variable ("volume of enzyme solution", "temperature"), never "the amount of stuff".
- How to control temperature: **thermostatically controlled water bath**. pH: **buffer solution**.

**Results tables**
- Ruled table with a border; independent variable in the **first column**.
- Column headings with **units in the heading only** (e.g. "time / s"), not in the cells.
- Same number of decimal places in a column, matching the instrument.

**Graphs**
- Independent variable on the **x-axis**; both axes labelled with units, copying the table headings.
- Linear scale using **more than half** of the grid in each direction; no awkward scales (avoid multiples of 3, 7).
- Plot with small crosses or dot-in-circle, accurate to half a small square.
- Join points with **ruled straight lines point to point** or a smooth best-fit line — do what the mark scheme convention asks; **do not extrapolate** beyond the data unless asked.
- Bar charts for discontinuous data (bars not touching); histograms for continuous data.

**Biological drawings**
- Large (fill at least half the space), sharp pencil, **clear continuous lines**, no shading, no sketchy lines.
- Correct proportions and the same number of parts as the specimen.
- Label lines drawn with a ruler, touching the structure, not crossing.

**Magnification and measuring**
- Measure in mm to the nearest mm; show the line you measured; convert units before dividing.

**Planning an investigation (usually 6 marks)** — cover these points:
1. What you change and how (with at least 5 values, e.g. 20, 30, 40, 50, 60 °C).
2. What you measure and how (instrument, units, time).
3. Variables kept constant (at least two, specific).
4. A **control** where appropriate (boiled enzyme, no plant, water instead of solution).
5. **Repeats** (at least 3) and calculate a **mean**.
6. **Safety** (eye protection, hot water, chemicals, sharp tools).

**Evaluation**
- Sources of error: hard to judge colour change (subjective); temperature drift; timing reaction start; bubbles vary in size; only one trial.
- Improvements must match the error: colorimeter; water bath; gas syringe instead of counting bubbles; more repeats; smaller intervals near the optimum.
- **Anomalous result**: one that does not fit the pattern — identify it, suggest a cause, exclude it from the mean.

**Calculations**
- Percentage change = (final − initial) ÷ initial × 100.
- Rate = amount ÷ time, or 1 ÷ time.
- Mean: ignore anomalous values; give to the same precision as the data.

## Checklist while marking

- [ ] Table: ruled, headings, units in headings, consistent decimal places?
- [ ] Graph: axes right way round, labels + units, scale, accurate plotting, line correct?
- [ ] Drawing: size, lines, proportions, labels?
- [ ] Variables named specifically?
- [ ] Planning: all six points covered?
- [ ] Errors matched with improvements?
- [ ] Food test colours and conditions (heating Benedict's) correct?
- In the error log, record each lost mark under the **skill** it belongs to — Paper 6 rewards the same skills every year, so fixing one skill gains marks on every paper.

## Quick-fire revision

- Iodine: orange-brown → blue-black (starch). Benedict's + heat: blue → green/yellow/orange/brick-red. Biuret: blue → purple. Ethanol emulsion: cloudy white. DCPIP: blue → colourless.
- Hydrogencarbonate indicator: yellow (more CO₂), red/orange, purple (less CO₂). Limewater: milky with CO₂.
- Starch in leaf: boil in water, hot ethanol (water bath, no flame), rinse, iodine.
- Osmosis: use % change in mass; blot cylinders dry; same size and same potato.
- Enzymes: equilibrate solutions in water bath before mixing; control with boiled enzyme.
- Germinating seeds: control = boiled seeds or glass beads.
- Potometer: set up under water, no air bubbles, seal joints.
- Safety: eye protection; ethanol flammable; hot water; sharp scalpels; wash hands with biological material.
