# Motivation

> **In the exam:** Comes up in most series. Paper 1: "Define motivation" [2], "Identify two non-financial rewards" [2], "Outline two needs in Maslow's hierarchy" [4], "Explain two ways to motivate workers" [6], "Should the business use bonuses or job enrichment? Justify your answer" [6]. Paper 2 often has a 12-mark question asking you to evaluate methods of motivating a specific group of workers in the case study (e.g. factory workers, sales staff, nurses).

## What you need to know

- Why people work and what motivation means; the importance of a well-motivated workforce (labour productivity, low absenteeism, low labour turnover, high quality).
- Motivation theories: Taylor, Maslow (hierarchy of needs) and Herzberg (two-factor theory: hygiene factors and motivators).
- Methods of motivation:
  - Financial rewards: wage (time-rate, piece-rate), salary, bonus, commission, profit sharing.
  - Non-financial rewards: fringe benefits; job satisfaction through job rotation, job enlargement, job enrichment, teamworking, training, opportunities for promotion.
- Recommending and justifying appropriate methods of motivation in given circumstances.

## Notes

### What is motivation and why does it matter?

**Motivation** is the reason why employees want to work hard and work effectively for the business.

People work to earn money for needs and wants, but also for security, social contact, status and job satisfaction.

| Signs of a well-motivated workforce | Signs of poor motivation |
|---|---|
| High labour productivity | Low output, poor quality |
| Low absenteeism | High absenteeism |
| Low labour turnover (fewer leave) → lower recruitment and training costs | High labour turnover |
| Good ideas, cooperation, fewer disputes | Complaints, conflict, strikes |

### Taylor — scientific management

- Workers are mainly motivated by **money** ("economic man").
- Break jobs into simple tasks; find the most efficient way; pay by **piece rate** (paid per unit produced).
- Led to higher productivity in factories and influenced flow production.
- **Limitations:** ignores social and esteem needs; repetitive work is boring; quality may suffer as workers rush; does not work for jobs where output cannot be counted (e.g. teachers, nurses).

### Maslow — hierarchy of needs

From the bottom (must be met first) to the top:

| Level | Need | How a business can meet it |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Self-actualisation | Challenging work, chance to reach full potential (job enrichment, promotion) |
| 4 | Esteem | Recognition, status, praise, job titles, responsibility |
| 3 | Social | Teamworking, friendly colleagues, social events |
| 2 | Security / safety | Job security, contract, safe working conditions, pension |
| 1 | Physiological | Wages high enough for food, housing; breaks; rest areas |

- Once a level is satisfied, the next level becomes the motivator.
- **Limitations:** not everyone follows the order; hard to know which level each worker has reached; some needs (e.g. self-actualisation) are hard to satisfy at work.

### Herzberg — two-factor theory

| Hygiene factors (prevent dissatisfaction, do not motivate) | Motivators (create job satisfaction and motivate) |
|---|---|
| Pay, working conditions, company policy, supervision, relationships with colleagues, job security | Achievement, recognition, responsibility, interesting work, promotion / personal growth |

- Hygiene factors must be right first, otherwise workers are dissatisfied; but improving them only removes dissatisfaction.
- To motivate, use the motivators → through **job enrichment**.
- Money is a hygiene factor for Herzberg — the opposite view from Taylor.

### Financial rewards

| Method | How it works | Advantage | Disadvantage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wage – time rate | Paid per hour worked, usually weekly | Easy to calculate; worker knows pay | No reward for extra effort |
| Wage – piece rate | Paid per unit produced | Encourages higher output | Quality may fall; pay varies (insecure) |
| Salary | Fixed annual amount, paid monthly | Security; status | Not linked to performance |
| Bonus | Extra lump sum for meeting targets | Rewards good performance | Targets may cause stress; resentment if missed |
| Commission | % of the value of sales made, often on top of a basic salary | Encourages sales staff to sell more | Pushy selling; unstable income |
| Profit sharing | Part of profit shared among workers | Workers feel part of business, support cost-cutting | Small amount each; not linked to individual effort |

Example: a sales worker earns a basic salary of €1,200 per month plus 5% commission on sales. Sales in March are €30,000. Commission = 5% × €30,000 = €1,500. Total pay = €1,200 + €1,500 = €2,700.

### Non-financial rewards

**Fringe benefits:** extra benefits on top of pay — company car, health insurance, discounts on products, free meals, pension, gym membership.

| Method | Meaning | Theory link |
|---|---|---|
| Job rotation | Workers swap between tasks at the same level of difficulty | Reduces boredom; workers multi-skilled (flexible cover for absence) — but tasks still simple |
| Job enlargement | Extra tasks of a similar level added to the job | Variety; but may be seen as "more work for the same pay" |
| Job enrichment | Giving workers more challenging tasks and responsibility, using more of their skills | Herzberg's motivators; Maslow esteem / self-actualisation. Needs training; not all workers want it |
| Teamworking | Workers grouped in teams, often with some decision-making power | Maslow social needs; workers share ideas |
| Training | Developing skills | Esteem and self-actualisation; better quality — but costly, trained staff may leave |
| Opportunities for promotion | Chance to move to a higher position | Esteem, self-actualisation; motivates ambitious staff |

### Choosing methods — application tips

- **Sales staff:** commission or bonus — sales are measurable.
- **Factory workers making standard products:** piece rate (if quality is checked) or job rotation to reduce boredom.
- **Skilled / professional staff** (nurses, designers, engineers): job enrichment, training, promotion — output hard to measure.
- **Small business with little cash:** non-financial methods (praise, teamworking, responsibility) are cheaper.
- A business with poor hygiene factors (low pay, unsafe conditions) must fix these first.

## Key terms and definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Motivation | The reasons why employees want to work hard and work effectively for the business |
| Labour turnover | The rate at which employees leave a business and need to be replaced |
| Hygiene factors | Factors (e.g. pay, working conditions) that, if not satisfied, lead to dissatisfaction, but do not motivate in themselves (Herzberg) |
| Motivators | Factors (e.g. recognition, responsibility, achievement) that give job satisfaction and motivate (Herzberg) |
| Piece rate | A payment for each unit produced |
| Commission | Payment relating to the number of sales made, usually a percentage of the value of sales |
| Bonus | A lump sum paid to workers in addition to normal pay, usually for good work or reaching a target |
| Profit sharing | A system in which a proportion of the company's profits is paid to employees |
| Fringe benefits | Non-financial rewards given to employees in addition to pay, e.g. company car, health insurance |
| Job rotation | Workers swap around doing each specific task at the same level of difficulty |
| Job enlargement | Extra tasks of a similar level of difficulty are added to a worker's job description |
| Job enrichment | Adding tasks that require more skill and/or responsibility to a job |

## Worked examples

**Case context:** *PackFresh* employs 60 workers on a production line packing salads for supermarkets. Labour turnover is 35% a year and absenteeism is high. Workers are paid an hourly wage just above the minimum wage. The production manager says the work is "repetitive and dull". The owner has €30,000 to spend on improving motivation.

**Q1. Identify two signs that PackFresh workers are poorly motivated. [2]**

High labour turnover (35% a year) (1); high absenteeism (1).

**Q2. Explain two reasons why high labour turnover is a problem for PackFresh. [6]**

- Higher recruitment and training costs (K): with 35% of 60 workers leaving each year, about 21 new workers must be recruited and trained (App), which raises costs and reduces profit (An).
- Lower productivity and quality (K): new packers are slower and less experienced on the salad line (App), so output falls and more mistakes may lead to supermarket orders being rejected, damaging PackFresh's reputation (An).

**Q3. PackFresh is considering two methods: (A) a piece-rate payment system; (B) job rotation with teamworking. Which should it choose? Justify your answer. [6]**

- (A) Piece rate pays per salad packed (K). It could raise output because, as Taylor argued, money motivates and pay is only just above the minimum wage (App/An). But workers may rush, so packing quality falls — risky because supermarkets reject faulty packs (An).
- (B) Job rotation means workers move between tasks such as washing, packing and labelling (K). This reduces the boredom of "repetitive and dull" work, and teamworking meets social needs (Maslow), which may reduce turnover (App/An). However, tasks are still simple, so the effect may be limited (An).
- **Decision (Ev):** PackFresh should choose B. Its main problem is boredom causing workers to leave, and rotation tackles this directly while keeping quality high; it also creates multi-skilled workers who can cover for absent colleagues. It is also cheaper, so part of the €30,000 could be used for a team bonus based on quality targets, which combines both benefits.

## Common mistakes

- Saying Herzberg believed money motivates — pay is a **hygiene factor**.
- Mixing up job **enlargement** (more tasks at the same level) and job **enrichment** (more responsibility/challenge).
- Calling commission a "non-financial reward", or a company car a "financial reward" (it is a fringe benefit).
- Listing Maslow's levels without linking them to a practical method the business could use.
- Recommending expensive methods for a business with cash-flow problems, or piece rate where quality is critical, without addressing the risk.
- Not using the case facts (type of worker, finance available, current pay) when justifying a choice.

## Practice questions

1. Define "job enrichment". [2]
2. Identify two hygiene factors in Herzberg's theory. [2]
3. A salesperson earns €1,500 a month plus 4% commission on sales. In June she sells €42,000 of products. Calculate her total pay for June. [2]
4. Outline two needs in Maslow's hierarchy and how a hotel could meet them for its receptionists. [4]
5. Explain two disadvantages of using piece rate for workers making hand-made jewellery. [6]
6. A hospital wants to improve the motivation of its nurses. Should it use financial or non-financial methods? Justify your answer. [6]

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Adding tasks to a job that require more skill and/or responsibility (2). (1 for partial.)
2. Any two: pay, working conditions, company policy, supervision, relationships with colleagues, job security.
3. 4% × €42,000 = €1,680 (1); €1,500 + €1,680 = €3,180 (1).
4. e.g. Social needs — teamworking on shifts / staff social events (2). Esteem needs — "employee of the month" recognition, job titles (2). Security — permanent contracts (2).
5. e.g. Quality may fall (K) as workers rush to make more pieces of delicate hand-made jewellery (App), leading to returns and damage to the brand's image (An). Pay becomes unpredictable (K) because intricate pieces take different times (App), so workers feel insecure and may leave (An).
6. Financial (bonus, higher salary) meets basic needs and is a hygiene factor — prevents dissatisfaction, but hospitals may have limited budgets and output is hard to measure. Non-financial (training, promotion, job enrichment, teamworking) meets esteem and self-actualisation needs; nurses are skilled professionals. Justified choice, e.g. "Mainly non-financial, especially training and promotion, because nurses' work is hard to measure so bonuses are unfair — but only if pay is already competitive; if nurses are leaving for better-paid jobs, pay must be fixed first (Herzberg)."
