# Organisation and people

> **In the exam:** Very frequent. Paper 1 asks "Define span of control" [2], "Identify two methods of external recruitment" [2], "Outline two benefits of on-the-job training" [4], "Explain two barriers to communication" [6], "Which leadership style should the manager use? Justify your answer" [6]. Paper 2 commonly asks you to evaluate recruitment methods, training methods or communication methods for the case-study business, or the effects of reducing the workforce.

## What you need to know

- Simple organisational structures and organisation charts; hierarchy, levels, chain of command, span of control; the effect of tall and flat structures.
- Roles and responsibilities of directors, managers, supervisors and other employees.
- Functions of management: planning, organising, coordinating, commanding, controlling.
- Delegation: benefits and trust.
- Leadership styles: autocratic, democratic, laissez-faire — features and when appropriate.
- Trade unions: what they are and the benefits of membership.
- Recruitment and selection: job analysis, job description, person specification, advertising, application forms/CVs, interviews and tests; internal and external recruitment.
- Benefits and limitations of part-time and full-time employees.
- Importance of training; induction, on-the-job and off-the-job training.
- Reducing the size of the workforce: dismissal and redundancy; how to choose who to make redundant.
- Legal controls over employment: health and safety, discrimination, minimum wage, contracts.
- Effective communication; internal and external communication; methods (verbal, written, visual, electronic); communication barriers and how to reduce them.

## Notes

### Organisation charts

An **organisation chart** shows the internal structure of a business: levels of hierarchy, who reports to whom, and the departments.

- **Hierarchy:** the levels of management, from highest to lowest.
- **Chain of command:** the route through which decisions and instructions are passed down (and information up).
- **Span of control:** the number of subordinates reporting directly to one manager.

| Tall structure (many levels, narrow spans) | Flat structure (few levels, wide spans) |
|---|---|
| Close supervision; more promotion opportunities | Faster communication; fewer managers (lower costs) |
| Slow communication; messages distorted | Managers may be overloaded; less supervision |
| Senior managers far from workers | More delegation → workers more motivated |

**Delegation:** giving a subordinate the authority to do tasks. Benefits: managers have time for important work; subordinates feel trusted and motivated (job enrichment). Risks: manager is still responsible; subordinate may lack skills; managers may fear losing control.

### Roles in a business

- **Directors:** responsible for strategy and long-term objectives.
- **Managers:** responsible for a department; plan, organise, coordinate, command and control.
- **Supervisors:** watch over workers and check that tasks are done.
- **Workers (operatives):** carry out tasks.

**Functions of management:** planning (setting objectives), organising (resources), coordinating (making departments work together), commanding (guiding and instructing staff), controlling (measuring performance against targets).

### Leadership styles

| Style | Features | Suitable when |
|---|---|---|
| Autocratic | Manager makes decisions alone; one-way communication; little delegation | Emergencies, quick decisions, unskilled or new staff, e.g. armed forces, safety-critical work |
| Democratic | Workers involved in decisions; two-way communication; delegation | Skilled, experienced staff; when ideas and commitment matter (e.g. design team) |
| Laissez-faire | Manager sets broad objectives and lets workers decide; very little direction | Highly skilled, creative, self-motivated staff (e.g. research scientists) — risk of lack of direction |

### Trade unions

A **trade union** is a group of workers who join together to protect their interests (pay, conditions, job security). Benefits to members: collective bargaining (stronger together), legal advice and support, improved conditions. Businesses may benefit from negotiating with one body; but union action (strikes, work-to-rule) can disrupt production.

### Recruitment and selection

Process:
1. Identify the vacancy.
2. **Job analysis** — identify the tasks of the job.
3. **Job description** — job title, duties, responsibilities, working conditions.
4. **Person specification** — qualifications, skills, experience and personal qualities required.
5. Advertise (internally or externally).
6. Applicants send application forms / CVs; shortlist.
7. Selection: interviews, tests (skills, aptitude, personality), references.
8. Appoint and send a contract of employment.

| | Internal recruitment (existing employee) | External recruitment (outside the business) |
|---|---|---|
| Advantages | Cheaper and quicker; person already known; motivates staff (promotion); less induction | Wider choice of candidates; new ideas and skills |
| Disadvantages | Narrow choice; no new ideas; creates another vacancy; jealousy | More expensive (advertising, agencies); slower; longer induction; risk of unknown person |

External methods: local newspapers, specialist magazines, recruitment agencies, government job centres, online job websites and social media.

**Part-time vs full-time:**
- Part-time: flexible cover for busy times; lower total wages; easier to adjust staffing — but less commitment, harder to communicate with, more workers to train.
- Full-time: more commitment and continuity — but less flexible, higher cost.

### Training

| Type | What it is | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Induction | Introduces new employee to the business: layout, policies, health & safety, colleagues | New staff settle quickly and become productive sooner | Takes time; new worker not yet producing |
| On-the-job | Trained by watching/working with an experienced worker at the workplace | Cheap; work still done; specific to the job | Bad habits copied; trainer's output falls |
| Off-the-job | Training away from the workplace, e.g. college course, training centre | Wider skills; expert trainers; may motivate | Expensive; work time lost; trained staff may leave for better jobs |

### Reducing the workforce

- **Dismissal:** being sacked for unsatisfactory work or behaviour (e.g. theft, repeated lateness). Not the employer's fault.
- **Redundancy:** worker loses their job because they are no longer needed — not their fault (e.g. factory closes, automation, falling demand). Usually given redundancy payment.
- Choosing who: voluntary redundancy, early retirement, last-in-first-out, or by skills/performance. Must be fair and legal.
- Effects: lower costs; but survivors' morale and security fall; bad publicity.

### Legal controls over employment

Protect workers against: **unfair dismissal**, **discrimination** (gender, race, religion, age, disability), unsafe conditions (**health and safety** law), very low pay (**minimum wage**). Contracts of employment set out pay, hours, duties. Effects on business: higher costs (training, equipment, wages), but better motivated staff and a good reputation.

### Communication

**Effective communication** = exchange of information between people that is understood. Elements: **sender → message → medium → receiver → feedback**.
- Internal (within the business) vs external (with customers, suppliers, government).
- One-way (no feedback) vs two-way (feedback).

| Method | Examples | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verbal | Meetings, phone calls, video calls | Immediate feedback; body language helps | No permanent record; may be misunderstood |
| Written | Letters, reports, notices, email, text | Permanent record; can be detailed | Slow feedback; may be ignored; too long |
| Visual | Charts, diagrams, videos, posters | Clear, attractive, easy to understand data | Needs explanation; can be misread |
| Electronic | Email, intranet, messaging apps | Fast; reaches many people at once | Information overload; needs equipment; security |

**Barriers and solutions:**

| Barrier | Solution |
|---|---|
| Language too technical (jargon) or poor language skills | Use simple language; translation |
| Message too long / unclear | Keep it short and clear |
| Wrong medium (e.g. notice for urgent safety warning) | Choose medium that suits message and receiver |
| Long chain of command — message distorted | Shorter chain / flatter structure |
| Receiver not listening, poor attitude, distrust | Explain why the message matters; build trust; ask for feedback |
| No feedback | Use two-way communication |
| Noise / distance / technology failure | Quiet setting; check equipment; back-up |

## Key terms and definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Organisation chart | A diagram showing the internal structure of a business: levels of hierarchy, departments and who reports to whom |
| Hierarchy | The levels of management in a business, from highest to lowest |
| Chain of command | The structure in an organisation that allows instructions to be passed down from senior management to lower levels |
| Span of control | The number of subordinates working directly under a manager |
| Delegation | Giving a subordinate the authority to perform particular tasks |
| Job description | A document that outlines the responsibilities and duties to be carried out by someone employed to do a specific job |
| Person specification | A document that outlines the required qualifications, expertise, experience and qualities of the ideal person for a job |
| Induction training | An introduction given to a new employee, explaining the business's activities, customs and procedures |
| Dismissal | When employment is ended against the will of the employee, usually for not working to the required standard |
| Redundancy | When an employee is no longer needed and so loses their job, not due to any aspect of their work being unsatisfactory |
| Trade union | A group of workers who have joined together to ensure their interests are protected |
| Effective communication | The exchange of information between people or groups, with feedback |

## Worked examples

**Case context:** *TecnoServe* is a computer-repair business with 4 shops. The owner, Marta, makes all decisions herself. She wants to recruit a manager for a new fifth shop and is choosing between promoting Luis, a senior technician who has worked for her for 6 years, or advertising externally.

**Q1. Identify two documents used in the recruitment process. [2]**

Job description (1); person specification (1). (Also accept application form, CV.)

**Q2. Explain two advantages to TecnoServe of internal recruitment. [6]**

- Cheaper and faster (K): no need to pay for job advertisements or an agency (App), so the fifth shop can open sooner and costs are kept low (An).
- Candidate already known (K): Marta has seen Luis's work for 6 years and he knows the repair procedures (App), so there is less risk of appointing the wrong person and less induction training is needed (An). Accept also: motivates other technicians who see a promotion route.

**Q3. Marta uses an autocratic leadership style. Do you think she should change to a democratic style as the business grows? Justify your answer. [6]**

- Autocratic: Marta makes all decisions (K). Quick decisions and consistent standards across shops (An). But with five shops she cannot be everywhere, so decisions in other shops wait for her, and skilled technicians may feel undervalued (App/An).
- Democratic: involves staff and delegates (K). Skilled technicians are likely to have good ideas about repairs; delegation to shop managers lets each shop respond quickly to customers, and greater responsibility motivates (Herzberg) (App/An). Risk: slower decisions and some managers may make mistakes.
- **Decision (Ev):** Yes — with five shops a democratic style is more suitable, because Marta's span of control is too wide for her to take every decision, and her staff are skilled. She should still set clear standards and control results (e.g. monthly targets), so that delegation does not lead to inconsistent service.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing **dismissal** and **redundancy**, or saying redundant workers did something wrong.
- Mixing up **job description** (about the job) and **person specification** (about the person).
- Saying a wide span of control means a tall structure — wide spans usually mean a **flat** structure.
- Choosing a leadership style without linking it to the type of worker and situation in the case.
- In communication questions, naming a barrier without a matching solution, or giving a solution that does not fit the case.
- Saying "on-the-job training is free" — it still costs the experienced worker's time and lower output.
