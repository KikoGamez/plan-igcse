# The marketing mix

> **In the exam:** One of the most tested topics. Paper 1: "Define brand loyalty" [2], "Identify two extension strategies" [2], a price calculation such as cost-plus [2], "Explain two pricing methods the business could use" [6], "Which method of promotion should the business use? Justify your answer" [6]. Paper 2 nearly always has at least one marketing question, often 12 marks: "Consider these two promotion/distribution methods. Recommend which one…" or "Explain the most important element of the marketing mix for…".

## What you need to know

- **Product:** costs and benefits of developing new products; brand name, brand image and brand loyalty; role of packaging; the product life cycle (stages, and how stages affect marketing decisions); extension strategies.
- **Price:** pricing methods — cost-plus, competitive, penetration, skimming, promotional (and dynamic pricing); benefits and limitations; the significance of price elasticity (price-elastic and price-inelastic demand) for pricing decisions.
- **Place:** distribution channels (direct; via retailer; via wholesaler and retailer; via agent); choosing a channel; e-commerce and its benefits and limitations to businesses and consumers.
- **Promotion:** aims of promotion; types — advertising (media), sales promotion; choosing a method; the marketing budget; use of technology (social media, websites, targeted online advertising).
- Recommending and justifying a marketing strategy; the four Ps working together for a target market.

## Notes

### The marketing mix

The **marketing mix** is the four elements of **product, price, place and promotion** that a business combines to satisfy its target market. The four Ps must be consistent: a luxury product needs a high price, exclusive shops and up-market promotion.

### Product

**Developing new products**

| Benefits | Costs / risks |
|---|---|
| Attracts new customers; increases sales and market share | Market research and R&D are expensive |
| Spreads risk (product portfolio) | Many new products fail — money lost |
| Keeps up with competitors and technology | Production may need new machinery and training |
| Can charge higher prices for innovative products | Takes time; competitors may copy |

**Branding:** a **brand** is a name, symbol or design that distinguishes a product from competitors. **Brand image** is the image or identity given to a product, giving it a personality different from its competitors' brands. **Brand loyalty** is when consumers keep buying the same brand again and again instead of choosing a competitor's. A strong brand allows higher prices and makes new products easier to launch.

**Packaging:** protects the product, provides information (ingredients, instructions, legal information), makes it easy to use and transport, and helps promotion/brand image (eye-catching design). Downside: cost and environmental concerns (waste).

**Product life cycle** — stages a product goes through from introduction to withdrawal:

| Stage | Sales | Profit | Typical marketing decisions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Introduction | Low, growing slowly | Usually a loss (high development and promotion costs) | Informative advertising; skimming or penetration price; limited distribution |
| Growth | Rising quickly | Profit starts | Increase distribution; persuasive advertising to build brand loyalty |
| Maturity | Highest, growing slowly | Highest | Competitive pricing; promotion to defend market share |
| Saturation | Stop growing — many competitors | Falling | Price cuts; consider extension strategies |
| Decline | Falling | Falling | Reduce promotion, cut price, withdraw product, or extend life |

**Extension strategies** (used at saturation/maturity): find new markets (e.g. export), find new uses, redesign or improve the product, new packaging, target a different segment, increase advertising / sales promotions.

### Price

| Method | How it works | When suitable | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost-plus | Unit cost + a percentage mark-up | Easy; ensures each unit covers its cost | Ignores competitors' prices and demand |
| Competitive | Price at or near competitors' prices | Similar products, many competitors | Must keep costs low to make profit |
| Penetration | Low price at launch to gain market share, then raise | Mass market, new entrant, price-sensitive buyers | Low profit at first; customers may leave when price rises |
| Price skimming | High price at launch for a new or unique product, lowered later | Innovative, high-tech products; strong brand | Attracts competitors; some customers wait for lower price |
| Promotional | Low price for a short time (e.g. "buy one get one free") | Clear old stock; boost sales of a declining product | Lower revenue per unit; may damage brand image |
| Dynamic | Price changes with demand, often online (e.g. flights, hotels) | Perishable capacity, online sales | Customers may feel unfairly treated |

**Cost-plus example:** unit cost €8, mark-up 25%. Price = €8 + (25% × €8) = €8 + €2 = €10.

**Price elasticity of demand (PED)** — how responsive the quantity demanded is to a change in price.
- **Price-elastic demand:** quantity demanded changes by a greater percentage than the price. Many substitutes, non-essential. A price **cut** increases revenue; a price rise reduces revenue.
- **Price-inelastic demand:** quantity demanded changes by a smaller percentage than the price. Few substitutes, necessity, strong brand loyalty. A price **rise** increases revenue.
- Use: deciding whether to raise or cut price. Branding aims to make demand more price-inelastic.

### Place (distribution)

| Channel | Route | Suits |
|---|---|---|
| Direct | Producer → consumer | Online sellers, farm shops, services; producer keeps full profit and control |
| One intermediary | Producer → retailer → consumer | Large retailers buying in bulk (supermarkets, car dealers) |
| Two intermediaries | Producer → wholesaler → retailer → consumer | Many small shops spread out (e.g. snacks, newspapers) |
| Agent | Producer → agent → (retailer) → consumer | Selling in foreign markets the producer does not know |

Choosing a channel depends on: type of product (perishable, technical, expensive?), how often bought, where customers are, cost, the image wanted, and how much control the producer wants.

**E-commerce (selling online):**
- Business benefits: reach global market, lower costs (no shop), open 24/7, data on customers.
- Business limitations: delivery costs and returns; strong online competition; website and security costs; customers cannot touch products.
- Consumer benefits: convenience, easy price comparison, wider choice. Limitations: cannot try before buying, delivery delays, security/fraud risk, need internet access.

### Promotion

**Aims:** inform customers about a new product; persuade them to buy; create or develop brand image; remind customers; increase sales and market share.

**Advertising** — paid-for communication using media:

| Media | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Television | Huge audience; sound and movement | Very expensive; message short |
| Radio | Cheaper; local targeting | No visuals |
| Newspapers / magazines | Can target readers (specialist magazines); detail | Black-and-white / static; short life (newspapers) |
| Posters / billboards | Seen repeatedly by many | Quick glance only; can be damaged |
| Cinema | Targets age groups; strong impact | Limited audience |
| Social media / internet | Cheap per viewer; targeted by age, interests; measurable; interactive | Easy to ignore/skip; negative comments spread quickly |

- **Informative advertising:** gives information (e.g. new product details).
- **Persuasive advertising:** tries to convince consumers to buy (brand image).

**Sales promotion:** incentives to encourage short-term sales — price reductions, gifts, buy-one-get-one-free, coupons, competitions, point-of-sale displays, free samples, loyalty schemes, after-sales service.

**Marketing budget:** the amount of money a business plans to spend on marketing over a period. Choose the promotion method that reaches the **target market** within the budget: e.g. a small café → social media and leaflets, not TV.

**Technology in marketing:** websites, social media (targeted adverts, influencers, viral marketing), online sales, email marketing. Benefits: low cost, targeted, measurable, two-way. Limitations: negative reviews spread fast, need to keep content up to date, information overload.

### Marketing strategy

A **marketing strategy** is a plan to combine the four Ps to achieve a marketing objective for a target market. To justify a strategy: identify the objective (e.g. increase market share by 10%), the target market, then choose consistent Ps. Legal controls protect consumers (no misleading adverts, products must be safe and as described).

Entering foreign markets: growth opportunities when the home market is saturated; problems include lack of knowledge of the market, cultural and language differences, import tariffs and quotas, exchange-rate changes. Solutions include joint ventures, licensing, using local agents, franchising.

## Key terms and definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Marketing mix | The four Ps — product, price, place and promotion — used to satisfy the target market |
| Brand image | An image or identity given to a product which gives it a personality of its own and distinguishes it from competitors' brands |
| Brand loyalty | When consumers keep buying the same brand again and again instead of choosing a competitor's brand |
| Product life cycle | The stages a product goes through from its introduction, through growth and maturity, to decline |
| Extension strategy | A way of keeping a product at the maturity stage of the life cycle and extending the cycle |
| Cost-plus pricing | The cost of producing the product plus a profit mark-up added on |
| Penetration pricing | Setting a price lower than competitors' prices in order to enter a new market |
| Price skimming | Setting a high price for a new product when it is first launched |
| Competitive pricing | Setting a price similar to that of competitors' products |
| Price-elastic demand | When consumers are sensitive to price changes, so the % change in quantity demanded is greater than the % change in price |
| Price-inelastic demand | When consumers are not very sensitive to price changes, so the % change in quantity demanded is less than the % change in price |
| Channel of distribution | The means by which a product passes from the place of production to the customer |
| Marketing budget | A financial plan for the marketing of a product for a specified period of time |
| Sales promotion | Incentives such as special offers or special deals directed at consumers to encourage short-term sales |

## Worked examples

**Case context:** *Ondina* makes premium waterproof speakers. Its newest model, the Ondina X, uses a new long-life battery that no competitor offers. Unit cost is €48. Its older model, the Ondina 2, has been on sale for five years and sales have stopped growing.

**Q1. Calculate the price of the Ondina X if Ondina uses cost-plus pricing with a 60% mark-up. [2]**

60% × €48 = €28.80 (1); €48 + €28.80 = €76.80 (1).

**Q2. Explain two extension strategies Ondina could use for the Ondina 2. [6]**

- Redesign / new features (K): add new colours or a stronger bass mode to the Ondina 2 (App), giving existing customers a reason to buy again and moving sales back into growth (An).
- New market (K): sell the Ondina 2 in export markets or to a new segment such as schools or gyms (App), so sales rise without the high cost of developing a completely new product (An).

**Q3. Should Ondina use price skimming or penetration pricing for the Ondina X? Justify your answer. [6]**

- Skimming: high launch price (K). The new long-life battery is unique (App), so early adopters who want the latest technology will pay more; demand is likely price-inelastic; it recovers development costs quickly and supports a premium brand image (An). Risk: high profits attract competitors who will copy the battery.
- Penetration: low launch price (K) to gain market share fast and build brand loyalty (An), but it would earn a low profit margin and could damage Ondina's premium image (App/An).
- **Decision (Ev):** Price skimming, because the product has a unique feature and Ondina is a premium brand, so customers are less sensitive to price at launch. Ondina should plan to lower the price once competitors launch similar batteries, to keep sales in the growth stage.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing **promotional pricing** (a pricing method) with **promotion** (the P) or sales promotion.
- Describing the product life cycle stages without linking them to a **decision** (price, promotion, extension).
- Wrong cost-plus calculation: the mark-up is a percentage **of cost**, then added to cost.
- Saying "price-inelastic means demand doesn't change" — it changes, but by a smaller percentage.
- Recommending TV advertising for a small business with a tiny marketing budget.
- Treating the four Ps separately; strong answers show how they **fit together** for the target market.
