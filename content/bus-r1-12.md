# Case study technique

> **In the exam:** Paper 2 (1 h 30, 80 marks, 50% of the qualification) is based on a case study of one business given in an insert, usually with several appendices (data tables, charts, financial extracts, options). There are four questions worth 20 marks each, typically split into an (a) part of about 8 marks and a (b) part of about 12 marks. Questions can come from any part of the syllabus. Most marks are for **application, analysis and evaluation**, not for knowledge — the same skills also earn the top marks on the 6-mark "justify" questions in Paper 1.

## What you need to know

- The four assessment objectives (AOs) and what each looks like in an answer:
  - **AO1 Knowledge and understanding** — correct business terms, concepts and theories.
  - **AO2 Application** — using the case: names, facts, figures, the type of business, its situation.
  - **AO3 Analysis** — explaining causes and consequences: a logical chain showing *why* or *how* something affects the business (its costs, sales, profit, cash, workers, objectives).
  - **AO4 Evaluation** — making a reasoned judgement and justifying it: weighing up options, deciding, and saying why the chosen option is better than the others and what it depends on.
- Command words: identify, state, define, outline, explain, analyse, calculate, consider, discuss, recommend, justify, evaluate.
- How to read a case study and appendices quickly and accurately.
- How to plan and structure 8-mark and 12-mark answers.
- How to use calculations from the appendices as evidence.

## Notes

### How marks are awarded (levels of response)

Longer Paper 2 answers are marked by **levels**. A typical progression:

| Level (low → high) | What the examiner sees |
|---|---|
| Knowledge only | Correct points but generic — could be about any business |
| Application | Points linked to the case business (uses its facts/figures) |
| Analysis | Developed chains of reasoning: point → because → which means → effect on the business |
| Evaluation | A clear decision/judgement, justified with reasons from the case, comparing alternatives |

You cannot reach the top level without a **justified** judgement. You cannot get high analysis marks with lists.

### Command words

| Command word | What to do |
|---|---|
| Identify / State | Give a short answer, no explanation |
| Define | Give the precise meaning of a term |
| Outline | Set out the main points briefly (point + brief development) |
| Calculate | Work out from given data; show working; give units |
| Explain | Set out purposes or reasons; make relationships clear; say why/how, with supporting detail |
| Analyse | Examine in detail to show meaning, identify elements and the relationship between them |
| Consider | Review and respond to information — weigh up advantages and disadvantages |
| Discuss | Write about issues in depth, giving different points of view |
| Recommend / Justify / Evaluate | Make a judgement; support it with evidence and explain why it is better than the alternatives |

### Reading the case (about 10–12 minutes)

1. Read the questions **first**, so you know what to look for.
2. Read the case once fully. Underline: type and size of business, legal form, objectives, target market, problems, and anything that changes.
3. For each appendix, note what it shows and one key fact or trend.
4. In the margin, write which question each paragraph/appendix is useful for.
5. Do any calculations you will need on the insert and copy them into your answer.

### Planning an "Explain" answer (e.g. 8 marks)

Typical wording: "Explain **four** ways X could…" or "Explain the advantages and disadvantages to X of…". Use a short paragraph for each point:

**Point (K) → Apply (App) → Develop the consequence (An)**

> *Job rotation* (K). Workers at the bakery's packing line could move between mixing, baking and packing (App). This reduces boredom on repetitive tasks, so absenteeism may fall and output per worker rise (An).

Use the exact number of points asked for — extra points rarely earn marks, and missing ones cost marks.

### Planning a "Recommend / Justify" answer (e.g. 12 marks)

Typical wording: "Consider the advantages and disadvantages of these three options. Recommend which option X should choose. Justify your answer."

Structure (about 15–17 minutes):
1. **Each option in turn** — one advantage and one disadvantage, each applied to the case and analysed (consequence for costs, sales, profit, cash, objectives). Use figures from the appendix.
2. **Decision paragraph** — start with "I recommend option …" then:
   - **Why it is best:** the decisive factor, linked to the business's main problem or objective in the case.
   - **Why the others are worse:** compare directly ("Option A costs €40,000 more and the business only has €25,000 in cash…").
   - **It depends on / condition:** what would change the decision, or what the business must do to make it work.

A judgement that just repeats earlier points ("Option B is best because it has many advantages") is not evaluation.

### Using the data well

- Quote figures and do calculations: percentage change, break-even, ratios, cost differences, payback of savings, market share.
- Compare: "Option A's cost (€30,000) is more than the business's retained profit last year (€22,000)".
- Combine two pieces of information: e.g. "Sales fell 15% *and* the main competitor cut prices, so…".
- Be specific about the business: use its name, products, location, workers, customers.

### Timing

- About 1 mark per minute after reading: 8-mark part ≈ 8–9 min, 12-mark part ≈ 14–15 min.
- Plan: 10 min reading + 4 × 20 min = 90 min. Do not spend 30 minutes on Question 1.
- If time is short, always write the decision paragraph — it carries the evaluation marks.

### Analysis connectives

"This means that…", "As a result…", "This leads to…", "Therefore…", "Because…". Each one moves the chain one step further towards an outcome for the business (cost, revenue, profit, cash flow, reputation, objectives).

### Evaluation phrases

"The most important factor is… because…", "Overall… is better than… because…", "However, this depends on…", "In the short term… but in the long term…", "This would only work if…".

## Key terms and definitions

| Term | Meaning for the exam |
|---|---|
| Application | Using the context and data of the case-study business in your answer |
| Analysis | A developed chain of reasoning showing cause and effect for the business |
| Evaluation | A justified judgement or recommendation that weighs up alternatives |
| Justify | Give reasons, based on evidence, why your decision is better than the alternatives |
| Short term / long term | Useful time frame for evaluation — effects may differ over time |

## Worked examples

**Case extract — Verdecasa**
*Verdecasa* is a private limited company that makes eco-friendly cleaning products in Seville. It has 45 employees and its products are sold through 120 independent shops in southern Spain. Its objective for the next two years is to increase revenue by 20%. Last year revenue was €2.4 million and profit was €180,000. The directors are considering three options:

| Option | Cost | Expected extra revenue per year | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| A: Sell through a national supermarket chain | €60,000 (packaging redesign, marketing) | €700,000 | Supermarket wants a 15% lower price than shops pay |
| B: Sell online direct to consumers | €90,000 (website, warehouse staff) | €350,000 | Delivery costs to be paid by customers |
| C: Launch a new product range for hotels | €150,000 (R&D, new machine) | €500,000 | Verdecasa has no experience of the hotel market |

Verdecasa has €110,000 in cash.

**Q1. Explain two disadvantages to Verdecasa of option A. [8]** *(a typical 8-mark: two developed points, each with knowledge, application and analysis)*

- **Lower profit margin per unit.** The supermarket wants a price 15% lower than the price shops pay (App). Each bottle sold earns less contribution, so although €700,000 of extra revenue is expected, the extra profit could be small, and Verdecasa will need to cut costs to keep its margin (An). If the supermarket later demands further price cuts, Verdecasa may have little choice because it would depend on one powerful customer (An).
- **Conflict with existing retailers.** Its 120 independent shops may see the supermarket as competition selling the same products more cheaply (App). They may stop stocking Verdecasa or ask for lower prices, so part of the extra €700,000 could just be sales moved from the shops, not new revenue (An).

**Q2. Consider the advantages and disadvantages of each option. Recommend which option Verdecasa should choose. Justify your answer. [12]**

*Calculations used as evidence:* target extra revenue = 20% × €2.4 m = €480,000. A: €700,000 (exceeds target). B: €350,000 (below target). C: €500,000 (just exceeds target).

- **Option A:** the only option that clearly beats the €480,000 target and the cheapest to set up at €60,000, which Verdecasa can pay from its €110,000 cash. It also gives national brand awareness. But the 15% lower price reduces the profit margin, and independent shops may react badly (An).
- **Option B:** selling online lets Verdecasa keep the full retail price and build direct relationships with eco-conscious customers. But €350,000 is well below the €480,000 needed, and €90,000 uses most of its cash, leaving only €20,000 for any cash-flow problems (An).
- **Option C:** €500,000 meets the target and diversifies into a new market. But it costs €150,000, which is €40,000 more than the cash available, so Verdecasa would need to borrow; with no experience of hotels, the revenue forecast is uncertain and the risk is high (An).
- **Recommendation (Ev):** I recommend option A. It is the only option that meets the 20% revenue objective with a large margin and is affordable from current cash, so no borrowing is needed. Option C is riskier because it needs a loan and targets a market Verdecasa does not know, and option B does not meet the objective. The main risk of A is the lower price, so Verdecasa should only accept if the contribution per bottle remains positive after the 15% discount and it agrees a contract that guarantees order volumes. It should also reassure its independent shops, for example with products sold only in shops, to avoid losing existing revenue.

## Common mistakes

- Writing a textbook answer with no reference to the case — limits you to the knowledge level.
- Lists of advantages and disadvantages with no development ("It is expensive. It increases sales.").
- Ignoring the appendices or copying figures without explaining what they mean.
- Recommending an option without comparing it with the others, or sitting on the fence ("all options have advantages").
- Running out of time and never writing the decision paragraph.
- Answering a different question: e.g. writing about advantages only when asked for advantages *and* disadvantages, or two points when four were asked for.
