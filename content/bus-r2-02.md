# Past paper · Paper 1 timed (2/2)

> **Goal:** Finish any unanswered parts of the Paper 1 you started, mark it strictly with the official mark scheme, and turn every lost mark into an action in your error log.

## Before you start

- Your answer script from session 1/2 (the same Paper 1, variant 11/12/13, from a recent series such as 2024 or 2025).
- The **official mark scheme** for that exact paper and variant (Cambridge publishes these; check the variant number matches).
- If available, the **examiner report** (Principal Examiner Report for Teachers) for that series — it explains common errors.
- Different-coloured pen for marking; your error log; calculator.

## Exam format reminder

- 1 h 30, 80 marks, 50% of the IGCSE; four compulsory 20-mark questions based on short stimulus material.
- Typical part structure: Define [2] · Identify/Calculate [2] · Outline [4] · Explain [6] · Justify/Do you think [6].
- Mark schemes for 6-mark parts usually list the marks available for knowledge, application, analysis and (in the final part) evaluation — check how they are split in your paper.

## How to do it

| Time | What to do |
|---|---|
| 0–10 min | Finish any parts left unanswered in session 1, in a different colour (so you can compare "in time" and "with extra time" marks). |
| 10–40 min | Mark question by question with the mark scheme. Be strict — only give a mark if your answer clearly matches the scheme's point *and* level. |
| 40–50 min | Add up: mark in time (out of 80) and mark with extra time. Convert to a percentage. |
| 50–60 min | Error log + rewrite your two weakest 6-mark answers to full-mark standard. |

## Exam technique for this paper

When you compare your answers with the mark scheme, look for these patterns:

- **Definitions:** did you have both parts of the definition? Mark schemes often give 1 mark for a partial definition. Learn the scheme's wording for any you missed.
- **Application:** mark schemes credit points that refer to the specific business. If your answer could be about any business, you probably lost the application mark.
- **Analysis:** the mark goes to the **consequence** — "…so the business's costs rise and profit falls". Did each point go one step further than the knowledge?
- **Evaluation (justify questions):** look for the marks for a decision that is supported. A one-line conclusion that repeats a point usually gets limited credit; a comparison ("better than X because…") plus a condition ("depends on…") gets more.
- **Calculations:** the "own figure rule" (OFR) often lets you get later marks using your earlier wrong answer, if your method is correct — so always show working.
- **Command words:** "Identify" needs no explanation; "Explain" needs development; "Outline" sits in between. Note any time you wrote too much for a 2-mark part (wasted time) or too little for a 6-mark part.

## Checklist while marking

- [ ] Each mark you award matches a point in the scheme — no "generous" marks.
- [ ] For each 6-mark answer, circle K, App, An (and Ev) in your answer. Missing letters = lost marks.
- [ ] Calculations: correct formula, correct numbers from the data, units, sensible rounding.
- [ ] Did you answer the number of points requested (two, not three)?
- [ ] Timing: which question took longest? Did you finish in 1 h 30?

**Error log format** (one line per lost mark or group of marks):

| Paper / Q | Topic | Marks lost | Type of error | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| e.g. 2024 P1 Q3(d) | Motivation | 2 | No application — generic | Rewrite using stimulus facts |
| e.g. Q2(b) | Break-even | 1 | Divided by price not contribution | Redo 3 break-even questions |

Error types to use: knowledge gap · definition incomplete · no application · no analysis · no judgement · calculation · misread command word · timing.

Then pick the **two most frequent error types** and write a one-line target for your next paper (e.g. "Every 6-mark point ends with an effect on profit or sales").
