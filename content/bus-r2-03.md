# Past paper · Paper 2 case study (1/2)

> **Goal:** Sit a full Paper 2 case study in 1 h 30 under exam conditions, practising fast reading of the insert and full-length, justified 12-mark answers.

## Before you start

- **Paper:** a Cambridge IGCSE Business Studies 0450 **Paper 2** (variant 21, 22 or 23) **and its insert** (the case study booklet), from the most recent series available — e.g. May/June or October/November 2024 or 2025 — that you have **not** done. Papers from 2023 onwards follow the current syllabus format.
- **Mark scheme** for the same variant — keep it closed until session 2/2.
- Timer set for 1 h 30; calculator (allowed); highlighters or two pen colours for annotating the insert; ruler; black pen.
- No notes, no phone.

## Exam format reminder

- **Duration:** 1 hour 30 minutes. **Marks:** 80. **Weighting:** 50% of the IGCSE.
- **One case study** about a single business, given in a separate insert with several **appendices** (e.g. financial data, survey results, options, an organisation chart, a cash-flow forecast, a location map).
- **Four compulsory questions, 20 marks each**, each usually split into:
  - (a) about **8 marks** — typically "Explain…" (several applied and developed points);
  - (b) about **12 marks** — typically "Consider… Recommend / Do you think… Justify your answer", requiring evaluation.
- Always follow the marks in brackets on your paper.
- **Timing:** ~10 min reading + ~20 min per question (≈ 8 min for the 8-mark part, ≈ 12–14 min for the 12-mark part).

## How to do it

| Time | What to do |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | Read all the questions first and underline the command words and which appendix each refers to. |
| 0:03–0:12 | Read the insert. Annotate: business name, legal form, size, objectives, products, target market, problems, changes. For each appendix write one key fact/trend in the margin. Do needed calculations. |
| 0:12–0:31 | Question 1 (a) then (b). |
| 0:31–0:50 | Question 2. |
| 0:50–1:09 | Question 3. |
| 1:09–1:28 | Question 4. |
| 1:28–1:30 | Check every (b) part ends with a clear recommendation. |

Stop at 1 h 30 exactly. If unfinished, mark where you stopped; finish in session 2/2 in a different colour. Use any time left in the hour to list what slowed you down (reading? calculations? long (a) answers?).

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Application is everything.** The whole paper is about one business — every paragraph should name it or use one of its facts or figures. Generic textbook answers stay in the lowest level.
- **(a) "Explain" parts:** write the number of points asked for, one paragraph each: **point (K) → link to the case (App) → consequence (An)**. Aim for each point to reach an outcome for the business (sales, costs, profit, cash flow, motivation, objectives).
- **(b) "Consider… Recommend… Justify" parts:**
  1. For each option/factor: one advantage and one disadvantage, applied and analysed, using appendix data.
  2. A final paragraph: "I recommend… because [decisive reason from the case]. This is better than [other option] because [comparison]. However, it depends on / the business should also… [condition]."
- **Use the appendices:** calculate where it helps (percentage change, break-even, ratios, cost difference vs cash available) — calculated evidence makes analysis and evaluation more convincing.
- **Don't repeat the case.** Copying out sentences earns nothing; use them to support a point.
- **Balance time:** a 12-mark part deserves almost twice as long as an 8-mark part. Examiners' reports regularly note candidates who write too much in (a) and run short in (b).
- **Answer the question set** — "to the business" vs "to its employees"; "advantages and disadvantages" means both.

## Checklist while marking

(Detailed marking happens in session 2/2.) Note now:
- Which questions you did not finish or rushed.
- Whether each (b) part has a decision paragraph with comparison and a condition.
- Which appendix data you used — and which you ignored.

Error log line format: **Paper/Q · topic · marks lost · error type · fix** (error types: no application · weak analysis · no/weak judgement · ignored data · calculation · misread question · timing).

## Quick-fire revision

Last check before starting:
- K → App → An chain for every point.
- Decision paragraph = **decision + why best + why others worse + depends on**.
- Calculations you may need from appendices: percentage change; break-even; profit = revenue − total costs; gross profit margin, profit margin, ROCE; current ratio, acid test; cash-flow closing balance; market share; labour productivity.
- Typical case topics: choosing a source of finance; a location; a marketing mix for a new product; motivation/recruitment/training; growth (merger vs internal growth); responding to external change (recession, exchange rate, new law); improving cash flow; environmental and ethical issues.
- Short term vs long term and stakeholders' views are good ways to develop a judgement.
