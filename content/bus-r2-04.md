## Exam format reminder

- 1 h 30, 80 marks, 50% of the IGCSE; one case study with appendices in an insert.
- Four compulsory 20-mark questions, typically (a) ≈ 8 marks "Explain…" and (b) ≈ 12 marks "Consider… Recommend… Justify".
- Longer parts are marked with **levels of response**: knowledge → application → analysis → evaluation. The mark scheme lists the indicative content (possible points) and the level descriptors.

## Exam technique for this paper

How to judge the level of your answer honestly:

- **Knowledge only:** correct business points but no reference to the case → could be about any business.
- **Application:** points are linked to the business (its name, products, workers, data).
- **Analysis:** each point is developed — "this means… so… which leads to…" — to an effect on the business. Two well-developed points beat five undeveloped ones.
- **Evaluation:** a supported judgement. Top-level evaluation in a recommendation question usually:
  - chooses one option clearly;
  - explains why it is better than the alternatives, using case evidence (e.g. cost compared with cash available, effect on the stated objective);
  - adds a condition or qualification ("this depends on…", "in the long term…").

Compare your answers with the indicative content. Ask:
- Did I use the appendix data the examiner expected (look for figures quoted in the mark scheme)?
- Did my judgement just repeat earlier points? If so, it is probably not credited as evaluation.
- Did I cover both advantages and disadvantages when asked to "consider"?
- Was any part far too long for its marks?

## Quick-fire revision

A model structure for a 12-mark recommendation (learn it):

1. **Option A** — advantage (App + An); disadvantage (App + An).
2. **Option B** — advantage (App + An); disadvantage (App + An).
3. **(Option C** if given — same.)
4. **Decision:** "I recommend B because… [link to objective/main problem, use data]. A is less suitable because… C is less suitable because… However, B will only succeed if…"

Useful analysis links: → higher/lower costs → profit; → sales/revenue → market share; → cash flow → ability to pay debts; → motivation → productivity/labour turnover; → brand image → customer loyalty.

Useful evaluation angles: short term vs long term; cost vs benefit; the business's objectives; its size, finance and legal form; risk; stakeholders' reactions; what the data does not tell you.
