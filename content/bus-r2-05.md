# Formulae and definitions

> **Goal:** Be able to write every Business Studies formula and the most-tested definitions from memory, and apply each formula correctly in under two minutes.

## Before you start

- This page, blank paper, calculator (allowed in both papers), flashcards, your error log.
- Optional: a recent Paper 1 (2024 or 2025, variant you have not done) to pick out its calculation and "Define" questions for extra practice.

## Exam format reminder

- **Paper 1** (1 h 30, 80 marks): four 20-mark questions; "Define" [2] and "Calculate" [2] parts appear in almost every question.
- **Paper 2** (1 h 30, 80 marks): case study; calculations from appendices are used as evidence in (a) and (b) answers.
- A full definition usually needs **two elements** for 2 marks. A calculation usually gets **1 mark for method, 1 for the answer** — show working and units.

## How to do it

| Time | What to do |
|---|---|
| 0–10 min | Read the formula tables; cover the right column and recall. |
| 10–25 min | Do the worked mini-examples below without looking at the answers. |
| 25–45 min | Definitions: cover the right column, say/write each one, tick or cross. Make flashcards for crosses. |
| 45–55 min | Blank-page test: write all formulae and 20 definitions from memory. |
| 55–60 min | Check and log gaps in the error log. |

## Exam technique for this paper

- Write the **formula in words first**, then substitute numbers — it secures the method mark.
- Give units: €, %, units of output, or "times"/ratio (current ratio 1.5 or 1.5:1).
- Round sensibly (2 decimal places for ratios and %; **round break-even up** to a whole unit).
- After calculating, **interpret**: "the acid test ratio of 0.6 means the business may struggle to pay its short-term debts".
- For definitions, use the precise terms below; vague answers ("money a business makes") lose marks.

## Checklist while marking

- [ ] Correct formula and correct data used?
- [ ] Units and rounding correct?
- [ ] Definition contains both key elements?
- [ ] Log any formula you could not recall as "knowledge gap — formula" and retest tomorrow.

## Quick-fire revision

### Costs, revenue and break-even

| Item | Formula |
|---|---|
| Total revenue | selling price × quantity sold |
| Total variable cost | variable cost per unit × output |
| Total cost | fixed costs + total variable costs |
| Average cost per unit | total cost ÷ output |
| Profit | total revenue − total costs |
| Contribution per unit | selling price − variable cost per unit |
| Break-even output | total fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit |
| Margin of safety | actual output − break-even output |
| Added value | selling price − cost of bought-in materials |

Mini-example: FC €9,000, price €15, VC €9. Contribution = €6; break-even = 9,000 ÷ 6 = 1,500 units. Sales 2,000 → margin of safety 500 units; profit = 500 × €6 = €3,000.

### Cash flow and working capital

| Item | Formula |
|---|---|
| Net cash flow | total cash inflows − total cash outflows |
| Closing balance | opening balance + net cash flow |
| Next month's opening balance | this month's closing balance |
| Working capital | current assets − current liabilities |

Mini-example: opening €1,000, inflows €4,500, outflows €6,000 → net cash flow (€1,500); closing balance (€500).

### Financial statements and ratios

| Item | Formula |
|---|---|
| Gross profit | revenue − cost of sales |
| Profit | gross profit − expenses |
| Retained profit | profit − tax − dividends |
| Gross profit margin (%) | gross profit ÷ revenue × 100 |
| Profit margin (%) | profit ÷ revenue × 100 |
| Capital employed | shareholders' equity + non-current liabilities |
| ROCE (%) | profit ÷ capital employed × 100 |
| Current ratio | current assets ÷ current liabilities |
| Acid test ratio | (current assets − inventories) ÷ current liabilities |

Mini-example: revenue €400,000, cost of sales €260,000, expenses €100,000, capital employed €250,000. Gross profit €140,000 → GPM 35%; profit €40,000 → profit margin 10%; ROCE = 40,000 ÷ 250,000 × 100 = 16%.

### Other calculations

| Item | Formula |
|---|---|
| Labour productivity | output ÷ number of employees (per time period) |
| Market share (%) | firm's sales ÷ total market sales × 100 |
| Percentage change | (new − old) ÷ old × 100 |
| Cost-plus price | unit cost + (mark-up % × unit cost) |
| Commission | commission rate × value of sales |
| Re-order level | buffer inventory + (daily usage × lead time in days) |
| Exchange rate: € price → foreign price | € price × foreign currency per €1 |
| Exchange rate: foreign price → € | foreign price ÷ foreign currency per €1 |

### 40 key definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Opportunity cost | The next best alternative given up by choosing another item |
| Added value | The difference between the selling price and the cost of bought-in materials and components |
| Specialisation | People and businesses concentrating on what they are best at |
| Entrepreneur | A person who organises, operates and takes the risk for a new business venture |
| Limited liability | Shareholders can lose only the amount they invested in the business |
| Franchise | A business based on the use of the brand names, logos and trading methods of an existing successful business |
| Joint venture | Two or more businesses agree to work together on a project, sharing capital, risks and profits |
| Stakeholder | Any person or group with a direct interest in the performance and activities of a business |
| Merger | The owners of two businesses agree to join their businesses together to make one business |
| Takeover | One business buys out the owners of another business |
| Multinational | A business with factories, production or service operations in more than one country |
| Motivation | The reasons why employees want to work hard and work effectively for the business |
| Labour turnover | The rate at which employees leave a business and need to be replaced |
| Job enrichment | Adding tasks to a job that require more skill and/or responsibility |
| Span of control | The number of subordinates working directly under a manager |
| Delegation | Giving a subordinate the authority to perform particular tasks |
| Job description | A document outlining the responsibilities and duties of a specific job |
| Person specification | A document outlining the qualifications, experience and qualities of the ideal person for a job |
| Redundancy | An employee is no longer needed and loses their job, not because of unsatisfactory work |
| Market segment | An identifiable sub-group of a market in which consumers have similar characteristics or preferences |
| Primary research | The collection of original data (first-hand) |
| Secondary research | The use of information already collected and available for use by others |
| Brand loyalty | Consumers keep buying the same brand instead of choosing a competitor's |
| Price skimming | Setting a high price for a new product when it is first launched |
| Penetration pricing | Setting a price lower than competitors' to enter a new market |
| Productivity | A measure of the efficiency of inputs used in production over a period of time |
| Lean production | Techniques used to reduce waste and therefore increase efficiency |
| Just-in-time | Materials arrive just as they are needed, so no inventories are held |
| Quality assurance | Checking for quality standards throughout the production process |
| Economies of scale | Factors that lead to a reduction in average costs as a business increases in size |
| Break-even level of output | The quantity that must be produced/sold for total revenue to equal total costs |
| Margin of safety | The amount by which sales exceed the break-even point |
| Overdraft | A bank lets a business take out more than it has in its account, up to an agreed limit |
| Cash-flow forecast | An estimate of future cash inflows and outflows, usually month by month |
| Working capital | Current assets minus current liabilities: finance for day-to-day running |
| Capital employed | Shareholders' equity plus non-current liabilities |
| Liquidity | The ability of a business to pay back its short-term debts |
| Inflation | The general rise in prices in an economy |
| External costs | Costs of an activity paid by the rest of society, not by the business |
| Exchange-rate depreciation | A fall in the value of a currency compared with other currencies |
