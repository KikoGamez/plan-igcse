# States of matter and atoms

> **In the exam:** In Co-ordinated Sciences 0654 (Extended) chemistry is about one third of every paper, mixed in with biology and physics. **Paper 2** (Multiple Choice, 45 min, 40 questions, 30%) usually has 1–2 questions here (particle diagrams, changes of state, counting protons/neutrons/electrons, isotopes). **Paper 4** (Theory, 2 h, 120 marks, 50%) often has a chemistry part on atomic structure, electronic configuration, isotopes or kinetic particle theory (typically 3–8 marks). **Paper 6** (Alternative to Practical, 1 h 30 min, 60 marks, 20%) can ask you to plot or interpret a heating or cooling curve or explain a diffusion experiment.

## What you need to know

- State the distinguishing properties of solids, liquids and gases.
- Describe the structure of solids, liquids and gases in terms of particle separation, arrangement and motion.
- Describe changes of state in terms of melting, boiling, evaporating, freezing and condensing.
- Explain changes of state in terms of kinetic particle theory, including the interpretation of heating and cooling curves. *(Supplement)*
- Describe the effects of temperature and pressure on the volume of a gas.
- Explain, in terms of kinetic particle theory, the effects of temperature and pressure on the volume of a gas. *(Supplement)*
- Describe and explain diffusion in terms of kinetic particle theory.
- Describe and explain the effect of relative molecular mass on the rate of diffusion of gases. *(Supplement)*
- Describe the differences between elements, compounds and mixtures.
- Describe the structure of the atom as a central nucleus containing neutrons and protons, surrounded by electrons in shells.
- State the relative charges and relative masses of a proton, a neutron and an electron.
- Define proton number / atomic number as the number of protons in the nucleus of an atom, and mass number / nucleon number as the total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus of an atom.
- Determine the electronic configuration of elements with proton number 1 to 20, e.g. 2,8,3.
- State that Group VIII noble gases have a full outer shell, that the number of outer-shell electrons is equal to the group number in Groups I to VII, and that the number of occupied electron shells is equal to the period number.
- Define isotopes as different atoms of the same element that have the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons; interpret and use symbols for atoms, e.g. ¹²₆C, and ions, e.g. ³⁵₁₇Cl⁻.
- State that isotopes of the same element have the same chemical properties because they have the same number of electrons and therefore the same electronic configuration. *(Supplement)*

*Not in 0654:* calculating a relative atomic mass from isotope abundances (that is in separate Chemistry 0620). You only need to **describe** Ar as the average mass of the isotopes compared with 1/12th of the mass of a ¹²C atom (see the stoichiometry topic).

## Notes

### Solids, liquids and gases

| | Solid | Liquid | Gas |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shape | fixed | takes shape of container | fills container |
| Volume | fixed | fixed | not fixed (fills container) |
| Compressible? | no | no (very slightly) | yes |
| Separation | very close, touching | close, touching | far apart |
| Arrangement | regular (lattice) | random | random |
| Motion | vibrate about fixed positions | move around / slide past each other | move quickly in all directions (random) |

Particle diagrams: draw solids as a regular grid of touching circles, liquids as touching circles in an irregular arrangement (at least half the circles touching), gases as a few widely spaced circles.

### Changes of state

| Change | From → to | Energy |
|---|---|---|
| Melting | solid → liquid | taken in |
| Boiling / evaporating | liquid → gas | taken in |
| Freezing | liquid → solid | given out |
| Condensing | gas → liquid | given out |

- **Boiling** happens at a fixed temperature (the boiling point) throughout the liquid. **Evaporation** happens only at the surface and at any temperature below the boiling point.
- When a solid is heated, the particles gain kinetic energy and vibrate more. At the melting point the particles have enough energy to overcome the forces of attraction holding them in fixed positions.

### Heating and cooling curves

On a heating curve (temperature against time) the **flat sections** are changes of state. The temperature stays constant because the energy supplied is used to overcome the forces of attraction between particles, not to increase their kinetic energy. The first flat section is the melting point, the second the boiling point. On a cooling curve, the flat sections are condensing and freezing; energy is released as attractions form, so temperature stays constant.

A **pure** substance has a sharp, fixed melting point. An impure substance melts over a range of temperatures (and lower than the pure substance) — this links to Topic 12 (purity).

### Effect of temperature and pressure on gas volume

Core: describe the effect (hotter → bigger volume at constant pressure; higher pressure → smaller volume at constant temperature). Supplement: explain it with particles:


- **Increase temperature (at constant pressure):** particles gain kinetic energy and move faster, they collide with the walls harder and more often, so the gas expands (volume increases).
- **Increase pressure (at constant temperature):** particles are pushed closer together, so the volume decreases.

### Diffusion

**Diffusion** is the net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration, due to the random movement of particles. It is faster in gases than in liquids (gas particles move faster and are further apart) and faster at higher temperatures.

*Supplement:* gases with a **lower relative molecular mass diffuse faster**. Classic experiment: cotton wool soaked in concentrated ammonia at one end of a glass tube, concentrated hydrochloric acid at the other. A white ring of ammonium chloride forms **nearer the hydrochloric acid end** because NH₃ (Mr = 17) diffuses faster than HCl (Mr = 36.5).

NH₃(g) + HCl(g) → NH₄Cl(s)

### Elements, compounds and mixtures

- **Element:** a substance made of only one type of atom; cannot be broken down into simpler substances by chemical means.
- **Compound:** a substance made of two or more elements chemically combined in a fixed ratio.
- **Mixture:** two or more substances not chemically combined; can be separated by physical methods (filtration, distillation, chromatography).

### Atomic structure

| Particle | Relative charge | Relative mass | Location |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proton | +1 | 1 | nucleus |
| Neutron | 0 | 1 | nucleus |
| Electron | −1 | 1/1840 | shells around nucleus |

- **Proton number (atomic number), Z:** the number of protons in the nucleus of an atom.
- **Mass number (nucleon number), A:** the total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus of an atom.
- Neutrons = mass number − proton number. In a neutral atom, electrons = protons.
- Symbol ²³₁₁Na: 11 protons, 12 neutrons, 11 electrons. The ion ²³₁₁Na⁺ has 11 protons, 12 neutrons, **10** electrons.
- Atoms are neutral because the number of protons equals the number of electrons.

### Electronic configuration (Z = 1 to 20)

Shells fill in the order 2, 8, 8, 2 (first shell holds 2, second 8, third 8 before the fourth starts for K and Ca).

| Element | Z | Configuration | Group | Period |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C | 6 | 2,4 | IV | 2 |
| O | 8 | 2,6 | VI | 2 |
| Na | 11 | 2,8,1 | I | 3 |
| Cl | 17 | 2,8,7 | VII | 3 |
| Ar | 18 | 2,8,8 | VIII | 3 |
| Ca | 20 | 2,8,8,2 | II | 4 |

- **Group number = number of outer-shell electrons** (Groups I–VII). Noble gases (Group VIII) have a full outer shell.
- **Period number = number of occupied shells.**
- Ions have noble-gas configurations: Na⁺ 2,8; Cl⁻ 2,8,8; O²⁻ 2,8; Ca²⁺ 2,8,8.

### Isotopes

**Isotopes** are different atoms of the same element that have the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons. Example: ³⁵₁₇Cl (18 neutrons) and ³⁷₁₇Cl (20 neutrons).

*Supplement:* isotopes have the **same chemical properties** because they have the same number of electrons and therefore the same electronic configuration (chemistry depends on electrons, not neutrons). Physical properties that depend on mass (e.g. density) can differ slightly.

### Relative atomic mass (description only)

Ar is the **average** mass of the isotopes of an element compared with 1/12th of the mass of an atom of ¹²C. This is why Ar values are not always whole numbers (chlorine is 35.5 because it is a mixture of ³⁵Cl and ³⁷Cl, with more ³⁵Cl). In 0654 you are not asked to calculate Ar from abundances.

### Paper 6 skills on this topic

- Read a thermometer to the nearest 0.5 °C (or 1 °C, depending on scale) and record temperatures to the same precision.
- Plot a cooling curve: time on the x-axis, temperature on the y-axis, sensible scales using at least half the grid, a smooth curve, and identify the freezing point from the flat section.
- Safety: when melting a solid such as stearic acid or heating a substance, use a water bath for flammable substances; wear eye protection.
- Explain why ammonia/HCl diffusion is done in a fume cupboard (toxic, corrosive gases).

## Key terms and definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Proton number / atomic number | The number of protons in the nucleus of an atom |
| Mass number / nucleon number | The total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus of an atom |
| Isotopes | Different atoms of the same element that have the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons |
| Relative atomic mass, Ar | The average mass of the isotopes of an element compared to 1/12th of the mass of an atom of ¹²C |
| Diffusion | The net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration, due to random movement of particles |
| Element | A substance that cannot be broken down into simpler substances by chemical methods; contains only one type of atom |
| Compound | A substance containing two or more elements chemically combined |
| Mixture | Two or more substances not chemically combined |

## Worked examples

**1.** Explain, in terms of particles, why the temperature of a pure liquid stays constant while it is boiling. [2]

- Energy (heat) supplied is used to overcome the forces of attraction between particles (1)
- so the kinetic energy of the particles does not increase / energy is not used to raise the temperature (1)

**2.** Complete the table for the particles ²⁷₁₃Al³⁺ and ³²₁₆S²⁻. [4]

| | Protons | Neutrons | Electrons |
|---|---|---|---|
| ²⁷₁₃Al³⁺ | 13 | 14 | 10 |
| ³²₁₆S²⁻ | 16 | 16 | 18 |

One mark per correct row of two values; one mark for each electron count (4 in total; electrons are the most common error).

**3.** An element X has proton number 15. (a) Give its electronic configuration. (b) Use your answer to state the group and period of X in the Periodic Table. [3]

- (a) 2,8,5 (1)
- (b) Group V, because it has 5 outer-shell electrons (1); Period 3, because it has 3 occupied electron shells (1)

**4.** A student places cotton wool soaked in concentrated ammonia at one end of a long glass tube and cotton wool soaked in concentrated hydrochloric acid at the other end. A white solid forms after a few minutes. Explain why the white solid forms closer to the hydrochloric acid end. [3]

- Ammonia has a lower relative molecular mass (17) than hydrogen chloride (36.5) (1)
- so ammonia particles move faster / diffuse faster (1)
- so ammonia travels further along the tube before the two gases meet and react (1)

## Common mistakes

- Writing "particles expand" or "particles melt" — particles themselves never change size or state; the spacing and motion change.
- Saying gas particles "vibrate" — they move randomly at high speed; only solid particles vibrate about fixed positions.
- Getting electrons wrong for ions: positive ions have **fewer** electrons than protons, negative ions **more**.
- Defining isotopes as "same element, different mass" — mark schemes want **same number of protons, different numbers of neutrons**.
- Saying isotopes have the same chemical properties "because they have the same number of protons" — the reason is the **same number of (outer) electrons / same electronic configuration**.
- On heating curves, failing to say that energy is used to **overcome forces of attraction** between particles.
- Mixing up boiling and evaporation (evaporation is at the surface, below the boiling point).

## Practice questions

1. Describe the arrangement and motion of particles in a liquid. [2]
2. State the electronic configuration of (a) a magnesium atom, (b) a sulfide ion, S²⁻, (c) a potassium ion. [3]
3. Explain why a balloon filled with air gets bigger when it is moved into a warm room. Assume the pressure stays the same. [3]
4. Bromine has two isotopes, ⁷⁹Br and ⁸¹Br, present in almost equal amounts. The Periodic Table gives the relative atomic mass of bromine as 80. Explain why it is 80 and not 79 or 81. [2]
5. ³⁹₁₉K and ⁴⁰₁₉K are isotopes of potassium. (a) State one difference and one similarity in the structure of their atoms. (b) Explain why they react with water in the same way. [4]
6. Which gas diffuses faster at the same temperature: carbon dioxide or methane? Explain. [2]
7. Salt water is a mixture. Sodium chloride is a compound. Explain the difference. [2]

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Particles close together/touching, randomly arranged (1); move around/slide past each other (1).
2. (a) 2,8,2 (b) 2,8,8 (c) 2,8,8 (1 each).
3. Particles gain kinetic energy / move faster (1); collide with the inside of the balloon harder/more often (1); so the gas expands / volume increases (1).
4. Ar is the average mass of the isotopes (compared with 1/12th of a ¹²C atom) (1); bromine is a mixture of ⁷⁹Br and ⁸¹Br in about equal amounts, so the average is halfway, 80 (1).
5. (a) Difference: ³⁹K has 20 neutrons, ⁴⁰K has 21 neutrons (1). Similarity: both have 19 protons / 19 electrons (1). (b) Same number of electrons / same electronic configuration (2,8,8,1) (1); chemical properties depend on electrons / same number of outer-shell electrons (1).
6. Methane (1); lower relative molecular mass (16 compared with 44) so particles move faster (1).
7. In salt water the salt and water are not chemically combined and can be separated by physical means, e.g. evaporation (1); in sodium chloride, sodium and chlorine are chemically combined in a fixed ratio (1).
