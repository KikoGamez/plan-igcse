# The mole: calculations

> **In the exam:** Paper 4 has at least one multi-step mole calculation every series, usually 4–8 marks, often split into small steps ("calculate the number of moles of …", "deduce …", "calculate the volume of …"). Paper 2 has 2–4 calculation questions. Paper 6 may ask you to process titration results. Use the Ar values from the Periodic Table on the data sheet (e.g. Cl = 35.5, Cu = 64) and the molar gas volume 24 dm³ at r.t.p., both given on the paper.

## What you need to know

- Describe relative atomic mass, Ar, as the average mass of the isotopes of an element compared to 1/12th of the mass of an atom of ¹²C.
- Define relative molecular mass, Mr, as the sum of the relative atomic masses; relative formula mass, Mr, is used for ionic compounds.
- Calculate reacting masses in simple proportions (core).
- State that concentration can be measured in g/dm³ or mol/dm³.
- *Extended:* state that the mole, mol, is the unit of amount of substance and that one mole contains 6.02 × 10²³ particles (the Avogadro constant).
- *Extended:* use the relationship amount of substance (mol) = mass (g) ÷ molar mass (g/mol) to calculate amount, mass, molar mass, Ar/Mr, number of particles (using the Avogadro constant).
- *Extended:* use the molar gas volume, 24 dm³ at room temperature and pressure (r.t.p.), in calculations involving gases.
- *Extended:* calculate stoichiometric reacting masses, limiting reactants, volumes of gases at r.t.p., volumes of solutions and concentrations of solutions expressed in g/dm³ and mol/dm³, including conversion between cm³ and dm³.
- *Extended:* use experimental data from a titration to calculate the moles of solute, or the concentration or volume of a solution.

(Empirical formulae, percentage yield, purity and limiting reactants are in the next session.)

## Notes

### Relative masses

Mr = sum of Ar values. Example: Ca(NO₃)₂ = 40 + 2 × (14 + 3 × 16) = 164.
Molar mass (g/mol) has the same number as Mr.

Percentage by mass of an element = (Ar × number of atoms ÷ Mr) × 100.

### The mole

One mole of any substance contains **6.02 × 10²³ particles** (Avogadro constant).

| To find | Formula |
|---|---|
| moles from mass | n = m ÷ M |
| moles from gas volume (r.t.p.) | n = V(dm³) ÷ 24 or V(cm³) ÷ 24 000 |
| moles from solution | n = c (mol/dm³) × V (dm³) |
| number of particles | N = n × 6.02 × 10²³ |
| concentration in g/dm³ | c(g/dm³) = c(mol/dm³) × M |

Unit conversions: 1 dm³ = 1000 cm³, so cm³ → dm³ divide by 1000.

Watch "particles": 1 mol of H₂O contains 6.02 × 10²³ molecules but 3 × 6.02 × 10²³ atoms.

### The four-step method for reacting quantities

1. **Equation** — write or check the balanced equation.
2. **Moles of known** — convert what you are given into moles.
3. **Ratio** — use the balanced equation to get moles of the unknown.
4. **Convert** — moles to mass, volume or concentration as asked.

Show every step: method marks are awarded even if the final answer is wrong ("error carried forward", ecf).

### Gas volumes

At r.t.p. one mole of any gas occupies 24 dm³ (24 000 cm³). Because equal volumes of gases contain equal numbers of moles, **gas volume ratios = mole ratios** (at the same temperature and pressure).
Example: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O. 20 cm³ of methane needs 40 cm³ of oxygen and forms 20 cm³ of CO₂.

### Concentration and titration calculations

- concentration (mol/dm³) = moles ÷ volume (dm³)
- In a titration: moles of the solution with known concentration → use ratio → moles of other → concentration.
- Use the **mean of concordant titres** (titres within 0.20 cm³ of each other).

### Answers: significant figures and units

Give answers to 3 significant figures unless told otherwise (or the same number as the data). Always include units: g, mol, dm³, cm³, mol/dm³, g/dm³.

### Paper 6 skills on this topic

- Reading a burette to the nearest 0.05 cm³, recording initial and final readings, and calculating the titre (final − initial).
- Choosing concordant results, calculating a mean titre.
- Measuring gas volume with a gas syringe or upturned measuring cylinder; reading a measuring cylinder at the bottom of the meniscus.

## Key terms and definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Relative atomic mass, Ar | The average mass of the isotopes of an element compared to 1/12th of the mass of an atom of ¹²C |
| Relative molecular mass, Mr | The sum of the relative atomic masses (relative formula mass for ionic compounds) |
| Mole | The unit of amount of substance; one mole contains 6.02 × 10²³ particles |
| Avogadro constant | 6.02 × 10²³ particles per mole |
| Molar gas volume | 24 dm³ (the volume of one mole of any gas at r.t.p.) |
| Concentration | Amount of solute per unit volume of solution, in g/dm³ or mol/dm³ |

## Worked examples

**1.** Calculate the volume of carbon dioxide, in cm³ at r.t.p., produced when 5.00 g of calcium carbonate reacts with excess dilute hydrochloric acid.
CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂ [3]

- Mr CaCO₃ = 40 + 12 + 48 = 100; n = 5.00 ÷ 100 = 0.0500 mol (1)
- 1 : 1 ratio, so 0.0500 mol CO₂ (1)
- V = 0.0500 × 24 000 = 1200 cm³ (1)

**2.** 25.0 cm³ of aqueous sodium hydroxide is exactly neutralised by 20.0 cm³ of 0.150 mol/dm³ sulfuric acid.
2NaOH + H₂SO₄ → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O
Calculate the concentration of the sodium hydroxide in (a) mol/dm³, (b) g/dm³. [4]

- n(H₂SO₄) = 0.150 × 20.0/1000 = 0.00300 mol (1)
- n(NaOH) = 2 × 0.00300 = 0.00600 mol (1)
- c = 0.00600 ÷ (25.0/1000) = 0.240 mol/dm³ (1)
- (b) Mr NaOH = 40, so 0.240 × 40 = 9.60 g/dm³ (1)

**3.** Calculate the mass of magnesium oxide formed when 3.60 g of magnesium burns completely in oxygen. 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO [3]

- n(Mg) = 3.60 ÷ 24 = 0.150 mol (1)
- n(MgO) = 0.150 mol (1)
- mass = 0.150 × 40 = 6.00 g (1)

**4.** How many atoms are in 0.500 mol of water molecules? [1]

0.500 × 3 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 9.03 × 10²³ atoms (1)

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting to convert cm³ to dm³ (dividing by 1000) in concentration calculations.
- Ignoring the mole ratio — using 1 : 1 when the equation is 2 : 1.
- Using Mr of the element as diatomic incorrectly: n(Cl₂) uses Mr = 71, not 35.5; n(O₂) uses 32.
- Rounding too early: keep at least 3–4 significant figures in intermediate steps.
- Using 22.4 dm³ (wrong — Cambridge uses 24 dm³ at r.t.p.).
- Missing units, or giving 1.2 when the answer needs cm³ (1200 cm³).
- Not showing working — an unsupported wrong answer scores zero.

## Practice questions

Use Ar: H = 1, C = 12, N = 14, O = 16, Na = 23, Mg = 24, S = 32, Cl = 35.5, K = 39, Ca = 40, Cu = 64.

1. Calculate Mr of (a) (NH₄)₂SO₄, (b) Ca(NO₃)₂, (c) CuSO₄·5H₂O. [3]
2. (a) Calculate the number of moles in 8.00 g of sodium hydroxide. (b) How many molecules are in 0.200 mol of carbon dioxide? [2]
3. 4.00 g of sodium hydroxide is dissolved in water to make 250 cm³ of solution. Calculate the concentration in mol/dm³. [2]
4. Calculate the volume of hydrogen, in cm³ at r.t.p., produced when 0.480 g of magnesium reacts with excess dilute hydrochloric acid. Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂ [3]
5. 25.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ potassium hydroxide is neutralised by 22.50 cm³ of hydrochloric acid. Calculate the concentration of the hydrochloric acid. [3]
6. Calculate the mass of copper(II) oxide needed to react exactly with 50.0 cm³ of 2.00 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid. CuO + 2HCl → CuCl₂ + H₂O [3]
7. 2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(l). What volume of oxygen reacts with 60 cm³ of hydrogen (same temperature and pressure)? [1]
8. Calculate the percentage by mass of nitrogen in ammonium nitrate, NH₄NO₃. [2]

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. (a) 2(14 + 4) + 32 + 64 = 132 (b) 40 + 2(14 + 48) = 164 (c) 64 + 32 + 64 + 5 × 18 = 250.
2. (a) 8.00 ÷ 40 = 0.200 mol (b) 0.200 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 1.20 × 10²³ molecules.
3. n = 4.00 ÷ 40 = 0.100 mol (1); c = 0.100 ÷ 0.250 = 0.400 mol/dm³ (1).
4. n(Mg) = 0.480 ÷ 24 = 0.0200 mol (1); n(H₂) = 0.0200 mol (1); V = 0.0200 × 24 000 = 480 cm³ (1).
5. n(KOH) = 0.100 × 0.0250 = 0.00250 mol (1); n(HCl) = 0.00250 mol (1); c = 0.00250 ÷ 0.02250 = 0.111 mol/dm³ (1).
6. n(HCl) = 2.00 × 0.0500 = 0.100 mol (1); n(CuO) = 0.0500 mol (1); mass = 0.0500 × 80 = 4.00 g (1).
7. 30 cm³.
8. Mr = 80 (1); (28 ÷ 80) × 100 = 35.0% (1).
