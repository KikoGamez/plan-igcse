# The mole: calculations

> **In the exam:** In Co-ordinated Sciences 0654 the mole is **Supplement** content, so it appears on the Extended papers. **Paper 4** (Theory, 2 h, 120 marks across biology, chemistry and physics) often has a short mole calculation inside a chemistry question (typically 2–4 marks, split into steps such as "calculate the number of moles of …", "calculate the volume of …"). **Paper 2** (Multiple Choice, 45 min, 40 questions) usually has 1–2 calculation questions. **Paper 6** (Alternative to Practical, 1 h 30 min, 60 marks) may ask you to process mass or gas-volume readings. The Periodic Table (with Ar values, e.g. Cl = 35.5, Cu = 64) and the molar gas volume, 24 dm³ at r.t.p., are printed on the paper.

## What you need to know

- Describe relative atomic mass, Ar, as the average mass of the isotopes of an element compared to 1/12th of the mass of an atom of ¹²C.
- Define relative molecular mass, Mr, as the sum of the relative atomic masses. Relative formula mass, Mr, will be used for ionic compounds.
- Calculate reacting masses in simple proportions (calculations will not involve the mole concept).
- State that concentration can be measured in g/dm³.
- *Supplement:* state that the mole, mol, is the unit of amount of substance and that one mole contains 6.02 × 10²³ particles, e.g. atoms, ions, molecules; this number is the Avogadro constant.
- *Supplement:* use the relationship amount of substance (mol) = mass (g) ÷ molar mass (g/mol) to calculate (a) amount of substance, (b) mass, (c) molar mass, (d) relative atomic mass or relative molecular/formula mass.
- *Supplement:* use the molar gas volume, taken as 24 dm³ at room temperature and pressure, r.t.p., in calculations involving gases.
- *Supplement:* calculate stoichiometric reacting masses, limiting reactants, volumes of gases at r.t.p., including conversion between cm³ and dm³.

*Not in 0654 (separate Chemistry 0620 only):* concentrations in mol/dm³, titration calculations, empirical formulae, percentage yield, percentage purity and percentage composition. Limiting reactants are practised in the next session.

## Notes

### Relative masses

Mr = sum of Ar values. Example: Ca(NO₃)₂ = 40 + 2 × (14 + 3 × 16) = 164.
Molar mass (g/mol) has the same number as Mr (Ca(NO₃)₂: 164 g/mol).

### Core: reacting masses in simple proportions

Without moles, use the fact that masses react in a fixed ratio.
Example: 24 g of magnesium forms 40 g of magnesium oxide. What mass of magnesium oxide forms from 6 g of magnesium?
6 g is 24 ÷ 4, so mass = 40 ÷ 4 = **10 g**.

### Core: concentration in g/dm³

concentration (g/dm³) = mass of solute (g) ÷ volume of solution (dm³). 4.00 g dissolved to make 250 cm³ (0.250 dm³) → 16.0 g/dm³.

### The mole (Supplement)

One mole of any substance contains **6.02 × 10²³ particles** (atoms, ions or molecules): the Avogadro constant.

| To find | Formula |
|---|---|
| amount (moles) from mass | n = m ÷ M |
| mass from moles | m = n × M |
| molar mass (and so Mr or Ar) | M = m ÷ n |
| moles from gas volume at r.t.p. | n = V(dm³) ÷ 24 or V(cm³) ÷ 24 000 |
| gas volume from moles | V = n × 24 dm³ (or × 24 000 cm³) |

Unit conversion: 1 dm³ = 1000 cm³, so cm³ → dm³ divide by 1000.

### The four-step method for reacting quantities

1. **Equation** — write or check the balanced equation.
2. **Moles of known** — convert what you are given into moles.
3. **Ratio** — use the balanced equation to get moles of the unknown.
4. **Convert** — moles to mass or gas volume as asked.

Show every step: method marks are awarded even if the final answer is wrong ("error carried forward", ecf).

### Gas volumes

At r.t.p. one mole of any gas occupies 24 dm³ (24 000 cm³). Because equal volumes of gases contain equal numbers of moles, **gas volume ratios = mole ratios** (at the same temperature and pressure).
Example: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O. 20 cm³ of methane needs 40 cm³ of oxygen and forms 20 cm³ of CO₂.

### Finding Ar or Mr from data

0.0250 mol of a metal oxide, MO, has a mass of 2.00 g. M = 2.00 ÷ 0.0250 = 80 g/mol, so Mr = 80. Ar of M = 80 − 16 = 64 → copper.

### Answers: significant figures and units

Give answers to 3 significant figures unless told otherwise (or the same number as the least precise data). Always include units: g, mol, g/mol, dm³, cm³, g/dm³.

### Paper 6 skills on this topic

- Measuring gas volume with a gas syringe or an upturned measuring cylinder; reading a measuring cylinder at the bottom of the meniscus.
- Recording masses from a balance to the precision shown (e.g. 0.01 g) and calculating a change in mass.
- Knowing that a titration uses a burette, a volumetric pipette and an indicator (C12.2); you may have to read a burette (to the nearest 0.05 cm³) and work out a titre, but not calculate a concentration in mol/dm³.

## Key terms and definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Relative atomic mass, Ar | The average mass of the isotopes of an element compared to 1/12th of the mass of an atom of ¹²C |
| Relative molecular mass, Mr | The sum of the relative atomic masses (relative formula mass for ionic compounds) |
| Mole | The unit of amount of substance; one mole contains 6.02 × 10²³ particles |
| Avogadro constant | 6.02 × 10²³, the number of particles in one mole |
| Molar gas volume | 24 dm³, the volume of one mole of any gas at r.t.p. |
| Molecular formula | The number and type of atoms in one molecule |

## Worked examples

**1.** *(Core)* 80 g of iron(III) oxide produces 56 g of iron in a blast furnace. Calculate the mass of iron produced from 20 g of iron(III) oxide. [1]

- 20 g is 80 ÷ 4, so mass of iron = 56 ÷ 4 = 14 g (1)

**2.** Calculate the volume of carbon dioxide, in cm³ at r.t.p., produced when 5.00 g of calcium carbonate reacts with excess dilute hydrochloric acid.
CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂ [3]

- Mr CaCO₃ = 40 + 12 + 48 = 100; n = 5.00 ÷ 100 = 0.0500 mol (1)
- 1 : 1 ratio, so 0.0500 mol CO₂ (1)
- V = 0.0500 × 24 000 = 1200 cm³ (1)

**3.** Calculate the mass of magnesium oxide formed when 3.60 g of magnesium burns completely in oxygen. 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO [3]

- n(Mg) = 3.60 ÷ 24 = 0.150 mol (1)
- n(MgO) = 0.150 mol (2 : 2 ratio) (1)
- mass = 0.150 × 40 = 6.00 g (1)

**4.** 0.0250 mol of an oxide of metal M, formula MO, has a mass of 2.00 g. Calculate the relative atomic mass of M and identify M. [3]

- M(MO) = 2.00 ÷ 0.0250 = 80 g/mol (1)
- Ar(M) = 80 − 16 = 64 (1)
- copper (1)

## Common mistakes

- Ignoring the mole ratio — using 1 : 1 when the equation is 2 : 1.
- Using Ar for a diatomic gas: n(Cl₂) uses Mr = 71, not 35.5; n(O₂) uses 32.
- Forgetting to convert between cm³ and dm³ (24 dm³ = 24 000 cm³).
- Rounding too early: keep at least 3–4 significant figures in intermediate steps.
- Using 22.4 dm³ (wrong — Cambridge uses 24 dm³ at r.t.p.).
- Missing units, or giving 1.2 when the answer needs cm³ (1200 cm³).
- Not showing working — an unsupported wrong answer scores zero.

## Practice questions

Use Ar: H = 1, C = 12, N = 14, O = 16, Na = 23, Mg = 24, S = 32, Cl = 35.5, Ca = 40, Fe = 56, Cu = 64.

1. Calculate Mr of (a) (NH₄)₂SO₄, (b) Ca(NO₃)₂, (c) CuSO₄·5H₂O. [3]
2. *(Core)* 4.00 g of sodium hydroxide is dissolved in water to make 250 cm³ of solution. Calculate the concentration in g/dm³. [2]
3. Calculate the number of moles in 8.00 g of sodium hydroxide. [1]
4. Calculate the volume of hydrogen, in cm³ at r.t.p., produced when 0.480 g of magnesium reacts with excess dilute hydrochloric acid. Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂ [3]
5. *(Core)* 160 g of iron(III) oxide is reduced to 112 g of iron. What mass of iron is produced from 40 g of iron(III) oxide? [1]
6. Calculate the mass of copper(II) oxide needed to make 8.00 g of copper(II) sulfate. CuO + H₂SO₄ → CuSO₄ + H₂O [3]
7. 2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(l). What volume of oxygen reacts with 60 cm³ of hydrogen (same temperature and pressure)? [1]
8. Calculate the mass of 1.20 dm³ of carbon dioxide at r.t.p. [2]

1. (a) 2(14 + 4) + 32 + 64 = 132 (b) 40 + 2(14 + 48) = 164 (c) 64 + 32 + 64 + 5 × 18 = 250.
2. 250 cm³ = 0.250 dm³ (1); 4.00 ÷ 0.250 = 16.0 g/dm³ (1).
3. 8.00 ÷ 40 = 0.200 mol.
4. n(Mg) = 0.480 ÷ 24 = 0.0200 mol (1); n(H₂) = 0.0200 mol (1); V = 0.0200 × 24 000 = 480 cm³ (1).
5. 40 g is 160 ÷ 4, so 112 ÷ 4 = 28 g.
6. Mr CuSO₄ = 160; n = 8.00 ÷ 160 = 0.0500 mol (1); n(CuO) = 0.0500 mol (1); mass = 0.0500 × 80 = 4.00 g (1).
7. 30 cm³.
8. n = 1.20 ÷ 24 = 0.0500 mol (1); mass = 0.0500 × 44 = 2.20 g (1).
