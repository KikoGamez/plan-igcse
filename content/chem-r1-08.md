# Equilibrium and redox

> **In the exam:** Paper 4 very often has a Haber or Contact process question (conditions, equilibrium shifts, compromise; 6–10 marks) and redox questions ("identify the oxidising agent", "explain in terms of electrons", oxidation numbers; 2–4 marks). Paper 2 has 2–3 questions. In Paper 6, redox appears through colour changes of acidified potassium manganate(VII) and potassium iodide, and reversible reactions through hydrated copper(II) sulfate / cobalt(II) chloride.

## What you need to know

**Reversible reactions and equilibrium**
- State that some chemical reactions are reversible, shown by the symbol ⇌.
- Describe how changing the conditions can change the direction of a reversible reaction for: the effect of heat on hydrated compounds; the addition of water to anhydrous compounds — limited to copper(II) sulfate and cobalt(II) chloride.
- *Extended:* state that a reversible reaction in a closed system is at equilibrium when the rate of the forward reaction is equal to the rate of the reverse reaction and the concentrations of reactants and products are no longer changing.
- *Extended:* predict and explain, for a reversible reaction, how the position of equilibrium is affected by changing temperature, pressure, concentration and using a catalyst, using information provided.
- State the symbol equation for the production of ammonia in the Haber process; state the sources of the hydrogen (methane) and nitrogen (air).
- *Extended:* state the typical conditions in the Haber process as 450 °C, 20 000 kPa / 200 atm and an iron catalyst.
- *Extended:* state the symbol equation for the conversion of sulfur dioxide to sulfur trioxide in the Contact process; state the sources of the sulfur dioxide (burning sulfur or roasting sulfide ores) and oxygen (air); state the typical conditions as 450 °C, 200 kPa / 2 atm and a vanadium(V) oxide catalyst.
- *Extended:* explain, in terms of rate of reaction and position of equilibrium, why the typical conditions stated are used, including safety and economic considerations.

**Redox**
- Use a Roman numeral to indicate the oxidation number of an element in a compound.
- Define redox reactions as involving simultaneous oxidation and reduction; define oxidation as gain of oxygen and reduction as loss of oxygen.
- *Extended:* identify redox reactions by changes in oxidation number; define oxidation as loss of electrons and an increase in oxidation number, and reduction as gain of electrons and a decrease in oxidation number.
- *Extended:* identify redox reactions by the colour changes involved when using acidified aqueous potassium manganate(VII) or aqueous potassium iodide.
- *Extended:* define an oxidising agent as a substance that oxidises another substance and is itself reduced; a reducing agent as a substance that reduces another substance and is itself oxidised; identify oxidising and reducing agents in redox reactions.

## Notes

### Reversible reactions with hydrated salts

| Reaction | Forward (heat) | Reverse (add water) |
|---|---|---|
| CuSO₄·5H₂O ⇌ CuSO₄ + 5H₂O | blue → white | white → blue (exothermic) |
| CoCl₂·6H₂O ⇌ CoCl₂ + 6H₂O | pink → blue | blue → pink |

These colour changes are also the chemical **tests for water** (anhydrous copper(II) sulfate white → blue; anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride blue → pink).

### Dynamic equilibrium (Extended)

In a **closed system** (nothing can enter or leave), a reversible reaction reaches equilibrium when:
- the rate of the forward reaction = the rate of the reverse reaction, and
- the concentrations of reactants and products are no longer changing.
Both reactions are still happening ("dynamic").

### Predicting shifts in the position of equilibrium (Extended)

The position of equilibrium shifts to **oppose** the change.

| Change | Equilibrium shifts | Example: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃, ΔH negative |
|---|---|---|
| Increase temperature | in the **endothermic** direction | left → less NH₃ |
| Decrease temperature | in the **exothermic** direction | right → more NH₃ |
| Increase pressure (gases) | to the side with **fewer moles of gas** | right (4 mol gas → 2 mol) → more NH₃ |
| Increase concentration of a reactant | to the right (uses up the added reactant) | right |
| Remove product | to the right | right — ammonia is removed by cooling/liquefying |
| Add a catalyst | **no change** in position; equilibrium is reached faster | |

Always count **moles of gas** from the balanced equation when explaining pressure.

### The Haber process

N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g)   (forward reaction exothermic)

- Sources: nitrogen from **air**; hydrogen from **methane** (natural gas).
- Conditions: **450 °C, 20 000 kPa (200 atm), iron catalyst**.
- Unreacted N₂ and H₂ are recycled.

### The Contact process (Extended)

2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g)   (forward reaction exothermic)

- Sources: SO₂ from **burning sulfur** or **roasting sulfide ores**; O₂ from **air**.
- Conditions: **450 °C, 200 kPa (2 atm), vanadium(V) oxide (V₂O₅) catalyst**.
- SO₃ is then used to make sulfuric acid.

### Explaining the conditions (Extended) — compromise

| Condition | Yield argument | Rate argument | Safety / economics |
|---|---|---|---|
| Temperature 450 °C | lower temperature gives higher yield (exothermic forward reaction) | but too low a temperature gives a slow rate | 450 °C is a compromise between rate and yield |
| Haber 200 atm | high pressure gives higher yield (fewer moles of gas on right) and faster rate | | higher pressures are expensive (energy, equipment) and dangerous; 200 atm is a compromise |
| Contact 2 atm | high pressure would increase yield | | equilibrium already lies far to the right at 2 atm, so high pressure is not worth the cost |
| Catalyst | no effect on yield | increases rate, allows lower temperature to be used | saves energy/cost |

### Oxidation numbers (Extended)

Rules:
- Uncombined element = 0 (Fe, O₂, Cl₂).
- Monatomic ion = its charge (Na⁺ = +1, Cl⁻ = −1, Fe³⁺ = +3).
- O is usually −2; H is usually +1.
- Sum in a compound = 0; sum in a compound ion = the charge on the ion.

Examples: S in SO₄²⁻: x + 4(−2) = −2 → x = +6. Mn in MnO₄⁻: x − 8 = −1 → +7. Cr in Cr₂O₇²⁻: 2x − 14 = −2 → +6. N in NH₄⁺: x + 4 = +1 → −3.

Roman numerals give the oxidation number: iron(III) oxide contains Fe +3; manganese(IV) oxide, MnO₂, contains Mn +4.

### Oxidation and reduction

| | Oxidation | Reduction |
|---|---|---|
| Oxygen | gain of oxygen | loss of oxygen |
| Electrons *(Ext)* | **loss** of electrons | **gain** of electrons |
| Oxidation number *(Ext)* | increase | decrease |

**OIL RIG:** Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain (of electrons).

Example: Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂. Fe₂O₃ is reduced (loses oxygen; Fe +3 → 0); CO is oxidised (gains oxygen; C +2 → +4). CO is the **reducing agent**; Fe₂O₃ is the **oxidising agent**.

Example: Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu. Zn loses electrons (oxidised, reducing agent); Cu²⁺ gains electrons (reduced, oxidising agent).

### Colour-change tests for oxidising and reducing agents (Extended)

| Reagent | Acts as | Colour change if a reaction happens | So the test substance is… |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acidified aqueous potassium manganate(VII) | oxidising agent | **purple → colourless** | a reducing agent |
| Aqueous potassium iodide | reducing agent | **colourless → brown** (iodine formed) | an oxidising agent |

### Paper 6 skills on this topic

- Heating hydrated copper(II) sulfate: observations (blue crystals → white powder; condensation/droplets of colourless liquid at the top of the tube); then adding water back (white → blue, the tube gets warm).
- Describing colour changes as "from X to Y" — always give both colours.

## Key terms and definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Reversible reaction | A reaction that can go in both directions, shown by ⇌ |
| Equilibrium (dynamic) | In a closed system, the rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the reverse reaction and the concentrations of reactants and products are no longer changing |
| Redox | A reaction involving simultaneous oxidation and reduction |
| Oxidation | Gain of oxygen / loss of electrons / increase in oxidation number |
| Reduction | Loss of oxygen / gain of electrons / decrease in oxidation number |
| Oxidising agent | A substance that oxidises another substance and is itself reduced |
| Reducing agent | A substance that reduces another substance and is itself oxidised |

## Worked examples

**1.** For 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g), where the forward reaction is exothermic, explain the effect on the yield of SO₃ of (a) increasing the temperature, (b) increasing the pressure. [4]

- (a) Yield decreases (1); equilibrium shifts in the endothermic direction / to the left (1)
- (b) Yield increases (1); equilibrium shifts to the side with fewer moles of gas (3 mol → 2 mol) / to the right (1)

**2.** Explain why the Haber process uses an iron catalyst. [2]

- Increases the rate of reaction / equilibrium reached faster (1)
- allows a lower temperature to be used / saves energy or cost; no effect on yield (1)

**3.** Cl₂ + 2Br⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + Br₂. Identify the oxidising agent and explain your answer in terms of electrons. [2]

- Chlorine / Cl₂ (1)
- It gains electrons (is reduced), taking them from bromide ions which are oxidised (1)

**4.** Deduce the oxidation number of nitrogen in NO₃⁻. [1]

x + 3(−2) = −1, so x = +5 (1)

## Common mistakes

- Saying that at equilibrium "the reaction has stopped" or "concentrations are equal" — rates are equal; concentrations are **constant**.
- Saying a catalyst "increases yield" or "shifts equilibrium to the right".
- Explaining pressure without counting moles of gas ("fewer molecules" is fine; "smaller side" is not).
- Quoting wrong conditions: Haber 200 atm, Contact 2 atm (not the other way round); Contact catalyst is vanadium(V) oxide, not iron.
- Forgetting that a lower temperature gives a higher yield but a slower rate — "compromise" needs both halves.
- Defining oxidising agent as "a substance that is oxidised" — it is **reduced**.
- Describing KMnO₄ as turning "clear" — say **colourless** (clear means transparent).
