# Physical and chemical change; redox

> **In the exam:** Important for 0654: **reversible reactions, equilibrium, the Haber process and the Contact process are not in the Co-ordinated Sciences 0654 syllabus** (they are in separate Chemistry 0620). This session therefore covers what 0654 does include from topic C6 *Chemical reactions*: **physical and chemical changes** and **redox**, plus the hydrated/anhydrous colour changes used as tests for water. On **Paper 4** (Theory, 2 h, 120 marks across biology, chemistry and physics), redox usually appears as 1–3 marks inside another chemistry question ("identify the substance that is oxidised", "explain in terms of electrons"). **Paper 2** (Multiple Choice, 45 min, 40 questions) may have one redox or physical/chemical change question. **Paper 6** (Alternative to Practical, 1 h 30 min, 60 marks) lists "tests for oxidising and reducing agents" and "chemical tests for water" among its practical contexts.

## What you need to know

**Physical and chemical changes**
- Identify physical and chemical changes, and understand the differences between them.

**Redox**
- Define redox reactions as involving simultaneous oxidation and reduction.
- Define oxidation as gain of oxygen and reduction as loss of oxygen.
- Identify redox reactions as reactions involving gain and loss of oxygen.
- Identify oxidation and reduction in redox reactions. (Oxidation number limited to its use to name ions, e.g. iron(II), iron(III), copper(II).)
- *Supplement:* define oxidation in terms of (a) loss of electrons, (b) an increase in oxidation number (determination of oxidation numbers is not required).
- *Supplement:* define reduction in terms of (a) gain of electrons, (b) a decrease in oxidation number (determination of oxidation numbers is not required).

**Linked content (C7.3, C10.1)**
- Define a hydrated substance as a substance that is chemically combined with water and an anhydrous substance as a substance containing no water.
- Describe chemical tests for the presence of water using anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride and anhydrous copper(II) sulfate.

*Not in 0654:* the ⇌ symbol and reversible reactions, dynamic equilibrium and shifts in its position, the Haber and Contact processes, calculating oxidation numbers, and the definitions of oxidising and reducing agents. If your notes are from Chemistry 0620, skip those parts.

## Notes

### Physical and chemical changes

| | Physical change | Chemical change |
|---|---|---|
| New substance formed? | **no** | **yes** |
| Easily reversed? | usually (e.g. by cooling or heating) | usually not |
| Energy change | usually small | often a large energy change (heat, light) |
| Examples | melting, boiling, freezing, condensing, dissolving sugar or salt in water, separating by filtration or distillation | burning, rusting, neutralisation, a metal reacting with acid, thermal decomposition, cooking an egg |

Signs of a chemical change: gas given off (effervescence), precipitate formed, colour change that cannot be reversed simply, temperature change, a new substance with different properties.

### Redox: three ways to describe it

| | Oxidation | Reduction |
|---|---|---|
| Oxygen (Core) | gain of oxygen | loss of oxygen |
| Electrons *(Supp)* | **loss** of electrons | **gain** of electrons |
| Oxidation number *(Supp)* | increase | decrease |

**OIL RIG:** Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain (of electrons).

Oxidation and reduction always happen **together** (simultaneously) — that is why the reaction is called **redox**.

### Identifying oxidation and reduction (oxygen)

- CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O: copper(II) oxide **loses** oxygen → reduced; hydrogen **gains** oxygen → oxidised.
- Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂ (blast furnace): iron(III) oxide is reduced; carbon monoxide is oxidised.
- 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO: magnesium gains oxygen → oxidised (and oxygen is reduced).

### Identifying oxidation and reduction (electrons, Supplement)

- Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu: Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ (zinc **loses** electrons → oxidised); Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu (copper ions **gain** electrons → reduced).
- Cl₂ + 2Br⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + Br₂: bromide ions lose electrons (oxidised); chlorine gains electrons (reduced).
- Electrolysis: at the cathode ions **gain** electrons → reduction (e.g. Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu).
- Rusting / sacrificial protection: Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻ is oxidation.

### Oxidation number and Roman numerals

You only need oxidation numbers to **name ions**: iron(II) = Fe²⁺, iron(III) = Fe³⁺, copper(II) = Cu²⁺. When Fe²⁺ changes to Fe³⁺ the oxidation number **increases** (+2 → +3), so iron(II) is **oxidised** (it has lost an electron). When Fe³⁺ changes to Fe, the oxidation number decreases → reduced. You will not be asked to work out oxidation numbers from formulae.

### Hydrated and anhydrous: the tests for water

| Test reagent | Colour of anhydrous form | Colour when water is added |
|---|---|---|
| Anhydrous copper(II) sulfate | white | turns **blue** |
| Anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride | blue | turns **pink** |

- Heating the hydrated salt removes the water (blue copper(II) sulfate crystals → white powder; droplets of colourless liquid condense at the top of the tube).
- These tests show that **water is present**, not that it is pure. To test **purity**, measure the boiling point (100 °C) or melting point (0 °C).

### Paper 6 skills on this topic

- Describing colour changes as "from X to Y" — always give both colours.
- **Tests for oxidising and reducing agents** are listed as a practical context. The question will give you the reagent; know these two common results:
  - acidified aqueous potassium manganate(VII): **purple → colourless** if the test substance is a reducing agent (it is itself oxidised).
  - aqueous potassium iodide: **colourless → brown** (iodine forms) if the test substance is an oxidising agent.
- Heating hydrated copper(II) sulfate: record observations (blue → white; condensation at the mouth of the tube); adding water back (white → blue, the tube gets warm).

## Key terms and definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Physical change | A change in which no new substance is formed, e.g. a change of state |
| Chemical change | A change in which one or more new substances are formed |
| Redox | A reaction involving simultaneous oxidation and reduction |
| Oxidation | Gain of oxygen / *(Supp)* loss of electrons / an increase in oxidation number |
| Reduction | Loss of oxygen / *(Supp)* gain of electrons / a decrease in oxidation number |
| Hydrated substance | A substance that is chemically combined with water |
| Anhydrous substance | A substance containing no water |

## Worked examples

**1.** In the blast furnace: Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂. Explain why this is a redox reaction. [2]

- Iron(III) oxide loses oxygen, so it is reduced (1)
- Carbon monoxide gains oxygen, so it is oxidised; both happen at the same time (1)

**2.** Magnesium reacts with aqueous copper(II) sulfate: Mg + Cu²⁺ → Mg²⁺ + Cu. Identify the species that is oxidised and explain your answer in terms of electrons. [2]

- Magnesium / Mg (1)
- It loses (two) electrons to form Mg²⁺ (1)

**3.** State whether each change is physical or chemical: (a) ice melting, (b) magnesium burning, (c) salt dissolving in water. [3]

- (a) physical (1) (b) chemical (1) (c) physical (1)

**4.** Describe a chemical test for water and give the result. [2]

- Add anhydrous copper(II) sulfate (1)
- turns from white to blue (1) (or anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride, blue to pink)

## Common mistakes

- Defining oxidation as "gain of electrons" — OIL RIG: oxidation is **loss** of electrons.
- Saying only one substance in a redox reaction is "redox" — one substance is oxidised and another is reduced.
- Saying an ion is oxidised "because it gains oxygen" when no oxygen is involved — use electrons for ionic reactions.
- Calling dissolving a chemical change — no new substance forms, it is physical.
- Giving the test for water as "turns blue" without saying from **white**; or using "cobalt chloride paper turns blue" (it turns **pink**).
- Saying the water test shows water is **pure** — purity is tested by boiling point / melting point.
- Describing manganate(VII) as turning "clear" — say **colourless** (clear means transparent).

## Practice questions

1. Describe what you would see when water is added to anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride. [1]
2. Define redox. [1]
3. Hydrogen is passed over heated copper(II) oxide: CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O. Name the substance that is reduced and explain your answer. [2]
4. Zn + 2Ag⁺ → Zn²⁺ + 2Ag. (a) Which species gains electrons? (b) Is it oxidised or reduced? [2]
5. Aqueous iron(II) sulfate slowly turns into iron(III) sulfate in air. State and explain, in terms of oxidation number, whether iron is oxidised or reduced. [2]
6. Classify each of these as a physical or chemical change: (a) rusting of iron, (b) boiling water, (c) neutralising an acid with an alkali, (d) separating ink by chromatography. [4]
7. A student heats blue crystals of hydrated copper(II) sulfate. Describe two observations. [2]
8. Acidified potassium manganate(VII) is added to a solution of an unknown substance. The solution changes from purple to colourless. What does this show? [1]
