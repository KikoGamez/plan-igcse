# Acids, bases and salts

> **In the exam:** **Paper 4** (Theory, 2 h, 120 marks across biology, chemistry and physics) often has a salt preparation part (describe a method, 3–5 marks) and short questions on acid/base reactions, indicators and pH. Ion and gas tests appear in **Paper 2** (Multiple Choice, 45 min, 40 questions; usually 1–2 questions on acids, salts or tests) and are a major part of the chemistry in **Paper 6** (Alternative to Practical, 1 h 30 min, 60 marks): a question often gives a series of tests on unknown substances, with observations to record or interpret. The *Notes for use in qualitative analysis* are printed in both Paper 5 and Paper 6 — but you still need to know them for Papers 2 and 4, and to describe tests in planning questions. The tables below use the exact 0654 wording.

## What you need to know

- Describe the characteristic properties of acids in terms of their reactions with metals, bases and carbonates.
- Describe acids in terms of their effect on the indicators litmus and methyl orange.
- State that bases are oxides or hydroxides of metals and that alkalis are soluble bases.
- Describe the characteristic properties of bases in terms of their reactions with acids.
- Describe alkalis in terms of their effect on the indicators litmus and methyl orange.
- Describe how to compare neutrality, relative acidity and relative alkalinity in terms of colour and pH using universal indicator.
- Describe the neutralisation reaction between an acid and an alkali to produce a salt and water (the ionic equation for this reaction is not required).
- Classify oxides as either acidic, including SO₂ and CO₂, or basic, including CuO and CaO, related to metallic and non-metallic character.
- *Supplement:* describe amphoteric oxides as oxides that react with acids and with bases to produce a salt and water; classify Al₂O₃ and ZnO as amphoteric oxides.
- Describe the preparation, separation and purification of soluble salts by reaction of an acid with: an alkali by titration; excess metal; excess insoluble base; excess insoluble carbonate (the general solubility rules for salts are not required).
- Define a hydrated substance as a substance that is chemically combined with water and an anhydrous substance as a substance containing no water.
- *Supplement:* describe the preparation of insoluble salts by precipitation (the general solubility rules for salts are not required).
- Describe tests to identify the anions, aqueous cations and gases, and the flame tests, listed in C12.5 (tables below).

*Not in 0654 (separate Chemistry 0620 only):* acids as proton donors / bases as proton acceptors, strong and weak acids, H⁺ and OH⁻ ion concentrations and pH, thymolphthalein, reactions of bases with ammonium salts (as a property of bases), the general solubility rules, water of crystallisation; the tests for Al³⁺, Cr³⁺, sulfite and sulfur dioxide, and the flame colours of Ca²⁺ and Ba²⁺.

## Notes

### Acids and alkalis: indicators

| Indicator | Acid | Neutral | Alkali |
|---|---|---|---|
| Litmus | red | — | blue |
| Methyl orange | red | — | yellow |
| Universal indicator | red (pH 1–2, strongly acidic), orange/yellow (pH 3–6, weakly acidic) | green (pH 7) | blue (pH 8–11), purple (pH 12–14, strongly alkaline) |

pH below 7 = acidic (lower = more acidic); pH 7 = neutral; pH above 7 = alkaline (higher = more alkaline).

### Reactions of acids (learn the general equations)

- acid + metal → salt + hydrogen   Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂
- acid + base → salt + water   CuO + H₂SO₄ → CuSO₄ + H₂O
- acid + alkali → salt + water (neutralisation)   NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H₂O
- acid + carbonate → salt + carbon dioxide + water   CaCO₃ + 2HNO₃ → Ca(NO₃)₂ + CO₂ + H₂O

Bases are **oxides or hydroxides of metals** (CuO, CaO, MgO, NaOH, Ca(OH)₂). **Alkalis are soluble bases** (NaOH, KOH, Ca(OH)₂ solution = limewater, aqueous ammonia).

Naming salts: hydrochloric acid → chlorides; sulfuric acid → sulfates; nitric acid → nitrates.

### Oxides

| Type | Examples | Reacts with | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acidic | CO₂, SO₂ (non-metal oxides) | bases/alkalis | non-metallic |
| Basic | CuO, CaO (metal oxides) | acids | metallic |
| Amphoteric *(Supp)* | Al₂O₃, ZnO | **both** acids and bases, producing a salt and water | |

ZnO + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂O (the reaction with sodium hydroxide would be given if an equation were needed).

### Preparing salts — choose the method

The question will tell you (or it will be obvious) whether a salt is soluble; the solubility rules are not required.

1. **Is the salt insoluble?** Yes → **precipitation** (Supplement).
2. Soluble, and the acid is reacted with an **alkali** (e.g. sodium, potassium salts) → **titration**.
3. Soluble, made from a **metal, insoluble base or insoluble carbonate** → **add excess solid** to the acid.

**Method A: acid + excess metal, insoluble base or insoluble carbonate** (e.g. copper(II) sulfate from copper(II) oxide and dilute sulfuric acid)
1. Warm the acid; add the solid a little at a time with stirring until **in excess** (no more dissolves / solid remains / no more fizzing for metals and carbonates).
2. **Filter** to remove the excess solid.
3. Heat the filtrate to **evaporate some of the water** until the solution is saturated (crystals form on a cold glass rod dipped in it).
4. Leave to **cool and crystallise**.
5. Filter off the crystals, wash with a little cold distilled water, **dry** between filter papers (or in a low-temperature oven).

Why excess? To make sure all the acid is used up, so the salt is not contaminated with acid.

**Method B: titration** (e.g. sodium chloride from sodium hydroxide and hydrochloric acid)
1. Use a **volumetric pipette** to put 25.0 cm³ of alkali into a conical flask; add a few drops of a suitable indicator (e.g. methyl orange).
2. Add acid from a **burette** until the indicator just changes colour (the end-point); record the volume.
3. **Repeat without indicator** using the same volumes (so the salt is not contaminated with indicator).
4. Evaporate some water and crystallise as above.

**Method C: precipitation** (Supplement) (e.g. barium sulfate)
1. Mix solutions of two soluble salts: BaCl₂(aq) + Na₂SO₄(aq) → BaSO₄(s) + 2NaCl(aq).
2. Filter to collect the precipitate (residue).
3. Wash the residue with distilled water (removes soluble impurities).
4. Dry in a warm oven or between filter papers.

### Hydrated and anhydrous

Hydrated copper(II) sulfate (blue crystals, CuSO₄·5H₂O) is chemically combined with water; heating gives white anhydrous copper(II) sulfate, which contains no water.

### Tests for aqueous cations (0654 notes)

| Cation | Effect of aqueous sodium hydroxide | Effect of aqueous ammonia |
|---|---|---|
| Ammonium, NH₄⁺ | ammonia produced on warming | — |
| Calcium, Ca²⁺ | white ppt., insoluble in excess | no ppt. or very slight white ppt. |
| Copper(II), Cu²⁺ | light blue ppt., insoluble in excess | light blue ppt., soluble in excess, giving a dark blue solution |
| Iron(II), Fe²⁺ | green ppt., insoluble in excess, ppt. turns brown near surface on standing | green ppt., insoluble in excess, ppt. turns brown near surface on standing |
| Iron(III), Fe³⁺ | red-brown ppt., insoluble in excess | red-brown ppt., insoluble in excess |
| Zinc, Zn²⁺ | white ppt., soluble in excess, giving a colourless solution | white ppt., soluble in excess, giving a colourless solution |

Telling the white precipitates apart: with excess NaOH, **Zn²⁺** dissolves (colourless solution) but **Ca²⁺** does not. With aqueous ammonia, Zn²⁺ gives a white ppt. that dissolves in excess; Ca²⁺ gives no ppt. or only a very slight one.

### Tests for anions (0654 notes)

| Anion | Test | Test result |
|---|---|---|
| Carbonate, CO₃²⁻ | add dilute acid, then test for carbon dioxide gas | effervescence, carbon dioxide produced |
| Chloride, Cl⁻ [in solution] | acidify with dilute nitric acid, then add aqueous silver nitrate | white ppt. |
| Bromide, Br⁻ [in solution] | acidify with dilute nitric acid, then add aqueous silver nitrate | cream ppt. |
| Iodide, I⁻ [in solution] | acidify with dilute nitric acid, then add aqueous silver nitrate | yellow ppt. |
| Nitrate, NO₃⁻ [in solution] | add aqueous sodium hydroxide, then aluminium foil; warm carefully | ammonia produced |
| Sulfate, SO₄²⁻ [in solution] | acidify with dilute nitric acid, then add aqueous barium nitrate | white ppt. |

Why acidify with nitric acid? To remove carbonate ions, which would also give a precipitate. (Don't use hydrochloric acid for the halide test — it adds Cl⁻; don't use sulfuric acid for the sulfate test — it adds SO₄²⁻.)

### Tests for gases (0654 notes)

| Gas | Test and test result |
|---|---|
| Ammonia, NH₃ | turns damp red litmus paper blue |
| Carbon dioxide, CO₂ | turns limewater milky |
| Chlorine, Cl₂ | bleaches damp litmus paper |
| Hydrogen, H₂ | "pops" with a lighted splint |
| Oxygen, O₂ | relights a glowing splint |

### Flame tests for metal ions (0654 notes)

| Metal ion | Flame colour |
|---|---|
| Lithium, Li⁺ | red |
| Sodium, Na⁺ | yellow |
| Potassium, K⁺ | lilac |
| Copper(II), Cu²⁺ | blue-green |

Method: dip a clean flame test wire (e.g. nichrome or platinum, cleaned in acid) into the solid, and hold it in a hot (blue) Bunsen flame.

### Paper 6 skills on this topic

- Record observations exactly: "white precipitate", "precipitate dissolves in excess to give a colourless solution", "effervescence; gas turns limewater milky". "No change" is a valid observation — write it, never leave a blank.
- When asked to identify, name the ion **and** give the evidence.
- Planning a salt preparation: name reagents, apparatus, the step "add until in excess", and how to obtain dry crystals.
- "Test-tube reactions of dilute acids" is a listed practical context: know the observations (fizzing with metals and carbonates; solid dissolving with a base; blue solution with copper(II) oxide).

## Key terms and definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Base | An oxide or hydroxide of a metal |
| Alkali | A soluble base |
| Neutralisation | The reaction between an acid and an alkali to produce a salt and water |
| Acidic oxide | An oxide of a non-metal that reacts with bases, e.g. CO₂, SO₂ |
| Basic oxide | An oxide of a metal that reacts with acids, e.g. CuO, CaO |
| Amphoteric oxide (Supp) | An oxide that reacts with acids and with bases to produce a salt and water |
| Hydrated substance | A substance that is chemically combined with water |
| Anhydrous substance | A substance containing no water |

## Worked examples

**1.** Describe how to prepare pure, dry crystals of zinc sulfate from zinc carbonate and dilute sulfuric acid. [5]

- Add zinc carbonate to (warm) dilute sulfuric acid until in excess / no more effervescence (1)
- Filter to remove excess zinc carbonate (1)
- Heat the filtrate to evaporate some water / until saturated (1)
- Leave to cool so crystals form (1)
- Filter/remove crystals and dry between filter papers (1)

**2.** Solution X gives a light blue precipitate with aqueous sodium hydroxide. When aqueous barium nitrate is added to X acidified with dilute nitric acid, a white precipitate forms. Identify X. [2]

- Copper(II) (1) sulfate (1) — CuSO₄

**3.** Universal indicator is added to three solutions. A turns purple, B turns green and C turns orange. (a) Give a possible pH for each. (b) Which solution could be aqueous sodium hydroxide? [4]

- (a) A: 12–14 (1); B: 7 (1); C: 3–6 (1)
- (b) A (1)

## Common mistakes

- Writing "clear" instead of "colourless" (copper(II) sulfate solution is clear but blue).
- Forgetting to say "in excess" in salt preparations, or filtering in the titration method (no solid to remove).
- Heating to dryness instead of evaporating until saturated and crystallising.
- Mixing up the NaOH and NH₃ results for calcium and zinc, or the "dark blue solution" for copper(II) with ammonia.
- Gas tests: "limewater goes cloudy" is usually accepted as milky; but "a splint pops" or "glowing splint pops" are wrong — a **lighted** splint pops with hydrogen; a **glowing** splint relights with oxygen.
- Using "dry" litmus — gas tests need **damp** litmus paper.
- For the nitrate test, forgetting aluminium foil or the warming step.
- Learning extra tests from 0620 books (Al³⁺, Cr³⁺, sulfite, SO₂, Ca²⁺ flame) — not needed for 0654.
