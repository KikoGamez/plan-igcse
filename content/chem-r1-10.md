# The Periodic Table

> **In the exam:** Paper 2 has 2–4 questions (trends, predicting properties, displacement). Paper 4 usually has a question on Group I or Group VII (4–8 marks), often asking you to **predict** the properties of an element you have not studied (rubidium, astatine) from a data table, to write displacement equations, or to describe transition element properties. Paper 6 can include halogen displacement observations.

## What you need to know

- Describe the Periodic Table as an arrangement of elements in periods and groups and in order of increasing proton number/atomic number.
- Describe the change from metallic to non-metallic character across a period.
- Describe the relationship between group number and the charge of the ions formed from elements in that group.
- Explain similarities in the chemical properties of elements in the same group of the Periodic Table in terms of their electronic configuration.
- Explain how the position of an element in the Periodic Table can be used to predict its properties.
- Identify trends in groups, given information about the elements.
- Describe the Group I alkali metals lithium, sodium and potassium as relatively soft metals with general trends down the group, limited to: decreasing melting point, increasing density, increasing reactivity.
- Predict the properties of other elements in Group I, given information about the elements.
- Describe the Group VII halogens chlorine, bromine and iodine as diatomic non-metals with general trends down the group, limited to: increasing density, decreasing reactivity.
- State the appearance of the halogens at r.t.p. as: chlorine, a pale yellow-green gas; bromine, a red-brown liquid; iodine, a grey-black solid.
- Describe and explain the displacement reactions of halogens with other halide ions.
- Predict the properties of other elements in Group VII, given information about the elements.
- Describe the transition elements as metals that have high densities, have high melting points, form coloured compounds, and often act as catalysts as elements and in compounds.
- *Extended:* describe transition elements as having ions with variable oxidation numbers, including iron(II) and iron(III).
- Describe the noble gases, in Group VIII or 0, as unreactive, monatomic gases and explain this in terms of electronic configuration.

## Notes

### Structure of the table

- Elements in order of increasing **proton number**.
- **Groups** (vertical columns): same number of outer-shell electrons → similar chemical properties.
- **Periods** (horizontal rows): same number of occupied electron shells.
- Across a period: change from **metallic** (left) to **non-metallic** (right) character. E.g. Period 3: Na, Mg, Al are metals; Si is a metalloid (giant covalent); P, S, Cl, Ar are non-metals.

| Group | I | II | III | IV | V | VI | VII | VIII/0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Outer electrons | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 (He: 2) |
| Typical ion charge | +1 | +2 | +3 | — | −3 | −2 | −1 | none |

Metals form positive ions (lose electrons); non-metals form negative ions (gain electrons).

### Group I — alkali metals

Li, Na, K: relatively **soft** metals (cut with a knife), shiny when freshly cut, tarnish quickly in air, stored under oil.

Trends down the group:
- **Melting point decreases** (Li 181 °C, Na 98 °C, K 63 °C)
- **Density increases** (general trend)
- **Reactivity increases**

Reaction with cold water: metal + water → metal hydroxide + hydrogen
2Na(s) + 2H₂O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + H₂(g)

| Metal | Observations with water |
|---|---|
| Lithium | floats, fizzes steadily, gradually disappears |
| Sodium | floats, melts into a ball, moves around on the surface, fizzes rapidly, disappears |
| Potassium | as sodium but more vigorous; hydrogen ignites with a lilac flame |

The solution formed is alkaline (turns universal indicator blue/purple).

Why similar? All have **one electron in the outer shell**, which is lost to form a +1 ion.
Why more reactive down the group? The outer electron is further from the nucleus (more shells), so it is lost more easily.

**Predicting (rubidium, caesium):** softer, lower melting point, more dense, reacts even more violently (explosively) with water to form RbOH and hydrogen; forms Rb⁺ ions; compounds are white solids that dissolve to give colourless solutions.

### Group VII — halogens

| Halogen | Appearance at r.t.p. | Colour in aqueous solution |
|---|---|---|
| Chlorine, Cl₂ | pale yellow-green gas | pale green / very pale yellow |
| Bromine, Br₂ | red-brown liquid | orange (to yellow) |
| Iodine, I₂ | grey-black solid | brown |

Trends down the group:
- **Density increases** (and melting/boiling points increase: gas → liquid → solid)
- **Reactivity decreases**
- Colour gets darker

All exist as **diatomic** molecules; all have **seven outer-shell electrons** and gain one electron to form −1 halide ions.

### Displacement reactions

A **more reactive** halogen displaces a **less reactive** halogen from an aqueous solution of its halide.

| Added halogen ↓ / solution → | KCl(aq) | KBr(aq) | KI(aq) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chlorine | — | orange/yellow solution (Br₂) | brown solution (I₂) |
| Bromine | no reaction | — | brown solution (I₂) |
| Iodine | no reaction | no reaction | — |

Cl₂ + 2KBr → 2KCl + Br₂    ionic: Cl₂ + 2Br⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + Br₂
Br₂ + 2KI → 2KBr + I₂    ionic: Br₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Br⁻ + I₂

These are **redox** reactions: the halogen is reduced (gains electrons; oxidising agent), the halide ion is oxidised (loses electrons).

**Predicting (fluorine, astatine):** fluorine — pale yellow gas, most reactive, displaces all other halogens. Astatine — black solid, highest density, least reactive, does not displace iodine; At⁻ ion is displaced by all the other halogens.

### Transition elements

Found in the central block (e.g. Cr, Mn, Fe, Ni, Cu, Zn area). Compared with Group I metals they have:
- high densities
- high melting points
- form **coloured compounds** (e.g. CuSO₄ blue, Fe³⁺ compounds red-brown/yellow)
- often act as **catalysts**, as elements and in compounds (iron in the Haber process; vanadium(V) oxide in the Contact process; nickel for hydrogenation)
- *Extended:* ions with **variable oxidation numbers**, e.g. iron(II), Fe²⁺, and iron(III), Fe³⁺; copper(I) and copper(II). This is why Roman numerals are used in their names.

### Noble gases (Group VIII / 0)

He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe: **unreactive, monatomic gases**. They have a **full outer shell** (stable electronic configuration), so they do not need to lose, gain or share electrons. Uses: argon in lamps (inert atmosphere); helium in balloons (low density, unreactive).

### Using data tables

Exam questions give data (melting point, density, radius) for some elements and ask you to estimate a missing value: look at the trend and continue it (e.g. if values go 181, 98, 63, the next is lower than 63, around 40 °C). Give a value **within the trend**, not outside it.

### Paper 6 skills on this topic

- Halogen displacement: add a few drops of chlorine water/bromine water to aqueous potassium halides in test-tubes; record colour changes ("colourless to orange", "colourless to brown") and conclude the order of reactivity.
- Safety: halogens are toxic — use a fume cupboard; Group I metals — small pieces, safety screen, eye protection, tongs.

## Key terms and definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Group | A vertical column of the Periodic Table; elements have the same number of outer-shell electrons |
| Period | A horizontal row; elements have the same number of occupied electron shells |
| Monatomic | Exists as single atoms |
| Diatomic | Molecules containing two atoms |
| Displacement reaction | A more reactive element takes the place of a less reactive element in a compound |
| Transition element (properties) | Metal with high density and high melting point, forming coloured compounds and acting as a catalyst; *(Ext)* ions with variable oxidation numbers |

## Worked examples

**1.** Chlorine water is added to aqueous potassium iodide. (a) State the colour change. (b) Write an ionic equation. (c) Explain why bromine water does not react with aqueous potassium chloride. [4]

- (a) Colourless to brown (1)
- (b) Cl₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + I₂ (1)
- (c) Bromine is less reactive than chlorine (1), so cannot displace chlorine / chloride ions are not oxidised (1)

**2.** Caesium is below potassium in Group I. Predict two physical properties and one chemical property of caesium. [3]

- Soft / can be cut easily (1)
- Lower melting point than potassium / higher density (1)
- Reacts (explosively) with cold water, forming caesium hydroxide and hydrogen (1)

**3.** Explain why argon is unreactive. [2]

- Argon has a full outer shell (of electrons) / stable electronic configuration (1)
- so it does not need to gain, lose or share electrons (1)

## Common mistakes

- Saying halogen reactivity increases down the group (that is Group I).
- Describing chlorine as "green gas" (needs "pale yellow-green") or iodine as "purple solid" (it is grey-black; purple is its vapour).
- Saying "chlorine displaces bromine" from "bromine" — it displaces bromine from **bromide** ions.
- Giving the colour of the halogen in the displacement as the colour of the *added* halogen — e.g. chlorine + KI: the brown colour is **iodine**.
- Transition metals: writing "they are coloured" — it is their **compounds** that are coloured.
- Noble gases: "they have 8 outer electrons" is not true for helium — say **full outer shell**.
- Predictions outside the trend (e.g. predicting caesium melts at 100 °C).

## Practice questions

1. Explain why sodium and potassium have similar chemical properties. [2]
2. Describe what you would see when a small piece of lithium is added to water containing universal indicator. [3]
3. Write a balanced equation, with state symbols, for the reaction of potassium with water. [2]
4. A student adds bromine water to aqueous potassium chloride and aqueous potassium iodide. Predict the observations and explain. [4]
5. State two properties of transition elements that are not properties of Group I metals. [2]
6. Iron forms two chlorides. Give their formulae and names. [2]
7. Astatine is below iodine. Predict its state at r.t.p. and whether it reacts with aqueous potassium iodide. [2]

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Both have one electron in the outer shell (1); both lose it to form +1 ions (1).
2. Fizzing/bubbles (1); lithium floats and moves/gradually disappears (1); indicator turns from green to blue/purple (1).
3. 2K(s) + 2H₂O(l) → 2KOH(aq) + H₂(g) — equation (1), states (1).
4. With KCl: no change (1) because bromine is less reactive than chlorine (1). With KI: colourless/orange solution turns brown (1) because bromine is more reactive than iodine and displaces it (1).
5. Any two: high density; high melting point; coloured compounds; act as catalysts; variable oxidation numbers.
6. FeCl₂, iron(II) chloride (1); FeCl₃, iron(III) chloride (1).
7. Solid (1); no reaction — astatine is less reactive than iodine (1).
