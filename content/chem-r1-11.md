## Notes

### Properties of metals and non-metals

| Property | Metals | Non-metals |
|---|---|---|
| Thermal conductivity | good | poor |
| Electrical conductivity | good | poor (except graphite) |
| Malleability and ductility | malleable and ductile | brittle when solid |
| Melting and boiling points | mostly high | mostly low |

### Uses of metals

| Metal | Use | Property |
|---|---|---|
| Aluminium | aircraft | low density |
| Aluminium | overhead electrical cables | low density and good electrical conductivity |
| Aluminium | food containers | resistance to corrosion |
| Copper | electrical wiring | good electrical conductivity |

### Alloys

An **alloy** is a mixture of a metal with other elements. Brass = copper + zinc. Stainless steel = iron + other elements such as chromium, nickel and carbon — used in **cutlery** because of its hardness and resistance to rusting.

Diagrams: a pure metal is a regular grid of identical circles; an alloy shows a few circles of a **different size** among them (you may have to pick out which diagram is an alloy).

*Supplement:* in a pure metal, the layers of atoms are regular and slide easily. In an alloy, **different-sized atoms** disrupt the layers, so the layers **can no longer slide** over each other → harder and stronger.

### Reactivity series

K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > (C) > Zn > Fe > (H) > Cu > Ag > Au

| Metal | Cold water | Steam | Dilute HCl |
|---|---|---|---|
| K, Na, Ca | react: metal hydroxide + hydrogen (K most vigorous) | — | (not done — dangerous) |
| Mg | very slow | reacts: MgO + H₂ (bright white glow) | rapid fizzing |
| Zn, Fe | no | — | fizzing (Zn faster than Fe) |
| Cu, Ag, Au | no | no | **no reaction** (below hydrogen) |

Ca + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + H₂   Mg + H₂O(g) → MgO + H₂   Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂

Metals **above hydrogen** react with dilute acids to give hydrogen; those below do not.

**Displacement (Supplement):** a more reactive metal displaces a less reactive metal from an aqueous solution of its ions, because the more reactive metal has a **greater tendency to form positive ions** (lose electrons).
Example: Zn(s) + Cu²⁺(aq) → Zn²⁺(aq) + Cu(s) (grey zinc gets a pink-brown coating; blue solution fades).
Mg + Fe²⁺ → reaction; Cu + Zn²⁺ → no reaction; Cu + 2Ag⁺ → Cu²⁺ + 2Ag (silver crystals form, solution turns blue).
The **bigger the gap** in reactivity, the bigger the temperature rise.

### Rusting and its prevention

Rusting of iron needs **both oxygen and water**.

Classic experiment: three tubes of iron nails — (1) air + water → rusts; (2) boiled water (removes dissolved air) under a layer of oil → no rust; (3) dry air with a drying agent (anhydrous calcium chloride) → no rust.

- **Barrier methods:** painting, greasing, coating with plastic — they **exclude oxygen and water**.
- **Galvanising** *(Supp)*: coating steel with **zinc** — a barrier method, and also sacrificial protection if scratched.
- **Sacrificial protection** *(Supp)*: zinc is **more reactive** than iron, so it **loses electrons more readily** and is oxidised (corrodes) instead of the iron. Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻.

### Extraction of metals

- The **more reactive** the metal, the **harder** it is to obtain from its ore.
- Metals **below carbon** (Zn, Fe, Cu…) can be extracted by **reduction with carbon**.
- Metals **above carbon** (e.g. aluminium) are extracted by **electrolysis**. The main ore of aluminium is **bauxite**.
- Unreactive metals (Au) are found uncombined.

**Iron from hematite (blast furnace)** — iron(III) oxide is **reduced**. Raw materials: hematite (Fe₂O₃), coke (carbon), limestone (CaCO₃), hot air.

| Stage (Supplement) | Equation |
|---|---|
| Carbon (coke) burns to provide heat and produce carbon dioxide | C + O₂ → CO₂ |
| Carbon dioxide is reduced to carbon monoxide | C + CO₂ → 2CO |
| Iron(III) oxide reduced by carbon monoxide | Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂ |
| Thermal decomposition of limestone to calcium oxide | CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ |
| Formation of slag | CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃ |

Molten iron and molten slag are tapped off at the bottom; slag floats on iron.

### Water

- Tests for water: **anhydrous copper(II) sulfate** white → blue; **anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride** blue → pink.
- Purity: pure water boils at exactly 100 °C and melts at 0 °C; impurities change these values (and give a range).
- Distilled water is used in practical chemistry because it contains **fewer chemical impurities** than tap water.
- **Treatment of domestic water:** sedimentation and filtration (remove solids) → carbon (removes tastes and odours) → chlorination (kills microbes / pathogens).

### Air and pollutants

Clean, dry air: approximately **78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen**, the remainder a mixture of noble gases and carbon dioxide.

| Pollutant | Source | Adverse effect |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon dioxide | complete combustion of carbon-containing fuels | higher levels lead to increased global warming, which leads to climate change |
| Carbon monoxide | incomplete combustion of carbon-containing fuels | toxic gas |
| Particulates | incomplete combustion of carbon-containing fuels | increased risk of respiratory problems and cancer |
| Methane | decomposition of vegetation; waste gases from digestion in animals | higher levels lead to increased global warming → climate change |
| Oxides of nitrogen | car engines | acid rain and respiratory problems |
| Sulfur dioxide | combustion of fossil fuels which contain sulfur compounds | acid rain |

**Strategies — climate change (Core):** planting trees (absorb CO₂ in photosynthesis); reduction in livestock farming (less methane); decreasing use of fossil fuels (less CO₂); increasing use of hydrogen and renewable energy, e.g. wind, solar.

**Strategies — acid rain (Supplement):** reduce emissions of sulfur dioxide by using **low-sulfur fuels** and **flue gas desulfurisation with calcium oxide** (CaO, a basic oxide, reacts with acidic SO₂).

**Greenhouse effect (Supplement):** the Earth's surface emits thermal energy; carbon dioxide and methane **absorb** this thermal energy and **emit** it in all directions, including back towards the Earth (some is reflected), so **less thermal energy is lost to space** → global warming.

**Oxides of nitrogen and catalytic converters (Supplement):** at the high temperature in a car engine, nitrogen and oxygen from the air react to form oxides of nitrogen. A catalytic converter removes them: **2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂**.

### Paper 6 skills on this topic

- Rusting experiment: identify control variables (same type and size of nail, same time); explain the purpose of boiled water, oil layer and drying agent.
- Displacement/temperature-rise experiments: use a polystyrene cup (insulation), same volume and concentration of solution, same mass of powdered metal, stir, record the maximum temperature.
- Comparing reactivity by rate of bubbling with acid: same surface area and mass of metal, same acid concentration and volume.

## Key terms and definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Alloy | A mixture of a metal with other elements |
| Barrier method | Painting, greasing or coating with plastic to exclude oxygen and water |

## Worked examples

**1.** Explain why galvanised steel does not rust even when the zinc coating is scratched. [3]

- Zinc is more reactive than iron (1)
- Zinc loses electrons / is oxidised more readily than iron (1)
- so zinc corrodes instead of iron (sacrificial protection) (1)

**2.** A student adds four metals to aqueous copper(II) sulfate. Results: magnesium — pink-brown solid forms, large temperature rise; zinc — pink-brown solid, medium rise; iron — pink-brown solid, small rise; silver — no change. Deduce the order of reactivity and explain the result for silver. [3]

- Mg > Zn > Fe > Ag (1)
- Silver is less reactive than copper (1)
- so it cannot displace copper / has a smaller tendency to form positive ions than copper (1)

**3.** Explain how carbon dioxide in the atmosphere causes global warming. [3]

- The Earth's surface emits thermal energy (1)
- CO₂ absorbs the thermal energy and emits it (back towards the Earth) (1)
- so less thermal energy is lost to space (1)

## Common mistakes

- "Rusting needs air **or** water" — it needs **both** oxygen **and** water.
- Saying galvanising works because zinc "doesn't rust" — explain sacrificial protection (zinc is more reactive / loses electrons).
- In the blast furnace, saying carbon reduces iron(III) oxide directly — iron(III) oxide is reduced by **carbon monoxide**. Limestone's job is to remove impurities (forms slag), not to reduce the ore.
- Mixing pollutant sources: CO comes from **incomplete** combustion; oxides of nitrogen from **car engines** (N₂ and O₂ reacting at high temperature), not from nitrogen in the fuel.
- Confusing acid rain with global warming.
- Saying chlorine "cleans" water — it **kills microbes**.
- Saying aluminium is extracted with carbon — it is above carbon, so electrolysis is needed.
