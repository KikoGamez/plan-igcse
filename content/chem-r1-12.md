# Organic chemistry

> **In the exam:** The 0654 organic content (C11) is much shorter than in separate Chemistry 0620: fuels, alkanes, alkenes, ethanol (combustion and uses only) and polymers. **Paper 4** (Theory, 2 h, 120 marks across biology, chemistry and physics) often has an organic part (typically 4–8 marks): displayed formulae, naming, the bromine test, cracking, addition reactions, polymers. **Paper 2** (Multiple Choice, 45 min, 40 questions) usually has 1–2 organic questions. **Paper 6** (Alternative to Practical, 1 h 30 min, 60 marks) may include the bromine water test or a fractional distillation diagram. This is one of the highest-scoring topics if you learn the facts exactly.

## What you need to know

**Formulas and terminology**
- Draw and interpret the displayed formula of a molecule to show all the atoms and all the bonds.
- State that a saturated compound has molecules in which all carbon–carbon bonds are single bonds; an unsaturated compound has molecules in which one or more carbon–carbon bonds are not single bonds.
- *Supplement:* state that a homologous series is a family of similar compounds with similar chemical properties; describe the general characteristics of a homologous series as having the same general formula (recall of specific general formulas is not required) and displaying a trend in physical properties.

**Naming**
- Name and draw the displayed formulas of methane and ethane, ethene and ethanol.
- State the type of compound present, given a chemical name ending in -ane, -ene or -ol, or from a molecular formula or displayed formula.
- *Supplement:* name and draw the structural formulas and displayed formulas of unbranched alkanes and alkenes, including but-1-ene and but-2-ene (not cis/trans), containing up to four carbon atoms per molecule.

**Fuels**
- Name the fossil fuels: coal, natural gas and petroleum; name methane as the main constituent of natural gas.
- State that hydrocarbons are compounds that contain hydrogen and carbon only; state that petroleum is a mixture of hydrocarbons.
- Describe the separation of petroleum into useful fractions by fractional distillation; name the uses of the fractions: refinery gas (gas for heating and cooking), gasoline/petrol (fuel for cars), naphtha (chemical feedstock), diesel oil/gas oil (fuel for diesel engines), bitumen (making roads).
- *Supplement:* describe how the properties of fractions change from the bottom to the top of the fractionating column, limited to decreasing chain length and lower boiling points.

**Alkanes, alkenes, alcohols**
- State that the bonding in alkanes is single covalent and that alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons; describe alkanes as generally unreactive, except in terms of combustion.
- State that the bonding in alkenes includes a double carbon–carbon covalent bond and that alkenes are unsaturated hydrocarbons; describe the test to distinguish between saturated and unsaturated hydrocarbons by their reaction with aqueous bromine.
- *Supplement:* describe the manufacture of alkenes and hydrogen by the cracking of larger alkane molecules using a high temperature and a catalyst.
- *Supplement:* describe the properties of alkenes in terms of addition reactions with bromine, hydrogen in the presence of a nickel catalyst, and steam in the presence of an acid catalyst.
- Describe the complete combustion of ethanol; state the uses of ethanol as a solvent and a fuel.

**Polymers**
- Define polymers as large molecules built up from many smaller molecules called monomers; describe the formation of poly(ethene) as an example of addition polymerisation using ethene monomers.
- *Supplement:* identify the repeat units in addition polymers and in condensation polymers; deduce the structure or repeat unit of an addition polymer from a given alkene and vice versa; describe the differences between addition and condensation polymerisation; describe and draw the structure of nylon, a polyamide.

*Not in 0654 (separate Chemistry 0620 only):* carboxylic acids and esters, manufacture of ethanol (fermentation and hydration comparison), naming alcohols other than ethanol, structural isomers, functional group definition, recall of general formulae, substitution of alkanes with chlorine, kerosene/fuel oil/lubricating oil fractions, plastics disposal, PET and proteins as polymers.

## Notes

### Displayed and structural formulae

- **Displayed formula:** every atom and every bond drawn (including the O–H bond in ethanol).
- **Structural formula** *(Supp)*: shows the arrangement without drawing every bond, e.g. CH₃CH₂CH₃ (propane), CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃ (but-1-ene), CH₃CH=CHCH₃ (but-2-ene).
- **Molecular formula:** the number and type of atoms in one molecule, e.g. C₄H₈.

| Name | Type (ending) | Molecular formula | Structural formula |
|---|---|---|---|
| methane | alkane (-ane) | CH₄ | CH₄ |
| ethane | alkane | C₂H₆ | CH₃CH₃ |
| propane *(Supp)* | alkane | C₃H₈ | CH₃CH₂CH₃ |
| butane *(Supp)* | alkane | C₄H₁₀ | CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃ |
| ethene | alkene (-ene) | C₂H₄ | CH₂=CH₂ |
| propene *(Supp)* | alkene | C₃H₆ | CH₂=CHCH₃ |
| but-1-ene *(Supp)* | alkene | C₄H₈ | CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃ |
| but-2-ene *(Supp)* | alkene | C₄H₈ | CH₃CH=CHCH₃ |
| ethanol | alcohol (-ol) | C₂H₅OH (C₂H₆O) | CH₃CH₂OH |

Prefixes: meth- 1, eth- 2, prop- 3, but- 4. The number shows the position of the double bond: but-1-ene (C=C starts at carbon 1), but-2-ene (C=C at carbon 2).
Recognising the type from a formula: only C and H with only single bonds → alkane; C and H with a C=C → alkene; contains –OH → alcohol.

### Homologous series (Supplement)

A **homologous series** is a family of similar compounds with similar chemical properties. Members have the **same general formula** (you do not need to recall it) and show a **trend in physical properties** — e.g. boiling point increases as the chain gets longer.

### Fuels and fractional distillation

Fossil fuels: coal, natural gas (mainly **methane**), petroleum. **Hydrocarbons** contain hydrogen and carbon only. Petroleum is a **mixture** of hydrocarbons, separated by **fractional distillation**: petroleum is heated and vaporised; the vapour rises up a fractionating column that is **hotter at the bottom and cooler at the top**; fractions condense at different heights according to their boiling points.

| Fraction (top → bottom) | Use |
|---|---|
| Refinery gas | gas used in heating and cooking |
| Gasoline / petrol | fuel used in cars |
| Naphtha | chemical feedstock |
| Diesel oil / gas oil | fuel used in diesel engines |
| Bitumen | making roads |

*Supplement:* from the **bottom to the top** of the column: **chain length decreases** and **boiling points are lower**.

### Alkanes

- Single covalent bonds only → **saturated** hydrocarbons.
- Generally unreactive, except for **combustion**. Complete combustion: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O. (Incomplete combustion gives carbon monoxide and particulates — see the environment topic.)

### Alkenes

- Contain a C=C double bond → **unsaturated** hydrocarbons.
- **Test:** add aqueous bromine (bromine water) and shake. **Alkene: orange → colourless.** Alkane: stays orange (no change).

**Cracking (Supplement):** larger alkane molecules are broken down using a **high temperature** and a **catalyst** to make **alkenes** and **hydrogen** (and smaller alkanes).
C₁₀H₂₂ → C₈H₁₈ + C₂H₄   C₂H₆ → C₂H₄ + H₂

**Addition reactions (Supplement)** — the C=C opens and atoms add across it; one product only:

| Reagent | Condition | Equation |
|---|---|---|
| Bromine | room temperature | C₂H₄ + Br₂ → C₂H₄Br₂ |
| Hydrogen | nickel catalyst | C₂H₄ + H₂ → C₂H₆ |
| Steam | acid catalyst | C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH |

### Ethanol

- Complete combustion: **C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O**.
- Uses: as a **solvent** and as a **fuel**.

### Polymers

**Polymers** are large molecules built up from many smaller molecules called **monomers**.

**Addition polymerisation:** monomers containing C=C join together; the polymer is the only product.
n CH₂=CH₂ → –[CH₂–CH₂]ₙ– poly(ethene)
*Supplement:* repeat unit from an alkene: change C=C to C–C, keep all other atoms in place, add a continuation bond on each side and brackets with n. Monomer from a repeat unit: do the reverse (put the C=C back between the two main-chain carbons). E.g. propene CH₂=CHCH₃ → repeat unit –CH₂–CH(CH₃)–.

**Condensation polymerisation (Supplement):** monomers each with two reactive groups join, and a **small molecule (e.g. water) is also formed** at each link.

| | Addition | Condensation |
|---|---|---|
| Monomers | contain C=C | two different functional groups (e.g. –COOH and –NH₂) |
| Products | polymer only | polymer + small molecule (water) |
| Example | poly(ethene) | nylon |

**Nylon** is a **polyamide**. Draw it with boxes for the carbon chains and the **amide link –CO–NH–** between them:

–C(=O)–□–C(=O)–N(H)–□–N(H)–C(=O)–□–C(=O)–N(H)–□–N(H)–

(each C has a double-bonded O drawn above it; each N has an H drawn below it). The repeat unit contains one of each box and two amide links.

### Paper 6 skills on this topic

- Bromine water test: record "orange to colourless" (not "clear").
- Fractional distillation diagram: thermometer bulb at the side arm, fractionating column, water in at the bottom of the condenser.
- Safety: hydrocarbons and ethanol are flammable — heat with a water bath or electric heater, not a naked flame.

## Worked examples

**1.** Propene reacts with bromine. (a) State the observation. (b) Name the type of reaction. (c) Give the molecular formula of the product. [3]

- (a) Bromine (water) turns from orange to colourless (1)
- (b) Addition (1)
- (c) C₃H₆Br₂ (1)

**2.** Write the equation for the complete combustion of ethanol and give one use of ethanol. [3]

- C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O — formulae (1), balancing (1)
- fuel / solvent (1)

**3.** Draw the repeat unit of the polymer formed from chloroethene, CH₂=CHCl. [2]

- –CH₂–CHCl– with single C–C bond, all atoms shown (1)
- Continuation bonds at both ends, brackets with n (1)

**4.** Describe two differences between addition polymerisation and condensation polymerisation. [2]

- Addition: monomers contain C=C; condensation: monomers have two (different) reactive/functional groups (1)
- Addition: polymer is the only product; condensation: a small molecule such as water is also formed (1)

## Common mistakes

- Missing hydrogen atoms or the O–H bond in displayed formulae.
- Describing cracking without the conditions (**high temperature** and a **catalyst**), or forgetting that it makes **alkenes** (and hydrogen).
- Saying "bromine water goes clear" — it decolourises (orange → colourless).
- Wrong addition conditions: hydrogen needs a **nickel** catalyst; steam needs an **acid** catalyst.
- Saying fractions at the **top** have **higher** boiling points — the top has the **shortest chains and lowest boiling points**.
- Saying condensation polymers form "with no other product" — a small molecule (water) is eliminated.
- Learning esters, carboxylic acids or fermentation from 0620 books — not in 0654.

## Practice questions

6. Ethene reacts with hydrogen and with steam. Give the condition and name the product for each. [4]
7. State how the chain length and boiling point of fractions change from the bottom to the top of the fractionating column. [2]
8. The repeat unit of a polymer is –CH₂–CH(CH₃)–. Name and draw the monomer. [2]
