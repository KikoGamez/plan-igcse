# Organic chemistry

> **In the exam:** Paper 4 has one long organic question every series (often 12–18 marks): naming, drawing displayed formulae, isomers, reactions and conditions, polymers. Paper 2 has 4–6 questions. Paper 6 may include the bromine water test, fermentation set-up or a fractional distillation diagram. This is one of the highest-scoring topics if you learn conditions exactly.

## What you need to know

- Draw and interpret displayed formulae; write and interpret general formulae, structural formulae and molecular formulae.
- State the type of compound present given a chemical name ending in -ane, -ene, -ol, -oic acid, or a molecular/displayed formula; *Extended:* esters (-yl -oate).
- Name and draw displayed formulae of unbranched alkanes, alkenes (including but-1-ene and but-2-ene), alcohols (including propan-1-ol, propan-2-ol, butan-1-ol and butan-2-ol) and carboxylic acids, containing up to four carbon atoms per molecule; *Extended:* esters from alcohols and acids with up to four carbon atoms.
- Define homologous series, functional group, saturated and unsaturated; describe the general characteristics of a homologous series.
- *Extended:* define structural isomers and identify them.
- Name fossil fuels (coal, natural gas, petroleum); state methane is the main constituent of natural gas; describe separation of petroleum by fractional distillation and the uses of the fractions.
- Describe alkanes as generally unreactive except combustion; substitution with chlorine using UV light (photochemical reaction) — *Extended:* equations.
- Describe cracking; state that alkenes and hydrogen can be produced; explain why cracking is done.
- Distinguish saturated and unsaturated hydrocarbons using aqueous bromine.
- *Extended:* addition reactions of alkenes with bromine, hydrogen (nickel catalyst) and steam (acid catalyst).
- Describe the manufacture of ethanol by fermentation and by the catalytic addition of steam to ethene; *Extended:* advantages and disadvantages of each.
- Describe the combustion of ethanol; state the uses of ethanol as a solvent and as a fuel.
- Describe the reactions of ethanoic acid with metals, bases and carbonates; *Extended:* formation of ethanoic acid by oxidation of ethanol (acidified potassium manganate(VII) and bacterial oxidation); formation of esters.
- Define polymers; describe the formation of poly(ethene) by addition polymerisation; plastics, disposal problems. *Extended:* addition vs condensation polymerisation; nylon and PET structures; proteins as natural polyamides.

## Notes

### Key definitions and formulae

- **Homologous series:** a family of similar compounds with similar chemical properties due to the presence of the same functional group. Characteristics: same general formula; same functional group; differ from one member to the next by a –CH₂– unit; trend in physical properties (e.g. boiling point increases with chain length); similar chemical properties.
- **Functional group:** an atom or group of atoms that determine the chemical properties of a homologous series.
- **Saturated:** all carbon–carbon bonds are single bonds. **Unsaturated:** one or more carbon–carbon double (or triple) bonds.
- **Structural isomers** *(Ext)*: compounds with the same molecular formula but different structural formulae (e.g. butane and methylpropane, C₄H₁₀; but-1-ene and but-2-ene, C₄H₈; propan-1-ol and propan-2-ol).

| Series | General formula | Functional group | Ending | First member(s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alkanes | CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ | C–C single only | -ane | methane CH₄, ethane C₂H₆ |
| Alkenes | CₙH₂ₙ | C=C | -ene | ethene C₂H₄, propene C₃H₆ |
| Alcohols | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH | –OH | -ol | methanol CH₃OH, ethanol C₂H₅OH |
| Carboxylic acids | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH | –COOH | -oic acid | methanoic acid HCOOH, ethanoic acid CH₃COOH |
| Esters *(Ext)* | | –COO– | -yl -oate | ethyl ethanoate CH₃COOC₂H₅ |

Prefixes: meth- 1, eth- 2, prop- 3, but- 4. Numbers show position: but-1-ene (C=C at carbon 1), propan-2-ol (OH on carbon 2).
Structural formula example: ethanol CH₃CH₂OH; propanoic acid CH₃CH₂COOH. In displayed formulae show **every** atom and **every** bond (including O–H).

### Fuels and fractional distillation

Fossil fuels: coal, natural gas (mainly **methane**), petroleum (crude oil). Petroleum is a mixture of hydrocarbons, separated by **fractional distillation**: heated, vapour rises up a column that is **hotter at the bottom and cooler at the top**; fractions condense at different heights according to boiling point.

| Fraction (top → bottom) | Use |
|---|---|
| Refinery gas | heating and cooking |
| Gasoline / petrol | fuel for cars |
| Naphtha | chemical feedstock |
| Kerosene / paraffin | jet fuel |
| Diesel oil / gas oil | fuel for diesel engines |
| Fuel oil | fuel for ships and home heating systems |
| Lubricating oil | lubricants, waxes and polishes |
| Bitumen | making roads |

Down the column: chain length increases, boiling point increases, viscosity increases, volatility decreases.

### Alkanes

- Generally unreactive except for combustion. Complete: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O. Incomplete (limited oxygen) gives CO and/or C (soot).
- **Substitution** with chlorine in **ultraviolet light** (photochemical): CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl. One H atom is replaced by Cl.

### Cracking

Long-chain alkanes are heated (high temperature) with a **catalyst** and break down into shorter alkanes and alkenes (and sometimes hydrogen).
C₁₀H₂₂ → C₈H₁₈ + C₂H₄
Why? To match supply and demand: there is more long-chain alkane than needed and not enough short-chain (petrol); alkenes are needed to make polymers and ethanol.

### Alkenes

Test for unsaturation: **aqueous bromine turns from orange to colourless** with alkenes; stays orange with alkanes.

Addition reactions *(Ext)* — the C=C double bond opens and atoms add across it:

| Reagent | Conditions | Product |
|---|---|---|
| Bromine | room temperature | C₂H₄ + Br₂ → C₂H₄Br₂ (1,2-dibromoethane) |
| Hydrogen | nickel catalyst (heat) | C₂H₄ + H₂ → C₂H₆ |
| Steam | acid catalyst (phosphoric acid), high temperature (about 300 °C) and pressure (about 6000 kPa) | C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH |

### Ethanol

| | Fermentation | Steam + ethene |
|---|---|---|
| Equation | C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂ | C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH |
| Conditions | yeast, 25–35 °C, absence of oxygen (anaerobic), aqueous | acid catalyst, about 300 °C, 6000 kPa |
| Advantages *(Ext)* | renewable raw material (sugar from plants); low temperature, less energy | fast; continuous process; pure product; high yield |
| Disadvantages *(Ext)* | slow; batch process; product is impure (needs fractional distillation); lower yield | non-renewable raw material (ethene from petroleum); high energy cost |

Combustion: C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O. Uses: **solvent** and **fuel**.

### Carboxylic acids

Ethanoic acid is a **weak acid** with typical acid reactions:
- with metals: 2CH₃COOH + Mg → (CH₃COO)₂Mg + H₂ (magnesium ethanoate)
- with bases: CH₃COOH + NaOH → CH₃COONa + H₂O (sodium ethanoate)
- with carbonates: 2CH₃COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂

*Extended:* made by oxidation of ethanol — with **acidified aqueous potassium manganate(VII)** (purple → colourless), or by **bacterial oxidation** (vinegar production).

**Esters** *(Ext)*: carboxylic acid + alcohol ⇌ ester + water, with an acid catalyst (concentrated sulfuric acid) and warming.
CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O (ethyl ethanoate). Name: alcohol part first (-yl), acid part second (-oate). Methanol + propanoic acid → methyl propanoate.

### Polymers

**Polymers** are large molecules built up from many smaller molecules called **monomers**.

- **Addition polymerisation:** monomers with a C=C join; the polymer is the **only** product. n CH₂=CH₂ → –[CH₂–CH₂]ₙ– poly(ethene). To draw the repeat unit from a monomer, turn C=C into C–C and add continuation bonds; to find the monomer from a repeat unit, do the reverse.
- **Condensation polymerisation** *(Ext)*: monomers with two functional groups join and a **small molecule (water)** is also formed.
  - **Nylon** (a polyamide): dicarboxylic acid + diamine → amide links –CO–NH–.
  - **PET** (a polyester): dicarboxylic acid + diol → ester links –CO–O–.
  - **Proteins** are natural polyamides made from **amino acids** (amide/peptide link –CO–NH–).

**Plastics** are made from polymers. Problems: non-biodegradable → disposal in landfill (take up space, persist), accumulation in oceans (harm aquatic life), burning releases toxic gases. PET can be converted back into monomers and re-polymerised (recycling).

### Paper 6 skills on this topic

- Bromine water test: record "orange to colourless" (not "clear").
- Fermentation apparatus: flask with sugar solution and yeast, airlock/delivery tube into limewater (shows CO₂ and keeps air out); warm water bath at about 30 °C. Explain why not too hot (enzymes in yeast denatured).
- Fractional distillation of ethanol and water: thermometer bulb at the side arm, fractionating column, water in at the bottom of the condenser; collect the fraction at about 78 °C.
- Safety: ethanol is flammable — heat with a water bath or electric heater, not a naked flame.

## Worked examples

**1.** Propene reacts with bromine. (a) State the observation. (b) Name the type of reaction. (c) Give the molecular formula of the product. [3]

- (a) Bromine (water) turns from orange to colourless (1)
- (b) Addition (1)
- (c) C₃H₆Br₂ (1)

**2.** Give two advantages of making ethanol by fermentation rather than from ethene. [2]

- Uses renewable raw materials (sugar/plants) (1)
- Low temperature / less energy needed (1)

**3.** Name the ester formed from propan-1-ol and ethanoic acid, and give the conditions. [3]

- Propyl ethanoate (1)
- Acid catalyst / concentrated sulfuric acid (1)
- Heat / warm (1)

**4.** Draw the repeat unit of the polymer formed from chloroethene, CH₂=CHCl. [2]

- –CH₂–CHCl– with single C–C bond, all atoms shown (1)
- Continuation bonds at both ends, brackets with n (1)

## Common mistakes

- Missing hydrogen atoms or the O–H bond in displayed formulae.
- Calling cracking "breaking down hydrocarbons" with no catalyst / high temperature, or forgetting that it makes alkenes.
- Saying "bromine water goes clear" — it decolourises (orange → colourless).
- Wrong conditions: substitution needs **UV light**; hydrogenation needs a **nickel** catalyst; fermentation needs **yeast**, no oxygen, and 25–35 °C.
- Naming esters the wrong way round (ethanoic acid + methanol → **methyl ethanoate**, not ethyl methanoate).
- Saying condensation polymers form "with no other product" — water is eliminated.
- Saying alkanes and alkenes are isomers of each other when molecular formulae differ.
