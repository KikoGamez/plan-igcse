# Co-ordinated Sciences · Paper 6 (Alternative to Practical) — Timed

> **Goal:** Sit one complete 0654 Paper 6 under timed conditions (1 hour 30 minutes for the real paper), so you know exactly how the biology, chemistry and physics practical questions feel at exam pace.

## Before you start

- **Paper:** a Cambridge IGCSE **Co-ordinated Sciences (Double Award) 0654 Paper 6 (Alternative to Practical)** — variant 61, 62 or 63 — from the most recent series you have **not** done (e.g. May/June 2025, Oct/Nov 2025 or May/June 2026). Do not use a Chemistry 0620 Paper 6: it is 1 hour, 40 marks and chemistry only.
- **Mark scheme** for that exact variant (you will use it in the next session, *Mark Paper 6*). Do not look at it today.
- **Timer.** The real paper is **1 hour 30 minutes**. This session is 60 minutes, so either:
  - **best:** add 30 minutes today and sit the whole paper in one go; or
  - **if you can't:** sit questions in order for 60 minutes today (roughly 40 marks' worth), stop, and finish the remaining questions in 30 minutes at the start of the next session — still timed, no notes in between.
- **Equipment:** pen, sharp HB pencil (graphs, drawings, diagrams), 30 cm ruler, eraser, calculator, protractor (physics optics questions sometimes need it).
- The *Notes for use in qualitative analysis* are printed in the paper — use them, but don't rely on reading them for the first time in the exam.

## Exam format reminder

- **1 hour 30 minutes, 60 marks, 20% of the qualification.** All questions compulsory. You do no experiments: you read descriptions, diagrams and data from experiments.
- Questions come from **all three sciences** — typically about two each from biology, chemistry and physics, each worth roughly 8–12 marks — and one of them is usually a **planning question** (about 6–7 marks) where you design an investigation. (Exact numbers vary by paper; check the front cover of yours.)
- Typical tasks: read scales on diagrams of apparatus (thermometer, burette, measuring cylinder, gas syringe, stop-watch, ruler, ammeter/voltmeter); complete a results table; plot a graph and draw a best-fit line; calculate a gradient or a mean; record observations from tests (food tests, ion and gas tests); draw a biological specimen and calculate magnification; identify variables; suggest sources of error and improvements; describe safety precautions.
- **Timing:** 1.5 minutes per mark. A 10-mark question gets 15 minutes. Leave about 10 minutes for the planning question.

## How to do it

1. **0–2 min:** flick through the whole paper. Note which questions are biology, chemistry and physics and where the planning question is.
2. **2–88 min (or 2–58 min if splitting):** answer in order. Write the clock time at the start of each question; if one runs more than 3 minutes over its allowance, leave a gap and move on.
3. **Last 2 minutes:** fill any blanks with your best attempt — readings and "no change" observations are quick marks.
4. Put the paper away unmarked. Marking and the practical-skills review happen in the next session.

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Readings:** read to the nearest half of the smallest scale division; record to the precision of the instrument (burette to 2 decimal places, e.g. 23.40 cm³; thermometer usually to 0.5 °C). Burettes read downwards from 0. Check stop-watch diagrams for minutes and seconds.
- **Tables:** each column headed with quantity **and** unit, using a solidus: "time / s", "temperature / °C". No units in the body of the table. Consistent decimal places.
- **Graphs:** independent variable on the x-axis; axes labelled with units; scales linear and using more than half the grid; points plotted to within half a small square; best-fit straight line or smooth curve (not dot-to-dot); anomalous points circled and ignored. Show construction lines when reading values or a gradient.
- **Observations:** say what you **see** — "white precipitate", "effervescence", "blue-black colour", "no change". Never write the name of a product as an observation.
- **Calculations:** show working; the number of significant figures in the answer should match the least precise data used.
- **Variables:** name them precisely ("concentration of hydrochloric acid", not "the acid"; "light intensity / distance of lamp", not "the light").
- **Errors and improvements:** be specific — "heat lost to the surroundings — use a lid / insulated cup"; "difficult to judge the end-point — use a colorimeter or the same observer"; "only one result — repeat and calculate a mean". "Human error" and "be more careful" score nothing.
- **Comparing results:** results are equal within the limits of experimental accuracy if they agree to within about **±10%** (the 0654 syllabus assumption).

## Quick-fire revision

- Food tests (biology): starch — iodine solution, orange-brown → blue-black; reducing sugar — Benedict's, heat, blue → green/yellow/orange/brick-red; protein — biuret, blue → purple; fats and oils — ethanol emulsion test, cloudy white emulsion; DCPIP (listed for Papers 5 and 6 only) is decolourised by vitamin C.
- Gas tests (chemistry): NH₃ damp red litmus → blue; CO₂ limewater → milky; Cl₂ bleaches damp litmus; H₂ "pops" with a lighted splint; O₂ relights a glowing splint.
- Flame tests: Li⁺ red; Na⁺ yellow; K⁺ lilac; Cu²⁺ blue-green.
- Cations with NaOH: Cu²⁺ light blue ppt.; Fe²⁺ green ppt.; Fe³⁺ red-brown ppt.; Zn²⁺ white ppt. soluble in excess; Ca²⁺ white ppt. insoluble in excess; NH₄⁺ ammonia on warming.
- Anions: carbonate → effervescence, CO₂; Cl⁻/Br⁻/I⁻ (dilute HNO₃ then AgNO₃) → white/cream/yellow ppt.; SO₄²⁻ (dilute HNO₃ then Ba(NO₃)₂) → white ppt.; NO₃⁻ (NaOH, Al foil, warm) → ammonia.
- Water: anhydrous copper(II) sulfate white → blue; anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride blue → pink.
- Physics: zero error — subtract it; read a meniscus at eye level; time several oscillations and divide; resistance R = V ÷ I.
- Magnification = image size ÷ actual size (same units).
