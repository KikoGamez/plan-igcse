# Mark Paper 6 — Mark scheme and practical skills review

> **Goal:** Mark the 0654 Paper 6 you sat last session strictly against the mark scheme, then review the practical skills that cost you marks across biology, chemistry and physics.

## Before you start

- Your completed **0654 Paper 6** script (variant 61, 62 or 63) from the previous session. If you split the paper, first finish the remaining questions in a strict 30 minutes, then start marking.
- The **mark scheme** for that exact variant, and the **examiner report** (Principal Examiner Report for Teachers) for that series if you have it — it says which answers were and were not accepted.
- Two pen colours (one for ticks, one for corrections), ruler, pencil, calculator, your error log.

## Exam format reminder

- **Paper 6, Alternative to Practical: 1 hour 30 minutes, 60 marks, 20%** of the 0654 qualification; it tests assessment objective AO3 (experimental skills and investigations) only.
- Biology, chemistry and physics experiments; usually one planning question.
- Mark schemes award one mark per creditworthy point. Look for: **/** (alternative answers), **underlined words** (must be present), **ignore**, **reject / not**, **ecf** (error carried forward), **owtte** (or words to that effect).

## How to do it

1. **0–25 min — mark** question by question. Be strict: if the scheme asks for a unit or a word you left out, no mark. Write the mark in the margin.
2. **25–35 min — sort** every lost mark into one skill category (table below) in your error log.
3. **35–55 min — review** the two weakest skill categories using the notes below; redo those questions properly.
4. **55–60 min** — write your score out of 60 and three specific targets for the next practical paper.

| Skill category | Examples of lost marks |
|---|---|
| Readings | wrong precision; scale misread; zero error ignored |
| Tables | missing unit in heading; units in the body; inconsistent decimal places |
| Graphs | axes the wrong way round; non-linear scale; dot-to-dot line; poor plotting |
| Observations / tests | product named instead of what is seen; vague colours |
| Calculations | no working; wrong significant figures; missing unit |
| Conclusions | not referring to the data; no trend stated |
| Evaluation | vague error ("human error"); improvement not linked to the error |
| Planning | missing variable control, quantities, repeat, or how results give the conclusion |
| Safety / apparatus | wrong apparatus named; hazard without a precaution |

## Exam technique for this paper

### Variables
- **Independent variable:** the one you change. **Dependent variable:** the one you measure. **Controlled variables:** kept constant so the test is fair (valid).
- Name them precisely, with units where possible: "temperature of the water / °C", "volume of gas produced / cm³".
- Suggest **at least five** values for the independent variable, spread over a sensible range (e.g. 20, 30, 40, 50, 60 °C).
- Include a **control experiment** where relevant (biology especially): e.g. boiled enzyme, no plant in the tube.

### Tables
- Columns: independent variable first, then readings, then calculated values. Heading = quantity / unit, e.g. "mass / g".
- Record all repeats and the mean. Same number of decimal places down a column, matching the instrument (burette 0.05 cm³ → 2 d.p.; balance 0.01 g → 2 d.p.).

### Graphs
- Independent variable on the x-axis. Label both axes with quantity and unit.
- Linear scales using **more than half** the grid in each direction; avoid awkward scales (3s, 7s).
- Plot with small crosses or dots in circles to within half a small square.
- **Best-fit** straight line or smooth curve with points evenly either side; do not force through the origin unless the science says so.
- Gradient: use a large triangle (more than half the line), show the values read off.

### Apparatus and readings
- Chemistry: burette (accurate variable volume, 2 d.p.), volumetric pipette (fixed volume, e.g. 25.0 cm³), measuring cylinder (approximate), gas syringe, balance, thermometer, stop-watch.
- Biology: hand lens, measuring cylinder, thermometer, water bath, ruler for drawings (magnification = image size ÷ actual size, same units).
- Physics: ruler/metre rule (avoid parallax — eye level, perpendicular), stop-watch (time many oscillations and divide), ammeter in series, voltmeter in parallel, newton meter, protractor; correct for **zero errors**.
- Read to the nearest **half of the smallest division**.

### Accuracy, errors and improvements
- **Random error** → scatter in repeats → repeat and take a mean; use a more precise instrument.
- **Systematic error** → all readings shifted (e.g. zero error, heat loss every time) → correct the zero, insulate, recalibrate.
- **Anomalous result** → identify it, explain why it doesn't fit, repeat that reading and exclude it from the mean.
- Two results agree within experimental accuracy if they are within about **±10%** of each other.
- Every improvement must say **what** to change and **why** it helps.

### Safety
- Name the hazard **and** the precaution: "acid is corrosive — wear eye protection"; "ethanol is flammable — heat with a water bath, no naked flame"; "chlorine is toxic — use a fume cupboard"; "scalpel is sharp — cut away from the body on a tile"; "hot water — allow to cool before handling".

### Planning questions (all sciences)
Use this checklist; mark schemes usually credit most of these points:
1. Apparatus and materials, with sizes or quantities.
2. What you change (independent variable) and how, with a range of values.
3. What you measure (dependent variable) and how/when.
4. Variables kept constant (at least two, named).
5. Repeats and a mean.
6. How the results are used to reach a conclusion (e.g. plot a graph of rate against temperature; the shortest time = fastest).
7. A safety precaution, where relevant.

## Checklist while marking

- Did each table heading have a unit with a solidus? Did every reading have the right precision?
- Graph: scale, labels, plotting, line — often four separate marks. Award yourself each one honestly.
- Did you write "no change" where nothing happened? Did you describe colours with "from … to …"?
- Did each calculation show working, a unit and sensible significant figures?
- In the planning question, tick each checklist point the scheme credited; circle the ones you missed.
- Error log: record each lost mark as *skill category — what I wrote — what earns the mark*.

## Quick-fire revision

- Chemistry contexts listed in the syllabus: rates, salt preparation, filtration, crystallisation, distillation, chromatography, electrolysis, ion and gas tests, tests for water, acid reactions, oxidising/reducing agent tests, heating and cooling curves, titrations, solubility, melting and boiling points, displacement, temperature changes, rusting.
- Biology contexts: diffusion, osmosis, food tests, enzyme rates, photosynthesis and limiting factors, transpiration, heart and breathing rate, respiration, seeds and flowers, germination, variation, drawings of specimens.
- Physics contexts: length, volume, force, time, density, springs, cooling and heating, oscillations, circuits (current and p.d.), optics (reflection, refraction, lenses).
- Precision: half the smallest division. Significant figures: match the least precise raw data.
- Ratios as x : y. Means: ignore anomalies. Agreement: within ±10%.
