# Mark Paper 4

> **Goal:** Mark the Paper 4 you sat in the last session strictly against the official mark scheme, use the examiner report to understand lost marks, and turn every mistake into an error-log entry.

## Before you start

- Your completed **Paper 4** script from the previous session.
- The **mark scheme** for that exact series and variant (e.g. 0620/42 May/June 2025 — the variant number must match).
- The **Principal Examiner Report for Teachers** for the same series (one report covers all variants; find the section for your variant).
- A **different-coloured pen** for marking, your error log, highlighters.
- Calculator for re-checking calculations.

## Exam format reminder

- Paper 4: 1 h 15 min, 80 marks, 50% of the qualification; 6–7 structured questions.
- Mark schemes list acceptable answers, alternatives separated by "/" or "OR", words in brackets that are **not required**, underlined words that are **essential**, "ignore" and "reject"/"not" lists, and "ecf" for error carried forward.

## How to do it

1. **0–5 min:** read the mark scheme's general notes (abbreviations: "owtte" = or words to that effect, "ecf", "ora" = or reverse argument, "M1/A1" style marks for calculations, "max" limits).
2. **5–35 min:** mark question by question. Be strict: if the scheme rejects a word you used, no mark. Write the mark for each part in the margin and the total per question.
3. **35–45 min:** read the examiner report for the questions where you lost the most marks. Note what "good answers" included.
4. **45–55 min:** fill in the error log (see below).
5. **55–60 min:** add up the total, convert to a percentage, and list your 3 weakest topics to revisit.

## Exam technique for this paper

What the mark scheme and examiner reports reveal:
- **Key words are essential.** E.g. "electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions", "delocalised electrons", "more frequent collisions", "proportion of particles with energy greater than activation energy". Learn these as phrases.
- **Observations vs conclusions.** "Bubbles" or "effervescence" scores; "hydrogen is produced" does not, if the question asks what you would **see**.
- **Colours must be precise:** "colourless" not "clear"; "red-brown" for Fe³⁺ hydroxide; "light blue" for Cu(OH)₂; "orange to colourless" for bromine water.
- **Calculations:** look at how ecf works — if you made one slip early, check whether you would have got later marks. This shows that showing working is worth marks.
- **Lists:** if a question asks for two things and you give three, a wrong extra answer can cancel a correct one.
- **Equations:** formulae must be correct for any mark; balancing is often a separate mark.
- **Sig figs:** final answers usually need 2–3 s.f.; check the scheme for the range accepted.

## Checklist while marking

For each lost mark, record in the error log:

| Column | What to write |
|---|---|
| Question | e.g. 3(b)(ii) |
| Topic | syllabus topic, e.g. "Electrochemistry — half-equations" |
| What I wrote | short summary |
| What scored | the mark-scheme wording |
| Type of error | K = knowledge gap; W = wording/key word missing; C = calculation/units; R = misread question; T = ran out of time |
| Fix | the exact phrase or fact to learn, or "redo question on [date]" |

Then:
- Count errors of each type. Mostly **W**? Practise model answers. Mostly **K**? Re-read the Round 1 notes. Mostly **C**? Do extra mole questions. Mostly **R**? Underline command words and quantities next time.
- Re-attempt every calculation you got wrong, from scratch, without looking.
- Highlight in the mark scheme any phrase you want to memorise and copy it onto a flashcard.

## Quick-fire revision

Mark-scheme phrases worth learning:
- Isotopes: "same number of protons, different numbers of neutrons".
- Same chemical properties: "same number of outer-shell electrons / same electronic configuration".
- Metallic bond: "attraction between positive ions and delocalised electrons".
- Graphite conducts: "delocalised electrons that are free to move".
- Rate: "more collisions per unit time" / "more frequent collisions".
- Catalyst: "provides an alternative pathway with lower activation energy".
- Equilibrium: "rate of forward reaction equals rate of reverse reaction; concentrations no longer change".
- Strong acid: "completely dissociated in aqueous solution"; weak acid "partially dissociated".
- Sacrificial protection: "zinc is more reactive, loses electrons more readily than iron".
- Greenhouse: "absorbs thermal energy emitted by the Earth / reduces thermal energy loss to space".
- Electrolysis: "ions are free to move" (molten or aqueous).
