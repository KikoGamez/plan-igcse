# Past paper · Paper 6

> **Goal:** Complete one full Paper 6 (Alternative to Practical) in 1 hour under exam conditions, then use the remaining time to mark the planning and data-handling questions carefully.

## Before you start

- **Paper:** a Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620 **Paper 6 (Alternative to Practical)** from a recent series — ideally 2024 or 2025 (May/June or Oct/Nov), variant 61, 62 or 63 — that you have **not** done.
- **Mark scheme** for that exact variant; the **examiner report** for the series if available.
- **Timer** for 1 hour. If this session is only 60 minutes, sit the paper today and mark at the start of your next session; otherwise allow 80–90 minutes.
- **Equipment:** pen, sharp pencil (graphs and diagrams), ruler, eraser, calculator.

## Exam format reminder

- 1 hour, **40 marks**, 20% of the qualification. Written paper about practical work — no lab.
- Usually **4–6 questions**. Typical mix:
  - an **experiment with readings** to record (thermometer, burette, gas syringe, stop-clock diagrams), a table to complete and a **graph** to plot;
  - a **qualitative analysis** question (tests on unknown substances — record observations or identify ions/gases using the syllabus test notes);
  - questions on **apparatus, separation techniques or chromatography**;
  - a **planning question** (about 6 marks) asking you to design an investigation.
- Timing: about **1.5 minutes per mark**; leave 8–10 minutes for the planning question.

## How to do it

1. **0–60 min:** sit the paper. Read each experiment description fully before answering — details like "the mixture was stirred" or "excess acid" matter.
2. **60 min onwards (or next session):** mark with the mark scheme. Check graph marks very carefully (axes, scale, plotting, line).
3. Fill in the error log by skill: readings, tables, graphs, observations, conclusions, errors/improvements, planning.

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Reading apparatus:** thermometer to the nearest 0.5 °C or 1 °C as the scale allows; burette to 2 decimal places (e.g. 23.40 cm³); measuring cylinder and gas syringe to the nearest graduation. Burettes read **downwards** from 0.
- **Tables:** headings with units in the form "volume / cm³"; consistent decimal places.
- **Graphs:** scales using more than half the grid; axes labelled with units; points plotted to ±½ small square; smooth curve or straight line of best fit **through or near** the points (not dot-to-dot); circle anomalous points and **exclude** them from the line. Show construction lines when reading values from a graph.
- **Observations:** describe what you see — "white precipitate", "effervescence", "colourless solution", "no change". Do not name products as observations.
- **Identifying ions:** use the exact syllabus tests: e.g. Cl⁻ with dilute nitric acid then aqueous silver nitrate → white precipitate; Fe²⁺ with NaOH → green precipitate, insoluble in excess.
- **Sources of error and improvements:** be specific. "Heat loss to the surroundings — use a lid / insulate the cup"; "gas escapes before the bung is replaced — use a divided flask"; "measuring cylinder not precise — use a burette or pipette". "Human error" scores nothing.
- **Planning question:** include
  1. apparatus (with sizes, e.g. 50 cm³ burette, 250 cm³ conical flask);
  2. quantities and concentrations (realistic);
  3. method in numbered steps;
  4. what to measure and how;
  5. what to keep constant (fair test);
  6. how to use the results to reach a conclusion (e.g. "the one with the greatest temperature rise is most reactive");
  7. safety if relevant.
- **Variables:** independent, dependent, control — name them precisely ("concentration of hydrochloric acid", not "the acid").

## Checklist while marking

- Did every table heading include a unit? Did every reading have the correct number of decimal places?
- Graph: count scale, labels, plots, line — four separate marks in many schemes.
- Did you write "no change" / "no reaction" where nothing happened?
- In the planning question, tick off each element from the list above that the mark scheme credited and which you missed.
- Error log: record each lost mark with the skill type (Reading / Table / Graph / Observation / Conclusion / Evaluation / Planning) and the corrected answer.

## Quick-fire revision

- Gas tests: NH₃ damp red litmus → blue; CO₂ limewater → milky; Cl₂ bleaches damp litmus; H₂ pops with lighted splint; O₂ relights glowing splint; SO₂ acidified KMnO₄ purple → colourless.
- Flame tests: Li⁺ red; Na⁺ yellow; K⁺ lilac; Ca²⁺ orange-red; Ba²⁺ light green; Cu²⁺ blue-green.
- NaOH: Cu²⁺ light blue; Fe²⁺ green; Fe³⁺ red-brown; Cr³⁺ green (soluble in excess); Al³⁺, Zn²⁺ white (soluble in excess); Ca²⁺ white (insoluble).
- NH₃: Zn²⁺ white, soluble in excess; Al³⁺ white, insoluble; Cu²⁺ light blue → dark blue solution in excess.
- Anions: carbonate (acid → CO₂); halides (HNO₃ + AgNO₃: white/cream/yellow); sulfate (HNO₃ + Ba(NO₃)₂: white); sulfite (acidified KMnO₄ decolourised); nitrate (NaOH + Al foil, warm → NH₃).
- Indicators: methyl orange red (acid) / yellow (alkali); thymolphthalein colourless (acid) / blue (alkali).
- Rf = distance moved by substance ÷ distance moved by solvent; baseline in pencil, above solvent level.
- Heat to constant mass; concordant titres within 0.20 cm³.
