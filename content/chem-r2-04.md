## Exam format reminder

- **Paper 2** (Multiple Choice, 45 min, 40 marks, 30%): mole calculations, ion tests and organic each usually appear as single questions.
- **Paper 4** (Theory, 2 h, 120 marks, 50%): chemistry is about a third; mole steps are typically 2–4 marks, ion tests 2–4 marks, organic 4–8 marks across a question.

## How to do it

1. **0–5 min — choose.** From your error log, rank the three areas from weakest to strongest. Spend the most time on the weakest.
2. **5–20 min — moles drill** (no notes, full working, units):
   1. Calculate the mass of 0.250 mol of calcium carbonate, CaCO₃.
   2. Calculate the volume, in cm³ at r.t.p., of 0.88 g of carbon dioxide.
   3. Fe + 2HCl → FeCl₂ + H₂. Calculate the volume of hydrogen, in dm³ at r.t.p., from 2.80 g of iron with excess acid.
   4. Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂. 1.20 g of magnesium is added to a solution containing 1.46 g of hydrogen chloride. Show which reactant is limiting and calculate the volume of hydrogen in cm³ at r.t.p.
   5. 0.100 mol of an element that is a diatomic gas has a mass of 3.20 g. Calculate its Mr and identify it.
3. **20–35 min — ion and gas tests drill.** Write the four test tables (cations, anions, gases, flame tests) from memory, then check against *Acids, bases and salts* in a different colour. Then answer:
   6. Solution A: white ppt. with NaOH(aq), dissolves in excess; white ppt. with dilute HNO₃ then AgNO₃(aq). Identify A.
   7. Solution B: green ppt. with NaOH(aq), insoluble in excess, turning brown near the surface on standing; white ppt. with dilute HNO₃ then Ba(NO₃)₂(aq). Identify B.
   8. Solid C gives a yellow flame; its solution, warmed with NaOH(aq) and aluminium foil, gives a gas that turns damp red litmus blue. Identify C.
4. **35–50 min — organic drill:**
   9. Draw the displayed formula of but-1-ene and give the type of compound.
   10. Draw the repeat unit of poly(propene) from propene, CH₂=CHCH₃.
   11. Complete: C₈H₁₈ → C₆H₁₄ + ____ and state two conditions for cracking.
   12. Give the condition and product for ethene + steam, and the equation for the complete combustion of ethanol.
5. **50–55 min — mark** with the answers below. Redo every wrong answer immediately.
6. **55–60 min — error log**: one line per mistake, and one flashcard per fact you didn't know.

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Moles:** always write the four steps — equation, moles of known, ratio, convert. Each step is usually a mark, so a wrong final answer can still score most marks (ecf). Use Mr 71 for Cl₂ and 32 for O₂. 24 dm³ = 24 000 cm³. For limiting reactants, compare **moles adjusted for the ratio**, never masses.
- **Ion tests:** give the **test and the result** — "add aqueous sodium hydroxide; light blue precipitate, insoluble in excess". For halides and sulfate, always say **acidify with dilute nitric acid** first. Name the ion with its charge or the full compound name ("iron(II)", not "iron").
- **Gas tests:** "damp" litmus; "lighted" splint for hydrogen, "glowing" splint for oxygen; limewater turns "milky".
- **Organic:** displayed formulae show **every** bond, including O–H. Name the type of reaction (addition, combustion, cracking, polymerisation). Learn conditions exactly: nickel catalyst (hydrogen), acid catalyst (steam), high temperature + catalyst (cracking).
- **Polymers:** addition — monomer has C=C, polymer is the only product; condensation — a small molecule (water) is also formed; nylon is a polyamide (–CO–NH– links).

## Checklist while marking

- Moles: did you write the unit and give 3 significant figures? Did you use the mole ratio?
- Tests: did your answer include **both** the reagent and the observation? Any "clear" instead of "colourless"?
- Organic: any missing H atoms or bonds? Brackets, n and continuation bonds on repeat units?
- Record each mistake as *topic — what I wrote — correct version*; anything wrong twice goes on a flashcard to review daily until 11 January.

## Quick-fire revision

- n = m ÷ M; n = V(dm³) ÷ 24; 1 mol = 6.02 × 10²³ particles; concentration in 0654 only in g/dm³.
- Limiting reactant: runs out first; use it to calculate products.
- Cations (NaOH): NH₄⁺ ammonia on warming; Ca²⁺ white, insoluble in excess; Cu²⁺ light blue; Fe²⁺ green; Fe³⁺ red-brown; Zn²⁺ white, soluble in excess. With NH₃: Cu²⁺ dissolves in excess → dark blue solution; Zn²⁺ dissolves in excess; Ca²⁺ no or very slight ppt.
- Anions: CO₃²⁻ effervescence, CO₂; Cl⁻ white, Br⁻ cream, I⁻ yellow (HNO₃ + AgNO₃); SO₄²⁻ white (HNO₃ + Ba(NO₃)₂); NO₃⁻ ammonia (NaOH + Al, warm).
- Flames: Li⁺ red, Na⁺ yellow, K⁺ lilac, Cu²⁺ blue-green.
- Alkanes saturated, unreactive except combustion; alkenes unsaturated, decolourise aqueous bromine.
- Fractions top → bottom: refinery gas, gasoline, naphtha, diesel oil, bitumen; chain length and boiling point increase downwards.
- Ethanol: solvent and fuel; C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O.
