# Weak topics: moles and ion tests

> **Goal:** Use your error log from the past papers to fix the two areas that lose most marks across Papers 2, 4 and 6 — mole calculations and the tests for ions and gases.

## Before you start

- Your **error log** from the Paper 2, Paper 4 and Paper 6 sessions.
- The three marked past papers and their mark schemes.
- The Round 1 notes for **The mole: calculations**, **The mole: empirical formula and yield** and **Acids, bases and salts** (ion and gas test tables).
- Extra questions: from one or two **other** recent past papers (2024 or 2025, any variant you have not done) pick out only the mole-calculation parts from Paper 4 and the qualitative-analysis question from Paper 6, with their mark schemes.
- Calculator, blank paper, timer, flashcards.

## Exam format reminder

- **Moles** appear in every Paper 4 (usually 4–8 marks in one or more questions), in 2–4 Paper 2 questions, and in Paper 6 titration/gas volume processing.
- **Ion and gas tests** appear in 2–3 Paper 2 questions every series and in at least one Paper 6 question (often 6–10 marks), plus "describe a test" parts in Paper 4.
- Together these are worth roughly 10–15% of your total marks — high value for one hour.

## How to do it

1. **0–10 min — sort the error log.** List every mole and ion-test error. Classify each: K (knowledge), C (calculation/units), W (wording), R (misread).
2. **10–20 min — ion and gas tests from memory.** On blank paper, write the full cation table (NaOH and NH₃), anion tests, gas tests and flame tests. Mark against your notes in another colour. Make a flashcard for every cell you got wrong.
3. **20–45 min — mole drill.** Redo every mole calculation you lost marks on, from scratch. Then do the drill below and the extra past-paper parts. Write all four steps: equation → moles → ratio → convert.
4. **45–55 min — ion test application.** Do the Paper 6 qualitative-analysis question from another paper. Mark strictly — exact colours and "in excess" wording.
5. **55–60 min — update the error log**: tick fixed errors, carry forward any that are still wrong to your final revision list.

## Exam technique for this paper

**Moles**
- Write the formula you are using before substituting: n = m ÷ M, n = c × V, n = V ÷ 24.
- Convert cm³ → dm³ (÷ 1000) **before** using c × V.
- Always use the mole **ratio** from the balanced equation — circle the numbers in front of the formulae.
- Keep 3–4 s.f. in intermediate steps; give the answer to 3 s.f. with a unit.
- Diatomic gases: Mr(Cl₂) = 71, Mr(O₂) = 32, Mr(H₂) = 2.
- If a step is "show that", still show full working.

**Ion tests**
- Always give **reagent + result**. For anion tests the reagents come in an order: e.g. "acidify with dilute nitric acid, **then** add aqueous silver nitrate".
- Precipitate colour + "soluble/insoluble in excess" for cations; give both NaOH and NH₃ results if asked to distinguish.
- Gas tests need the exact result words: "relights" (O₂), "pops" (H₂), "milky" (CO₂), "bleaches" (Cl₂), "red to blue" (NH₃), "purple to colourless" (SO₂).

## Checklist while marking

- Did you lose the mark for the method or only the final answer? If only the answer, the fix is checking units and rounding.
- Did you mix up NaOH and NH₃ results for Zn²⁺/Al³⁺/Cu²⁺? Write the pair as a single flashcard.
- Did you write a product name where an observation was needed?
- Record each remaining weakness in the error log with a date to revisit before 11 January.

## Quick-fire revision

**Mole drill** (Ar: C = 12, O = 16, Na = 23, Cl = 35.5, Fe = 56, Ag = 108)

1. Volume of hydrogen at r.t.p. when 2.80 g of iron reacts with excess hydrochloric acid. Fe + 2HCl → FeCl₂ + H₂
2. Volume of 0.200 mol/dm³ HCl needed to react with 20.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ Na₂CO₃. Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂
3. Mass of silver chloride precipitated when excess aqueous silver nitrate is added to 25.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol/dm³ sodium chloride.

**Ion test drill**

4. A solution gives a green precipitate with NaOH that is insoluble in excess, and a white precipitate with acidified barium nitrate. Identify it.
5. How do you distinguish Zn²⁺ from Al³⁺?
6. Which anion is identified by warming with NaOH and aluminium foil?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. n(Fe) = 2.80 ÷ 56 = 0.0500 mol; n(H₂) = 0.0500 mol; V = 0.0500 × 24 = 1.20 dm³.
2. n(Na₂CO₃) = 0.100 × 0.0200 = 0.00200 mol; n(HCl) = 0.00400 mol; V = 0.00400 ÷ 0.200 = 0.0200 dm³ = 20.0 cm³.
3. n(NaCl) = 0.200 × 0.0250 = 0.00500 mol = n(AgCl); Mr(AgCl) = 143.5; mass = 0.718 g.
4. Iron(II) sulfate (a green precipitate with NaOH could also be Cr³⁺, but that one dissolves in excess).
5. Add aqueous ammonia in excess: Zn²⁺ white precipitate dissolves (colourless solution); Al³⁺ white precipitate does not dissolve.
6. Nitrate — ammonia is produced (turns damp red litmus blue).

</details>
