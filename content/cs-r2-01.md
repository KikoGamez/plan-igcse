# Past paper · Paper 1 Computer Systems timed

> **Goal:** Sit a full Cambridge IGCSE Computer Science Paper 1 in 1 h 45 under exam conditions, with no notes and no calculator, so you know your real mark and which topics lose you marks before the mock.

## Before you start

- **Paper:** a Cambridge IGCSE Computer Science **0478 Paper 1** (variant 11, 12 or 13) from a recent series that you have **not** seen, e.g. May/June 2025 Paper 12, or October/November 2024 Paper 11 (the Feb/March series has variant 12 only). Use a paper from **2023 onwards** if you can: the syllabus has changed little since, but older papers include some different wording. If a question is on a topic you have not covered yet, still attempt it, then flag it.
- **Mark scheme** for the **same paper and variant**: keep it closed or in another room until the next session.
- **Timer** for **1 hour 45 minutes**. This session is longer than your usual hour; if you cannot find 1 h 45 in one go, sit it in **two blocks** (about 55 + 50 min) and stop the clock exactly between them. Do not pause for any other reason.
- **No calculator** (Cambridge does not allow one in either Computer Science paper). Have pencil, black pen, ruler and spare paper for binary/hex working.
- Phone away, notes closed, quiet desk.

## Exam format reminder

- **Paper 1 Computer Systems:** 1 hour 45 minutes, **75 marks**, **50%** of the IGCSE. **All questions compulsory**; short-answer and structured questions, no choice. (Paper 2 is the other 50%.)
- It covers syllabus sections 1–6: data representation, data transmission, hardware, software, the internet and its uses, and automated and emerging technologies. All 6 areas appear every time.
- Typical question types: **state/name** (1 mark), **describe/explain** (2–3 marks), **calculations** (binary, hex, file size; show working), **tables to complete**, and **6-mark** extended answers on a scenario.
- **Timing:** 75 marks in 105 minutes is **1.4 minutes per mark**, so about 1.5 min for a 1-mark item, 3 min for 2 marks, 8 min for 6 marks. Leave 5–8 minutes to check.

## How to do it

| Time | What to do |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | Skim the paper: note how many pages and any scenario questions. Do not write yet. |
| 0:03–0:35 | Work through the first third. Answer every question; put a ring around anything you skip. |
| 0:35–1:10 | Middle third. Keep to 1.4 min per mark. |
| 1:10–1:35 | Last third, including the long scenario answers. |
| 1:35–1:45 | Go back to the ringed questions, check binary/hex working, check every question has an answer. |

Rules: stop at **1 h 45 exactly**. If you carry on, change pen colour so you can see what you got in time. **Never leave a blank**: a guess in the blank box can score; nothing never does. When finished, **do not look at the mark scheme**; just write on the front page the three questions you found hardest.

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Read the command word:** *State* = short fact; *Describe* = say what happens; *Explain* = give a reason with "because"/"so that"; *Give* = just the answer.
- **Number of marks = number of points.** For "Describe… [3]" write three clear, separate points.
- **Show all working** in calculations (binary addition carry, file size = width × height × colour depth). Method marks are given even when the final answer is wrong.
- **Use technical words:** "packet", "hash", "encrypts", "IP address", "kernel"; avoid "it makes it faster" or "it keeps it secure".
- **Apply to the scenario** (school, bank, game): use the context in the answer.
- **Do not repeat the question** in your answer; do not give two answers where one is required: the examiner marks the first.
- In 6-mark questions, write **three or four developed points**, with a reason for each; if there is a "discuss/evaluate" element, add a judgement.

## Checklist while marking

(Do this next session, but note it now.)

- Every question has an **answer**, even a guess.
- Binary and hex answers have **working** and the right number of bits (8-bit unless told).
- Units on file sizes and data rates.
- No question where you answered the wrong command word (e.g. listed when asked to explain).

## Quick-fire revision

Read these 10 minutes before you sit down.

- Binary place values: 128 64 32 16 8 4 2 1; hex digits 0–9 then A=10 … F=15.
- **Overflow** happens when a result needs more bits than are available.
- Image file size = **width × height × colour depth** (bits); sound = **sample rate × bit depth × duration**.
- **Lossy** removes data permanently; **lossless** can be restored exactly.
- Error detection: **parity bit, checksum, echo check, check digit, ARQ**.
- Symmetric encryption uses **one key**; asymmetric uses a **public key and a private key**.
- Fetch–decode–execute: **PC → MAR → memory → MDR → CIR**.
- **RAM** volatile read/write; **ROM** non-volatile, holds the boot-up program.
- **SSD** has no moving parts (flash); **HDD** magnetic; **optical** uses a laser.
- A **MAC address** identifies the hardware; an **IP address** the network location.
- **OS functions:** manages memory, files, users, security, peripherals, multitasking, interrupts.
- **Compiler** translates all the source code at once; **interpreter** line by line.
- **DNS** converts a domain name to an IP address; **cookies** are text files.
- **Phishing** = fake message; **pharming** = redirect to fake site; **DDoS** = floods a server.
- **Sensor → microprocessor → actuator** in automated systems.
