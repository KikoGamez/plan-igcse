# Mark Paper 1 with the mark scheme

> **Goal:** Mark your Paper 1 strictly, find out which topics lost you marks, and turn every lost mark into an entry in your error log with the correct answer in exam wording.

## Before you start

- **Your completed Paper 1** from the last session (same paper and variant).
- The **official mark scheme** for exactly that paper (Cambridge publishes mark schemes with the past paper; ask your teacher if you cannot find it).
- A **different colour pen** (green or red) for marking; **do not rub anything out**.
- Your **error log**: a page or notebook with columns *Q number | Topic | What I wrote | Correct answer | Why I lost it | Fix*.
- Timer for **1 hour**.

## Exam format reminder

- Paper 1 was **75 marks, 1 h 45**, 50% of the IGCSE. Grade boundaries change each series and apply to the whole qualification, so use your percentage only as a rough guide.
- The mark scheme shows each **mark point** on a separate line, with alternative answers (OR, "accept", "ignore", "do not accept"). Some questions give marks for **any N points from a longer list**.
- Be aware: **"mark points" count, not sentences**. A long answer can score 1.

## How to do it

| Time | What to do |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:35 | Mark the paper **question by question**, with the mark scheme. Write the marks in the margin; total each question. |
| 0:35–0:40 | Add up the total. Work out % and note the **topic scores** (see below). |
| 0:40–0:55 | For every question where you lost marks, write an **error log entry** (aim for at least 10 entries). |
| 0:55–1:00 | Pick your **three weakest topics** and write them on your revision plan; put the next session of each on the calendar. |

**Marking rules:** mark what you **actually wrote**. Do not give yourself credit for what you "meant". If the mark scheme says "do not accept" your wording, it is a lost mark. Where two points are too similar, give **one** mark.

**Topic tally:** make a small table with the six areas (1 Data representation, 2 Transmission, 3 Hardware, 4 Software, 5 Internet and security, 6 Automated/emerging tech). Write the marks lost in each; the biggest numbers show what to study next.

## Exam technique for this paper

Ask for each lost mark: **what type of error was it?**

- **Knowledge gap:** I did not know the fact (relearn it).
- **Vague wording:** I knew the idea but used non-technical words (rewrite using the mark-scheme term).
- **Did not answer the question:** wrong command word, or ignored the scenario.
- **Calculation slip:** arithmetic or binary error (redo it, check the method).
- **Ran out of time:** note which questions you rushed.

Most students find **half** of their lost marks are vague wording or not applying to the scenario: these are the quickest to fix.

## Checklist while marking

- Every question is **marked and totalled**; the grand total is written on the front.
- For each lost mark, the **correct answer** is copied in exam wording.
- Error log has entries tagged with a **topic** and an **error type**.
- Calculations: redo the ones you lost marks on **without looking**, then compare.
- You have noticed the **mark-scheme phrases** that are repeated (e.g. "encrypts the data", "uses a public key").
- You have chosen **three topics** for revision and booked them.

## Quick-fire revision

Use the last minutes of the session to check you can answer these without notes.

- Convert 10110101 to decimal (181) and to hex (B5).
- Add 01101101 + 00110111 in binary (10100100).
- Why do computers use hex? **Shorter, easier for humans to read and write** than binary.
- State the difference between **lossy** and **lossless** compression.
- State three types of **error detection**.
- Name the **registers** used in the fetch–decode–execute cycle.
- State the function of the **MAC** and **IP** address.
- Give two differences between **RAM** and **ROM**.
- Explain what an **interrupt** is.
- Describe the difference between **compiler** and **interpreter**.
- Explain how **HTTPS** protects data.
- Name the three parts of an **automated system**.
