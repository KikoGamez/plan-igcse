# Exam format and mark scheme

> **In the exam:** Cambridge IGCSE English First Language 0500 has two papers, each 2 hours and 80 marks, each worth 50% of the grade. Paper 1 tests reading (with some marks for writing). Paper 2 tests writing (with some marks for reading in Section A). Every question type below appears on every paper, so knowing the format is worth marks in itself.

## What you need to know

The syllabus assesses five **Reading** objectives and five **Writing** objectives.

- **R1** Demonstrate understanding of explicit meanings
- **R2** Demonstrate understanding of implicit meanings and attitudes
- **R3** Analyse, evaluate and develop facts, ideas and opinions, using appropriate support from the text
- **R4** Demonstrate understanding of how writers achieve effects and influence readers
- **R5** Select and use information for specific purposes
- **W1** Articulate experience and express what is thought, felt and imagined
- **W2** Organise and structure ideas and opinions for deliberate effect
- **W3** Use a range of vocabulary and sentence structures appropriate to context
- **W4** Use register appropriate to context
- **W5** Make accurate use of spelling, punctuation and grammar

## Notes

### Paper 1 – Reading (2 hours, 80 marks)

You get a **reading booklet insert** with three texts (Text A, Text B, Text C) and a question paper. Answer **all** questions.

| Question | Text | What you do | Marks | Suggested time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Q1** short answers | Text A | Short comprehension questions: find facts, explain in your own words, show what is implied | 15 | 20 min |
| **Q1** summary (last part) | Text B | Continuous summary of a set topic, own words, **no more than 120 words** | 15 (10 reading + 5 writing) | 25 min |
| **Q2** short answers | Text C | Find words that carry an idea; explain phrases in your own words | 10 | 15 min |
| **Q2** writer's effect | Text C | Choose words/phrases from **two** given paragraphs and explain meaning and effect (about 200–300 words) | 15 | 25 min |
| **Q3** extended response | Text C | Write a letter, report, journal, speech, interview or article using and developing ideas from Text C (250–350 words) | 25 (15 reading + 10 writing) | 35 min |

Total: Q1 = 30, Q2 = 25, Q3 = 25. The exact sub-question letters (a), (b), (c)… can vary between series, so read the front page and the question paper carefully.

### Paper 2 – Directed Writing and Composition (2 hours, 80 marks)

| Section | What you do | Marks | Length | Suggested time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A: Directed writing** (compulsory) | Read **two** texts on the same topic; write an argumentative or discursive piece (letter, article or speech) that evaluates the ideas in both | 40 (15 reading + 25 writing) | 250–350 words | 1 hour |
| **B: Composition** (choose **one** of four) | Two **descriptive** titles and two **narrative** titles | 40 (16 content and structure + 24 style and accuracy) | 350–450 words | 1 hour |

### How the mark schemes work (plain English)

Examiners mark longer answers with **levels**: they read the whole answer, decide which level description fits best, then choose a mark inside that level. You can't "collect" marks for features; the overall quality decides.

**Short-answer questions (Q1, Q2 short parts)** use a point-based mark scheme: one mark per correct idea. "Own words" questions give no mark if you just copy key words.

**Summary – reading (10 marks):** top level = you include a wide range of relevant ideas, clearly understood, focused on the question. Lower levels = few ideas, some irrelevant, or copied without showing understanding.

**Summary – writing (5 marks):** top level = concise, well organised, in your own words, fluent. Low level = lifted phrases, lists, over the word limit, rambling.

**Writer's effect (15 marks):** top level = you choose precise, interesting words, explain their meaning and the *specific* effect on the reader, and show how the images work together (the overall impression). Middle level = sensible choices, meanings explained, effects general ("makes it vivid"). Low level = long quotes, meanings only, or devices named with no explanation.

**Extended response Q3 (15 reading + 10 writing):** top reading = you use many ideas from the text, develop them (draw inferences, go beyond the surface), and stay in role. Top writing = the right voice for the audience, well-structured, varied and accurate language.

**Directed writing (15 reading + 25 writing):** top reading = you *evaluate* the ideas in both texts (judge how strong or convincing they are), not just repeat them, and you build a clear argument. Top writing = confident, persuasive style, register exactly right for the audience, carefully paragraphed, highly accurate.

**Composition (16 + 24):** top content and structure = an original, engaging piece with a deliberate structure (narrative: tension, credible characters, a satisfying ending; descriptive: a vivid, developed picture without turning into a story). Top style and accuracy = precise and ambitious vocabulary, varied sentences used for effect, almost no errors.

### Key principle

Each long answer is marked for **what** you say (reading/content) and **how** you say it (writing/style). Plan for both.

## Worked examples

**1. Which question would this be, and how many marks?** "Write a summary of the dangers faced by deep-sea divers, according to Text B. Your summary should not be more than 120 words." [15]

Model answer: Paper 1, the summary part of Question 1. 10 marks for selecting relevant ideas (Reading), 5 marks for writing concisely in your own words (Writing).
*Why:* knowing the split tells you that half-copying the text costs you the writing marks, even if every idea is correct.

**2. A student writes 600 words for the Section B composition and makes many careless errors. Which marks suffer?** [2]

Model answer: Style and accuracy (out of 24) suffers because of the errors (W5); the excessive length risks loose structure and loss of control (W2), and leaves less time to check.
*Why:* length is guidance, not a target; examiners reward control, not quantity.

**3. Explain the difference between R1 and R2 using an example.** [2]

Model answer: R1 is explicit meaning – the text says "the village had no electricity". R2 is implicit – the text says "children did homework by candlelight", from which you infer there was no electricity and that life was hard.

## Common mistakes

- Spending too long on Q1 and rushing Q3, which is worth 25 marks.
- Writing a summary in note form or bullet points – it must be continuous prose.
- Going over 120 words in the summary; anything beyond the limit is ignored and concision is lost.
- In Paper 2 Section A, repeating ideas from the texts without evaluating them – this caps your reading mark.
- Choosing a descriptive title and then writing a story (or vice versa).
- Forgetting the form and audience: no greeting in a letter, no sense of an audience in a speech.

5. Name two possible forms for Q3. [2]

7. Draw your own timing plan for Paper 1 that adds up to 120 minutes. [3]
