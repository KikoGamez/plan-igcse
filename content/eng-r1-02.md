# Reading: explicit and implicit comprehension

> **In the exam:** Paper 1, the short-answer parts of Question 1 (on Text A, about 15 marks) and Question 2 (on Text C, about 10 marks). Questions are worth 1–3 marks each. They appear on every paper and are the quickest marks available – if you answer precisely.

## What you need to know

- R1: demonstrate understanding of explicit meanings
- R2: demonstrate understanding of implicit meanings and attitudes
- R3: analyse, evaluate and develop facts, ideas and opinions, using appropriate support from the text
- Answer "in your own words" when the question says so
- Select only the relevant part of the text (R5)

## Notes

### Read the question first, then the text

1. Underline the **command** and the **line/paragraph reference**.
2. Look at the **marks**: 1 mark = one point; 2 marks = two separate points (or a point + explanation); 3 marks = three points.
3. Find the exact lines. The answer is almost always inside the reference given.

### Types of question

| Question wording | What it really asks | How to answer |
|---|---|---|
| "Give two reasons…" / "Identify…" / "What…" | Explicit fact (R1) | Short phrase; brief quotation allowed unless "own words" is asked |
| "Using your own words, explain…" | Understanding, not copying (R1/R2) | Rewrite the key idea with different words |
| "What does the writer suggest / imply…" | Implicit meaning (R2) | State the inference: what is *not* said directly |
| "Explain why…" | Reasons/causes (R3) | Because… + the idea in your own words |
| "Give a word or phrase from paragraph X that suggests…" | Locate language (Q2) | Quote **only** the word/phrase, exactly |

### "Own words" – how to do it

- Change the **key words** (the ones that carry the meaning). You may keep small words like "the", "house", "water".
- Do not just change word order or swap one word.
- Explain meaning, don't translate word-for-word: "reluctant" → "did not want to".
- If a word is technical or has no synonym (e.g. "volcano"), you can keep it.

| Text says | Weak (lifted) | Strong (own words) |
|---|---|---|
| "the crowd grew restless" | the crowd were restless | people waiting became impatient |
| "an unremarkable building" | it was unremarkable | the building was ordinary and easy to ignore |

### Explicit vs implicit

- **Explicit:** "The shop closed in 2019 because rents doubled." → why did it close? Rents doubled.
- **Implicit:** "She checked the lock three times before she could walk away." → she is anxious / worried about security.

To infer: ask *what does this detail tell me about a person's feelings, attitude or situation?* Your answer must be based on the text, not a guess.

### Quote only what you need

When a quotation is allowed, keep it short. A whole sentence copied in the hope the right part is inside it is often not credited, because you haven't shown you know which part answers the question.

## Worked examples

Read Text A (original practice text).

> **Text A – The Lighthouse Keeper's Daughter**
> (1) For eleven years Mara had lived on Skerry Rock, a slab of granite two miles from the mainland, where her father kept the light. Supplies came once a fortnight by boat, weather permitting, which in winter meant hardly ever. (2) She knew the tides better than the alphabet. At the age of seven she could read the colour of the sea and tell her father, with a solemnity that made him smile, whether the boat would come. (3) When the authorities announced that the light would be automated, her father said nothing at dinner. He cut his bread into smaller and smaller pieces and did not eat them. (4) Mara, by contrast, felt something lift inside her. The mainland school, the noise of other children, a shop where you could buy things on a Tuesday – these seemed to her not losses but doors.

**1. Using your own words, explain why supplies rarely reached the island in winter (paragraph 1).** [2]

Model answer: The boat could only travel when conditions at sea were safe (1), and in winter the weather was usually too bad (1).
*Why it scores:* two separate points; "weather permitting" is rephrased as "only when conditions were safe" rather than copied.

**2. What does paragraph 2 suggest about Mara as a child?** [2]

Model answer: She was very observant / had a deep knowledge of the sea (1); she was serious and mature for her age (1).
*Why it scores:* each mark is an **inference**, supported by "read the colour of the sea" and "solemnity". It explains character, not just repeats events.

**3. Explain how the father feels about the news in paragraph 3. Use your own words.** [2]

Model answer: He is upset or distressed (1) – he is too troubled to speak or eat; he plays with his food without eating it, showing he has lost his appetite (1).
*Why it scores:* feeling + the evidence behind it, both in own words. "He cut his bread into smaller pieces" copied would be lifting.

**4. Give a phrase from paragraph 4 which suggests Mara sees the move as an opportunity.** [1]

Model answer: "not losses but doors".
*Why it scores:* exact, short. Copying the whole sentence would not be credited.

## Common mistakes

- Copying a whole sentence instead of selecting the relevant phrase.
- Ignoring "own words" and lifting the key vocabulary.
- Giving one point for a 2-mark question.
- Answering from outside the paragraph referenced.
- Inventing an inference with no basis in the text ("she probably hates her father").
- Writing long paragraphs for 1-mark questions and running out of time later.

## Practice questions

Read Text A2 (original practice text).

> **Text A2 – Night Market**
> (1) By six o'clock the car park behind the station had stopped being a car park. Trestle tables appeared from vans, strings of bulbs were looped between lamp posts, and the smell of frying onions drifted towards the commuters, who slowed down despite themselves. (2) Mr Okafor had run the same stall for twenty years. He sold nothing but pepper soup, in one size, at one price, and he refused all suggestions to expand. "A man who sells everything," he said, "is a man who cooks nothing well." (3) The council had twice tried to close the market, citing noise and litter. Both times a petition with thousands of signatures had arrived on the mayor's desk within a week. (4) Newer stallholders paid for glossy signs and online adverts. Mr Okafor's sign was a piece of cardboard, rewritten each winter when the rain had blurred it. His queue was always the longest.

1. Using your own words, explain how the car park changes in the evening (paragraph 1). [2]
2. What does "slowed down despite themselves" suggest about the commuters? [1]
3. Give **two** things that show Mr Okafor keeps his business simple (paragraph 2). [2]
4. Using your own words, explain what Mr Okafor's saying means. [2]
5. What does paragraph 3 suggest about how local people feel about the market? [2]
6. Give a word or phrase from paragraph 4 that suggests newer stalls spend money on presentation. [1]
7. What does the final sentence imply about Mr Okafor's soup? Explain your answer. [2]

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. It is transformed into a lively market (1): tables are set up and it is decorated with lights (1). Accept any two developed own-word points.
2. They are tempted / attracted by the smell even though they did not intend to stop.
3. He sells only one product (pepper soup); one size and one price; refuses to expand – any two.
4. If you try to sell too many different things (1), you cannot make any of them to a high standard (1).
5. They value it strongly / want to protect it (1); large numbers act quickly to defend it, showing strong community support (1).
6. "glossy signs" (or "paid for glossy signs and online adverts" – the shorter quote is better).
7. It must be excellent / very popular (1), because people queue for it even though his sign is cheap and basic – quality matters more than advertising (1).
