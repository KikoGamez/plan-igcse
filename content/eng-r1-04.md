# Reading: timed summary

> **In the exam:** Paper 1, the summary at the end of Question 1 (Text B): 15 marks (10 Reading + 5 Writing), no more than 120 words, continuous prose. In the real exam you have about 25 minutes for it. Today you practise doing it under that time limit.

## What you need to know

- R1: demonstrate understanding of explicit meanings
- R5: select and use information for specific purposes
- Write in continuous prose, in your own words, within 120 words
- Organise ideas logically and concisely (W2, W3, W5)

## Notes

### Which text to use today

- **First choice:** the summary question from a real Cambridge 0500 Paper 1 that you have **not** done (ask your teacher, or use a recent series such as May/June or Oct/Nov 2024 or 2025). Use the official mark scheme afterwards.
- **If you do not have one:** use the original practice text below (Text B – "Night shifts"), then mark with the model answer and checklist on this page.

### The 25-minute routine

| Minutes | Step |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Read the question. Underline the focus. Is it one part or two? |
| 2–7 | Read Text B. Highlight relevant ideas only. |
| 7–11 | Plan: list ideas in 2–4 words of your own; cross out repeats and examples; group related points. |
| 11–21 | Write the summary from the plan. |
| 21–25 | Count words; replace any lifted key words; check spelling and full stops. |

### How many ideas?

Most Text B passages contain roughly **10–15 creditable ideas**. Including most of them, clearly and concisely, is what puts you in the top level. If your plan has only 4 or 5 ideas, reread – you are probably skipping points hidden inside examples.

### Concise sentence patterns

- **List in one sentence:** "Night workers often suffer from poor sleep, weight gain and heart problems."
- **Cause–effect:** "Because they sleep in the day, they see less of their families."
- **Contrast inside the focus:** "Although better paid, the work isolates them."
- **Avoid:** "Another thing that the text says is that…" (7 wasted words).

### Mark-scheme language in plain words

- **Reading 10 marks:** top = "a wide range of relevant ideas", "clearly understood", "focused on the question". Missing ideas or including irrelevant ones pulls you down.
- **Writing 5 marks:** top = "concise", "well organised", "own words throughout". Lifting and padding pull you down.

## Worked examples

**Text B (original practice text) – Night shifts**

> Around one in seven workers in Europe works at night: nurses, bakers, security guards, delivery drivers and people who clean offices after everyone else has gone home. Without them, hospitals could not function and supermarket shelves would be empty in the morning. Yet scientists are increasingly worried about what night work does to the body. Our internal clock is set by daylight, and those who sleep during the day rarely get as much rest, or rest as deeply, as those who sleep at night. Long-term night workers have higher rates of obesity, partly because the body processes food less efficiently in the small hours, and partly because vending machines are often the only source of food. Several large studies have linked years of night shifts to heart disease. There are social costs, too. "I see my children for twenty minutes a day," says Marta, a cleaner in Madrid. Friends' birthdays and weekend plans become impossible. Some workers say they feel invisible. On the other hand, night shifts usually pay more, and some people genuinely prefer the quiet.

**Question:** Write a summary of the **problems faced by people who work at night**, according to Text B. [15]

**Plan:** sleep less / poorer quality (body clock) · weight gain – slower digestion at night · poor food options · heart disease · little time with family · miss social events · feel unnoticed/undervalued.
*Excluded:* who the workers are, why they are needed, higher pay and quiet (benefits), the quotation (keep only the point).

**Model answer (72 words):**
> People who work at night struggle to sleep well, because the body's natural rhythm is controlled by light, so daytime sleep is shorter and lighter. They are more likely to become overweight, since food is digested less effectively at night and healthy meals are hard to find. Over many years, their risk of heart disease increases. Their working hours also leave little time for family and make social life difficult, and some feel ignored by society.

*Why it scores highly:* seven distinct relevant ideas (wide range, top reading level); generalises the quotation into "little time for family"; own words throughout ("body's natural rhythm" for "internal clock"; "feel ignored" for "invisible"); one focused paragraph with linking words ("also", "since"); well under 120 words.

## Common mistakes

- Running out of time because you wrote a draft and then a neat copy – in the exam, plan in notes and write once.
- Spending too long reading before you know the focus.
- Copying the quotation from a person in the text.
- Including the "other side" (benefits when asked for problems).
- Not counting words – guessing is risky.
- Forgetting to check the final answer for lifted key words.

## Practice questions

**Timed task (25 minutes).** Use a real past-paper summary question if you have one. Otherwise:

**Text B2 (original practice text) – The return of the bicycle courier**

> In the 1990s, bicycle couriers were a familiar sight in big cities, racing documents between offices. Email almost killed the trade. Now couriers are back, and the reason is online shopping. Cargo bikes, some with electric motors, can carry up to 150 kilograms. In crowded centres they are often faster than vans, which get stuck in traffic and waste time looking for somewhere to park. They produce no exhaust fumes, which matters as more cities introduce low-emission zones that charge or ban polluting vehicles. A cargo bike costs a fraction of the price of a van to buy, insure and maintain, and riders do not need a full driving licence, so companies can recruit more easily. Riders themselves are less enthusiastic about the pay, which is often low and depends on the number of deliveries. Shopkeepers say bikes are quieter and less threatening than lorries on narrow streets, and some customers even choose companies because they use them.

1. Write a summary of the **reasons why businesses are using bicycle couriers**, according to Text B2. No more than 120 words. [15]
2. After writing, list the ideas you included and compare with the answer list. Score your reading out of 10. [—]
3. Score your writing out of 5 using the table in the Notes. [—]

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

Creditable ideas: growth of online shopping/home deliveries · bikes can carry heavy loads · faster than vans in busy centres · no time wasted in traffic or parking · no pollution · can enter/avoid charges in low-emission zones · cheaper to buy, insure and maintain · riders need no driving licence, so easier to recruit · quieter and less intimidating in narrow streets (popular with shopkeepers) · some customers prefer companies that use them.

Not relevant: the history in the 1990s and email (background); low pay for riders (a problem for riders, not a business reason – unless you argue "low wages are cheap for companies", which goes beyond the text; leave it out).

Model answer (about 85 words): *The boom in internet shopping has created demand for local deliveries, and cargo bikes can carry heavy loads. In busy city centres they are frequently quicker than vans, as they avoid traffic jams and parking problems. Because they cause no pollution, they can operate freely where cities penalise dirty vehicles. They are much cheaper to purchase and run than vans, and firms find riders easier to hire since no driving licence is needed. Bikes are also quieter and less intimidating, and some customers actively prefer firms that use them.*

Rough guide: 8–10 ideas well expressed → 9–10 reading marks; 6–7 → 7–8; 4–5 → 5–6.
