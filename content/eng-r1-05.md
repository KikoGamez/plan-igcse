# Writer's effect: technique

> **In the exam:** Paper 1, the last part of Question 2, on Text C. 15 marks, one level-marked answer of about 200–300 words, roughly 25 minutes. You are asked to explain how the writer uses language to convey meaning and create effect in **two named paragraphs**, choosing **three examples from each**, including imagery. On every paper.

## What you need to know

- R4: demonstrate understanding of how writers achieve effects and influence readers
- Select powerful words and phrases (including imagery) from the given paragraphs
- Explain **meaning** (what the words say in context) and **effect** (what they make the reader see, feel or understand)
- Show how the choices work together to create an overall impression

## Notes

### The task

Typical wording: *"Re-read paragraphs 3 and 6. Explain how the writer uses language to convey meaning and to create effect in these paragraphs. Choose three examples of words or phrases from each paragraph to support your answer. Your choices should include the use of imagery."*

### Choosing words

- Choose **short** quotations: 1–5 words. The examiner wants to see which words you think are powerful.
- Prefer **images** (metaphors, similes, personification), **strong verbs**, **unusual adjectives**, and **sound** effects.
- Choose from **each** paragraph – using only one paragraph limits your mark.
- Choose words that link to the paragraph's main idea (e.g. danger, beauty, decay).

### The three-step explanation (for each choice)

1. **Quote** the words.
2. **Meaning in context:** what is literally happening or being said? ("The wind is very strong.")
3. **Effect:** what does the *word choice* add? What does it make us picture, feel or associate? Unpack the connotations of individual words.

> **Example:** "the wind *clawed* at the tent"
> Meaning: the wind pulled hard at the tent.
> Effect: "clawed" suggests a wild animal attacking with sharp claws, so the wind seems aggressive and deliberate, as if it wants to tear the tent apart; the reader feels the campers are vulnerable and under attack.

### From "basic" to "top level"

| Level (plain words) | What the student does |
|---|---|
| Top | Precise choices; meanings clear; effects explained in detail, word by word; images explored; shows how choices build an **overall impression**; concise |
| Middle | Sensible choices; meanings mostly clear; effects general ("it is effective", "creates a picture") |
| Low | Long quotations; mostly repeats the text; names devices without explaining |


### Useful phrases

- "The word … has connotations of…, suggesting…"
- "The image of … makes the reader picture…"
- "Literally this means…, but the choice of … implies…"
- "Together with …, this builds an impression of…"

### Banned phrases (they earn nothing alone)

"makes the reader want to read on", "creates a vivid image", "this is a metaphor" (with no explanation), "it is very descriptive".

### Structure (about 250 words)

- **Paragraph 1:** three choices from the first named paragraph, each explained, ending with one sentence on the overall effect of the paragraph.
- **Paragraph 2:** the same for the second paragraph.
- No introduction or conclusion needed.

## Worked examples

**Text C extract (original practice text)**

> (3) The market at dawn was a beast waking up. Shutters rattled upwards like the eyelids of something enormous, and porters shouldered sacks of rice through lanes no wider than a corridor. Steam bled from the noodle stalls into the cold air.
> (6) By evening the beast had eaten its fill. Litter drifted in exhausted circles, a single bulb buzzed over an empty fish counter, and the old woman who sold herbs folded her cloth with the care of someone burying a flag.

**Question:** Explain how the writer uses language to convey meaning and to create effect in paragraphs 3 and 6. Choose three examples from each. [15]

**Model answer (extract, paragraph 3):**
> "A beast waking up" presents the market as a huge living animal, meaning that it slowly comes to life in the morning. The image suggests energy and power, but also slight danger, as if the market is wild and impossible to control. This is developed by "shutters rattled upwards like the eyelids of something enormous": literally, stallholders are opening their shop fronts, but the simile makes the metal shutters seem like the eyes of the creature opening, and "rattled" adds a harsh, noisy sound to the scene. "Steam bled" means steam escaped from the stalls, but "bled" suggests something leaking out of a wound, as if the market's life is pouring out into the cold; it adds warmth and colour against the chill. Together, the images create an impression of a vast, noisy creature stirring into busy life.

*Why it scores highly:* short quotations; each one has meaning **and** a word-level effect ("rattled", "bled" unpacked); images linked to each other (the beast metaphor carried through); a summing-up sentence showing the overall impression.

**Model answer (paragraph 6, briefer):**
> "The beast had eaten its fill" continues the animal image: the market is now full and sleepy, suggesting the trading is over and the energy is spent. "Litter drifted in exhausted circles" means rubbish is blowing about, but "exhausted" transfers human tiredness to the litter, so even the rubbish seems worn out. The simile "with the care of someone burying a flag" shows the old woman folds her cloth slowly and carefully; it suggests a solemn ceremony, as if each day's ending is a small defeat or loss, which adds sadness. The paragraph contrasts with the energy of dawn to create a mood of weariness.

## Common mistakes

- Quoting whole sentences – the examiner cannot tell which words you are analysing.
- Feature-spotting ("this is personification") with no explanation of effect.
- Explaining only meaning, never effect (or the reverse).
- Writing about only one paragraph, or choosing words outside the named paragraphs.
- General comments ("it makes it interesting") that could apply to any text.
- Writing far too much on one choice and skipping others.

## Practice questions

Use this extract (original practice text):

> (2) The flood came in the night. It slid under doors without knocking, swallowed the bottom step, then the second, and stood in the kitchen as if it had paid rent there all its life. Chairs floated, bewildered, bumping against the cupboards.
> (5) Morning revealed a town that had been rinsed of colour. Mud lay over everything like a grey blanket, gardens were drowned photographs of themselves, and people stood on their doorsteps with the stunned silence of passengers after a crash.

1. For "slid under doors without knocking", explain meaning and effect. [3]
2. Explain the effect of "swallowed". [2]
3. Explain the effect of "as if it had paid rent there all its life". [3]
4. Explain the effect of "bewildered". [2]
5. Choose three examples from paragraph 5 and explain meaning and effect of each. [6]
6. In one sentence for each, state the overall impression of paragraph 2 and paragraph 5. [2]

1. Meaning: the water entered houses quietly under the doors. Effect: personification – like an uninvited intruder who doesn't ask permission; "slid" suggests silent, sly, snake-like movement, making the flood seem sinister and impossible to stop.
2. The water covers the step completely; "swallowed" makes the flood a hungry creature consuming the house piece by piece, suggesting greed and unstoppable power.
3. The water is now deep inside the kitchen; the image of a tenant suggests it has taken over and behaves as though it owns the home, with an ironic, almost casual confidence that emphasises the family's helplessness.
