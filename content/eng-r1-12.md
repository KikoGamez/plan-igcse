# Timed composition and accuracy

> **In the exam:** Paper 2, Section B: one composition (descriptive or narrative) from four titles, 350–450 words, 40 marks (16 content and structure + 24 style and accuracy). Accuracy (W5) also counts in every other written answer: the summary, Q3 in Paper 1 and directed writing. Errors that block meaning, or frequent basic errors, keep you out of the top levels.

## What you need to know

- W5: make accurate use of spelling, punctuation and grammar
- W3: use a range of vocabulary and sentence structures appropriate to context
- W2: organise and structure ideas for deliberate effect
- Write one complete composition in about 1 hour

## Notes

### Timed composition routine (60 minutes)

| Minutes | Step |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Read all four titles. Choose the one you can **plan** fastest, not the one that sounds most exciting. Check: descriptive or narrative? |
| 5–12 | Plan: structure (5 paragraphs), key images or turning point, opening line, final line |
| 12–50 | Write about 400 words. Keep an eye on paragraphs and tense. |
| 50–60 | Proofread with the checklist below. Neat corrections are fine. |

### The proofreading checklist (last 10 minutes)

1. **Full stops:** any comma splices? (two sentences joined by a comma)
2. **Capital letters:** sentence starts, names, "I".
3. **Apostrophes:** its/it's, possession vs plural.
4. **Homophones:** their/there/they're, your/you're, where/were/wear, then/than, affect/effect, practice (noun)/practise (verb – British).
5. **Tense:** consistent past or present.
6. **Dialogue:** new speaker, new line; punctuation inside speech marks.
7. **Paragraphs:** clear breaks for new time, place, speaker or idea.

### Punctuation review

| Mark | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Full stop | Ends a complete sentence | The door was open. Nobody was inside. |
| Comma splice ✗ | Never join two full sentences with only a comma | ✗ The door was open, nobody was inside. |
| Comma | After a fronted clause; around extra information; in lists | When the bell rang, everyone stood. My brother, who hates noise, left early. |
| Semicolon | Joins two closely linked complete sentences; separates long list items | The door was open; nobody was inside. |
| Colon | Introduces an explanation, list or example after a complete clause | She had one aim: to win. |
| Dash | Adds an interruption or dramatic afterthought (use sparingly) | He opened the box – it was empty. |
| Apostrophe (omission) | Shows missing letters | it's = it is; don't; they're |
| Apostrophe (possession) | Singular: 's; plural ending in s: s' | the girl's bag (one girl); the girls' bags (several) |
| Its | Possessive – no apostrophe | The dog wagged its tail. |
| Question mark | Direct questions only | "Are you coming?" / ✗ I wondered if he was coming? |
| Speech marks | Speech + its punctuation inside | "Stop," she said. "It's dangerous." |

### Sentence variety for accuracy and effect

- **Simple:** The lights went out. (impact)
- **Compound:** The lights went out, and the crowd fell silent. (comma + and/but/so is correct)
- **Complex:** As the lights went out, the crowd, which had been roaring, fell silent.
- **Minor sentence** (deliberate, for effect): Darkness.
- Vary openings: with a verb ("Turning, she…"), an adverb ("Slowly, …"), a prepositional phrase ("Beneath the bridge, …").

### Common spelling traps

necessary, separate, definitely, environment, beginning, government, embarrass, rhythm, disappear, occasionally, believe, receive, achieve, until, tomorrow, accommodation, surprise, conscience, conscious, weird.

## Worked examples

**1. Correct the punctuation.** [4]
*its getting late said Maria we should go the bus leaves at ten*

Model answer:
*"It's getting late," said Maria. "We should go. The bus leaves at ten."*
(it's = it is; speech marks; comma inside before the tag; new sentence for "We should go".)

**2. Fix the comma splices in two different ways.** [2]
*The storm had passed, the streets were flooded.*

Model answer: *The storm had passed; the streets were flooded.* / *Although the storm had passed, the streets were flooded.*

**3. A timed descriptive composition – model opening and why it works.**
Title: "Describe a town square on the day of a festival."

> *At six in the morning the square is still grey and sleepy. A man in an orange vest sweeps last night's confetti into the gutter, where it lies like a crushed rainbow. By ten, the stalls have bloomed: striped awnings, pyramids of oranges, a stall selling nothing but bells. Children weave between adults' legs. The smell of churros – hot oil and sugar – drifts over everything, and somewhere a brass band is tuning up, each instrument arguing with the others.*

*Why it scores highly:* time-shift structure is clear from the start; precise detail ("a stall selling nothing but bells"); fresh imagery tied to the festival ("crushed rainbow", instruments "arguing"); correct apostrophes ("last night's", "adults' legs" – plural possessive); varied sentence openings; consistent present tense; dash used correctly for added information.

## Common mistakes

- Comma splices – the most frequently reported punctuation error.
- Missing or misused apostrophes (its/it's; "the dog's" for plurals).
- No paragraphs, or a new paragraph for every sentence.
- Tense switching mid-paragraph.
- Ambitious vocabulary misused ("the sky was very amicable").
- No time left to proofread.
- Lower-case "i" and missing capital letters at sentence starts.

## Practice questions

1. Correct: *the students bags were left in there classroom, nobody noticed* [3]
2. Add a semicolon or colon correctly: *There was only one problem nobody had the key.* [1]
3. Correct the speech: *"where were you" he asked angrily. "out" I said* [3]
4. Choose the right word: (a) The noise had a strange (affect/effect) on the dog. (b) She is taller (then/than) me. (c) I need to (practice/practise) my speech. [3]
5. Rewrite as one complex sentence: *The train stopped. Passengers looked up. They were confused.* [2]
6. **Timed composition (40 minutes today; 1 hour in the exam):** Choose one title and write 350–450 words. (a) Describe a hospital waiting room late at night. (b) Write a story that includes the words: "I should never have opened it." [40]
