# Past paper · Paper 2 timed (1/2)

> **Goal:** Complete Section A (directed writing) of a full 0500 Paper 2 in one hour under exam conditions.

## Before you start

- **Paper:** a Cambridge IGCSE English First Language 0500 Paper 2 (Directed Writing and Composition) from a recent series – May/June or Oct/Nov 2024 or 2025 – that you have **not** done. It comes with a reading booklet insert containing the two texts for Section A. Ask your teacher if you do not have one.
- **Mark scheme:** keep it aside until session 5.
- **Equipment:** black pen, highlighter, timer, lined paper. No dictionary.
- Use the same paper for session 4 (Section B), so keep it safe.

## Exam format reminder

- **Paper 2:** 2 hours, 80 marks, 50% of the qualification.
- **Section A – Directed writing (compulsory, 40 marks):** read two texts on one topic; write a letter, article or speech that evaluates the ideas in both texts and gives your own view. 15 marks for Reading, 25 for Writing. 250–350 words.
- **Section B – Composition (40 marks):** one of four titles (two descriptive, two narrative). 16 marks content and structure, 24 marks style and accuracy. 350–450 words.
- Suggested timing: about 1 hour per section.

## How to do it

| Time | Task |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | Read the task: underline **form**, **audience**, **purpose**, the two bullet points. |
| 0:03–0:13 | Read both texts. Annotate every idea: + strong, – weak, ? bias/assumption, ↔ links between texts. |
| 0:13–0:20 | Plan: overall position; 3–4 body paragraphs; for each, which ideas you will evaluate and what your judgement is; opening and closing lines. |
| 0:20–0:52 | Write about 300–350 words. |
| 0:52–0:60 | Proofread: both texts used? evaluation in each paragraph? register? accuracy? |

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Evaluate, don't summarise.** For each idea ask: how good is the evidence? Who is speaking, and could they be biased? Is it exaggerated? Does the other text challenge it? Top-level reading answers judge ideas and combine them into a developed argument.
- **Use both texts** – using only one restricts your reading mark.
- **Own words.** Short reference to a key phrase is fine; copying sentences is not.
- **Clear viewpoint** from the opening paragraph, maintained to the end; a balanced view is fine if it is a *reasoned* conclusion.
- **Register:** formal letter to an official ("Dear…", "Yours sincerely/faithfully"); lively but controlled article (headline); speech with direct address, rhetorical questions and a strong close.
- **Structure for effect:** topic sentences; connectives that show argument (however, by contrast, consequently, admittedly).
- **Accuracy** is part of the 25 writing marks – keep 8 minutes to check.

## Checklist while marking

(Full marking is in session 5. Today, after time:)

- Highlight in one colour every sentence that **evaluates**; in another, every sentence that only **reports**. Aim for more evaluation than reporting.
- Check every body paragraph uses at least one idea from a text.
- Count words (roughly): within 250–350?
- Error log: note whether problems were in reading (evaluation, coverage) or writing (register, structure, accuracy).

## Quick-fire revision

- Section A: 40 marks = 15 R + 25 W; 250–350 words; two texts; letter/article/speech.
- Evaluation words: convincing, persuasive, one-sided, anecdotal, exaggerated, reliable, biased, limited, overlooks, assumes.
- Structure: opening position → evaluate side 1 → evaluate side 2 → own view linking both → conclusion/appeal.
- Comma splices and apostrophes are the most common accuracy errors.
