# Mark Paper 1 and Paper 2

> **Goal:** Mark the Paper 1 and Paper 2 you completed in sessions 1–4 with the official mark schemes, compare with model answers, and turn your mistakes into a short action list for the mock.

## Before you start

- **Your scripts:** the full Paper 1 (sessions 1–2) and full Paper 2 (sessions 3–4).
- **Mark schemes:** the official Cambridge 0500 mark schemes for the **same series** (your teacher can provide them). If available, also the **examiner report** for that series and any **example candidate responses** – these show real answers with examiner comments, which help you judge levels.
- **Model answers:** the annotated models in your Round 1 pages (summary, writer's effect, Q3, directed writing, composition) for comparison.
- **Equipment:** red/green pens, highlighters, error log, calculator for totals.

## How to do it

| Time | Task |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | **Paper 1 short answers** (Q1, Q2 short parts): mark point by point. Be strict on "own words". |
| 0:15–0:25 | **Summary:** tick each idea that matches the mark scheme's list; count them; read the reading and writing level descriptors and choose a mark for each. |
| 0:25–0:35 | **Writer's effect and Q3:** read the level descriptors; find the level that fits **best overall**, then decide whether you are at the top or bottom of it. Compare with example responses if you have them. |
| 0:35–0:50 | **Paper 2:** mark Section A (reading level + writing level) and Section B (content and structure + style and accuracy) the same way. |
| 0:50–0:60 | Add up totals; fill in the error log; write a 3-point action list for the mock. |

## Exam technique for this paper

How level-marked answers are judged (in plain words):
- **"Best fit":** examiners do not add up features; they choose the description that matches the answer as a whole. A strong answer with a few slips still reaches the top level.
- **Reading marks** depend on range (how many relevant ideas), understanding (explicit and implicit) and, for Q3 and directed writing, **development** and **evaluation**.
- **Writing marks** depend on audience and register, structure, vocabulary and sentence variety, and accuracy.
- **Summary writing marks** fall quickly with lifting and wordiness.
- **Writer's effect** top levels need effects explained precisely at word level, imagery explored, and an overall impression – not just meanings.
- Examiners' reports for recent series repeatedly mention: copying from the text, summarising rather than evaluating in directed writing, narrative drift in descriptive compositions, and comma splices.

## Checklist while marking

- [ ] Short answers: one mark per creditable idea; own-words rule applied.
- [ ] Summary: ideas counted; lifted phrases circled; word count ≤120.
- [ ] Writer's effect: both paragraphs; meaning **and** effect for each choice; overall impression.
- [ ] Q3: all bullets; details used and developed; voice and form right.
- [ ] Directed writing: both texts; evaluation not reporting; clear viewpoint; register.
- [ ] Composition: right type (descriptive vs narrative); structure; error count per paragraph.
- [ ] Timing: which question overran?
- **Error log format:** Question | Marks lost | Cause (knowledge / technique / timing / accuracy) | Fix for next time.
- Turn totals into a rough percentage and compare with your target grade boundaries (your teacher can give you the boundaries for that series – they change each session).

## Quick-fire revision

- Paper 1 timing: Q1 ≈ 45 · Q2 ≈ 40 · Q3 ≈ 35 min. Paper 2: ≈ 1 h per section.
- Summary ≤120 words, continuous prose, own words, 10–15 ideas in the text.
- Writer's effect: 3 + 3 short choices with imagery; meaning → effect → overall.
- Q3 and directed writing: 250–350 words; composition: 350–450 words.
- Evaluate = judge evidence, bias, exaggeration, limits.
- Accuracy: full stops not comma splices; its/it's; their/there/they're; dialogue punctuation.
