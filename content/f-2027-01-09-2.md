## Biology 0610: must-know definitions

Learn these with the key words in **bold** — mark schemes look for them.

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Characteristics of living organisms | movement, respiration, sensitivity, growth, reproduction, excretion, nutrition |
| Species | a group of organisms that can **reproduce to produce fertile offspring** |
| Diffusion | the **net movement** of particles from a region of **higher concentration** to a region of **lower concentration** (down a concentration gradient), as a result of their **random movement** |
| Osmosis | the net movement of **water molecules** from a region of **higher water potential** (dilute solution) to a region of **lower water potential** (concentrated solution), through a **partially permeable membrane** |
| Active transport | the movement of particles through a cell membrane from a region of **lower concentration to higher concentration** (against a concentration gradient), using **energy from respiration** |
| Catalyst | a substance that **increases the rate** of a chemical reaction and is **not changed** by the reaction |
| Enzymes | **proteins** that function as **biological catalysts** |
| Photosynthesis | the process by which plants **synthesise carbohydrates** from raw materials using **energy from light** |
| Respiration | the chemical reactions in cells that **break down nutrient molecules and release energy** for metabolism |
| Aerobic respiration | uses oxygen; releases a relatively **large** amount of energy |
| Anaerobic respiration | **without oxygen**; releases much **less energy per glucose** molecule |
| Ingestion | taking substances (food and drink) into the body through the mouth |
| Digestion | the **breakdown of large, insoluble** molecules into **small, soluble** molecules (mechanical and chemical) |
| Absorption | movement of nutrients from the intestines into the blood |
| Assimilation | uptake and use of nutrients by cells |
| Egestion | passing out of food that has not been digested or absorbed, as faeces, through the anus |
| Transpiration | loss of **water vapour** from leaves (evaporation from mesophyll cell surfaces, then diffusion through the **stomata**) |
| Translocation | movement of **sucrose and amino acids** in the **phloem** from **sources** to **sinks** |
| Excretion | removal of **waste products of metabolism** and substances in excess of requirements |
| Homeostasis | maintenance of a **constant internal environment** |
| Hormone | a chemical substance, **produced by a gland** and **carried by the blood**, which alters the activity of one or more specific **target organs** |
| Synapse | a junction between two neurones |
| Gene | a length of **DNA** that **codes for a protein** |
| Allele | an alternative form of a gene |
| Genotype / phenotype | genetic make-up (alleles present) / observable features |
| Homozygous / heterozygous | two identical alleles of a gene / two different alleles of a gene |
| Dominant / recessive | allele expressed if present / allele only expressed when there is no dominant allele |
| Mitosis | nuclear division giving rise to **genetically identical** cells |
| Meiosis | reduction division in which the chromosome number is **halved from diploid to haploid**, producing **genetically different** cells |
| Haploid / diploid nucleus | one set of chromosomes / two sets of chromosomes |
| Mutation | genetic change |
| Fertilisation | **fusion of the nuclei** of gametes |
| Pollination | transfer of **pollen grains** from the **anther** to the **stigma** |
| Gravitropism / phototropism | a response in which part of a plant grows towards or away from **gravity** / from the direction **light** comes from |
| Adaptive feature | an inherited feature that helps an organism to survive and reproduce in its environment |
| Population | a group of organisms of **one species**, living in the **same area at the same time** |
| Community | all the populations of different species in an ecosystem |
| Ecosystem | a unit containing the **community** of organisms and their **environment**, interacting together |
| Food chain | shows the **transfer of energy** from one organism to the next, beginning with a **producer** |
| Trophic level | the position of an organism in a food chain or food web |

**Equations to write from memory:**
- Photosynthesis: carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen (light, chlorophyll); 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
- Aerobic respiration: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water; C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O
- Anaerobic, yeast: glucose → alcohol (ethanol) + carbon dioxide; C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂
- Anaerobic, muscles: glucose → lactic acid

**Food tests:** starch — iodine solution, orange-brown → blue-black · reducing sugars — Benedict's solution, heat, blue → green/yellow/orange/brick red · protein — biuret, blue → purple · fats and oils — ethanol emulsion test, cloudy white emulsion · vitamin C — DCPIP, blue → colourless.

## Chemistry 0620: moles

| What | Formula | Units |
|---|---|---|
| Amount from mass | n = m ÷ M (M = Mr in g/mol) | mol |
| Concentration | c = n ÷ V | mol/dm³ (V in **dm³**: cm³ ÷ 1000) |
| Concentration by mass | mass ÷ volume | g/dm³ |
| Gas volume at r.t.p. | V = n × 24 | dm³ (24 000 cm³ per mol) |
| Avogadro constant | 6.02 × 10²³ particles per mole | |
| Percentage yield | actual yield ÷ theoretical yield × 100 | % |
| Percentage purity | mass of pure product ÷ mass of impure sample × 100 | % |
| Percentage by mass of element | (number of atoms × Ar) ÷ Mr × 100 | % |
| Empirical formula | % ÷ Ar → divide by smallest → whole-number ratio | |

**The mole method in 3 steps:** (1) moles of what you know; (2) use the equation's ratio; (3) convert to what is asked (mass, volume, concentration).

### Quick self-test (answers below)
1. 4.0 g of NaOH is dissolved to make 250 cm³ of solution. Concentration in mol/dm³? (Mr NaOH = 40)
2. CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂. What volume of CO₂ at r.t.p. comes from 10.0 g of CaCO₃? (Mr = 100)
3. 25.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ NaOH is neutralised by 20.0 cm³ of HCl. Concentration of HCl?
4. A hydrocarbon is 85.7% carbon and 14.3% hydrogen by mass. Empirical formula?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. n = 4.0 ÷ 40 = 0.100 mol; c = 0.100 ÷ 0.250 = **0.400 mol/dm³**
2. n = 10.0 ÷ 100 = 0.100 mol CaCO₃ → 0.100 mol CO₂ (1 : 1); V = 0.100 × 24 = **2.40 dm³**
3. n(NaOH) = 0.100 × 0.0250 = 0.002 50 mol; NaOH + HCl 1 : 1 → n(HCl) = 0.002 50; c = 0.002 50 ÷ 0.0200 = **0.125 mol/dm³**
4. C: 85.7 ÷ 12 = 7.14; H: 14.3 ÷ 1 = 14.3; ratio 1 : 2 → **CH₂**

</details>

## Chemistry 0620: identification tests

### Cations — aqueous sodium hydroxide and aqueous ammonia

| Ion | NaOH(aq) | NH₃(aq) |
|---|---|---|
| Al³⁺ | white ppt, **soluble in excess** → colourless solution | white ppt, insoluble in excess |
| NH₄⁺ | ammonia gas produced on warming | — |
| Ca²⁺ | white ppt, insoluble in excess | no ppt or very slight white ppt |
| Cr³⁺ | green ppt, soluble in excess | green ppt, insoluble in excess |
| Cu²⁺ | light blue ppt, insoluble in excess | light blue ppt, **soluble in excess → dark blue solution** |
| Fe²⁺ | green ppt, insoluble in excess | green ppt, insoluble in excess |
| Fe³⁺ | red-brown ppt, insoluble in excess | red-brown ppt, insoluble in excess |
| Zn²⁺ | white ppt, soluble in excess → colourless solution | white ppt, soluble in excess → colourless solution |

**Flame tests:** Li⁺ red · Na⁺ yellow · K⁺ lilac · Ca²⁺ orange-red · Ba²⁺ light green · Cu²⁺ blue-green

### Anions

| Ion | Test | Result |
|---|---|---|
| CO₃²⁻ | add dilute acid; bubble gas through limewater | effervescence; CO₂ turns limewater milky |
| Cl⁻ | acidify with dilute nitric acid, add aqueous silver nitrate | white ppt |
| Br⁻ | as above | cream ppt |
| I⁻ | as above | yellow ppt |
| NO₃⁻ | add aqueous sodium hydroxide and aluminium foil, warm | ammonia produced (damp red litmus → blue) |
| SO₄²⁻ | acidify with dilute nitric acid, add aqueous barium nitrate | white ppt |
| SO₃²⁻ | add acidified aqueous potassium manganate(VII) | purple → colourless |

### Gases

| Gas | Test and result |
|---|---|
| NH₃ | turns damp red litmus paper blue |
| CO₂ | turns limewater milky |
| Cl₂ | bleaches damp litmus paper |
| H₂ | 'pops' with a lighted splint |
| O₂ | relights a glowing splint |
| SO₂ | turns acidified aqueous potassium manganate(VII) from purple to colourless |

### Other tests
- **Water:** anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride blue → pink; anhydrous copper(II) sulfate white → blue. **Pure** water: melts at 0 °C, boils at 100 °C.
- **Purity:** a pure substance has a sharp melting point and boiling point; impurities lower the melting point and raise the boiling point (and melt over a range).
- **Unsaturation (C=C):** aqueous bromine orange → colourless.
- **Chromatography:** Rf = distance moved by substance ÷ distance moved by solvent front.

**Common error:** "precipitate" alone is not enough — always give the **colour** and whether it is **soluble or insoluble in excess**. "Limewater goes cloudy/milky" — never "turns white" alone.
