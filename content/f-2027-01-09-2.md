## Biology 0654: must-know definitions

Learn these with the key words in **bold** — mark schemes look for them. (S) = Supplement (Extended).

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Characteristics of living organisms | movement, respiration, sensitivity, growth, reproduction, excretion, nutrition |
| Growth | a **permanent** increase in size and **dry mass** |
| Species | a group of organisms that can **reproduce to produce fertile offspring** |
| Diffusion | the **net movement** of particles from a region of their **higher concentration** to a region of their **lower concentration** (down a concentration gradient), as a result of their **random movement** |
| Osmosis (S) | the net movement of **water molecules** from a region of **higher water potential** (dilute solution) to a region of **lower water potential** (concentrated solution), through a **partially permeable membrane** |
| Active transport | the movement of particles through a cell membrane from a region of **lower concentration to higher concentration** (against a concentration gradient), using **energy from respiration** |
| Catalyst | a substance that **increases the rate** of a reaction and is **unchanged** at the end of the reaction |
| Enzymes | **proteins** that are involved in all metabolic reactions, where they function as **biological catalysts** |
| Photosynthesis | the process by which plants **synthesise carbohydrates** from raw materials using **energy from light** |
| Respiration | the chemical reactions in cells that **break down nutrient molecules and release energy** for metabolism |
| Aerobic respiration | the chemical reactions in cells that **use oxygen** to break down nutrient molecules to release energy |
| Anaerobic respiration (S) | the chemical reactions in cells that break down nutrient molecules to release energy **without using oxygen** |
| Ingestion | the taking of substances (e.g. food and drink) into the body |
| Digestion | the breakdown of food |
| Physical / chemical digestion | breakdown of food into smaller pieces **without chemical change** to the food molecules / breakdown of **large insoluble** molecules into **small soluble** molecules |
| Absorption | movement of nutrients from the intestines into the blood |
| Assimilation | uptake and use of nutrients by cells |
| Egestion | removal of **undigested food** from the body as faeces |
| Transpiration | loss of **water vapour** from leaves |
| Translocation (S) | movement of **sucrose and amino acids** in the **phloem** from **sources** to **sinks** |
| Excretion | removal of **waste products of metabolism** and substances in excess of requirements |
| Pathogen / transmissible disease | a disease-causing organism / a disease in which the pathogen can be **passed from one host to another** |
| Active immunity (S) | defence against a pathogen by **antibody production in the body** |
| Homeostasis (S) | maintenance of a **constant internal environment** |
| Hormone | a chemical substance, **produced by a gland** and **carried by the blood**, which alters the activity of one or more specific **target organs** |
| Reflex action | a means of **automatically and rapidly** integrating and coordinating stimuli with the responses of **effectors** (muscles and glands) |
| Drug | any substance taken into the body that **modifies or affects chemical reactions** in the body |
| Gene | a length of **DNA** that **codes for a protein** |
| Allele | an alternative form of a gene |
| Genotype / phenotype | genetic make-up (alleles present) / observable features |
| Homozygous / heterozygous | two identical alleles of a gene / two different alleles of a gene |
| Dominant / recessive | allele expressed if present / allele only expressed when there is no dominant allele |
| Mitosis (S) | nuclear division giving rise to **genetically identical** cells |
| Meiosis (S) | reduction division in which the chromosome number is **halved from diploid to haploid**, resulting in **genetically different** cells |
| Haploid / diploid nucleus (S) | one set of chromosomes / two sets of chromosomes |
| Mutation | genetic change |
| Fertilisation (humans) | **fusion of the nuclei** of a male gamete and a female gamete |
| Pollination | transfer of **pollen grains** from an **anther** to a **stigma** |
| Food chain | shows the **transfer of energy** from one organism to the next, beginning with a **producer** |
| Trophic level (S) | the position of an organism in a food chain and food web |
| Ecosystem | a unit containing the **community** of organisms and their **environment**, interacting together |
| Biodiversity | the number of different species that live in an area |

**Equations to write from memory:**
- Photosynthesis: carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen (light, chlorophyll); (S) 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
- Aerobic respiration: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water; (S) C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O
- Anaerobic, muscles (S): glucose → lactic acid (builds up and causes an oxygen debt)

**Food tests:** starch — iodine solution, orange-brown → blue-black · reducing sugars — Benedict's solution, heat, blue → green/yellow/orange/brick red · protein — biuret, blue → purple · fats and oils — ethanol emulsion test, cloudy white emulsion.

## Chemistry 0654: moles

| What | Formula | Units |
|---|---|---|
| Amount from mass (S) | n = m ÷ M (M = Mr in g/mol) | mol |
| Concentration | mass of solute ÷ volume of solution | g/dm³ (V in **dm³**: cm³ ÷ 1000) |
| Gas volume at r.t.p. (S) | V = n × 24 | dm³ (24 000 cm³ per mol) |
| Avogadro constant (S) | 6.02 × 10²³ particles per mole | |
| Limiting reactant (S) | compare moles **adjusted for the equation's ratio**; the one that runs out first decides the product | |

**The mole method in 3 steps:** (1) moles of what you know; (2) use the equation's ratio; (3) convert to what is asked (mass or gas volume).

### Quick self-test (answers below)
1. 4.0 g of NaOH is dissolved to make 250 cm³ of solution. Concentration in g/dm³? How many moles of NaOH is that? (Mr NaOH = 40)
2. CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂. What volume of CO₂ at r.t.p. comes from 10.0 g of CaCO₃? (Mr = 100)
3. Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂. 1.20 g of Mg is added to 1.46 g of HCl. Which is limiting? Volume of H₂ in cm³ at r.t.p.?
4. How many molecules are in 0.88 g of CO₂? (Mr = 44)

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. c = 4.0 ÷ 0.250 = **16 g/dm³**; n = 4.0 ÷ 40 = **0.10 mol**
2. n = 10.0 ÷ 100 = 0.100 mol CaCO₃ → 0.100 mol CO₂ (1 : 1); V = 0.100 × 24 = **2.40 dm³**
3. n(Mg) = 1.20 ÷ 24 = 0.0500 mol needs 0.100 mol HCl; n(HCl) = 1.46 ÷ 36.5 = 0.0400 mol → **HCl is limiting**; n(H₂) = 0.0200 mol; V = **480 cm³**
4. n = 0.88 ÷ 44 = 0.0200 mol; 0.0200 × 6.02 × 10²³ = **1.20 × 10²² molecules**

</details>

## Chemistry 0654: identification tests

### Cations — aqueous sodium hydroxide and aqueous ammonia

| Ion | NaOH(aq) | NH₃(aq) |
|---|---|---|
| NH₄⁺ | ammonia gas produced on warming | — |
| Ca²⁺ | white ppt, insoluble in excess | no ppt or very slight white ppt |
| Cu²⁺ | light blue ppt, insoluble in excess | light blue ppt, **soluble in excess → dark blue solution** |
| Fe²⁺ | green ppt, insoluble in excess (turns brown near the surface on standing) | green ppt, insoluble in excess |
| Fe³⁺ | red-brown ppt, insoluble in excess | red-brown ppt, insoluble in excess |
| Zn²⁺ | white ppt, soluble in excess → colourless solution | white ppt, soluble in excess → colourless solution |

**Flame tests:** Li⁺ red · Na⁺ yellow · K⁺ lilac · Cu²⁺ blue-green

### Anions

| Ion | Test | Result |
|---|---|---|
| CO₃²⁻ | add dilute acid; bubble gas through limewater | effervescence; CO₂ turns limewater milky |
| Cl⁻ | acidify with dilute nitric acid, add aqueous silver nitrate | white ppt |
| Br⁻ | as above | cream ppt |
| I⁻ | as above | yellow ppt |
| NO₃⁻ | add aqueous sodium hydroxide and aluminium foil, warm | ammonia produced (damp red litmus → blue) |
| SO₄²⁻ | acidify with dilute nitric acid, add aqueous barium nitrate | white ppt |

### Gases

| Gas | Test and result |
|---|---|
| NH₃ | turns damp red litmus paper blue |
| CO₂ | turns limewater milky |
| Cl₂ | bleaches damp litmus paper |
| H₂ | 'pops' with a lighted splint |
| O₂ | relights a glowing splint |

### Other tests
- **Water:** anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride blue → pink; anhydrous copper(II) sulfate white → blue. **Pure** water: melts at 0 °C, boils at 100 °C.
- **Purity:** a pure substance has a sharp melting point and boiling point; an impure substance melts over a range of temperatures, lower than the pure substance.
- **Unsaturation (C=C):** aqueous bromine orange → colourless.
- **Chromatography (S):** Rf = distance travelled by substance ÷ distance travelled by solvent.

**Common error:** "precipitate" alone is not enough — always give the **colour** and whether it is **soluble or insoluble in excess**. "Limewater goes cloudy/milky" — never "turns white" alone.
