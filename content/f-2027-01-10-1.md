# English and Spanish: writing technique

> **Goal:** have a ready-made structure for each of the three writing tasks — directed writing, composition (descriptive or narrative) and summary — and the phrases to carry it out in English (0500) and in Spanish (0502), so you can plan any question in five minutes.

Both First Language papers test the same skills: reading for meaning, selecting and adapting ideas, and writing accurately for a purpose and audience. Your school's mock papers follow the real format, but check the front cover of your paper for the exact questions, marks and suggested word counts — they are printed there and they differ between the two subjects.

## What to do (60 minutes)

| Time | Task |
|---|---|
| 0–15 min | Read the three structures; write each one from memory on an index card |
| 15–30 min | **Directed writing plan:** take a past-paper directed writing question; 5-minute plan in English, then the same type of plan for a Spanish question |
| 30–45 min | **Composition:** plan one descriptive and one narrative piece (one in each language); write the opening paragraph of each |
| 45–55 min | **Summary:** do a summary question from a past paper, timed |
| 55–60 min | Error log: look at your past writing feedback; write your 3 personal "always do" rules |

## 1. Directed writing

You read one or two texts and write in a given **form** (letter, article, speech, report, journal entry) for a given **audience**, using and **evaluating** ideas from the texts. Marks reward both **reading** (how well you use, develop and evaluate the ideas) and **writing** (structure, style, register, accuracy).

**Before writing (5 minutes):**
1. Underline: form, audience, purpose, the bullet points you must cover.
2. Go through the texts and number every relevant idea; note where texts **agree, disagree or are weak** (exaggeration, opinion presented as fact, out-of-date or one-sided evidence).
3. Decide your **position** (agree / partly agree / disagree).

**Structure:**
| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| Opening | Correct conventions for the form (Dear…, headline, "Good morning everyone"); state the purpose and your position |
| Body (3–4 paragraphs) | One paragraph per bullet point; each paragraph: idea from the text → developed in your own words → **evaluated** (Is it convincing? Who benefits? What is missing?) |
| Conclusion | Clear judgement or recommendation; call to action; appropriate sign-off |

**Top-band moves:** evaluate, don't just repeat ("While the article claims…, it offers no evidence that…"); use your own words; keep the register consistent with the audience; refer to all bullet points.

| English | Español (gloss) |
|---|---|
| It has been suggested that… | Se ha sugerido que… (it has been suggested that) |
| Although the writer claims…, … | Aunque el autor afirma que…, … (although the author states…) |
| This argument overlooks… | Este argumento pasa por alto… (this argument overlooks) |
| On closer inspection… | Si lo analizamos con detenimiento… (if we analyse it carefully) |
| I would therefore urge you to… | Por lo tanto, les insto a que… + subjuntivo (therefore I urge you to) |
| Yours faithfully / sincerely | Atentamente / Un cordial saludo (yours faithfully / kind regards) |

## 2. Composition: descriptive and narrative

Marks reward **content and structure** and **style and accuracy**. Quality beats length: keep within the recommended word count.

### Descriptive — "show a scene, don't tell a story"
| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| Opening | Zoom in: one precise detail or sound, present tense often works well |
| Body | Move through **space** (near → far, outside → inside) or **time** (dawn → night); each paragraph a different focus; all five senses; a person or two seen in detail |
| Shift | A change in mood, weather or perspective halfway through gives shape |
| Ending | Return to the opening image, changed |

### Narrative — "a short story with one turning point"
| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| Opening | Start in the middle of action or with a striking line; establish character and setting quickly |
| Development | One problem or tension; build it with detail and dialogue (correctly punctuated) |
| Climax | The turning point — slow down here |
| Resolution | Brief; can be open, but must feel deliberate |

**Style tools (both languages):** varied sentence openings and lengths (a one-line paragraph for impact); precise verbs and nouns rather than piles of adjectives; imagery used sparingly; avoid clichés.

| English | Español (gloss) |
|---|---|
| The silence was broken only by… | El silencio solo lo rompía… (the silence was broken only by) |
| Beneath the surface… | Bajo la superficie… (beneath the surface) |
| Little did I know that… | Lo que no sabía era que… (what I did not know was that) |
| At that very moment… | En ese preciso instante… (at that very moment) |
| As the light faded… | Mientras la luz se apagaba… (as the light faded) |

Spanish accuracy check: accents (está/esta, más/mas, él/el, tú/tu), **pretérito indefinido** for completed actions vs **imperfecto** for description and background, subjunctive after *cuando* (future), *para que*, *aunque* (hypothetical), *es importante que*. Avoid anglicisms ("aplicar" for a job → *solicitar*; "realizar" ≠ to realise → *darse cuenta*).

## 3. Summary

You select relevant information from a text and write it **in your own words**, concisely, within a word limit. Marks reward **reading** (relevant points found) and **writing** (concision, own words, organisation).

**Method:**
1. Underline the focus of the question (e.g. "the benefits of…" — not the problems).
2. Highlight every relevant point in the text; list them as short notes (aim for more points than you think).
3. Group similar points; put them in a logical order (not necessarily the text's order).
4. Write **one continuous paragraph** in formal, neutral register; no introduction, no opinion, no examples or quotations.
5. Count words; cut repetition.

| Don't | Do |
|---|---|
| Copy whole phrases from the text | Rephrase: "the town's economy depends on tourists" → "visitors keep local businesses alive" |
| Include examples, statistics, anecdotes | Generalise: "trains, buses and trams" → "public transport" |
| Add your own opinion | Stay neutral and objective |
| Go far over the word limit | Combine points into complex sentences |

Linkers for a fluent summary: furthermore, in addition, however, as a result, consequently / además, asimismo, sin embargo, por consiguiente, de ahí que + subjuntivo (hence).
