# Get ready for exam day

> **Goal:** everything for the mocks is packed, your timetable is clear, you have a timing plan for every paper — and you go to bed early.

## What to do (60 minutes)

| Time | Task |
|---|---|
| 0–15 min | Pack your exam bag using the equipment list |
| 15–25 min | Write out your mock timetable (from school) and the timing plan for the first papers |
| 25–40 min | Light review only: flick through your summary sheets and the error-log fixes for the **first** exam |
| 40–50 min | Read "In the exam room" |
| 50–60 min | Clothes, alarm, breakfast and route planned; then stop studying for the day |

---

## 1. Equipment

**Every exam** (in a clear plastic pencil case):
- At least two black ink pens (Cambridge asks for dark blue or black ink; use black) — and a spare
- HB pencil, eraser, sharpener — pencil only for diagrams and graphs
- 30 cm ruler
- Water in a clear bottle with the label removed (if your school allows it)
- Your candidate number written on a card (your school will tell you)
- **Not allowed in the room:** phone, smartwatch, notes, correction fluid on answers (cross out neatly with one line instead) — phones and smartwatches must be switched off and left where the invigilator says

| Subject | Extra equipment |
|---|---|
| Maths 0580 | Calculator (**Paper 4 only — Paper 2 is non-calculator**), compasses, protractor |
| Statistics 4ST1 | Calculator, protractor and compasses (pie charts), sharp pencil for graphs |
| Biology 0610 | Calculator, ruler, sharp pencil (graphs, diagrams) |
| Chemistry 0620 | Calculator, ruler, pencil (the Periodic Table is printed in the paper) |
| Physics 0625 | Calculator, ruler, protractor, pencil (ray diagrams, graphs) |
| Business 0450 | Calculator |
| English 0500, Spanish 0502 | Pens only; no dictionary |
| French 0520 | Pens only; no dictionary. Speaking test: arrive early and calm; you may get preparation time for the role play |

### Calculator rules
- A **scientific calculator** is what you need. Calculators that can do symbolic algebra (CAS), store text, communicate (wireless, Bluetooth) or are part of a phone/tablet/watch are not allowed. If you have anything other than a normal scientific calculator, ask your teacher before the exam.
- Lids and instruction leaflets are not allowed in the room.
- Put in a **new battery** (or bring a spare calculator) and check it is in **degrees** mode (D on the screen), not radians.
- Clear the memory if your school asks you to.

## 2. Mock timetable and timing plan

Copy your school's timetable here (date, time, subject, paper, duration, room). Then write next to each paper its **minutes per mark**: duration in minutes ÷ total marks (printed on the front cover).

| 11 Jan | | | | | | |
| | | | | | | |

Rule of thumb: about **1 to 1.2 minutes per mark**. For a 6-mark question you have roughly 6–7 minutes. In languages, decide in advance how long you will spend planning, writing and checking each writing task (e.g. 5 min plan, most of the time writing, 5 min check).

**Order of attack:** start at Question 1 unless you have a deliberate plan (e.g. in English, check the question order and do not spend too long on the first question). If a question is taking twice its time, leave a gap, write the question number on the front of your rough paper, and come back.

## 3. Tonight

- **Stop studying by about 20:00.** Late cramming lowers tomorrow's performance more than it helps.
- Normal dinner; avoid caffeine and energy drinks after midday.
- Screens off at least 30–60 minutes before bed; phone out of the bedroom if you can.
- In bed early enough for **8–9 hours' sleep**. If you cannot sleep, rest with eyes closed — resting still helps; do not get up to revise.
- Set two alarms. Lay out clothes and your packed bag by the door.

## 4. The morning

- Breakfast with slow-release energy (e.g. toast or cereal with milk, fruit, eggs); drink water.
- Leave with time to spare: aim to arrive **15 minutes early**.
- No new material. If you want, look at one summary sheet for five minutes, then put it away.
- Avoid anxious "what did you revise?" conversations at the door.

## 5. In the exam room

1. **Fill in your details** (name, centre and candidate number) exactly as instructed.
2. **Read the front cover:** number of questions, total marks, which questions to answer, whether a calculator is allowed.
3. **Read each question twice.** Underline the **command word** (state, describe, explain, calculate, suggest, evaluate) and key data. Answer what is asked, not what you hoped would be asked.
4. **Use the marks:** the number of marks ≈ the number of points needed; the space given hints at the length.
5. **Show working** in every calculation, with units. Write the formula first.
6. **Never leave a blank:** an answer that might be right can score; a blank cannot.
7. **If you panic:** pen down, slow breath in for 4 counts, out for 6, three times. Then start with the easiest question you can see.
8. **Last 5–10 minutes:** check units, significant figures, every part answered (turn every page — questions continue on the back), in languages check verb endings, accents and agreements.
9. Cross out unwanted work with a single line; if you write outside the answer space, say where ("continued on page 12").
10. After each exam, **do not do a post-mortem** with friends. Note anything you want to check in the error log, then switch to the next subject.

## Checklist

- [ ] Bag packed with the general and subject equipment
- [ ] Calculator: new battery, degrees mode, nothing forbidden in the room
- [ ] Mock timetable copied with minutes per mark for each paper
- [ ] Alarm(s) set, clothes ready, route and arrival time planned
- [ ] Studying finished for the day; screens off before bed
- [ ] In bed early — well done for getting here
