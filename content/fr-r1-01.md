# Exam format and topics A–E

> **In the exam:** Cambridge IGCSE French (Foreign Language) 0520 has four components — Listening, Reading, Speaking and Writing — each worth **25%** of the qualification. Every component draws on the same five topic areas (A–E), so vocabulary you learn once pays off four times. Your school mock will copy the format of the real papers; check the cover of each paper (or ask your teacher) for exact timings, marks and question numbering, because these details can change between syllabus versions.

## What you need to know

- The **four skills** and how each one is tested and marked.
- The **five topic areas** and the sub-topics inside each.
- The **command language** used in the papers: questions and instructions are mainly in French (e.g. *Répondez en français*, *Cochez la bonne case*, *Écrivez la lettre*), so you must recognise them instantly.
- What earns marks: **communication first** in Listening and Reading; **communication + range + accuracy** in Speaking and Writing.

## Notes

### The five topic areas

| Area | Title | Typical sub-topics |
|---|---|---|
| A | Everyday activities | home life, daily routine, school, food and drink, eating out, health and fitness |
| B | Personal and social life | self, family and friends, hobbies, sport, holidays, special occasions and festivals |
| C | The world around us | home town and region, shopping, public services, natural environment, weather, transport, places and customs |
| D | The world of work | further education and training, careers and jobs, part-time work, language at work, communication (phone, email) |
| E | The international world | travel abroad, tourism, accommodation, life in other countries, world events and issues (environment, technology, global issues) |

Treat this table as a map: every vocabulary list, speaking answer and writing plan you prepare should be filed under one of these letters.

### Listening (25%)

- You hear recordings of French speakers: short announcements, messages, conversations, then longer interviews and discussions.
- Each recording is normally played **twice**, with pauses. You get reading time before each section — use it to read the questions and predict the answers.
- Question types (typically): **multiple choice** (tick a box / choose a picture), **matching** speakers to statements, **true/false or correcting statements**, and **short answers in French**.
- Marking: you are marked on **comprehension**. A short answer in French with a spelling mistake still scores if the meaning is unambiguous. A wrong word that changes the meaning (e.g. *mardi* instead of *mercredi*) scores zero.
- The later sections are harder: speakers give opinions, change their minds and use past and future tenses. Distractors are common ("*Je voulais aller au cinéma, mais finalement on est allés au parc*" — the answer is *le parc*).

### Reading (25%)

- Texts go from short signs, adverts and messages to longer articles, emails, blogs and interviews.
- Question types (typically): **matching** (texts to people, headings to paragraphs), **multiple choice**, **true/false with justification**, and **answers in French** to comprehension questions.
- Marking: comprehension again. You can often **lift** words from the text, but only the relevant part. Copying a whole sentence that contains the answer plus wrong extra information can lose the mark.
- Answers must be in **French** when the question says *Répondez en français*. A one-word or short-phrase answer is usually enough.

### Speaking (25%)

- Conducted and recorded by your teacher, then moderated by Cambridge. Lasts roughly 10–15 minutes in total.
- It includes **role play(s)** — you play a part in a situation (at a hotel, booking a ticket, talking to a host family) and must respond to prompts — and **conversation** on topics from areas A–E, where the examiner asks open questions.
- Marking (general picture): role plays reward **completing each task clearly**; conversations reward **communication**, **range of language** (tenses, opinions, justifications, linking words) and **accuracy/pronunciation**.
- Higher marks need **spontaneity**: developed answers, opinions with reasons, and confident use of past, present and future.
- Check with your teacher exactly how many role plays and conversation topics your school's mock uses and how the topics are chosen.

### Writing (25%)

- Usually a **short task** (for example a short email or message covering set bullet points) and a **longer task** (in this plan, **130–150 words**) with a choice of question.
- Check the paper for exact word counts and number of tasks. Writing far more than asked does not earn extra marks and increases errors.
- Marking (general picture): **communication** (covering every bullet point clearly), **range of language** (varied vocabulary, structures, tenses), and **accuracy**.
- Every bullet point matters: missing one caps your communication mark.

### What makes a top answer in Speaking and Writing

A simple checklist you will use all through this plan — aim to include in every longer answer:

1. **Three time frames**: passé composé/imparfait, présent, futur/conditionnel.
2. **Opinions + reasons**: *À mon avis… parce que…*, *Ce que j'aime, c'est…*
3. **Connectives**: *cependant, donc, d'abord, ensuite, par contre, pourtant*.
4. **One or two "wow" structures**: *Si j'avais le temps, je voyagerais*; *après avoir mangé*; *il faut que…* (only if you are sure of them).
5. **Accuracy in the basics**: agreements, verb endings, *être* verbs in the passé composé.

## Key terms and definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Distractor | Information in a text/recording that sounds like the answer but is not (e.g. a plan that is later cancelled). |
| Lifting | Copying words from the text as your answer; acceptable if only the relevant part is copied. |
| Unambiguous | Meaning is clear even with minor spelling mistakes — enough for comprehension marks. |
| Range | Variety of vocabulary, structures and tenses — rewarded in Speaking and Writing. |
| Development | Adding detail, reasons, examples to an answer instead of a one-sentence reply. |

## Worked examples

**1. Listening (multiple choice) [1]**
You hear: *« Normalement, je vais au collège à vélo, mais aujourd'hui il pleut, alors ma mère m'emmène en voiture. »*
Comment va-t-il au collège aujourd'hui ? A à vélo B en voiture C à pied

Answer: **B** (1). *Normalement… à vélo* is the distractor; *aujourd'hui… en voiture* is the answer.

**2. Reading (answer in French) [2]**
Text: *« Le club de théâtre se réunit le jeudi après les cours, de 17h à 18h30, dans la salle 12. »*
(a) Quel jour le club se réunit-il ? (b) Combien de temps dure la réunion ?

Answer: (a) **le jeudi** (1) (b) **une heure et demie / 1h30** (1). Writing *de 17h à 18h30* is also usually accepted because it gives the information.

**3. Writing (bullet point check) [communication]**
Task: Write an email to a French friend about your weekend. Mention: where you went; who with; what you ate; your plans for next weekend.
A model answer must cover all four, e.g. *« Samedi, je suis allé au centre commercial avec mon cousin. Nous avons mangé une pizza délicieuse. Le week-end prochain, je vais jouer au foot. »* — four bullets, three tenses.

## Common mistakes

- Answering in English (or Spanish) when the question says *Répondez en français*.
- Picking the first thing you hear in Listening instead of waiting for the final decision.
- Copying a whole sentence from the Reading text that includes wrong information alongside the answer.
- Missing a bullet point in Writing, or answering it in the wrong tense (a bullet about the past answered in the present).
- In Speaking, one-word answers with no development or opinion.
- Mixing Spanish into French: *es* for *est*, *muy* for *très*, *el/la* for *le/la*, *tengo 15 años* → *j'ai 15 ans*.

## Practice questions

1. Name the four components of 0520 and their weightings. [2]
2. Under which topic area (A–E) would you put: (a) a part-time job; (b) pollution; (c) a birthday party; (d) a visit to the doctor; (e) a holiday in Canada? [5]
3. Translate these instructions: (a) *Cochez la bonne case* (b) *Corrigez les phrases fausses* (c) *Répondez en français*. [3]
4. Listening: *« Je voulais acheter un pantalon, mais il était trop cher, alors j'ai pris un tee-shirt. »* Qu'est-ce qu'elle a acheté ? [1]
5. Rewrite this weak speaking answer to make it a strong one: *« J'aime le sport. »* [3]
6. Give three connectives in French with their meaning. [3]
