# Past paper · Listening + marking

> **Goal:** Do one complete 0520 Listening paper with its audio under exam conditions, mark it with the mark scheme and log every error.

## Before you start

- **Paper:** a recent 0520 Listening paper (e.g. May/June or Oct/Nov 2024 or 2025) that you have **not** done. Your teacher can get the question paper, **audio files**, **transcript** and **mark scheme** from the Cambridge School Support Hub. Check the variant number matches across all three (e.g. paper 12 with audio 12 and mark scheme 12).
- **Audio:** if the audio comes as one file with built-in pauses and repetitions, play it straight through without stopping — that is the real exam. If it comes as separate tracks, play each one twice with the pauses stated on the paper.
- Headphones or a speaker, a quiet room, a black pen, a timer, your error log.
- Keep the transcript and mark scheme in another room or a closed folder until you finish.

## Exam format reminder

- Check the cover of your paper for the exact time and total marks; the audio itself controls the timing.
- The paper is divided into exercises that get progressively harder: short items (announcements, messages, short dialogues) → conversations → longer interviews with opinions and explanations.
- Each recording is normally heard **twice**, with pauses for reading the questions and writing answers.
- Question types: multiple choice (letters or pictures), matching speakers to statements, ticking true statements, and short answers in French.
- At the end there is usually a short time to check your answers.

## How to do it

1. **0–3 min** — Set up. Write the start time. Read the instructions on the front cover.
2. **3–48 min (approx.)** — Play the audio and do the paper. Use every reading pause to read ahead and predict. No pausing, no rewinding.
3. **48–50 min** — Check: no blanks; every letter clearly written; answers in French where required.
4. **50–60 min** — Start marking (finish marking at the start of the next session if needed; don't rush it).

If your paper's audio is shorter or longer, adjust: marking always gets at least 10 minutes.

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Read ahead in every pause.** Underline question words (*Où, Quand, Pourquoi, Combien, Qui*). Predict the type of answer.
- **First listening: notes. Second listening: confirm.** Don't commit to the first thing you hear.
- **Distractors:** the recording often mentions two or three of the options. Listen for *mais, finalement, en fait, d'habitude… mais, ne… plus, au lieu de*.
- **Numbers and times:** write digits immediately. Watch *soixante-dix* (70), *quatre-vingts* (80), *quatre-vingt-dix* (90). Some questions need simple arithmetic (*vingt minutes de retard*).
- **Short French answers:** as short as possible while unambiguous. Spelling mistakes are tolerated if the meaning is clear; a different word is not.
- **Matching tasks:** cross out options as you use them, but note that some tasks allow an option to be used more than once — read the instructions.
- **Never leave a blank.** A guess has a chance; a blank has none.
- In longer interviews, questions usually follow the order of the recording — if you miss one, move on and catch the next.

## Checklist while marking

- Mark with a different coloured pen. Follow the mark scheme exactly: if it lists acceptable answers, yours must match the meaning; check the "reject" or "do not accept" column if there is one.
- If your answer included extra information that contradicts the correct answer (e.g. two options), give it 0 — that is how examiners mark.
- After marking, **open the transcript** and for every wrong answer, find the line in the transcript. Decide why you lost it:
  - **V** — vocabulary I didn't know
  - **D** — fell for a distractor
  - **N** — number/time error
  - **Q** — misread the question
  - **S** — answer not clear / not in French
- Record in the error log: paper and question number, your answer, correct answer, cause (V/D/N/Q/S), and the fix (e.g. "Anki: *la voie* = platform").
- Calculate your percentage per exercise. Which section loses most marks? That's your focus for the next listening session.
- Replay the audio for the questions you got wrong while reading the transcript — hearing it again with the text is the fastest way to improve.

## Quick-fire revision

- Question words: *qui* who · *que/qu'est-ce que* what · *où* where · *quand* when · *pourquoi* why · *comment* how · *combien* how much/many · *quel(le)* which.
- Signal words: *mais, cependant, par contre, finalement, en fait, d'habitude, au lieu de, sauf, seulement, ne… plus, ne… jamais*.
- Times: *et quart* .15 · *et demie* .30 · *moins le quart* .45 · *midi / minuit* · 24-hour clock is common (*seize heures* = 4 pm).
- Prices: *deux euros cinquante* = €2.50.
- Days/months, weather (*il pleut, il neige, il fait du vent*), places in town, food, jobs, school subjects — the bread-and-butter of the early exercises.
- Feelings/opinions in the later exercises: *content, déçu (disappointed), inquiet (worried), fier (proud), ennuyeux, passionnant, ça m'est égal (I don't mind)*.
