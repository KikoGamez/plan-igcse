# Speaking: mock

> **Goal:** Do a full speaking mock — role play and conversation — under timed conditions, record it, and assess it against the criteria to fix your weakest points before the exam.

## Before you start

- **Material:** role play cards and conversation questions. Best: ask your teacher for official 0520 speaking materials (teacher's notes / role play cards from a recent series). Otherwise use the cards below and the question bank from the "Speaking: role play and conversation" session.
- A partner to play the examiner (parent, sibling, friend — they can read the French lines below even with little French). If you're alone, record the examiner questions first with pauses, then play them back.
- Phone or laptop to **record** yourself; headphones; timer; error log.
- Your prepared answer cards — **only for the review stage**, not during the mock.

## Exam format reminder

- Conducted by your teacher, recorded, and moderated by Cambridge; roughly 10–15 minutes in total. Check with your teacher the exact structure of your mock (number of role plays, how conversation topics are chosen, any preparation time).
- **Role play:** you play a part in a situation; respond to each prompt, including at least one unexpected question, and you may need to ask a question.
- **Conversation:** questions on topics from areas A–E, getting more open and demanding. The examiner should give you the chance to use past, present and future.
- Assessment (general): role play → completion of tasks with clear communication; conversation → communication, range of language, accuracy and pronunciation, with credit for spontaneous, developed answers.

## How to do it

1. **0–5 min** — Set up; give your "examiner" the cards below. Start recording.
2. **5–20 min** — The mock: two role plays (about 2–3 minutes each), then conversation (about 8–10 minutes, two or three topic areas). No notes.
3. **20–40 min** — Listen to the whole recording once with the checklist below. Write timestamps of good and weak moments.
4. **40–55 min** — Redo the three weakest answers: rewrite them as key-word cards (not scripts), then record them again.
5. **55–60 min** — Error log.

### Mock role play A (examiner = shop assistant, use *vous*)
You are in a clothes shop in France.
1. *Bonjour, je peux vous aider ?* (say what you want to buy)
2. *Quelle taille faites-vous ?*
3. *De quelle couleur ?*
4. *Pour quelle occasion ?* (unexpected)
5. You ask the price and if you can pay by card.

### Mock role play B (examiner = French friend, use *tu*)
Your French friend is visiting you in Spain.
1. *Qu'est-ce qu'on va faire ce week-end ?*
2. *On y va comment ?*
3. *Qu'est-ce que tu as fait le week-end dernier ?*
4. *Qu'est-ce qu'on mange typiquement ici ?* (unexpected)
5. You ask your friend what they would like to visit.

### Conversation questions for the examiner (choose 8–10 across topics)
- *Décris ta maison.* · *Qu'est-ce que tu as mangé hier ?*
- *Qu'est-ce que tu as fait pour ton dernier anniversaire ?* · *Comment est ton meilleur ami / ta meilleure amie ?*
- *Qu'est-ce qu'il y a à faire dans ta ville ?* · *Comment est-ce qu'on pourrait améliorer ta ville ?*
- *Quel métier voudrais-tu faire et pourquoi ?* · *Est-ce que les jeunes devraient avoir un petit boulot ?*
- *Quel pays voudrais-tu visiter ?* · *Est-ce que la technologie est une bonne chose pour les jeunes ?*

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Role play:** one clear full sentence per prompt; answer exactly what is asked; *vous* in shops/hotels. If you don't understand: *Pouvez-vous répéter, s'il vous plaît ?*
- **Questions to ask:** *C'est combien ? Est-ce que je peux… ? Qu'est-ce que tu voudrais… ? À quelle heure… ?*
- **Conversation:** PEEF — Point, Explain (*parce que*), Example in another tense, Feeling. 3–5 sentences per answer.
- Use the examiner's question to build your answer: *Qu'est-ce que tu as fait…?* → *J'ai fait…*
- Fill silences: *Alors… / Voyons… / C'est une bonne question.*
- Use a mix of tenses across the conversation — this is what moves you up the bands.
- Sound natural rather than recited; vary your intonation.

## Checklist while marking

While listening to your recording, note:

- **Role plays:** was every prompt answered clearly? Did you ask the required question? *Tu/vous* correct?
- **Development:** how many conversation answers had 3+ sentences? Any one-liners?
- **Tenses:** tally past / present / future / conditional uses. Aim for several of each.
- **Opinions + reasons:** count them.
- **Accuracy:** note errors in verbs (*j'ai allé*), agreement, gender, *de* after negatives.
- **Pronunciation:** silent final consonants and *-ent*; *u* vs *ou*; nasal sounds (*vin, vent, bon*); the French *r*.
- **Fluency:** long pauses? Spanish words slipping in (*pero, bueno*)?
- **Error log:** each error with correction; plus one line on "what to change next time".

## Quick-fire revision

- Role play openers: *Je voudrais… / Est-ce que vous avez… ? / C'est combien ? / Je peux payer par carte ?*
- Opinion starters: *À mon avis… · Je trouve que… · Ce que j'aime, c'est… · Le problème, c'est que…*
- Time markers: *hier, le week-end dernier, l'année dernière* · *normalement, d'habitude* · *demain, l'année prochaine, plus tard, un jour*.
- Range boosters: *Si j'avais le choix, je… · J'espère… · Je viens de… · Après avoir… · Il faut que…*
- Repair: *Pouvez-vous répéter ? · Je ne suis pas sûr(e), mais… · Comment dit-on… ?*
