# Geographical skills: maps, graphs and fieldwork

> **In the exam:** **Paper 2 Geographical Skills** (1 h 30 min, 60 marks, about 27.5%) is almost all skills: a topographic map extract (grid references, distance, direction, height, relief, gradient, settlement and drainage), graphs, photographs, data tables and a short enquiry style question. **Paper 4 Alternative to Coursework** (1 h 30 min, 60 marks) tests fieldwork: hypotheses, methods, sampling, presenting and analysing data, conclusions and evaluation. Paper 1 also uses graphs and photographs. Most items are 1–4 marks; the last parts are longer (6–8).

## What you need to know

- **Topographic maps:** symbols and key, **4- and 6-figure grid references**, **scale and distance**, **compass directions and bearings**, **contours, spot heights and relief**, **gradient**, cross-sections and describing patterns.
- **Graphs and data:** bar, line, pie, scatter, histogram, divided and proportional bars, **flow and choropleth maps**, **population pyramids**; reading, completing, and describing them; **averages and percentages**.
- **Photographs and sketches:** describing, labelling and interpreting a photograph or satellite image.
- **Fieldwork enquiry:** hypotheses, data collection methods, **sampling**, presenting and analysing results, conclusions, and **evaluation** (reliability, limitations, improvements).

## Notes

### Reading a topographic map

**Grid references**
- Eastings (vertical lines) are read **first**, northings (horizontal lines) **second**: "along the corridor, then up the stairs".
- **4-figure:** the square, using the **bottom left corner** of the square, e.g. 3 5 5 8 → easting 35, northing 58. 
- **6-figure:** divide each square into tenths by eye. Third figure is tenths along, sixth tenths up, e.g. 355 584. Always give the six digits, no spaces or letters.

**Scale and distance**
- Scale **1:25 000** means 1 cm = 25 000 cm = **250 m**; 4 cm = 1 km. With **1:50 000**, 1 cm = 500 m, 2 cm = 1 km.
- Measure with a ruler or the edge of paper. For curved routes (roads, rivers), divide the line into short straight pieces or use a piece of string or paper.
- Check the **units**: km or m.

**Direction and bearings**
- **Compass points:** N, NE, E, SE, S, SW, W, NW. Read **from the first place to the second**, e.g. "the school is south-west of the station".
- **Bearing:** a **three-figure angle clockwise from north**, measured with a protractor: e.g. 045° is NE, 270° is W.

**Relief and height**
- **Contour lines** join points of equal height; the **contour interval** is the difference in height between two lines (check the key).
- **Close together = steep slope;** far apart = gentle slope. **V-shaped** contours pointing uphill show a valley; closed circles show a hill top.
- **Spot heights** and **trigonometric points** show exact heights.
- Describe relief with figures: "land rises from 50 m in the west to 200 m in the east".
- **Gradient = vertical height difference ÷ horizontal distance** (in the same units). A rise of 100 m over 2000 m is 1 in 20.
- A **cross-section** shows the height of the land along a straight line: mark heights on the edge of paper, plot on the graph, join smoothly.

**Describing map features**
- **Settlement:** nucleated (clustered), linear (along a road or river), dispersed (spread); sites such as river crossing, flat ground, near water.
- **Drainage:** a river's pattern, direction of flow (high to low), meanders, tributaries.
- **Land use and communications:** roads, railways, farmland, forest, industry; use 6-figure grid references as evidence.

### Graphs and data

| Graph | Use | Tips |
|---|---|---|
| **Bar / histogram** | Compare categories; histograms for continuous data (no gaps) | Bars start at zero; equal widths |
| **Line graph** | Change over time | Plot points, join with ruler; label axes with units |
| **Pie chart** | Parts of a whole (%) | Each 1% = 3.6°; label segments |
| **Scatter graph** | Relationship between two variables | Draw a **line of best fit**; describe as positive, negative or no correlation |
| **Divided / proportional bar** | Parts of total | Order consistently |
| **Choropleth map** | Shading shows density or rate by area | Darker = higher, check the key |
| **Flow map** | Arrows show movement (migration, trade); thicker = more | Quote values |
| **Population pyramid** | Age and sex structure | Wide base = high births; narrow top = few elderly |
| **Climate graph** | Rainfall bars + temperature line | See Weather and climate |

**Describing a graph (get all the marks)**
1. State the **overall trend** (rises, falls, stays the same).
2. **Quote figures** with units (start, end, peak) and years.
3. Note **anomalies** (the one that does not fit).
4. If asked, add a **link or reason** using geographic knowledge.

**Quick data skills**
- **Mean** = total ÷ number of values. **Median** = middle value (ordered). **Mode** = most common. **Range** = highest − lowest.
- **Percentage change** = (new − old) ÷ old × 100.
- **Percentage** = part ÷ whole × 100.
- If asked to **complete** a graph, plot exactly (to half a small square), and use the same scale.

### Photographs, sketches and satellite images

- **Foreground, middle ground, background** helps you describe location.
- Describe what you see (physical features, human features, land use), then **interpret** (why it is there, effects).
- **Labelled sketch:** outline main features only, label them with brief notes (not a drawing contest), give a title and add orientation if needed.
- Satellite images: look for colours (green = vegetation, blue = water, grey = urban).

### Fieldwork enquiry (Paper 4 and Paper 2)

**The enquiry process:**
1. **Question or hypothesis** (a testable statement): e.g. "River velocity increases downstream."
2. **Data collection methods:** primary data (you collected) and secondary data (from others).
3. **Sampling:** **random**, **systematic** (every nth), **stratified**.
4. **Presentation:** graphs, maps and tables.
5. **Analysis and conclusion:** does the data support the hypothesis? Use figures.
6. **Evaluation:** limitations and improvements.

**Typical methods**

| Topic | Method |
|---|---|
| **River** | Width (tape), depth (ruler/metre stick), **velocity** (float timed over a set distance, or flow meter), gradient (clinometer), bedload size and shape (measure long axis; roundness scale) |
| **Coast** | Beach profile (ranging poles, clinometer), pebble size and shape, longshore drift (float movement), groyne data, sea defence quality |
| **Settlement and urban** | Questionnaires, pedestrian and traffic counts, land-use survey, environmental quality survey (scored) |
| **Weather** | Instruments at a weather station: thermometer, rain gauge, anemometer |
| **Tourism** | Questionnaires, visitor counts, footpath erosion survey |

**Questionnaire:** ask short, clear, unbiased questions to a sample; give **age groups**; **pilot** the questions.

**Evaluating fieldwork**
- **Accuracy (reliability):** small sample, equipment errors, human errors (timing a float), weather or time of day, subjective scoring; **repeat** measurements and take more sites.
- **Safety:** avoid deep water, wear a life jacket, work in pairs, tell staff.
- **Improvements:** larger sample, more sites, different times, better equipment (flow meter), repeat.

## Key terms and definitions

- **Grid reference:** numbers locating a place on a map, eastings first then northings.
- **Scale:** the relationship between distance on a map and the real distance.
- **Contour line:** a line on a map joining points of equal height above sea level.
- **Gradient:** the steepness of a slope (vertical change ÷ horizontal distance).
- **Bearing:** a direction given as a three-figure angle clockwise from north.
- **Hypothesis:** a testable statement or prediction you aim to prove or disprove.
- **Primary data:** data you collect yourself, first-hand.
- **Secondary data:** data collected by someone else (e.g. census, websites).
- **Sampling:** selecting a smaller number of sites or people to represent the whole.
- **Reliability:** how far results would be the same if repeated.
- **Anomaly:** a result that does not fit the pattern.

## Worked examples

**1. A church lies 7 tenths of the way along from easting line 36 and 2 tenths of the way up from northing line 82. Give its six-figure grid reference. [1]**

Easting 367, northing 822, so **367822** (1).

**2. The map scale is 1:25 000. Two villages are 6 cm apart. What is the distance in km? [2]**

6 × 25 000 = 150 000 cm (1) = 1.5 km (1). (Shortcut: 4 cm = 1 km, so 6 cm = 1.5 km.)

**3. A scatter graph shows number of visitors against distance from the car park. Describe the pattern. [3]**

There is a negative correlation (1): the further from the car park, the fewer visitors (1). For example, over 1,000 people visit within 100 m, but fewer than 100 at 1 km (1) (with figures from the graph).

**4. State one way to improve the accuracy of river velocity measurements. [2]**

Repeat the float measurement three times and calculate a mean (1) because this reduces the effect of timing errors or an unusual result (1).

## Common mistakes

- Giving the **northing first** in a grid reference (remember eastings, then northings).
- Measuring distance and forgetting to **convert** (cm to km) or give units.
- Mixing up **bearing** (angle from north, clockwise) and **compass direction**.
- Reading contours wrongly: **close together means steep**, not gentle.
- Describing a graph with **no figures** or only the highest value.
- Not using the **key** on a choropleth or flow map.
- Writing "the sample was too small" without saying **why it matters** (could be unrepresentative, less reliable).
- Evaluation as "we ran out of time" rather than a clear improvement.

## Practice questions

1. Give the four-figure grid reference for the square whose bottom left corner is at easting 41 and northing 76. [1]
2. A map is 1:50 000. Two bridges are 5 cm apart. Calculate the distance in km. [2]
3. State the compass direction from X (south-west of the map) to Y (north-east). [1]
4. Explain what close contour lines on a map show. [1]
5. A river rises 40 m over a 2 km stretch. Calculate the gradient as 1 in … [2]
6. Calculate the mean of 12, 15, 9, 14 and 10. [1]
7. Describe one way of sampling people in a street questionnaire. [2]
8. Suggest two limitations of using a float to measure river velocity. [2]
