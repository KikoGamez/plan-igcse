# Past paper · Paper 1 timed (1/2)

> **Goal:** Start a real Cambridge IGCSE Geography **Paper 1 Geographical Themes** under exam conditions: complete one question in full and begin a second, practising pace (about 1 minute per mark) and the longer final answers.

## Before you start

- **Paper:** Cambridge IGCSE Geography (0460) **Paper 1: Geographical Themes**. Use a real past paper you have not seen, for example **May/June 2025 Paper 12 (0460/12)**, or **October/November 2024 Paper 12**, or another variant (11, 12 or 13) from the same series your teacher can give you. Past papers and mark schemes are available from your teacher or from the Cambridge Schools Support Hub. Recent series follow the same format, so they are all suitable.
- **Mark scheme:** put it away. You will use it in the next session.
- **Have ready:** black pen, pencil, ruler, calculator, eraser, a clock or timer. No notes, no phone.
- **Timer:** the full paper is 1 h 45 min (105 min) and you answer **three questions**. This session is the first 60 minutes: write the finish time on the front, and in session 2 you will finish the paper.
- **Choice:** decide which question you will answer from each theme. Read the titles of both questions in a theme before choosing, and stick to your choice.

## Exam format reminder

- **Paper 1: 1 hour 45 minutes, 75 marks, about 45% of the qualification.**
- **Three themes:** Theme 1 Population and settlement, Theme 2 The natural environment, Theme 3 Economic development.
- **Answer three questions: one question from each theme** (each theme has two questions). **25 marks each.**
- Each question has parts (a), (b), (c) (sometimes (d)) with sub-parts. Early parts are short (1–4 marks: define, name, describe from a graph or map, calculate). The last part is a longer **7-mark** answer with levels, often "explain" or "discuss" and asking for a **named example or case study**. Read the question: you may need to use a resource (a graph, map or photo).
- Command words: **State/Name** (a fact), **Describe** (what you see, with data), **Explain** (reasons, "because"), **Suggest** (apply knowledge to the resource), **Compare** (both sides), **Assess/Discuss/Evaluate** (both sides and a judgement).

## How to do it

1. **0–3 min:** Read the front page. Choose your Theme 1 and Theme 2 questions. Write the start and finish time.
2. **3–35 min:** **Theme 1 question** (Population and settlement). Aim for about 32 minutes for the 25 marks. Do all the short parts first, then spend about 8–9 minutes on the final 7-mark answer. Use a plan: 3 points, each with a reason and example.
3. **35–60 min:** **Theme 2 question** (Natural environment). Work through as far as you can in the remaining 25 minutes. Put a line where you reach 60 minutes and **stop**.
4. **Stop at 60 minutes.** Write "stop" under the last line. Note the question and part where you stopped. Leave the paper as it is: you finish in session 2. Don't mark yet.
5. **After stopping:** list which parts you found hardest or ran out of time on.

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Match the mark to the effort:** 1 mark = one fact; 3 marks = three separate points or one point developed; no need to write lots for a 1-marker.
- **Describe with evidence:** from a graph or map, quote **figures and units** and the trend.
- **Explain:** use "because", "so", "which leads to". A reason must be linked to the effect.
- **7-mark answers:** state a point, support with a **named example with facts** (place, date, figure), and give a **link or judgement**. Aim for 3 clear developed paragraphs or points.
- **Use the resource:** if the question says "refer to Fig. 2", quote it; "suggest" means use the resource plus your knowledge.
- **Named examples:** use place names and facts (e.g. a named earthquake, city or river). Write them out rather than "a city in an LIC".
- **Show calculations:** and give units.
- If stuck: write something on every part. Blank means zero.

## Checklist while marking

(Do this in session 2, once you have finished.) For each part:
- Did I answer the **command word**?
- Did I give the **right number of points** for the marks?
- Did I use **data** from the resource when asked?
- Did the long answer have a **named example** with facts?

Mark lightly now: tally the questions you finished and the parts you left or guessed at.

## Quick-fire revision

- Theme 1: **natural increase** = birth rate − death rate; **population pyramid** shapes; **push and pull factors** in migration.
- Theme 1: **population density** = people ÷ area; sparse vs dense areas.
- Theme 1: settlement site and function; **urbanisation** causes and problems.
- Theme 2: **earthquakes and volcanoes**: plate margins (destructive, constructive, conservative).
- Theme 2: **river** erosion, transport, deposition; flooding and management.
- Theme 2: **coasts**: waves, erosion, longshore drift, management.
- Theme 2: weather instruments; equatorial and hot desert climates.
- Theme 2: rainforest layers and deforestation.
- Theme 3: **GNI per capita** and HDI; globalisation and TNCs.
- Theme 3: intensive/extensive, subsistence/commercial; tourism benefits and costs.
- Theme 3: renewable vs non-renewable energy; water supply; climate change.
- Always check: **named example, figures, explanation.**
