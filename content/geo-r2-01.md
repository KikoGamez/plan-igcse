# Past paper · Paper 1 timed

> **Goal:** Sit a complete Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Geography Paper 1 (Physical geography) in one go, in 70 minutes, so you practise choosing questions, pacing and writing the 8-mark answers under real exam conditions.

## Before you start

- **Paper:** Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Geography (4GE1) **Paper 1: Physical geography, 4GE1/01**. Use a real paper you have not seen: the **June or November series** past papers (for example November 2024 or June 2024) or the Sample Assessment Materials, all on the Pearson Qualifications website (Geography, International GCSE 2017, Exam materials). Print the **question paper and the Resource Booklet**. Put the mark scheme away: you mark in the last session.
- **Timer:** 70 minutes. This is slightly longer than an hour: plan for it. Stop at 70 minutes even if unfinished.
- **Equipment:** black pen, pencil, ruler, **calculator** (allowed), eraser. No notes, no phone.
- **Space:** quiet desk, as in the mock. Read the instructions on the front cover first.
- **Choose before you start:** know which two Section A topics (river, coastal, hazardous) you will answer and which Section B enquiry matches the fieldwork you did at school. Decide this from your revision strengths; you may change after reading the paper for 2 minutes.

## Exam format reminder

- **Paper 1: 1 hour 10 minutes, 70 marks, 40% of the qualification.** Calculator allowed. A Resource Booklet with the figures comes with the paper.
- **Section A (50 marks): answer TWO questions of three** (Q1 River environments, Q2 Coastal environments, Q3 Hazardous environments), **25 marks each**. Each question ends with an **8-mark** extended answer using a figure ("You must refer to the resource in your answer").
- **Section B (20 marks): answer ONE question of three** (Q4 river, Q5 coastal, Q6 hazardous): fieldwork questions. Some use **your own** enquiry (state the title, evaluate sampling); others use **unfamiliar data** (plot a point, calculate % increase, give a conclusion, define "anomaly").
- Question types: multiple-choice (put a cross in the box), "identify", "define", "explain one/two" (2–4 marks), "suggest" with a figure (4), "analyse" (8) and calculations (2).
- Spec content: hydrological cycle and drainage basins, river processes and landforms, flooding and water management; coastal processes and landforms, ecosystems (mangroves/coral/salt marsh), flooding and coastal management; hazards (earthquakes, volcanoes, tropical cyclones), measurement, impacts and management. Case studies: developed and developing/emerging country for each topic you answer.

## How to do it

1. **0–3 min** – Read the front cover and the Resource Booklet. Tick the two Section A questions and one Section B question you will do. Write start time and end time (70 min later).
2. **3–30 min** – Section A, first question (25 marks, aim for 25 min including the 8-marker). Pace of about 1 minute per mark. Spend about 8–9 minutes on the 8-mark question.
3. **30–55 min** – Section A, second question (25 marks, about 25 min).
4. **55–68 min** – Section B (20 marks, about 15 min): short items first (sampling, equipment, calculation, plot), then the 8-mark evaluation.
5. **68–70 min** – Check: answers to MCQs, units and working in calculations, that every question you chose has an answer. Put a cross in the box for the question you answered.
6. **At 70 minutes stop.** Write "70" under your last line. Note any question you ran out of time on. Do **not** mark until session 4.

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Command words.** *Identify/State* = one word or phrase. *Define* = give the meaning. *Describe* = what it looks like or shows, with data. *Explain* = reason with "because/so". *Suggest* = apply knowledge to an unfamiliar situation. *Analyse* = break into parts, link causes and effects, and use the resource. *Evaluate/Assess* = weigh strengths and weaknesses and reach a judgement.
- **Case studies:** "For a named ..." means you must **name** the place and give **specific facts** (dates, figures, locations). Without a name you get few marks.
- **"Explain two" for 4 marks** = two ideas, each with a development. **"Explain one" for 3** = idea + development + further link.
- **Resource questions** (8 marks): quote or paraphrase the figure at least twice, with data. Then explain what causes the pattern, using your knowledge. Use a short final judgement.
- **MCQs:** cross one box only; if you change your mind, put a line through the first box and cross a new one. Do not leave any blank.
- **Calculations** (range, % change): show working. Percentage change = change ÷ original × 100.
- **Fieldwork (Section B):** refer to **your** enquiry for the personal questions. Name the equipment, the sites, the sampling method and say how weaknesses affected results. For unfamiliar-data questions plot accurately and describe using numbers.
- **Spelling of key terms:** attrition, abrasion, hydraulic action, deposition, epicentre, longshore drift. Draw small labelled diagrams if they save words.

## Checklist while marking

(You will mark in session 4. For now check only:)
- Did you finish all three questions (two A + one B) in 70 minutes? Note where you were at 30 and 55 minutes.
- Did you leave any 8-mark answer blank or very short? Star it.
- Did you give a **named case study** wherever the question asked "for a named..."?
- Did you refer to the **resource** in each "analyse" question?
- Did you show working in every calculation and add units?

## Quick-fire revision

- Hydrological cycle stores: atmosphere, ice, ground, rivers, oceans; flows: evaporation, condensation, precipitation, infiltration, throughflow, runoff.
- River processes: erosion (hydraulic action, abrasion, attrition, solution), transport (traction, saltation, suspension, solution), deposition.
- River features: waterfall (hard rock over soft rock; plunge pool, gorge), meander, oxbow lake, floodplain, levées.
- Flood management: hard engineering (dams, embankments, straightening) v soft (floodplain zoning, afforestation, flood warnings).
- Coastal erosion: hydraulic action, abrasion, attrition; weathering and mass movement. Landforms: headland, cave, arch, stack, stump; beach, spit, bar.
- Constructive waves (strong swash, build beaches) v destructive (strong backwash, erode).
- Coastal management: hard (sea walls, groynes, rock armour) v soft (beach nourishment, managed retreat); conflict between users.
- Ecosystems: mangroves, coral reefs, salt marshes; threats; protection.
- Earthquakes: focus, epicentre, plate boundaries; Richter and Mercalli measures; primary and secondary effects.
- Volcanoes: crater, vent, magma chamber; pyroclastic flows, lava, ash; reasons people live near them.
- Tropical cyclones: warm sea above 26–27°C, low wind shear, latitude 5–30°; storm surge and flooding.
- Fieldwork: sampling (random/systematic/stratified), equipment, risk, accuracy; % increase = change ÷ original × 100; anomaly = does not fit the pattern.
