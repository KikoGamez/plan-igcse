# Past paper · Paper 2 Geographical Skills timed

> **Goal:** Do the map, graph and data questions of a real Cambridge IGCSE Geography **Paper 2 Geographical Skills** under timed conditions, then mark them with the mark scheme to find which skills are costing you marks.

## Before you start

- **Paper:** Cambridge IGCSE Geography (0460) **Paper 2: Geographical Skills**. Use a real past paper, for example **May/June 2025 Paper 22 (0460/22)** or **October/November 2024 Paper 22**, or another variant (21, 22, 23) from your teacher or the Cambridge Schools Support Hub. Print the **question paper and the Insert** (it holds the map extract, photographs and figures). Use the mark scheme only at the end.
- **Equipment:** pencil, sharp **ruler** (with mm), **protractor**, calculator, eraser, coloured pencils, black pen, timer. No notes.
- **Timer:** the full paper is **1 h 30 min for 60 marks** (1.5 minutes per mark). For this session do **about half the paper (about 30 marks) in 45 minutes**. Start with the **map-reading questions**, then do as many graph and data questions as fit. Note which questions you chose.
- **Before you start:** read the **map key** and **scale** from the Insert. Write the scale at the top of your paper.

## Exam format reminder

- **Paper 2: 1 hour 30 minutes, 60 marks, about 27.5% of the qualification.** Answer **all** questions.
- The paper tests **skills** on topics from Paper 1: topographic **map** (usually 1:25 000), **photographs**, **graphs**, **tables**, and sketches. It also includes a longer geographical **investigation** or data-based question near the end.
- Typical tasks: grid references (4 and 6 figures), distance with scale, direction, bearings, height and relief from contours, gradient, describing land use and settlement, completing graphs (bar, line, scatter), calculating averages and percentages, describing a pattern, and drawing a labelled sketch.
- Most questions are 1–4 marks. Longer ones (up to 6–8 marks) need described data plus a conclusion.

## How to do it

1. **0–3 min:** Check the map key, scale, north arrow, and the contour interval. Skim the questions.
2. **3–25 min:** **Map questions.** Use the Insert and a ruler. Show your measurement or working next to each answer: marks may be given for method.
3. **25–45 min:** **Graph and data questions.** Do completion tasks first (they are quick marks), then the description questions.
4. **At 45 min: stop.** If you have more time at home, finish the rest later in a separate sitting (and note it).
5. **45–60 min: Mark.** Use the mark scheme. Tick correct points; write the right answer in a different colour. Add up the marks and divide by the marks attempted.
6. **Make an error log:** list each lost mark by skill (grid reference, scale, bearings, contour reading, graph drawing, describing, evaluating).

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Grid references:** eastings first, then northings; for six figures, estimate tenths carefully.
- **Distance:** measure to the nearest mm and use the **scale** in the key (1 cm = 250 m for 1:25 000). Give **km or m**. For rivers and roads, use a string or the edge of paper.
- **Bearings:** place the protractor centre on the first place, measure clockwise from north, give **three digits**.
- **Contours:** read the interval; close together = steep; the height of a point between lines is estimated.
- **Gradient:** vertical ÷ horizontal in the same units.
- **Graph drawing:** use a sharp pencil, plot points exactly, join with a ruler (line graph) or draw bars to the correct height, and label.
- **Describing:** **trend first, then figures, then anomaly.** Compare (higher/lower) and quote units.
- **Photographs:** use the words foreground, background, left, right; link to the map or the question topic.
- **Data analysis:** give **calculation steps**; percentage change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100.
- **Check every answer for units** (m, km, °C, %).

## Checklist while marking

- Grid reference: easting before northing? Right number of digits?
- Distance: measured accurately, scale applied, units?
- Bearing/direction: protractor from north; clockwise; three figures?
- Contours and relief: interval, steepness, valley or ridge correct?
- Graph completion: points plotted to the right scale, line or bar drawn, labelled?
- Description: trend, figures, units, anomaly?
- Calculations: working shown, correct units?
- Which **skill** lost the most marks? Make a list of skills to practise.
- Redo **two** questions that you got wrong, in your own words.

## Quick-fire revision

- **Eastings first** (along), then northings (up).
- **1:25 000:** 4 cm = 1 km. **1:50 000:** 2 cm = 1 km.
- **Bearing** = angle from north, **clockwise**, three figures.
- **Close contours** = steep; **spaced** = gentle; **V-shaped** points uphill = valley.
- **Gradient** = height difference ÷ horizontal distance.
- **Mean** = total ÷ number; **range** = highest − lowest; **median** = middle.
- **Percentage change** = (new − old) ÷ old × 100.
- **Pie chart:** 1% = 3.6°.
- **Scatter graph:** line of best fit; positive/negative/none.
- **Describe a graph:** trend + figures + anomaly.
- **Choropleth/flow maps:** read the key first.
- **Population pyramid:** wide base = high birth rate; narrow top = low life expectancy.
- **Photographs:** foreground / middle / background.
- **Show your working** — method marks.
