# Exam format and themes A–E

> **In the exam:** Pearson Edexcel International GCSE German (4GN1) has three papers, one per skill group, all sat at one level (no Foundation/Higher tiers). **Paper 1 Listening** (25%), **Paper 2 Reading and Writing** (50%) and **Paper 3 Speaking** (25%). Every question in every paper is drawn from the same five themes, so knowing the themes and what each paper asks you to do is the quickest way to pick up marks. This topic is the map for the rest of your revision.

## What you need to know

- The three papers: name, length, marks, weighting.
- The question types in each paper and what the marks are given for.
- The five themes (A–E) and their sub-topics, and which sub-topics are **not** used for the Speaking picture.
- How the writing and speaking tasks are marked (content, range of language, accuracy, spontaneity).
- Basic exam technique: how to use reading time, how to read the question, how to avoid losing easy marks.

## Notes

### The three papers at a glance

| Paper | What | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 Listening** | 7 recorded extracts, each played **twice** | 30 min, plus 5 min reading time | 40 | 25% |
| **2 Reading and Writing** | Section A: reading (5 passages). Section B: writing and grammar (Q6, Q7, Q8) | 1 h 45 min | 80 (40 + 40) | 50% |
| **3 Speaking** | Task A picture, Task B and Task C conversations, recorded | 8–10 min | 40 | 25% |

(Check the timings and marks on your own paper: they are the figures in the 2017 specification, but your teacher has the final word on how the mocks are run.)

### Paper 1: Listening

- Seven extracts: monologues, dialogues and conversations between three speakers. They start with short, easy statements and get longer and harder.
- Question types: **multiple choice** (four options, A–D), **multiple matching**, **note-taking** (a few words in German), **table completion** and **gap-fill**.
- Use the **5 minutes of reading time** to read every question and underline what is asked (who? when? why? how much?). Predict the type of answer: a number, a time, a place, an opinion.
- Play 1: answer what you can. Play 2: check and fill gaps. Never leave a blank, because there is no penalty for a guess.
- Numbers, times and dates are favourite traps: *dreizehn / dreißig*, *sechzehn / sechzig*, *halb acht* = 7:30, *Viertel nach drei* = 3:15, *Viertel vor drei* = 2:45.
- Listen for **negatives and changes of mind**: *nicht*, *kein*, *aber*, *leider*, *eigentlich*, *früher*. The first thing you hear is often not the answer.
- When you note-take, short answers in German are fine. **Spelling is not penalised** as long as the meaning is clear. Do not copy long chunks.

### Paper 2, Section A: Reading (40 marks)

- Five passages from authentic sources (adverts, emails, articles, blogs, interviews). One is a **literary text**: you need no literature knowledge, just the language.
- Types: multiple choice, matching, grid/table completion, note-taking, gap-fill and short answers. It gets harder; the last reading question needs a **written answer in German**.
- You may **lift** words from the text if they answer the question, but only the relevant part. A whole sentence copied with the answer buried in it can lose the mark.
- Look at the question first, then find the part of the text. Don't translate the whole passage.
- Watch for **false friends** and words that look English: *bekommen* = to get (not become), *Gymnasium* = grammar school, *der Chef* = boss, *sensibel* = sensitive, *eventuell* = possibly, *aktuell* = current.
- Use clues to guess unknown words: words you know inside a compound (*Hausaufgabe* = Haus + Aufgabe), the prefix, the context, similarity to English (*der Apfel*, *das Wasser*).

### Paper 2, Section B: Writing and grammar (40 marks)

| Question | Task | Length |
|---|---|---|
| **Q6** | Short writing task on a familiar topic. You are given words/phrases in boxes that you must use | **60–75 words** |
| **Q7** | Extended writing. A **choice of three** tasks, each with four bullet points | **130–150 words** |
| **Q8** | **Grammar task**: complete a text with the correct form of verbs and adjectives (word in brackets) | gap-fill |

- Marks are given for **communication and content** (do you cover every bullet point?), **range of language** (tenses, opinions, connectives, variety of vocabulary) and **accuracy**.
- Cover **all four bullet points**. Include **past, present and future**, and give **opinions with reasons** (*weil*, *denn*).
- Keep to the word count. Writing far over 150 words in Q7 gains nothing and gives more chances to make errors.
- In Q8 the word in brackets does not always need to change: look at the **context** (subject, tense, case, gender) before you change anything.
- Leave 5 minutes at the end to check **verb position, endings, capital letters on nouns, spelling of ß/ä/ö/ü**.

### Paper 3: Speaking (40 marks)

| Task | Time | What happens | Marks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A: Picture** | 2–3 min | You bring **one picture of your own choice**. The examiner asks **five questions** about it (what you see, past/future events related to it, opinion, etc.). No presentation. | 12 (8 communication and content, 4 linguistic knowledge and accuracy) |
| **B and C: Conversations** | 3–3½ min each | Two different themes chosen at random by Pearson. Spontaneous questions, no break between B and C | 28 in total across B and C (12 communication and content, 8 interaction and spontaneity, 8 linguistic knowledge and accuracy) |

- The test is **recorded**, entirely in German, and you may not take notes in (only the picture).
- Your picture may come from any sub-topic **except A3, C3, C5, D2, E4** (these are not assessed in Task A, and the same five are not assessed in the conversations either).
- Tasks B and C are **different themes from your picture's theme**, so you need to be ready on all five.
- Marks for **interaction and spontaneity**: answer the question asked, extend your answer, react naturally, and ask or say something unprepared. A learnt speech that does not fit the question loses these marks.
- Give opinions and reasons, and show **past, present and future** tenses in each conversation.

### The five themes (A–E) and sub-topics

| Theme | Sub-topics |
|---|---|
| **A Home and abroad** | 1 Life in the town and rural life; 2 Holidays, tourist information and directions; 3 Services*; 4 Customs; 5 Everyday life, traditions and communities |
| **B Education and employment** | 1 School life and routine; 2 School rules and pressures; 3 School trips, events and exchanges; 4 Work, careers and volunteering; 5 Future plans |
| **C Personal life and relationships** | 1 House and home; 2 Daily routines and helping at home; 3 Role models*; 4 Relationships with family and friends; 5 Childhood* |
| **D The world around us** | 1 Environmental issues; 2 Weather and climate*; 3 Travel and transport; 4 The media; 5 Information and communication technology |
| **E Social activities, fitness and health** | 1 Special occasions; 2 Hobbies, interests, sports and exercise; 3 Shopping and money matters; 4 Accidents, injuries, common ailments and health issues*; 5 Food and drink |

\* Not used in Speaking.

### Grammar you are expected to use (that is the rest of Round 1)

- Present tense, including irregular, separable and reflexive verbs.
- Perfect and imperfect, future, conditional, modal verbs.
- Cases (nominative, accusative, dative, genitive), articles, adjective endings, prepositions.
- Word order, conjunctions (*weil, dass, wenn, obwohl*), pronouns and relative pronouns.

The specification is aimed at roughly CEFR levels A2–B1, so simple, **correct** German with a few well-chosen complex structures beats long, ambitious, wrong sentences.

## Key terms and definitions

- **Theme (Topic Area):** one of the five areas A–E that all papers draw on.
- **Sub-topic:** one of the five parts of a theme (e.g. A2 Holidays, tourist information and directions).
- **Lifting:** copying words from the reading text into an answer. Allowed only if you copy exactly the part that answers the question.
- **Multiple matching:** linking statements or people to the correct options.
- **Bullet points:** the four prompts in Q7. You must address every one.
- **Communication and content:** how fully and relevantly you answer the task.
- **Linguistic knowledge and accuracy:** range of vocabulary and structures, and how correct they are.
- **Interaction and spontaneity:** how naturally and flexibly you respond in the Speaking conversations.

## Worked examples

**1. Listening note-taking.** *Question: Was macht Lena am Wochenende? Give one detail.* (1 mark). The recording: *"Am Samstag wollte ich eigentlich ins Kino gehen, aber meine Freundin ist krank. Deshalb gehen wir jetzt schwimmen."*
Model answer: **schwimmen gehen**. The first thing said (Kino) was cancelled. One mark is for the final plan only.

**2. Q7 planning (130–150 words).** *Task: Schreibe über deine Schule. Bullet points: was du in der Schule am liebsten machst / warum / was du gestern in der Schule gemacht hast / was du nach der Schule machen möchtest.*
Plan one paragraph per bullet: present + opinion (*Ich mag Sport am liebsten, weil es Spaß macht.*), a reason, perfect tense (*Gestern habe ich Mathe und Englisch gehabt.*), and *möchte/werde* for the future (*Nach der Schule möchte ich Medizin studieren.*). That covers all four bullets, three tenses and a reason, which is how top marks are reached.

**3. Q8 grammar.** *Meine Schwester (wohnen) in einer (klein) Stadt.* → **wohnt**, **kleinen**. Subject is *sie* (third person singular) so *wohnt*; *in* + dative (location) with feminine *Stadt* and an indefinite article gives *einer kleinen Stadt*.

## Common mistakes

- Starting to write without using the **reading time** in Listening to underline question words.
- Writing the first thing you hear in Listening when the speaker then changes their mind.
- Copying a whole sentence in Reading instead of just the answer.
- Missing a **bullet point** in Q7 (it can cost a whole band of marks), or writing under 130 words.
- Using only the present tense in writing and speaking.
- Learning a memorised speech for Speaking Tasks B/C instead of practising answering questions.
- Choosing a picture for Task A from A3, C3, C5, D2 or E4 sub-topics (not allowed).
- Changing the word in brackets in Q8 without checking the context, or forgetting that it sometimes stays the same.

## Plan for this hour

- **10 min:** read the paper table and the theme table. Cover them and write them from memory.
- **15 min:** open a past Listening paper (or the SAM) and use only the 5-minute reading time: underline question words and predict answer types for all questions.
- **15 min:** read a Q7 task and a Q8 task; plan Q7 as four paragraphs (one per bullet) and do Q8 with the context checks.
- **10 min:** pick a picture for Task A (not from A3, C3, C5, D2, E4) and write five questions an examiner might ask about it.
- **10 min:** do the practice questions and the quiz.
