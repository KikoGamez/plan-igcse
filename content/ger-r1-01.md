# Exam format and topic areas A–E

> **In the exam:** Cambridge IGCSE German (Foreign Language) **0525** has **four compulsory papers**, each worth **25%** of the grade: **Paper 1 Listening** (about 50 minutes, 40 marks), **Paper 2 Reading** (1 hour, 45 marks), **Paper 3 Speaking** (about 10 minutes, 40 marks, marked by your teacher and moderated by Cambridge) and **Paper 4 Writing** (1 hour, 45 marks). There are **no tiers**: every candidate sits the same papers and can get grades A* to G. All four papers draw on the same five topic areas A–E, so one vocabulary list pays off four times. Dictionaries are not allowed.

## What you need to know

- The four papers: duration, marks, question types and what each tests.
- The five topic areas A–E and their sub-topics.
- The task types: multiple choice and matching (Papers 1 and 2), short answers **in German** (Paper 2), role play and topic conversations (Paper 3), form, directed writing and extended writing (Paper 4).
- How marks are earned in Speaking and Writing: communication first, then range and accuracy of language.
- Command language: instructions in the papers are in German or English, so know the German question words and instructions.

## Notes

### The four papers at a glance

| Paper | Skill | Time | Marks | Weighting | Question types |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Listening | about 50 min (includes 6 min to transfer answers) | 40 | 25% | 37 multiple-choice and matching questions; each recording played **twice** |
| **2** | Reading | 1 hour | 45 | 25% | six question groups: matching and multiple choice, plus short answers in German |
| **3** | Speaking | about 10 min (plus 10 min preparation) | 40 | 25% | one role play and two topic conversations |
| **4** | Writing | 1 hour | 45 | 25% | form (Q1), directed writing (Q2), extended writing (Q3) |

All written answers are in **German**.

### Paper 1: Listening (40 marks)

| Questions | Marks | Task |
|---|---|---|
| 1–8 | 8 | Short texts (announcements, phone messages, news items, dialogues); multiple choice with **four options** |
| 9–14 | 6 | A monologue or dialogue with factual information; multiple choice, four options |
| 15–19 | 5 | An informal conversation: **match** people, places, items or activities with statements |
| 20–28 | 9 | A conversation, discussion or interview (or two short ones on one theme); multiple choice with **three options** |
| 29–34 | 6 | A conversation, discussion or interview; multiple choice, four options |
| 35–37 | 6 | A conversation; **five options**, you choose the **two** that are true (each question is 2 marks, one for each correct option) |

- You answer by **ticking or choosing**, so you do not need to write German in Paper 1. There is a transfer time at the end to copy your answers onto the answer sheet. Write clearly and never leave a blank: there is no penalty for a wrong guess.
- Use the time before each recording to read the options and underline the key word in each. Numbers, times, prices and negatives (*kein, nicht, nie*) are the usual traps: the speaker often mentions two options and corrects one (*Nein, nicht um acht, sondern um neun*).
- First playing: answer what you can. Second playing: check and fill the gaps.

### Paper 2: Reading (45 marks)

| Group | Marks | Task |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 | **Match** short statements with the correct pictures |
| 2 | 5 | **Match** signs, notices, ads or messages from one setting (e.g. a station or a shop) with explanations |
| 3 | 7 | Multiple choice (three options) on a short email, message, postcard or letter |
| 4 | 12 | A longer email, message, letter or blog: **short answers in German** |
| 5 | 5 | **Match** people's needs, interests or skills with places, events, services or activities (one common theme) |
| 6 | 11 | A longer article: **short answers in German** |

- In groups 4 and 6 you are marked on **understanding**: a short, clear answer in German with the key detail is enough. Copy only the part of the text that answers the question, and do not copy a whole sentence that also contains a wrong detail.
- Questions follow the order of the text. Look for **synonyms and paraphrases**: the question says *Wann beginnt der Kurs?* and the text says *Der Kurs startet im September.*
- You may meet unknown words. Use the context, the sentence structure, and words that look like English (*Problem, Information, Musik, Kaffee*).

### Paper 3: Speaking (40 marks)

Your teacher conducts and records the test. It lasts about 10 minutes, after **10 minutes of preparation** for the role play (you may **not** make notes).

| Part | Time | Marks | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | about 30 sec | not marked | Easy questions to settle you |
| **Role play** | about 2 min | **10** (5 questions × 2) | You answer five transactional questions in a situation from your card (e.g. buying tickets, ordering food, booking a room) |
| **Two topic conversations** | about 4 min each | **30**: 15 for *Communication* + 15 for *Quality of Language*, for both conversations together | Unprepared conversations on two topics from A–E, e.g. family and friends, school, free time, holidays, work |

- **Role play, per question:** **2** = the information is communicated and the language is appropriate and accurate (minor errors such as adjective endings and prepositions are allowed); **1** = partly communicated or ambiguous; **0** = nothing creditable. Give a **full, clear, polite answer** to each prompt, and use *Sie* when the role is formal.
- **Communication (15):** the top level means you answer confidently, give relevant information, **develop ideas and opinions, and justify and explain** answers. Do not stop after one short sentence: add a reason (*weil …*), an example or a contrast.
- **Quality of Language (15):** accurate use of a **wide range of the structures in the syllabus** (different tenses, subordinate clauses, the conditional), a wide range of vocabulary, good pronunciation, fluency and intonation.
- If you do not understand a question, the examiner can use an alternative question; ask politely: *Können Sie das bitte wiederholen?*

### Paper 4: Writing (45 marks)

| Question | Marks | Task |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | 5 | **Form-filling**: single words or short phrases from a given context (name, address, dates, reasons, etc.) |
| **2** | 12 | **Directed writing**, about **80–90 words**, on a familiar everyday topic |
| **3** | 28 | **Extended writing**, about **130–140 words**: you **choose one of two tasks**, an email/letter or an article/blog |

- Question 3 is worth more than half the paper. A plan is worth 2 minutes: cover **every bullet point**, and aim for **different time frames** (what you did, what you do, what you will do or would like to do), opinions with reasons, and linked sentences (*und, aber, weil, dass, wenn, obwohl*).
- Marking looks at **communication** (all bullets covered, relevant, developed) and **language** (range of vocabulary and structures, accuracy). Keep to the word count: far fewer words usually means too little to earn marks, and far more gives you more chances to make errors.
- Check your work for the Big Five: **verb in second place, verb at the end after *weil/dass*, capital letters on nouns, noun genders/cases, verb endings**.

### Topic areas A–E

| Area | Title | Sub-topics (from the syllabus) |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Everyday activities | time expressions (time, days, months, seasons); food and drink; the human body and health; travel and transport |
| **B** | Personal and social life | self, family and friends; in the home; colours; clothes and accessories; leisure time (hobbies, sport) |
| **C** | The world around us | people and places (continents, countries, nationalities, compass points); the natural world, environment, climate and weather; communications and technology; the built environment (buildings, services, urban areas, shopping); measurements; materials |
| **D** | The world of work | education (school, subjects, learning); work (jobs, careers, the workplace) |
| **E** | The international world | countries, nationalities and languages; culture, customs, faiths and celebrations |

The sub-topics are examples, not a fixed list, and the syllabus includes a vocabulary list in German by topic. The tasks are always set in **predictable, everyday situations**. In every topic, prepare: **likes and dislikes with a reason, what you did, what you are going to do, and your opinion**.

### Grammar the syllabus expects

The syllabus lists, among others: all cases of articles, adjective endings, comparatives and superlatives, personal, reflexive and relative pronouns, regular and irregular, separable and modal verbs, the present, imperfect, perfect, future and pluperfect, the conditional (*würde* + infinitive), the imperative, word order in main, subordinate and relative clauses, and the prepositions with their cases. **Indirect speech and the passive are for recognition only.** The next five topics revise this list.

## Key terms and definitions

- **Topic area:** one of the five broad themes (A–E) used to set every task in every paper.
- **Role play:** a Speaking task in which you play a part and answer five transactional questions from your card.
- **Topic conversation:** an unprepared Speaking conversation on a topic area (about four minutes each).
- **Directed writing:** Writing Q2, where the prompt tells you what to write about (about 80–90 words).
- **Extended writing:** Writing Q3, where you choose an email/letter or an article/blog (about 130–140 words).
- **Communication:** how fully and relevantly you get your message across, including developing and justifying opinions.
- **Quality of language:** the range and accuracy of your vocabulary and structures, plus pronunciation and fluency in Speaking.
- **Transfer time:** the six minutes at the end of Paper 1 for copying your answers onto the answer sheet.

## Worked examples

**1. Paper 1-style.** *You hear:* "Der Zug nach München fährt nicht um 8 Uhr, sondern um 8 Uhr 30, und zwar von Gleis 5." *Question: When does the train leave?* Options: A 8.00, B 8.15, C 8.30, D 8.50. → **C**. The speaker names 8 Uhr but corrects it with *nicht … sondern*. Marks go to the listener who waits for the correction. (Note: *8 Uhr 30* is half past eight, not 8.50.)

**2. Paper 2 group 4-style short answer.** *Text:* "Am Samstag bin ich mit meiner Schwester in die Stadt gegangen. Wir wollten neue Schuhe kaufen, aber die Geschäfte waren zu voll." *Question: Warum haben sie keine Schuhe gekauft?* Model: **Die Geschäfte waren zu voll.** (1 mark.) Do not write *Sie wollten neue Schuhe kaufen*: it does not answer the question. Spelling and grammar matter only if the meaning becomes unclear.

**3. Role play answer (2 marks).** *Prompt on card: Sag dem Kellner, was du essen möchtest.* → **Ich möchte bitte eine Pizza mit Pilzen und ein Glas Wasser.** This is complete, polite and correct (2). *Pizza.* alone gives the information but is not appropriate language, so it risks losing the second mark.

**4. Writing Q3 plan.** Task: an email to a German friend about your last holiday. Plan before writing: (1) where you went and with whom (perfect: *Ich bin mit meiner Familie nach Italien gefahren*), (2) what the place was like (imperfect: *Das Wetter war toll*), (3) one activity with an opinion and reason (*Ich fand das super, weil …*), (4) what you will do next summer (*werden* or *möchte* + infinitive). Four boxes, four tenses or structures, about 135 words.

## Common mistakes

- Leaving Paper 1 answers blank: guess, there is no penalty.
- Choosing the first option you hear in Listening: the speaker often corrects it.
- Writing long copied chunks in Reading groups 4 and 6: answer only the question.
- Answering role-play questions with one word: give a full, polite sentence.
- Giving short answers in the conversations (*Ja. Gut.*): add a reason, an example or a contrast.
- Writing Q3 without covering every bullet, or far over or under the word count.
- Forgetting to learn nouns with their gender (*die Schule, der Lehrer, das Zimmer*).
- Using *du* with an adult in a role play or a formal email: use *Sie* and *Sehr geehrte Frau …*.

## Plan for this hour

- **15 min:** read the four-paper table and the Paper 1 and Paper 2 tables; cover them and recall marks, times and question types.
- **15 min:** learn the Speaking and Writing mark structure; write the plan boxes (bullet, tense, opinion, future) for one Writing Q3 task.
- **15 min:** write the five topic areas with three sub-topics each, and list ten key German words per area you are unsure of.
- **10 min:** practice questions.
- **5 min:** quiz; note any paper detail you got wrong.
