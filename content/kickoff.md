## 2. Where the official material lives

**Syllabuses (Cambridge).** Go to **cambridgeinternational.org**, search for the subject code (e.g. "0610"), open the subject page and download the syllabus **for the year of your real exam** (the page lists syllabuses by exam years, e.g. "2026–2028"). Also download the **specimen papers** and any **example candidate responses** listed there.

**Past papers (Cambridge).** Full past papers, mark schemes, examiner reports and audio files (French listening) are on the **School Support Hub**, which your school accesses with a teacher login. Ask your teachers for them now — they are used to this request. A limited selection is public on the subject pages of cambridgeinternational.org. Avoid random sites that rename or edit papers; if you use them, check the paper code and series on the front page.

**Statistics (Pearson Edexcel).** Go to **qualifications.pearson.com**, search "International GCSE Statistics", and download the specification, the sample assessment materials and the past papers with mark schemes and examiners' reports (Pearson makes recent ones available after a delay; some may be restricted to teachers).

**What a "set" is.** For every paper you plan to sit: question paper (QP), mark scheme (MS), and examiner report (ER). Also inserts (English, Spanish reading texts) and audio + transcript (French listening).

**Do not do the most recent papers now.** Keep the two most recent series of every paper untouched for Round 2 (28 Dec–8 Jan). Use older papers for Round 1 practice.

## 3. Folder structure

Create one folder on your computer (and a copy in the cloud):

```
IGCSE Mocks 2027/
├── 00 Plan and error log/
├── Maths 0580/
│   ├── Syllabus/
│   ├── Past papers/        (e.g. 0580_s24_qp_22.pdf, 0580_s24_ms_22.pdf)
│   ├── Round 2 – do not open/
│   └── My notes/
├── Statistics 4ST1/
├── Biology 0610/
... (same four subfolders for every subject)
```

Name files the Cambridge way: **code_series_type_paper**. Series: s = May/June, w = Oct/Nov, m = Feb/March. So `0620_w23_qp_42` = Chemistry, Oct/Nov 2023, question paper, Paper 4 variant 2.

Paper folder: one ring binder or plastic folder per subject for printed papers you have done and marked.

## 4. The error log

The error log is the most valuable document you will make. Every mark you lose goes in it, and you revise from it in the last week. Use a spreadsheet (Google Sheets or Excel) with **one tab per subject** and these columns:

| Date | Paper / question | Topic | What I wrote | Correct answer / method | Type of error | Fix (rule to remember) | Anki card made? | Re-tested (date, ✓/✗) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18 Nov | 0625_s23_42 Q3(b) | Specific heat capacity | Used 25 °C not Δθ = 15 °C | ΔE = mcΔθ with temperature *change* | Method | Always calculate Δθ first and write it down | ✓ | 25 Nov ✓ |

**Error types** (use exactly these four so you can filter):
- **Knowledge** – I did not know the fact or definition.
- **Method** – I knew the topic but used the wrong method or formula.
- **Careless** – arithmetic slip, misread question, forgot units, wrong significant figures.
- **Technique** – did not answer the command word, too vague, ran out of time, not enough points for the marks.

**Rules for using it:**
- Fill it in straight after marking, never "later".
- One row per lost mark or per lost part-question.
- Every Knowledge and Method row becomes an Anki card.
- Every Sunday (weekly review) you re-test a selection of rows and mark them ✓ or ✗.
- In the final sprint (9–10 January) the error log is your main revision source.

## 5. Anki: setting it up

Anki is a free flashcard app that uses spaced repetition: cards you get wrong come back soon, cards you know come back less often.

1. Install Anki on your computer from **apps.ankiweb.net** (free). AnkiDroid on Android is free; the iPhone app is paid — you can use the free **ankiweb.net** website on your phone instead.
2. Create an AnkiWeb account so your phone and computer sync.
3. Create one deck per subject: `IGCSE::Maths`, `IGCSE::Biology` … (the `::` makes sub-decks under one parent deck, so you can study all subjects in one go).
4. Settings: new cards per day around 20 per deck at first; do your reviews **every day** (10–20 minutes, e.g. on the bus or before bed).

### How to make good cards

| Rule | Bad card | Good card |
|---|---|---|
| One fact per card | "Everything about osmosis" | "Osmosis: define it (3 marks)" |
| Use mark-scheme wording on the back | "water moves" | "net movement of water molecules from a region of higher water potential to lower water potential through a partially permeable membrane" |
| Ask in the direction the exam asks | "Ba²⁺ → ?" only | Front: "Flame test colour for barium ions?" Back: "light green" |
| Make cards from your mistakes | — | Front: "Specific heat capacity: what temperature goes in the equation?" Back: "the change Δθ, not the final temperature" |
| Use cloze for lists and equations | — | "Moment = {{c1::force}} × {{c2::perpendicular distance from pivot}}" |
| For languages, both directions | "la mairie" only | Front: "town hall (FR)" Back: "la mairie" |

Keep cards short. If you cannot answer in 10 seconds, split the card.

## 6. How the plan works

| Phase | Dates | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Round 1 – topics** | now → 27 December | One topic per session. Each page has notes, worked examples, common mistakes and practice questions. You make flashcards and log errors. |
| **Weekly review** | every Sunday, last hour | All flashcards from the week + 10 mixed questions + update the error log. |
| **Round 2 – past papers** | 28 December → 8 January | Full or half papers under timed conditions, marked with the official mark scheme. Every lost mark goes into the error log. |
| **Final sprint** | 9–10 January | Summary sheets, formula lists, error log, prepared speaking answers. Nothing new. Early night on the 10th. |
| **Mocks** | from 11 January | |

**How each Round 1 hour works:** read the notes actively (cover and recall), make 5–10 cards, do the practice questions *before* opening the answers, mark them, log errors.

**If you fall behind:** do not skip the weekly review and do not skip Round 2. Instead, do the practice questions of the missed topic only and read its "Common mistakes" section.
