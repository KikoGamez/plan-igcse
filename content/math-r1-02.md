# Number: fractions, percentages and ratio

> **In the exam:** In **both papers**. Paper 2 tests fraction arithmetic without a calculator (e.g. 2⅔ ÷ 1⅗, "show all your working"), ratio sharing and simple percentages, worth 2–3 marks each. Paper 4 usually opens with a long "context" question (a shop, a trip, a business) of 10–15 marks built from percentages, reverse percentages, ratio, proportion and money. Reverse percentage appears very often and is a common place to lose marks.

## What you need to know

- Use the four operations on fractions, including mixed numbers, and on decimals and negative numbers; use the correct order of operations.
- Calculate a given percentage of a quantity; express one quantity as a percentage of another.
- Calculate percentage increase or decrease; calculate with simple and compound interest (see the standard form and bounds page for compound interest).
- (Extended) Calculate using reverse percentages, e.g. finding the cost price given the selling price and the percentage profit.
- Understand and use ratio and proportion: simplify ratios, divide a quantity in a given ratio, use direct and inverse proportion.
- Use common measures of rate; calculate with money and convert between currencies.

## Notes

### Fractions (non-calculator)

| Operation | Method | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Add / subtract | common denominator | 3/5 + 2/3 = 9/15 + 10/15 = 19/15 = 1 4/15 |
| Multiply | top × top, bottom × bottom (cancel first) | 4/9 × 3/8 = 1/6 |
| Divide | keep, change, flip | 3/4 ÷ 9/10 = 3/4 × 10/9 = 5/6 |
| Mixed numbers | change to improper fractions first | 2⅔ = 8/3 |

- "Show all your working" means write each improper fraction and the common denominator; an answer alone gets no marks.
- Give answers in their **simplest form** (and as a mixed number if the question uses mixed numbers).
- Fraction of an amount: 3/8 of 240 = 240 ÷ 8 × 3 = 90.

### Percentages with multipliers

| Change | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| increase by 35% | × 1.35 |
| decrease by 15% | × 0.85 |
| increase by 4% | × 1.04 |
| decrease by 2.5% | × 0.975 |

- **Percentage of an amount:** 18% of 650 = 0.18 × 650 = 117.
- **One quantity as a percentage of another:** (part ÷ whole) × 100. Same units first.
- **Percentage change** = (change ÷ **original**) × 100.
  Profit/loss percentage uses the **cost price** as the original.
- **Successive changes multiply:** a 20% decrease then a 20% increase gives 0.8 × 1.2 = 0.96, an overall **4% decrease** (not 0%).

### Reverse percentages

You know the value **after** a percentage change and need the original.

**original = new value ÷ multiplier**

- After a 15% reduction a coat costs $68. Original = 68 ÷ 0.85 = **$80**.
- A price including 20% tax is $54. Price before tax = 54 ÷ 1.2 = $45.

Never take the percentage of the new value (15% of 68 added back gives $78.20, which is wrong). Check by applying the change to your answer.

### Ratio

- Simplify by dividing by the HCF; same units first (40 cm : 2 m = 40 : 200 = 1 : 5).
- Form 1 : n → divide both parts by the first part (4 : 10 = 1 : 2.5).
- **Sharing in a ratio:** add the parts, find one part, multiply.
  Share $720 in the ratio 3 : 4 : 5 → 12 parts, 1 part = $60 → $180, $240, $300. Check they add to 720.
- **Given a difference or one share:** "B gets $45 more than A" in 2 : 3 : 5 → 3 − 2 = 1 part = $45.
- **Combining ratios:** a : b = 3 : 5 and b : c = 2 : 7. Make b the same (LCM of 5 and 2 is 10): a : b = 6 : 10 and b : c = 10 : 35 → a : b : c = 6 : 10 : 35.
- Ratio to fraction: in boys : girls = 3 : 5, the fraction of girls is 5/8.

### Direct and inverse proportion

| Type | Meaning | Method |
|---|---|---|
| Direct | both increase at the same rate (double one → double the other) | find the value for **one** (unitary method) or use a scale factor |
| Inverse | one increases as the other decreases (double one → halve the other) | find the **total** (product), then divide |

- **Direct:** a recipe for 4 people uses 300 g flour. For 10 people: 300 ÷ 4 × 10 = 750 g.
- **Inverse:** 6 workers take 15 days. Total work = 6 × 15 = 90 worker-days. 9 workers: 90 ÷ 9 = 10 days.
- Best buy: compare cost per gram (or grams per cent).
- Currency: if €1 = $1.08, then $270 = 270 ÷ 1.08 = €250. Check the direction: euros are worth more, so you get fewer of them.
- Rates: speed = distance ÷ time; density = mass ÷ volume; pressure = force ÷ area. Watch units (minutes → hours: ÷ 60).

## Worked examples

**1.** Without using a calculator, work out 2⅔ ÷ 1⅗. Show all your working and give your answer as a mixed number in its simplest form. [3]

- 8/3 ÷ 8/5 — **M1** (both improper fractions)
- 8/3 × 5/8 — **M1** (reciprocal of the second fraction)
- = 5/3 = **1⅔** — **A1**

**2.** In a sale, all prices are reduced by 15%. A coat costs $68 in the sale. Calculate the original price. [2]

- 68 ÷ 0.85, or 68 ÷ 85 × 100 — **M1**
- **$80** — **A1**

**3.** Ali, Ben and Cara share some money in the ratio 2 : 3 : 5. Ben receives $45 more than Ali. Calculate the total amount of money. [2]

- 1 part = $45 — **M1**
- total = 10 × 45 = **$450** — **A1**

**4.** It takes 6 workers 15 days to build a wall. How long would 9 workers take, working at the same rate? [2]

- 6 × 15 = 90 — **M1**
- 90 ÷ 9 = **10 days** — **A1**

## Common mistakes

- Reverse percentages: finding the percentage of the **new** amount instead of dividing by the multiplier.
- Percentage change divided by the **new** value instead of the original.
- Adding successive percentages (20% up then 20% down is not "no change").
- In ratio sharing, dividing by the number of people instead of the total number of parts; or giving only one share when all are asked for.
- Not changing to the same units before writing a ratio.
- Treating inverse proportion as direct (more workers should take **less** time — sense-check).
- Non-calculator fractions: not showing the improper fractions or the common denominator, or giving an unsimplified answer.
- Rounding money in the middle of a calculation; give money to 2 decimal places at the end (e.g. $6040.16, not $6040.2).

## Practice questions

1. Increase 240 by 35%. [2]
2. The price of a book rises from $50 to $62. Calculate the percentage increase. [2]
3. After a 4% pay rise, Maria earns $31 200 per year. Calculate her salary before the rise. [2]
4. Share $720 in the ratio 3 : 4 : 5. [2]
5. A recipe for 4 people uses 300 g of flour. How much flour is needed for 10 people? [2]
6. Without a calculator, work out 3/5 + 2/3, giving your answer as a mixed number. [2]
7. a : b = 3 : 5 and b : c = 2 : 7. Find a : b : c in its simplest form. [2]
8. The exchange rate is €1 = $1.08. Change $270 into euros. [2]
9. A shop reduces a price by 20%, then later increases the new price by 20%. Find the overall percentage change. [2]
10. A box of 12 eggs costs $3.30 and a box of 30 eggs costs $8.10. Which is better value? Show your working. [2]
