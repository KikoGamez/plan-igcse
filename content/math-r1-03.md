# Number: standard form and bounds

> **In the exam:** **Paper 2** has short standard form questions without a calculator (multiply/divide numbers in standard form, write an ordinary number in standard form, 1–2 marks each) and simple bounds (write down the lower bound, 1 mark). **Paper 4** has bounds of a calculation (2–3 marks) and compound interest, depreciation or exponential growth (2–4 marks), often inside a longer context question. All three appear in most series.

## What you need to know

- Use the standard form A × 10ⁿ where n is a positive or negative integer and 1 ≤ A < 10; convert numbers into and out of standard form; calculate with values in standard form.
- Round values to a given number of decimal places or significant figures.
- Give upper and lower bounds for data rounded to a specified accuracy.
- (Extended) Find upper and lower bounds of the results of calculations which have used data rounded to a specified accuracy.
- Calculate simple and compound interest; (Extended) use exponential growth and decay, e.g. depreciation and population change.

## Notes

### Standard form

A number in standard form is **A × 10ⁿ**, with **1 ≤ A < 10** and n an integer.

| Ordinary number | Standard form |
|---|---|
| 4 700 000 | 4.7 × 10⁶ |
| 0.000 305 | 3.05 × 10⁻⁴ |
| 52 × 10³ (not standard form) | 5.2 × 10⁴ |
| 0.6 × 10⁻² (not standard form) | 6 × 10⁻³ |

- Large number → positive power; small number (less than 1) → negative power.
- **Multiplying without a calculator:** multiply the A parts, add the powers, then adjust.
  (6 × 10⁵) × (4 × 10⁻²) = 24 × 10³ = **2.4 × 10⁴**.
- **Dividing:** divide the A parts, subtract the powers.
  (8 × 10⁷) ÷ (2 × 10⁻³) = **4 × 10¹⁰**.
- **Adding/subtracting:** change to the same power of 10 (or to ordinary numbers) first.
  3.2 × 10⁴ + 5 × 10³ = 32 000 + 5000 = 37 000 = **3.7 × 10⁴**.
- On the calculator use the ×10ˣ (or EXP) key; put brackets round each number. Write the final answer in standard form if the question asks for it.

### Bounds

A measurement rounded to a given accuracy lies within **half a unit** either side.

| Value | Accuracy | Lower bound | Upper bound |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12.4 cm | nearest 0.1 cm | 12.35 | 12.45 |
| 7 cm | nearest cm | 6.5 | 7.5 |
| 3400 | 2 significant figures | 3350 | 3450 |
| 200 m | nearest 10 m | 195 | 205 |

- Write as an error interval: 12.35 ≤ l < 12.45. The upper bound is used in calculations even though l cannot actually equal it.

**Bounds of calculations** — choose the bounds that make the answer biggest or smallest:

| Calculation | Upper bound | Lower bound |
|---|---|---|
| a + b | a_max + b_max | a_min + b_min |
| a − b | a_max − **b_min** | a_min − **b_max** |
| a × b | a_max × b_max | a_min × b_min |
| a ÷ b | a_max ÷ **b_min** | a_min ÷ **b_max** |

Subtraction and division are the traps: to make a − b or a ÷ b big, make b **small**.

Show the bounds you are using (e.g. "205 ÷ 24.55") — the method mark depends on it. Do not round the bounds before the final answer.

### Simple and compound interest

- **Simple interest**: the same amount each year. I = P × r × n ÷ 100.
  £3000 at 2.5% for 4 years: I = 3000 × 2.5 × 4 ÷ 100 = £300.
- **Compound interest**: interest is added on, then earns interest itself.
  **Value = P × (1 + r/100)ⁿ**
  £5000 at 3.2% for 6 years: 5000 × 1.032⁶ = £6040.16. Interest = £1040.16.
- Read the question: "total amount" or "value" (the whole thing) vs "interest" (value − P).

### Exponential growth and decay

Same idea, with a multiplier each time period:

- Growth by r% → multiplier (1 + r/100); decay (depreciation) by r% → multiplier (1 − r/100).
- Car worth £18 000 loses 12% per year: after 3 years 18 000 × 0.88³ = £12 266.50.
- Population of 2400 grows by 15% each hour: after 3 hours 2400 × 1.15³ = 3650.1 → about 3650.
- **"After how many years…?"**: try values of n (trial and improvement) and show each trial. State the first whole number that works.
- **Reverse compound:** original = final ÷ multiplierⁿ. After 2 years at 5%: 4410 ÷ 1.05² = £4000.

## Worked examples

**1.** Without using a calculator, work out (6 × 10⁵) × (4 × 10⁻²). Give your answer in standard form. [2]

- 24 × 10³ or 24 000 — **M1**
- **2.4 × 10⁴** — **A1**

**2.** A rectangle has length 12.4 cm, correct to the nearest 0.1 cm, and width 7 cm, correct to the nearest centimetre. Calculate the upper bound of the area. [2]

- 12.45 × 7.5 — **M1** (both upper bounds)
- **93.375 cm²** — **A1**

**3.** A runner runs 200 m, correct to the nearest 10 m, in 24.6 seconds, correct to the nearest 0.1 second. Calculate the upper bound of her average speed. [3]

- 205 and 24.55 seen — **B1**
- 205 ÷ 24.55 — **M1**
- **8.35 m/s** (8.350…) — **A1**

**4.** Sam invests £5000 at 3.2% per year compound interest. Calculate the interest earned after 6 years. [3]

- 5000 × 1.032⁶ — **M1**
- = 6040.156… — **A1**
- interest = **£1040.16** — **A1**

## Common mistakes

- Writing 24 × 10³ as the final answer: A must be between 1 and 10.
- Giving 3.2 × 10⁴ + 5 × 10³ = 8.2 × 10⁷ (adding the A parts and the powers). Convert first.
- Bounds of a ÷ b or a − b: using the two upper bounds.
- Using 12.49 or 12.44999 as the upper bound instead of 12.45.
- Rounding bounds (or intermediate values) too early.
- Compound interest: using simple interest, using the multiplier 0.032 instead of 1.032, or giving the total when the question asks for the interest.
- Depreciation: subtracting 12% of the original value each year (that is simple, not compound).
- Not giving money to the nearest cent (£6040.2 should be £6040.16).

## Practice questions

1. Write 0.000 305 in standard form. [1]
2. Without a calculator, work out (8 × 10⁷) ÷ (2 × 10⁻³). Give your answer in standard form. [2]
3. Work out 3.2 × 10⁴ + 5 × 10³, giving your answer in standard form. [2]
4. The population of a town is 3400, correct to 2 significant figures. Write down the lower and upper bounds. [2]
5. A = 25 kg, correct to the nearest kilogram. B = 13.6 kg, correct to the nearest 0.1 kg. Calculate the upper bound of A − B. [2]
6. A car costs £18 000. Its value decreases by 12% each year. Calculate its value after 3 years. [2]
7. £2000 is invested at 4% per year compound interest. After how many complete years will the value first be more than £2500? [3]
8. An investment earns 5% per year compound interest. After 2 years it is worth £4410. Find the original amount invested. [2]
9. Calculate the simple interest on £3000 at 2.5% per year for 4 years. [2]

1. **3.05 × 10⁻⁴**
2. 8 ÷ 2 = 4, 7 − (−3) = 10 → **4 × 10¹⁰**
3. 32 000 + 5000 = 37 000 = **3.7 × 10⁴**
4. **3350 ≤ p < 3450**
5. 25.5 − 13.55 = **11.95 kg**
6. 18 000 × 0.88³ = **£12 266.50**
