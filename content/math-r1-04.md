# Algebra: expanding and factorising

> **In the exam:** In **both papers**, most often Paper 2. Typical questions: expand and simplify (2–3 marks), "factorise completely" (1–2 marks), factorise a quadratic (2 marks), simplify an algebraic fraction (3–4 marks), and add or subtract algebraic fractions as a single fraction (3 marks). These skills are also needed to solve quadratics and rearrange formulae, so they are tested indirectly in many other questions.

## What you need to know

- Expand products of algebraic expressions: a single bracket, two brackets, and (Extended) three binomials, e.g. (x + 2)(x − 3)(2x + 1).
- Factorise by extracting common factors.
- (Extended) Factorise expressions of the form: ax + bx + kay + kby (grouping); a²x² − b²y² (difference of two squares); a² + 2ab + b²; ax² + bx + c; ax³ + bx² + cx.
- (Extended) Manipulate algebraic fractions (add, subtract, multiply, divide) and factorise and simplify rational expressions.
- Simplify expressions by collecting like terms; use the laws of indices in algebra.

## Notes

### Expanding brackets

- Single bracket: multiply everything inside. −3(2x − 5) = −6x + 15 (watch the signs).
- Two brackets: multiply every term in the first by every term in the second (FOIL or a grid), then collect like terms.
  (2x − 3)(x + 5) = 2x² + 10x − 3x − 15 = **2x² + 7x − 15**.
- Squares: (x + a)² = x² + 2ax + a². (3x − 2)² = 9x² − 12x + 4. **Never** x² + a².
- Three brackets: expand two first, then multiply by the third.
  (x + 2)(x − 3) = x² − x − 6; × (2x + 1) = 2x³ + x² − 2x² − x − 12x − 6 = **2x³ − x² − 13x − 6**.
- "Expand and simplify" → you must collect like terms.

### Factorising — always look for a common factor first

| Type | Example | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Common factor (numbers and letters) | 12x²y − 18xy² | 6xy(2x − 3y) |
| Grouping (four terms) | 3x − 6y + ax − 2ay | 3(x − 2y) + a(x − 2y) = (x − 2y)(3 + a) |
| Difference of two squares | 4x² − 25 | (2x − 5)(2x + 5) |
| Common factor then DOTS | 50 − 2y² | 2(25 − y²) = 2(5 − y)(5 + y) |
| Quadratic, a = 1 | x² − 7x + 12 | (x − 3)(x − 4) |
| Quadratic, a ≠ 1 | 3x² + 10x − 8 | (3x − 2)(x + 4) |
| Perfect square | x² + 10x + 25 | (x + 5)² |
| Cubic with common x | x³ − 5x² + 6x | x(x − 2)(x − 3) |

**"Factorise completely"** means the HCF of all terms must be outside the bracket: 12x²y − 18xy² = 6xy(2x − 3y). Answers such as 6x(2xy − 3y²) get only 1 mark.

**Quadratics x² + bx + c:** find two numbers that **multiply to c** and **add to b**.
x² − 7x + 12: −3 × −4 = 12, −3 + −4 = −7 → (x − 3)(x − 4).

**Quadratics ax² + bx + c (a ≠ 1), splitting the middle term:**
3x² + 10x − 8: a × c = −24. Two numbers multiplying to −24 and adding to 10: 12 and −2.
3x² + 12x − 2x − 8 = 3x(x + 4) − 2(x + 4) = **(3x − 2)(x + 4)**.

**Always check by expanding back.**

**Difference of two squares (DOTS):** a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b).
- Spot it: two terms, both perfect squares, minus sign. x² − 49, 9a² − 16b², x⁴ − 1 = (x² − 1)(x² + 1) = (x − 1)(x + 1)(x² + 1).
- Useful for mental arithmetic: 101² − 99² = (101 − 99)(101 + 99) = 2 × 200 = 400.
- x² + 9 (a sum of squares) does **not** factorise.

### Algebraic fractions

**Simplifying:** factorise top and bottom fully, then cancel **common brackets** (whole factors only).
(x² − 9)/(x² + x − 6) = (x − 3)(x + 3) / ((x + 3)(x − 2)) = **(x − 3)/(x − 2)**.

You can never cancel terms that are added: (x + 3)/(x + 5) does not simplify.

**Adding/subtracting:** common denominator, then combine numerators with brackets.
2/(x + 1) + 3/(x − 2) = [2(x − 2) + 3(x + 1)] / ((x + 1)(x − 2)) = **(5x − 1)/((x + 1)(x − 2))**.
Leave the denominator factorised unless told otherwise.

With subtraction, bracket the whole second numerator: x/3 − (x − 2)/5 = (5x − 3(x − 2))/15 = (2x + 6)/15.

**Multiplying/dividing:** factorise everything, flip the second fraction if dividing, cancel, multiply.
(x + 3)/(x² − 4) × (x − 2)/(x + 3) = 1/(x + 2).

## Worked examples

**1.** Expand and simplify (2x − 3)(x + 5). [2]

- three of the four terms 2x², 10x, −3x, −15 correct — **M1**
- **2x² + 7x − 15** — **A1**

**2.** Factorise completely. [3]
(a) 12x²y − 18xy² [2]
(b) 4x² − 25 [1]

- (a) **6xy(2x − 3y)** — **B2** (B1 for a correct partial factorisation, e.g. 6x(2xy − 3y²) or 3xy(4x − 6y))
- (b) **(2x − 5)(2x + 5)** — **B1**

**3.** Simplify (x² − 9)/(x² + x − 6). [4]

- (x − 3)(x + 3) — **B1**
- (x + 3)(x − 2) — **B2** (B1 for factors giving x² + x + … or … + x − 6 when expanded)
- **(x − 3)/(x − 2)** — **B1**

**4.** Write 2/(x + 1) + 3/(x − 2) as a single fraction in its simplest form. [3]

- common denominator (x + 1)(x − 2) — **M1**
- 2(x − 2) + 3(x + 1) — **M1**
- **(5x − 1)/((x + 1)(x − 2))** — **A1**

## Common mistakes

- (x + 5)² written as x² + 25.
- Sign errors expanding −(x − 2) or −3(2x − 5); in subtraction of fractions, not bracketing the second numerator.
- "Factorise completely" with only part of the common factor taken out.
- Factorising a quadratic and not checking by expanding (e.g. (3x + 2)(x − 4) for 3x² + 10x − 8).
- Cancelling terms instead of factors: (x² + 3x)/(x + 3) is x, but (x² + 3)/(x + 3) does not simplify.
- Not factorising the denominator before trying to simplify, or stopping after factorising without cancelling.
- Losing a term when expanding three brackets — count: 2 terms × 2 terms × 2 terms = 8 products before collecting.

## Practice questions

2. Factorise 3x − 6y + ax − 2ay. [2]
3. Factorise (a) x² − 7x + 12 [2] (b) 3x² + 10x − 8 [2]
4. Factorise completely 50 − 2y². [2]
5. Simplify (2x² + 6x)/(x² − 9). [3]
6. Write x/3 − (x − 2)/5 as a single fraction in its simplest form. [3]
7. Use factorisation to work out 101² − 99² without a calculator. [2]
8. Factorise completely x³ − 5x² + 6x. [2]
9. Expand and simplify (3x − 2)² − (x + 1)(x − 1). [3]
