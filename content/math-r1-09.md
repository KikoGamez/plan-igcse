# Graphs: types and graphical solutions

> **In the exam:** **Paper 4** almost always has a "complete the table, draw the graph, use it" question worth 10–14 marks (a quadratic, cubic, reciprocal or exponential function, or a sum of these such as y = x² − 3/x). **Paper 2** has straight-line questions (gradient, y = mx + c, parallel and perpendicular lines, midpoint, length; 2–4 marks each) and "match the sketch to the equation" questions. Drawing accuracy matters: points are checked to within half a small square.

## What you need to know

- Use and interpret Cartesian coordinates; find the gradient, length and midpoint of a line segment from given coordinates.
- Interpret and obtain the equation of a straight-line graph (y = mx + c); find the equation of a line parallel or (Extended) perpendicular to a given line.
- Construct tables of values and draw, recognise and interpret graphs of functions of the form axⁿ (where n = −2, −1, −½, 0, ½, 1, 2, 3), abˣ + c, and simple sums of not more than three of these.
- (Extended) Solve associated equations graphically, including finding and interpreting roots; find the equation of a line to draw to solve a given equation.
- (Extended) Recognise, sketch and interpret graphs of linear, quadratic, cubic, reciprocal and exponential functions, including asymptotes and turning points.
- Draw and interpret graphs representing exponential growth and decay.

## Notes

### Straight lines

| Fact | Formula / rule |
|---|---|
| Gradient through (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂) | m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁) |
| Equation | y = mx + c (m = gradient, c = y-intercept) |
| Midpoint | ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2) |
| Length | √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²) |
| Parallel lines | same gradient |
| Perpendicular lines | m₁ × m₂ = −1 (negative reciprocal: 2 → −½) |

- **Line through two points:** find m, then substitute one point to find c. Through (1, 5) and (4, −1): m = −6/3 = −2; 5 = −2(1) + c → c = 7 → **y = −2x + 7**.
- **Rearrange to y = mx + c** before reading the gradient: 2x + 3y = 12 → y = −⅔x + 4, gradient −⅔. Intercepts: (6, 0) and (0, 4).
- Horizontal line: y = k (gradient 0). Vertical line: x = k.

### Recognising graph shapes

| Function type | Example | Shape and key features |
|---|---|---|
| Linear | y = 2x − 1 | straight line |
| Quadratic | y = x² − 2x − 3 | parabola, ∪ if a > 0, ∩ if a < 0; one turning point; symmetrical |
| Cubic | y = x³ − 3x | S-shaped; up to two turning points; y = x³ rises left to right, y = −x³ falls |
| Reciprocal | y = 4/x | two separate branches; asymptotes x = 0 and y = 0; y = −4/x is in the 2nd and 4th quadrants |
| Reciprocal squared | y = 1/x² | both branches above the x-axis |
| Exponential | y = 2ˣ | passes through (0, 1); gets very steep; asymptote y = 0 (the x-axis) |
| Exponential decay | y = (½)ˣ or 2⁻ˣ | passes through (0, 1); falls towards y = 0 |
| abˣ + c | y = 3 × 2ˣ − 1 | y-intercept a + c; asymptote y = c |

- A quadratic's line of symmetry is halfway between its roots (or x = −b/(2a)).
- y-intercept: put x = 0. x-intercepts (roots): put y = 0.

### Drawing graphs from a table

1. Complete the table carefully — use brackets for negatives on the calculator: (−2)² − 2(−2) − 3 = 5.
2. Use the symmetry of a quadratic table to check your values.
3. Plot every point accurately (a small cross or dot).
4. Join with a **smooth curve** through all the points — not straight segments, not a thick line. A quadratic's minimum is a curve, not a flat bottom or a point.
5. Do not join the two branches of a reciprocal graph across the asymptote.

### Solving equations using graphs

**f(x) = 0** → read the x-coordinates where the curve crosses the **x-axis**.
**f(x) = k** → draw the horizontal line **y = k**; read the x-coordinates where it meets the curve.
**f(x) = g(x)** → draw y = g(x); read the x-coordinates of the **points of intersection**.

**Which line must you draw?** Rearrange the new equation so that one side is the curve you have already drawn. The other side is the line.

Graph drawn: y = x² − 2x − 3. Equation to solve: x² − 4x + 1 = 0.
Add 2x − 4 to both sides: x² − 4x + 1 + 2x − 4 = 2x − 4 → x² − 2x − 3 = 2x − 4.
Draw **y = 2x − 4**.

Tip: subtract the new equation's left side from the curve's equation: (x² − 2x − 3) − (x² − 4x + 1) = 2x − 4.

- Read values to the accuracy of the grid (usually 1 decimal place); answers are accepted within a range.
- Give **all** solutions in the range drawn (a cubic may have three).
- "Number of solutions" = number of intersections.

## Worked examples

**1.** (a) Complete the table of values for y = x² − 2x − 3. [2]

| x | −2 | −1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 5 | 0 | −3 | −4 | −3 | 0 | 5 |

(b) Draw the graph for −2 ≤ x ≤ 4. [4]
(c) Use your graph to solve x² − 2x − 3 = 2. [2]

- (a) **B2** for all correct (B1 for 4 or 5 correct)
- (b) **B4** for a correct smooth curve through all 7 points (B3 for 6 or 7 points plotted correctly; B2 for 4 or 5; B1 for 2 or 3)
- (c) Line y = 2 drawn, or intersections used — **M1**; **x ≈ −1.4 and x ≈ 3.4** — **A1** (exact values −1.45 and 3.45; answers in a small range around these accepted)

**2.** The graph of y = x² − 2x − 3 has been drawn. Find the equation of the straight line that must be drawn to solve x² − 4x + 1 = 0. [2]

- (x² − 2x − 3) − (x² − 4x + 1) — **M1**
- **y = 2x − 4** — **A1**

**3.** Find the equation of the line through (1, 5) and (4, −1). [3]

- gradient (−1 − 5)/(4 − 1) — **M1**
- substitute a point into y = −2x + c — **M1**
- **y = −2x + 7** — **A1**

## Common mistakes

- Table errors with negative x: (−2)² entered as −2² = −4.
- Joining points with straight lines (ruled) or drawing a flat-bottomed parabola; joining across a reciprocal asymptote.
- Plotting points wrongly because the scales on the two axes are different — read the scale.
- Giving y-values instead of x-values as the solution of an equation.
- Missing one of the solutions, or reading the wrong curve when two are drawn.
- Perpendicular gradient: using −2 instead of −½ for a line of gradient 2.
- Reading the gradient of 2x + 3y = 12 as 2 without rearranging.

## Practice questions

1. Complete the table for y = x³ − 3x for x = −2, −1, 0, 1, 2. [2]
2. The graph of y = x³ − 3x has been drawn. Find the equation of the straight line that must be drawn to solve x³ − 5x + 1 = 0. [2]
3. Find the equation of the line parallel to y = 3x − 2 that passes through (2, 1). [2]
4. A is (−2, 7) and B is (4, −1). Find (a) the midpoint of AB [2] (b) the length of AB. [3]
5. Find the equation of the line perpendicular to y = −½x + 1 that passes through (3, 4). [3]
6. Match each equation to its description: y = 2ˣ, y = −2/x, y = 4 − x², y = x³.
   A: ∩-shaped parabola with maximum (0, 4). B: S-shaped curve through the origin, rising. C: through (0, 1), approaches the negative x-axis. D: two branches in the 2nd and 4th quadrants. [2]
7. Using the graph of y = 2ˣ, an estimate of the solution of 2ˣ = 5 is needed. Which line would you draw? Calculate a value, correct to 1 decimal place, to check your reading by trying values of x. [3]
8. The graphs of y = 5/x and y = x + 1 are drawn. Show that the x-coordinates of their points of intersection satisfy x² + x − 5 = 0, and find them to 2 decimal places. [4]
9. Find the gradient and the coordinates of the intercepts of 2x + 3y = 12. [3]
