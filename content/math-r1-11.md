# Coordinate geometry

> **In the exam:** Appears on both Paper 2 (non-calculator) and Paper 4 (calculator), almost every series. Typical questions: find the gradient, midpoint or length of a line segment (1–3 marks each), find the equation of a line through two points (3 marks), find the equation of a perpendicular line or perpendicular bisector (3–4 marks), read the gradient and y-intercept from an equation (2 marks). Often a 6–10 mark multi-part question on Paper 4 and several short questions on Paper 2.

## What you need to know

- Use and interpret Cartesian coordinates in two dimensions.
- Draw straight-line graphs for linear equations.
- Find the gradient of a straight line; calculate the gradient of a straight line from the coordinates of two points on it.
- Calculate the length and the coordinates of the midpoint of a straight line from the coordinates of its end points.
- Interpret and obtain the equation of a straight-line graph in the form y = mx + c.
- Find the gradient and equation of a straight line parallel to a given line.
- Find the gradient of parallel and perpendicular lines; find the equation of a line perpendicular to a given line, including the perpendicular bisector of a line segment.

## Notes

### Gradient

Gradient = change in y ÷ change in x = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁)

- Positive gradient: line goes up from left to right. Negative: goes down.
- Horizontal line (y = k): gradient 0. Vertical line (x = k): gradient undefined.
- Keep the order the same on top and bottom: if you start with point 2 on top, start with point 2 on the bottom.

### Midpoint and length

For A(x₁, y₁) and B(x₂, y₂):

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Midpoint of AB | ((x₁ + x₂) ÷ 2, (y₁ + y₂) ÷ 2) |
| Length of AB | √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²) (Pythagoras) |

- Midpoint = the *mean* of the x-coordinates and the *mean* of the y-coordinates.
- Length: square the differences first, so negative signs disappear. Leave as a surd (e.g. √52 = 2√13) on Paper 2 if the question does not ask for a decimal; on Paper 4 give 3 significant figures.
- Reverse midpoint problems: if M is the midpoint of AB and you know A and M, then B = (2 × M) − A for each coordinate.

### The equation y = mx + c

- m = gradient, c = y-intercept (where the line crosses the y-axis, at (0, c)).
- To read m and c, the equation must be rearranged to make y the subject. Example: 3x + 2y = 8 → 2y = −3x + 8 → y = −1.5x + 4, so m = −1.5, c = 4.
- To find where a line crosses the x-axis, put y = 0.
- To check whether a point lies on a line, substitute its coordinates.

### Finding the equation of a line

Method (through two points):
1. Find the gradient m from the two points.
2. Substitute one point into y = mx + c to find c (or use y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)).
3. Write the final answer as y = mx + c (or in the form the question asks for, e.g. ax + by = c with integers).

### Parallel and perpendicular lines

| Lines are… | Rule for gradients |
|---|---|
| Parallel | same gradient: m₁ = m₂ |
| Perpendicular | m₁ × m₂ = −1, so m₂ = −1 ÷ m₁ ("negative reciprocal") |

- Negative reciprocal: flip the fraction and change the sign. 2 → −½; −⅔ → 3/2; ¼ → −4.
- **Perpendicular bisector** of AB: passes through the midpoint of AB and has gradient = negative reciprocal of the gradient of AB.

### Intersection of two lines

Solve the two equations simultaneously; the solution (x, y) is the point of intersection.

### Drawing a straight-line graph

Make a table of at least three values of x, plot, and join with a ruler across the whole given range. Three points act as a check.

## Worked examples

**1.** A is the point (−2, 5) and B is the point (4, −3).
(a) Find the coordinates of the midpoint of AB. [2]
(b) Find the length of AB. [3]
(c) Find the equation of the line AB in the form y = mx + c. [3]

(a) ((−2 + 4) ÷ 2, (5 + (−3)) ÷ 2) M1 → **(1, 1)** A1
(b) √((4 − (−2))² + (−3 − 5)²) M1 = √(36 + 64) = √100 M1 → **10** A1
(c) m = (−3 − 5) ÷ (4 − (−2)) = −8/6 = −4/3 M1
5 = −4/3 × (−2) + c → c = 5 − 8/3 = 7/3 M1
**y = −4/3 x + 7/3** A1

**2.** Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of AB from Question 1. Give your answer in the form ax + by + c = 0 where a, b and c are integers. [4]

Gradient of AB = −4/3, so perpendicular gradient = 3/4 M1
Through midpoint (1, 1): 1 = 3/4 × 1 + c → c = 1/4 M1
y = 3/4 x + 1/4 → 4y = 3x + 1 M1
**3x − 4y + 1 = 0** A1 (check: 3(1) − 4(1) + 1 = 0 ✓)

**3.** The line L has equation 2x + 3y = 12.
(a) Find the gradient of L. [2]
(b) Find the coordinates of the point where L crosses the x-axis. [1]

(a) 3y = −2x + 12 → y = −⅔x + 4 M1 → gradient **−⅔** A1
(b) y = 0 → 2x = 12 → **(6, 0)** B1

**4.** Find the equation of the line perpendicular to y = 2x − 1 that passes through (4, 3). [3]

Perpendicular gradient = −½ B1
3 = −½ × 4 + c → c = 5 M1
**y = −½x + 5** A1

## Common mistakes

- Subtracting coordinates in a different order on the top and the bottom of the gradient fraction, giving the wrong sign.
- Reading the gradient straight from an equation like 4x − 2y = 10 without first making y the subject (the gradient is 2, not 4 or −2).
- Using the negative of the gradient (or the reciprocal only) for a perpendicular line instead of the negative reciprocal.
- For a perpendicular bisector, using one end point instead of the midpoint.
- Midpoint: subtracting instead of adding the coordinates; length: adding instead of subtracting before squaring.
- Errors with negative numbers: (−3)² = 9, and 5 − (−2) = 7.
- Not giving the answer in the form requested (e.g. integers a, b, c) and losing the final A1.

## Practice questions

1. P is (3, −1) and Q is (9, 7). Find (a) the midpoint of PQ [2], (b) the gradient of PQ [2], (c) the length of PQ [3].
2. Find the equation of the line through (2, 7) and (6, −5). Give your answer in the form y = mx + c. [3]
3. Write down the gradient and the y-intercept of the line 4x − 2y = 10. [2]
4. The midpoint of AB is (5, 2). A is the point (1, −4). Find the coordinates of B. [2]
5. Find the equation of the line perpendicular to y = 3x − 2 which passes through (6, 1). [3]
6. Find the coordinates of the point where the lines y = 2x + 1 and 3x + y = 11 intersect. [3]
7. The line through (k, 5) and (2, −1) has gradient 3. Find k. [2]
8. A is (−1, 4) and B is (5, 0). Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of AB in the form ax + by = c. [4]

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. (a) (6, 3) (b) 8/6 = **4/3** (c) √(6² + 8²) = √100 = **10**
2. m = (−5 − 7) ÷ (6 − 2) = −3; 7 = −6 + c → c = 13; **y = −3x + 13**
3. −2y = −4x + 10 → y = 2x − 5: gradient **2**, y-intercept **−5**
4. B = (2 × 5 − 1, 2 × 2 − (−4)) = **(9, 8)**
5. Gradient −⅓; 1 = −⅓ × 6 + c → c = 3; **y = −⅓x + 3**
6. 3x + (2x + 1) = 11 → 5x = 10 → x = 2, y = 5: **(2, 5)**
7. (5 − (−1)) ÷ (k − 2) = 3 → 6 = 3k − 6 → **k = 4**
8. Midpoint (2, 2); gradient AB = −4/6 = −⅔; perpendicular gradient 3/2; 2 = 3 + c → c = −1; y = 1.5x − 1 → **3x − 2y = 2**
