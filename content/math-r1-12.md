# Geometry: angles, polygons and circle theorems

> **In the exam:** Every series, on both Paper 2 and Paper 4. Typical questions: "Find the value of x. Give a geometrical reason for each answer" (2–5 marks), interior/exterior angles of regular polygons (2–3 marks), and a circle diagram with 3–5 angles to find (often 4–6 marks in total). The reason marks are lost more often than the angle marks — learn the exact phrases below.

## What you need to know

- Use and interpret the geometrical terms: point, line, parallel, perpendicular, acute, obtuse, reflex and right angles, interior and exterior angles, similar, congruent, scale factor; types of triangles, quadrilaterals and polygons; parts of a circle (centre, radius, diameter, chord, tangent, arc, sector, segment, circumference).
- Calculate unknown angles and give simple explanations using: angles at a point; angles on a straight line; vertically opposite angles; angles in a triangle and in a quadrilateral; angles formed by parallel lines (corresponding, alternate, co-interior).
- Know and use angle properties of regular and irregular polygons (interior and exterior angle sums).
- Circle theorems: angle in a semicircle; angle between tangent and radius.
- Extended: angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference; angles in the same segment are equal; opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°; alternate segment theorem.
- Extended: symmetry properties of circles: equal chords are equidistant from the centre; the perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the centre; tangents from an external point are equal in length.

## Notes

### Basic angle facts and their reasons

| Fact | Reason to write |
|---|---|
| Sum = 360° | Angles at a point add up to 360° |
| Sum = 180° | Angles on a straight line add up to 180° |
| Equal | Vertically opposite angles are equal |
| Sum = 180° | Angles in a triangle add up to 180° |
| Sum = 360° | Angles in a quadrilateral add up to 360° |
| Two equal base angles | Base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal |
| Equal (F shape) | Corresponding angles are equal |
| Equal (Z shape) | Alternate angles are equal |
| Sum = 180° (C/U shape) | Co-interior angles add up to 180° |

The examiner wants the **name** of the angle fact. "Because of parallel lines" or "Z angles" will not get the mark; "alternate angles are equal" will.

### Polygons

- Sum of interior angles of an n-sided polygon = (n − 2) × 180°.
- Sum of exterior angles of any polygon = 360°.
- Interior angle + exterior angle = 180° (angles on a straight line).
- Regular polygon: each exterior angle = 360° ÷ n; each interior angle = 180° − exterior angle.
- To find n for a regular polygon: n = 360 ÷ exterior angle. (If the answer is not a whole number, no such regular polygon exists.)

| Polygon | n | Interior sum | Regular interior angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pentagon | 5 | 540° | 108° |
| Hexagon | 6 | 720° | 120° |
| Octagon | 8 | 1080° | 135° |
| Decagon | 10 | 1440° | 144° |

Quadrilateral properties to know: square, rectangle, rhombus (diagonals bisect at 90°), parallelogram (opposite angles equal), kite (one pair of equal opposite angles, diagonals perpendicular), trapezium (one pair of parallel sides).

### Circle theorems

| Theorem | Reason to write |
|---|---|
| Angle in a semicircle = 90° | Angle in a semicircle is 90° |
| Tangent ⟂ radius | Tangent is perpendicular to the radius (at the point of contact) |
| Centre angle = 2 × circumference angle (same arc) | Angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference |
| Angles from the same chord, same side, equal | Angles in the same segment are equal |
| Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180° | Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180° |
| Angle between tangent and chord = angle in the alternate segment | Alternate segment theorem |
| Two tangents from a point are equal | Tangents from an external point are equal in length |
| Perpendicular from centre bisects chord | The perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the centre |

**Spotting tips**
- Two radii in a diagram → an isosceles triangle (base angles equal).
- Two tangents from one point → a kite with two 90° angles (angles in the quadrilateral OATB: angle AOB + angle APB = 180°).
- Cyclic quadrilateral: all **four** vertices must lie on the circle. If one vertex is the centre, it is not cyclic.
- Angle at the centre: the "arrowhead" or "bow" shape — both angles must stand on the **same arc**.

### Writing your answer

Write the angle, then the reason, for every step: "Angle OBA = 90° (tangent is perpendicular to radius)". Name angles with three letters (angle ABC means the angle at B).

## Worked examples

**1.** The exterior angle of a regular polygon is 24°.
(a) Find the number of sides. [2] (b) Find the size of one interior angle. [1]

(a) 360 ÷ 24 M1 = **15** A1
(b) 180 − 24 = **156°** B1

**2.** A, B, C and D lie on a circle, centre O. Angle ABC = 38°, where B is on the major arc AC. D is on the minor arc AC.
(a) Find angle AOC. Give a reason. [2] (b) Find angle ADC. Give a reason. [2]

(a) Angle AOC = 2 × 38 = **76°** B1 — angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference B1
(b) ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral, so angle ADC = 180 − 38 = **142°** B1 — opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180° B1

**3.** TA and TB are tangents to a circle, centre O, at A and B. Angle ATB = 50°. Find angle OAB. Give reasons. [4]

Angle OAT = angle OBT = 90° (tangent perpendicular to radius) B1
Angle AOB = 360 − 90 − 90 − 50 = 130° (angles in a quadrilateral add up to 360°) M1
Triangle OAB is isosceles (OA = OB, radii), so angle OAB = (180 − 130) ÷ 2 M1 = **25°** A1

**4.** PT is a tangent to the circle at P. Points P, Q and R lie on the circle. Angle TPQ = 64°, and R lies on the major arc PQ (on the other side of chord PQ from T). Find angle PRQ. Give a reason. [2]

Angle PRQ = angle TPQ = **64°** B1 — alternate segment theorem B1

## Common mistakes

- Giving a reason as a description ("Z angles", "it's a straight line") instead of the stated property.
- Using the cyclic quadrilateral rule when one vertex is the centre, not on the circumference.
- Halving or doubling the wrong way for the centre/circumference theorem.
- Forgetting that a triangle with two radii is isosceles.
- Using 360 ÷ n as the interior angle of a regular polygon (it is the exterior angle).
- Measuring angles with a protractor: diagrams are "not to scale" — the answer must be calculated.
- Writing angle "B" when there are several angles at B — use three letters.

## Practice questions

1. A pentagon has angles x + 30, 2x, 2x, 3x − 10 and 120 (in degrees). Find x. [3]
2. Each interior angle of a regular polygon is 160°. How many sides does it have? [2]
3. The interior angles of a polygon add up to 1980°. How many sides does it have? [2]
4. AB is a diameter of a circle and C is on the circumference. Angle CAB = 34°. Find angle ABC, giving reasons. [3]
5. O is the centre of a circle. A, B and C lie on the circumference, and the obtuse angle AOC = 140°. Find angle ABC where B is on the major arc. Give a reason. [2]
6. ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral. Angle DAB = 3y and angle BCD = y + 20. Find y. Give a reason. [3]
7. Two parallel lines are crossed by a transversal. One of the co-interior angles is 2x + 15 and the other is x + 30. Find x. [2]
8. PA and PB are tangents from P to a circle, centre O. Angle AOB = 112°. Find angle APB and angle PAB. Give reasons. [4]

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. 8x + 140 = 540 (interior angles of a pentagon add up to 540°) → **x = 50**
2. Exterior angle = 20°; 360 ÷ 20 = **18**
3. (n − 2) × 180 = 1980 → n − 2 = 11 → **n = 13**
4. Angle ACB = 90° (angle in a semicircle); angle ABC = 180 − 90 − 34 = **56°** (angles in a triangle add up to 180°)
5. 140 ÷ 2 = **70°** (angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference)
6. 3y + y + 20 = 180 (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral) → 4y = 160 → **y = 40**
7. 3x + 45 = 180 (co-interior angles add up to 180°) → **x = 45**
8. Angle OAP = angle OBP = 90° (tangent perpendicular to radius); angle APB = 360 − 90 − 90 − 112 = **68°** (angles in a quadrilateral). PA = PB (tangents from an external point are equal), so triangle PAB is isosceles: angle PAB = (180 − 68) ÷ 2 = **56°**

</details>
