# Mensuration

> **In the exam:** Every series, both papers. Paper 2: answers "in terms of π", unit conversions, simple areas (2–4 marks). Paper 4: arcs and sectors, cones, spheres, pyramids, frustums and compound solids, often as a 10–15 mark question combined with similarity or trigonometry. Many questions say "show that" — you must show enough working to reach the given value to more figures than given.

## What you need to know

- Use metric units of mass, length, area, volume and capacity; convert between units, including units of area and volume.
- Carry out calculations involving the perimeter and area of a rectangle, triangle, parallelogram and trapezium.
- Carry out calculations involving the circumference and area of a circle.
- Carry out calculations involving arc length and sector area as fractions of the circumference and area of a circle.
- Carry out calculations and solve problems involving the surface area and volume of a cuboid, prism, cylinder, sphere, pyramid and cone.
- Carry out calculations and solve problems involving perimeters and areas of compound shapes and surface areas and volumes of compound solids.
- Extended: answers may be required in terms of π.

## Notes

### Units

| Length | Area | Volume / capacity |
|---|---|---|
| 1 cm = 10 mm | 1 cm² = 100 mm² | 1 cm³ = 1000 mm³ |
| 1 m = 100 cm | 1 m² = 10 000 cm² | 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³ |
| 1 km = 1000 m | 1 km² = 1 000 000 m² | 1 litre = 1000 cm³, 1 ml = 1 cm³ |

Square the length factor for area, cube it for volume.

### 2D shapes

| Shape | Area |
|---|---|
| Rectangle | length × width |
| Triangle | ½ × base × perpendicular height |
| Parallelogram | base × perpendicular height |
| Trapezium | ½(a + b)h, where a and b are the parallel sides |
| Circle | πr²; circumference = 2πr = πd |

### Arcs, sectors and segments (θ = angle at the centre)

- Arc length = (θ/360) × 2πr
- Sector area = (θ/360) × πr²
- Perimeter of a sector = arc length + 2r (do not forget the two radii)
- Segment area = sector area − area of triangle (triangle = ½r² sin θ)
- To find θ: rearrange, e.g. θ = (sector area × 360) ÷ (πr²).

Learn these arc and sector formulae: do not rely on them being given.

### 3D solids

| Solid | Volume | Surface area |
|---|---|---|
| Cuboid | l × w × h | 2(lw + lh + wh) |
| Prism | area of cross-section × length | sum of all faces |
| Cylinder | πr²h | curved 2πrh; total 2πrh + 2πr² |
| Pyramid | ⅓ × base area × perpendicular height | base + triangular faces |
| Cone | ⅓πr²h | curved πrl (l = slant height); total πrl + πr² |
| Sphere | (4/3)πr³ | 4πr² |
| Hemisphere | (2/3)πr³ | curved 2πr²; solid hemisphere total 3πr² |

- Slant height of a cone: l² = r² + h² (Pythagoras).
- **Frustum** (cone with the top cut off): volume = volume of large cone − volume of small cone. The small cone is similar to the large one.
- Papers from 2025 onwards include a formula list with the cone, sphere and pyramid formulae; check what is given on a recent paper, and still know which length is which.

### Compound shapes and solids

- Split into simple parts; add or subtract.
- For surface area of compound solids, include only the faces you can see on the outside — e.g. a hemisphere on a cylinder: curved hemisphere + curved cylinder + one circular base.
- "Melted and recast" questions: the volume stays the same. Set volume 1 = volume 2 and solve.
- Rates: time to fill = volume ÷ rate of flow (check units: litres vs cm³).

### Accuracy

- Paper 4: give answers to 3 significant figures (angles to 1 d.p.) unless told otherwise. Keep full values in the calculator during working.
- Paper 2: leave answers in terms of π, e.g. 36π, unless told otherwise.

## Worked examples

**1.** A sector has radius 9 cm and angle 140°. Calculate (a) the perimeter [3], (b) the area [2] of the sector.

(a) Arc = (140/360) × 2 × π × 9 M1 = 21.99… ; perimeter = 21.99 + 9 + 9 M1 = **40.0 cm** A1
(b) (140/360) × π × 9² M1 = **99.0 cm²** A1

**2.** A solid cone has radius 5 cm and perpendicular height 12 cm. Find (a) the slant height [2], (b) the total surface area [2], (c) the volume [2]. Give (b) and (c) in terms of π.

(a) l = √(5² + 12²) M1 = **13 cm** A1
(b) π × 5 × 13 + π × 5² M1 = **90π cm²** A1
(c) ⅓ × π × 5² × 12 M1 = **100π cm³** A1

**3.** (Non-calculator) A solid metal sphere of radius 6 cm is melted and recast into a cylinder of radius 4 cm. Find the height of the cylinder. [3]

Sphere volume = (4/3)π × 216 = 288π M1
π × 4² × h = 288π M1 → h = 288 ÷ 16 = **18 cm** A1

**4.** A solid is made from a cylinder of radius 3 cm and height 10 cm with a hemisphere of radius 3 cm on top. Calculate its volume. [3]

Cylinder: π × 9 × 10 = 90π; hemisphere: ⅔ × π × 27 = 18π M1 M1
Total 108π = **339 cm³** A1

## Common mistakes

- Using the diameter in place of the radius (always halve first).
- Forgetting the two radii in the perimeter of a sector.
- Using the slant height in place of the perpendicular height in the cone volume, or vice versa in the curved surface area.
- Converting cm² to m² by dividing by 100 instead of 10 000.
- Rounding in the middle of a calculation, then the final answer is outside the accepted range.
- In "show that" questions, not writing the value to more figures than the given answer (e.g. write 527.78…, then "= 528").
- Including hidden faces in the surface area of a compound solid.

## Practice questions

1. A sector of a circle of radius 8 cm has area 30 cm². Find the angle of the sector. [2]
2. An arc of a circle of radius 7 cm has length 12 cm. Find the angle at the centre. [2]
3. A cylinder has volume 500 cm³ and radius 4 cm. Find its height. [2]
4. A sphere has surface area 200 cm². Find its volume. [3]
5. A prism has a trapezium cross-section with parallel sides 6 cm and 10 cm, perpendicular distance 4 cm apart. The prism is 15 cm long. Find its volume. [2]
6. Convert (a) 3.5 m² to cm² [1], (b) 2.4 litres to cm³ [1].
7. A cone has radius 6 cm and height 16 cm. The top is cut off by a plane parallel to the base, halfway up, leaving a frustum. Show that the volume of the frustum is 528 cm³ to 3 significant figures. [4]
8. A chord AB subtends an angle of 60° at the centre O of a circle of radius 10 cm. Find the area of the minor segment. [4]
