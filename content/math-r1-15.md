# Trigonometry: Pythagoras, SOHCAHTOA and bearings

> **In the exam:** Every series. Paper 4: right-angled triangle questions (3–4 marks each), often linked to bearings, angles of elevation/depression or 3D shapes. Paper 2: Pythagoras with exact numbers or surds, bearings and scale drawings (2–3 marks). Bearings questions are frequently combined with the sine and cosine rules in a longer Paper 4 question.

## What you need to know

- Know and use Pythagoras' theorem.
- Know and use the sine, cosine and tangent ratios for acute angles in calculations involving sides and angles of a right-angled triangle.
- Solve problems in two dimensions using Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometry.
- Interpret and use three-figure bearings measured clockwise from north (0° to 360°).
- Solve problems involving angles of elevation and depression.
- Draw and interpret scale drawings involving bearings.

## Notes

### Pythagoras' theorem

In a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse c (the side opposite the right angle, the longest side):

a² + b² = c²

- Finding the hypotenuse: c = √(a² + b²) — **add**.
- Finding a shorter side: a = √(c² − b²) — **subtract**.
- Check: the hypotenuse must be the longest side.
- Useful triples (good for Paper 2): 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, 7-24-25, and multiples (6-8-10, 9-12-15).
- Converse: if a² + b² = c², the triangle has a right angle.

### SOHCAHTOA

Label the sides relative to the angle θ you are using: **H**ypotenuse (opposite the right angle), **O**pposite (opposite θ), **A**djacent (next to θ, not the hypotenuse).

| Ratio | Formula | Memory |
|---|---|---|
| sin θ | opposite ÷ hypotenuse | SOH |
| cos θ | adjacent ÷ hypotenuse | CAH |
| tan θ | opposite ÷ adjacent | TOA |

Method:
1. Label O, A, H.
2. Choose the ratio that uses the two sides involved (the one you know and the one you want).
3. Write the equation, e.g. sin 35° = x ÷ 20.
4. Rearrange: x = 20 sin 35°. If the unknown is on the bottom: x = 12 ÷ tan 40°.
5. For an angle use the inverse: θ = tan⁻¹(8 ÷ 11).

Calculator must be in **degrees** (D on the screen).

### Angles of elevation and depression

- Elevation: angle measured **up** from the horizontal.
- Depression: angle measured **down** from the horizontal.
- The angle of depression from A to B equals the angle of elevation from B to A (alternate angles).
- Always draw a sketch with the horizontal line marked.

### Bearings

Three rules:
1. Measured from **north**.
2. Measured **clockwise**.
3. Written with **three figures** (e.g. 045°, not 45°).

- "The bearing of B from A" → stand at A, face north, turn clockwise to B.
- **Back bearing**: bearing of A from B = bearing of B from A ± 180°. Example: 125° → 305°; 250° → 070°.
- To use trigonometry, draw the north lines at each point; north lines are parallel, so use alternate or co-interior angles to find angles inside the triangle.
- Components of a journey of distance d on bearing θ: distance east = d sin θ, distance north = d cos θ (for 0° < θ < 90°; for other bearings draw the triangle).

### Scale drawings

Use the scale, measure angles from a north line with a protractor and lengths with a ruler. Accuracy: ±2 mm and ±2°.

## Worked examples

**1.** A ladder 6.5 m long leans against a vertical wall. The top of the ladder is 6 m above the horizontal ground.
(a) How far is the foot of the ladder from the wall? [2] (b) Find the angle between the ladder and the ground. [2]

(a) √(6.5² − 6²) M1 = √6.25 = **2.5 m** A1
(b) cos θ = 2.5 ÷ 6.5 M1 → θ = **67.4°** A1

**2.** A ship sails 12 km from port P on a bearing of 060° to point Q, then 5 km on a bearing of 150° to point R.
(a) Explain why angle PQR = 90°. [1]
(b) Find the distance PR. [2]
(c) Find the bearing of R from P. [3]

(a) Back bearing of P from Q is 240°; 240 − 150 = 90° B1
(b) √(12² + 5²) M1 = **13 km** A1
(c) Angle QPR = tan⁻¹(5 ÷ 12) M1 = 22.6° A1; bearing = 60 + 22.6 = **082.6°** A1

**3.** From a point 40 m from the base of a tower, on horizontal ground, the angle of elevation of the top is 32°. Find the height of the tower. [2]

tan 32° = h ÷ 40 → h = 40 tan 32° M1 = **25.0 m** A1

## Common mistakes

- Adding instead of subtracting when finding a shorter side with Pythagoras.
- Labelling the hypotenuse as the opposite or adjacent side.
- Calculator in radians or gradians — check before the exam.
- Writing a bearing with two figures (45° instead of 045°) or measuring anticlockwise.
- Confusing "bearing of A from B" with "bearing of B from A".
- Angle of depression marked from the vertical instead of the horizontal.
- Rounding intermediate values (e.g. a side to 2.5 when it is 2.53…) so the final answer is out of range. Keep at least 4 significant figures.
- Using SOHCAHTOA in a triangle that is not right-angled (use the sine or cosine rule instead).

## Practice questions

1. A right-angled triangle has shorter sides 7 cm and 24 cm. Find the hypotenuse. (Non-calculator) [2]
2. A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 15 cm and one side 9 cm. Find the third side. (Non-calculator) [2]
3. In a right-angled triangle, the side opposite angle x is 8 cm and the side adjacent to x is 11 cm. Find x. [2]
4. A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 20 cm and an angle of 35°. Find the side opposite the 35° angle. [2]
5. The bearing of B from A is 125°. Write down the bearing of A from B. [1]
6. From the top of an 80 m cliff, the angle of depression of a boat is 18°. Find the horizontal distance of the boat from the foot of the cliff. [2]
7. A plane flies 300 km on a bearing of 040°. How far (a) north [2] and (b) east [1] of its starting point is it?
8. An isosceles triangle has sides 10 cm, 10 cm and 12 cm. Find (a) its perpendicular height [2] and (b) the size of one of its base angles. [2]

1. √(49 + 576) = √625 = **25 cm**
2. √(225 − 81) = √144 = **12 cm**
3. x = tan⁻¹(8 ÷ 11) = **36.0°**
4. 20 sin 35° = **11.5 cm** (11.47…)
5. 125 + 180 = **305°**
6. tan 18° = 80 ÷ d → d = 80 ÷ tan 18° = **246 m**
7. (a) 300 cos 40° = **230 km** (229.8…) (b) 300 sin 40° = **193 km** (192.8…)
