# Statistics in the Maths syllabus

> **In the exam:** Every series. Paper 4: a 10–15 mark question with a grouped frequency table (estimate the mean, modal class), a cumulative frequency curve (median, quartiles, IQR, percentiles), and/or a histogram with unequal class widths. Paper 2: averages from a list or frequency table, reading scatter diagrams and stem-and-leaf diagrams (1–3 marks each). Your Statistics IGCSE covers the same skills in more depth — use it to reinforce this page.

## What you need to know

- Classify and tabulate statistical data; read, interpret and draw inferences from tables and statistical diagrams.
- Calculate the mean, median, mode and range for individual data and distinguish between the purposes for which these are used.
- Calculate an estimate of the mean for grouped discrete or continuous data; identify the modal class from a grouped frequency distribution.
- Draw and interpret bar charts, pie charts, pictograms, stem-and-leaf diagrams and simple frequency distributions.
- Draw and interpret scatter diagrams; understand what is meant by positive, negative and zero correlation; draw by eye, interpret and use a straight line of best fit.
- Extended: draw, interpret and use cumulative frequency diagrams; estimate and interpret the median, percentiles, quartiles and interquartile range from cumulative frequency diagrams.
- Extended: draw and interpret histograms; calculate with frequency density.

## Notes

### Averages and range

| Measure | How | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Mean | total ÷ number of values; from a table Σfx ÷ Σf | uses all data; affected by extreme values |
| Median | middle value when in order: the (n + 1)/2 th value | not affected by extreme values |
| Mode | most common value | the only average for non-numerical data |
| Range | largest − smallest | simple spread; affected by extreme values |

### Grouped data: estimating the mean

1. Find the **midpoint** of each class.
2. Multiply midpoint × frequency (fx).
3. Estimated mean = Σfx ÷ Σf. It is an *estimate* because the exact values are unknown.

The **modal class** is the class with the highest frequency (for equal widths). The class containing the median: find the (n/2)th value using running totals.

### Cumulative frequency

1. Add up frequencies to get running totals.
2. Plot each cumulative frequency at the **upper class boundary** (plus a point at the lowest boundary with cumulative frequency 0).
3. Join with a smooth curve (or straight lines if asked).

Readings for n values (large data sets): median at n/2, lower quartile at n/4, upper quartile at 3n/4, p-th percentile at p% of n. IQR = UQ − LQ. "How many more than x?" → read the cumulative frequency at x and subtract from n.

### Histograms

- **Frequency density = frequency ÷ class width.** The vertical axis is frequency density.
- The **area** of each bar represents frequency.
- To read frequency from a histogram: frequency = frequency density × class width.
- No gaps between bars for continuous data; use exact class boundaries.

### Scatter diagrams

- Positive correlation: as one variable increases, the other tends to increase. Negative: as one increases, the other tends to decrease. No (zero) correlation: no relationship.
- Line of best fit: straight, drawn by eye, following the trend with roughly equal numbers of points on each side; it does not have to pass through the origin.
- Use the line to estimate values. Estimates within the range of the data are reliable; outside the range (extrapolation) they may not be.
- Correlation does not prove that one variable causes the other.

### Stem-and-leaf diagrams

Leaves in order, with a key (e.g. 3 | 7 means 37). Count leaves to find n, then the median position.

## Worked examples

**1.** The times, t minutes, that 40 people spent in a shop are shown.

| Time (t min) | 0 < t ≤ 10 | 10 < t ≤ 20 | 20 < t ≤ 30 | 30 < t ≤ 50 | 50 < t ≤ 80 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 4 | 9 | 12 | 10 | 5 |

(a) Calculate an estimate of the mean. [4]
(b) Find the frequency densities for a histogram. [2]
(c) Complete the cumulative frequency table. [1]

(a) Midpoints 5, 15, 25, 40, 65 M1; Σfx = 20 + 135 + 300 + 400 + 325 = 1180 M1; 1180 ÷ 40 M1 = **29.5 min** A1
(b) 4÷10 = **0.4**, 9÷10 = **0.9**, 12÷10 = **1.2**, 10÷20 = **0.5**, 5÷30 = **0.167** B2 (B1 for 3 correct)
(c) Cumulative frequencies at t = 10, 20, 30, 50, 80: **4, 13, 25, 35, 40** B1

**2.** From the cumulative frequency curve in Example 1 (drawn accurately), the median is about 26 minutes and the quartiles about 17 and 40 minutes. Find the interquartile range and explain what it shows. [2]

IQR = 40 − 17 = **23 min** B1; the middle 50% of times are spread over about 23 minutes B1.

**3.** The table shows the number of pets owned by 30 students: 0 pets: 5, 1 pet: 8, 2 pets: 12, 3 pets: 3, 4 pets: 2. Find the mean, median and mode. [4]

Σfx = 0 + 8 + 24 + 9 + 8 = 49; mean = 49 ÷ 30 M1 = **1.63** A1
Median: 15th and 16th values; running totals 5, 13, 25 → both are 2: **median 2** B1; **mode 2** B1

## Common mistakes

- Using class widths or upper boundaries instead of midpoints for the estimated mean.
- Dividing Σfx by the number of classes instead of Σf.
- Plotting cumulative frequency at the midpoint instead of the upper boundary.
- Drawing a histogram with frequency (not frequency density) on the vertical axis when widths are unequal.
- Reading quartiles at (n + 1)/4 from a cumulative frequency graph of a large data set — use n/4.
- Describing correlation as "positive" without referring to context when asked to "interpret" (e.g. "students who revise for longer tend to score higher").
- Forgetting the key on a stem-and-leaf diagram.

## Practice questions

1. Using the data in Example 1, estimate the 90th percentile by linear interpolation. [2]
2. Using the data in Example 1, estimate how many people spent more than 45 minutes in the shop. [2]
3. In a histogram, the bar for the class 10 < h ≤ 15 has frequency density 2.4. Find the frequency. [1]
4. A class 15 < h ≤ 25 has frequency 30. Find the frequency density. [1]
5. The mean of 8 numbers is 12. When a ninth number is added, the mean becomes 13. Find the ninth number. [2]
6. A scatter diagram shows age of cars (years) against value (€). Describe the correlation you would expect and interpret it. [2]
7. A stem-and-leaf diagram shows: 2 | 3 5 8; 3 | 0 2 2 7; 4 | 1 6. Key 2 | 3 means 23. Find the median and range. [2]

1. 90% of 40 = 36th value; it lies in 50 < t ≤ 80 (cf 35 → 40): 50 + (1/5) × 30 = **56 min**
2. In 30 < t ≤ 50, the part above 45 is 5/20 of the class: 10 × 5/20 = 2.5; plus 5 in the last class: **about 7 or 8 people** (7.5)
3. 2.4 × 5 = **12**
4. 30 ÷ 10 = **3**
5. 9 × 13 − 8 × 12 = 117 − 96 = **21**
6. **Negative correlation**: older cars tend to be worth less.
