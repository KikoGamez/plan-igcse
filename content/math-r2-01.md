# Past paper · Paper 2 timed (1/2)

> **Goal:** Do the first hour of a full Extended Paper 2 under real exam conditions, without a calculator, and mark clearly where you stop.

## Before you start

- **Paper:** a Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 **Paper 2 (Extended)** from the 2025 syllabus onwards — e.g. May/June 2026, Oct/Nov 2025 or May/June 2025 (variant 21, 22 or 23), or the 2025 specimen Paper 2. Pick one you have **not** seen. Print it or have it on paper; do not work from a screen.
- Older Paper 2s (before 2025) were 1 h 30 min, 70 marks and allowed a calculator. Only use one if no new paper is left, and then skip questions that really need a calculator.
- **Mark scheme:** download it now but keep it closed until session 3. Also download the examiner report for the same series if available.
- **Calculator:** none. Put it in another room.
- **Equipment:** black pen, pencil (for diagrams and graphs), ruler, protractor, compasses, eraser, a watch or timer. Tracing paper is allowed.
- Quiet room, phone off and away, water.

## Exam format reminder

| | Paper 2 (Extended) |
|---|---|
| Duration | 2 hours |
| Marks | 100 (50% of the qualification) |
| Calculator | **Not allowed** |
| Questions | short-answer and structured questions; answer all of them |
| Coverage | any topic from the Extended syllabus |
| Pace | about **1.2 minutes per mark** (a 3-mark question ≈ 3–4 min) |

The rubric says you must show all necessary working clearly, and the answers must be exact where possible (fractions in their simplest form, surds, answers in terms of π if asked). The paper starts with short 1–3 mark questions and builds to longer multi-part questions.

## How to do it

This paper is split over two sessions. Today you work for **60 minutes**.

1. **0:00 – 0:02** Write the start time on the front of the paper. Read the instructions on the cover and the formula list, as you would in the exam.
2. **0:02 – 0:58** Work through the questions in order. Use the margin to note the time every 20 marks or so:
   - by about 25 min you should be around mark 20;
   - by about 50 min, around mark 40.
   If a question takes more than twice its "1.2 minutes per mark" allowance, put a star next to it and move on.
3. **0:58 – 1:00** Stop. Draw a line under your last line of working and write "STOP – session 1 – 60 min". List the starred questions you skipped.
4. Put the paper in a folder. **Do not** look at the remaining questions, the mark scheme or your notes on those topics before session 2 — that would make the timing meaningless.

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Show working on every question with more than 1 mark.** Most marks are M (method) and A (accuracy) marks. A correct answer with no working can score nothing when the question says "show all your working".
- **"Show that…"**: every step must be written; the final line must match the given result exactly.
- **Fractions:** write improper fractions and the common denominator; simplify the final answer. Mixed numbers if the question uses them.
- **Non-calculator arithmetic:** use written methods (column multiplication, short division). Estimate first so you can spot a silly answer: e.g. 0.38 × 41 ≈ 0.4 × 40 = 16.
- **Estimate questions:** round every number to **1 significant figure** and show the rounded values — this is often where the mark comes from.
- **Exact answers:** leave √, π and fractions in; don't convert to decimals.
- **Command words:** "write down" = no working needed (1 mark); "calculate"/"work out" = show working; "explain" = a reason in words; "sketch" = shape and key points, not an accurate plot.
- **Diagrams are not to scale** unless stated — never measure to get an answer.
- **Answer line:** put the final answer on the line, in the form asked (e.g. standard form, simplest form, units given).
- **Don't leave blanks.** A correct first step can earn M1 even if you cannot finish.

## Checklist while marking

You will mark this paper in session 3. Today, simply make sure:
- every answer is on the answer line;
- skipped questions are starred so you can return in session 2;
- your stopping point is clearly marked.

## Quick-fire revision

Before you start (2 minutes, from memory, not from notes), recall:
- index laws: a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ, a^(m/n) = (ⁿ√a)ᵐ;
- rationalise 1/(a + √b) by multiplying by (a − √b);
- reverse percentage: original = new ÷ multiplier;
- fg(x) = f(g(x)) — g first;
- (x + p)² + q has turning point (−p, q);
- dy/dx of axⁿ is naxⁿ⁻¹;
- interior angle sum of an n-sided polygon = (n − 2) × 180°;
- bounds: half a unit either side.
