# Past paper · Paper 2 timed (2/2)

> **Goal:** Finish the Paper 2 you started last session in the remaining 60 minutes, still under exam conditions and without a calculator, so you have one complete 2-hour attempt to mark.

## Before you start

- **Paper:** the same Paper 2 from session 1 (the one with "STOP – session 1" written on it). Do not start a different paper.
- **Mark scheme:** still closed.
- **Calculator:** none.
- **Equipment:** black pen, pencil, ruler, protractor, compasses, eraser, tracing paper, timer.
- Quiet room, phone away.

Count the marks still to do. If more than about 50 marks remain, you were behind in session 1: in this session aim to attempt every question, even if only the first part.

## How to do it

1. **0:00 – 0:02** Re-read the question you stopped at. Do not re-read your earlier answers yet.
2. **0:02 – 0:45** Carry on through the remaining questions in order. The later questions are longer and more structured: read the whole question first, because part (a) often helps with part (b).
3. **0:45 – 0:52** Go back to the questions you starred in session 1.
4. **0:52 – 1:00** Check (this is what you must also do in the real exam):
   - Every question attempted? Every answer on the answer line?
   - Units, form (standard form, simplest form, surds) as asked?
   - Quick reverse checks: substitute your solutions into equations; expand your factorisation; check that probabilities are between 0 and 1 and that angles in a triangle add to 180°.
5. At 1:00 stop and write the total time used on the front. You have now done the full 2 hours.

## Exam technique for this paper

The second half of Paper 2 usually contains the longer algebra, functions, geometry and statistics questions. Typical traps:

- **Algebraic fractions and rearranging:** factorise fully before cancelling; when the subject appears twice, collect the terms and factorise.
- **Simultaneous equations:** show the multiplication and the addition or subtraction; give both values.
- **Functions:** fg(x) means g first; f⁻¹(x) must be in terms of x.
- **Quadratics:** rearrange to = 0 before factorising; "complete the square" answers in the exact form requested.
- **Differentiation:** turning points need both coordinates; state max/min with a reason.
- **Circle theorems and angle questions:** give a reason for each step using the correct geometrical words (e.g. "angle in a semicircle = 90°", "alternate angles") when asked to give reasons.
- **Vectors:** write a route, e.g. OB = OA + AB, then simplify in terms of **a** and **b**.
- **Probability:** fractions do not need simplifying unless asked, but must be correct; "without replacement" changes the second denominator.
- **Constructions and loci:** leave construction arcs visible; use compasses, not freehand.
- **Running out of time:** pick off the first part of each remaining question — first parts are usually the easiest marks.

## Quick-fire revision

Non-calculator skills to have automatic:
- squares to 15², cubes to 10³, powers of 2 to 2¹⁰;
- fraction ↔ decimal ↔ percentage: ⅛ = 0.125 = 12.5%, ⅓ = 0.3̇, ⅕ = 0.2;
- sin, cos, tan of 30°, 45°, 60° (sin 30° = ½, cos 60° = ½, tan 45° = 1, sin 60° = √3/2, cos 30° = √3/2, sin 45° = cos 45° = √2/2, tan 60° = √3, tan 30° = 1/√3);
- multiplying and dividing in standard form;
- dividing by a decimal: multiply both numbers by 10 or 100 first (4.8 ÷ 0.06 = 480 ÷ 6 = 80).
