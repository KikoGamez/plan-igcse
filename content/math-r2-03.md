# Mark Paper 2 with the mark scheme

> **Goal:** Mark your Paper 2 strictly, the way a Cambridge examiner would, and turn every lost mark into an entry in your error log.

## Before you start

- Your completed Paper 2 (both sessions).
- The **mark scheme** for exactly the same series and variant (e.g. 0580/22 May/June 2025 — check the code on the front matches).
- The **examiner report** for that series (optional but useful: it says what most candidates got wrong).
- The **grade thresholds** document for that series (it gives the raw marks needed for each grade on that paper or component; do not guess them).
- A **red pen** (not the pen you wrote with), your error log (notebook or spreadsheet), and a calculator is fine now.

## Exam format reminder

Paper 2 (Extended): 2 hours, 100 marks, non-calculator, 50% of the final grade. Marks are given for method and accuracy, so you mark the working, not just the answer.

## How to do it

1. **0:00 – 0:05** Read the first pages of the mark scheme: the general marking principles and the list of abbreviations (below).
2. **0:05 – 0:40** Mark question by question.
   - Tick each mark you earned in the margin with its type: M1, A1, B1.
   - For a wrong final answer, check the alternatives in the mark scheme for method marks you still earned.
   - Be strict: if the mark scheme needs "x = 14" and you wrote "14" in a context where it is clear, that is fine; but a wrong form (e.g. 24 × 10³ when standard form is asked) loses the mark.
3. **0:40 – 0:45** Add up the total out of 100. Compare with the grade thresholds for that series. Write your score and the percentage on the front.
4. **0:45 – 0:58** Fill in the error log (see checklist below) for every question where you lost marks.
5. **0:58 – 1:00** Choose your **three** biggest weak topics — these go into the "weak topics" session.

## Exam technique for this paper

How Cambridge awards marks — knowing this changes how you write answers:

| Abbreviation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| M | method mark: a correct method applied (even with arithmetic slips) |
| A | accuracy mark: a correct answer or step that **depends on** the M mark |
| B | independent mark for a correct result or statement |
| SC | special case: partial credit for a specific common error |
| ft / FT | follow through: a later mark can be earned using your earlier wrong answer |
| oe | or equivalent (other correct forms accepted) |
| cao | correct answer only (no follow through, no alternative form) |
| isw | ignore subsequent working (a correct answer then spoiled by extra work still scores) |
| dep | dependent: this mark needs the previous mark |
| nfww | not from wrong working |

Lessons for the exam:
- **A marks need M marks.** A correct answer from no or wrong working may score 0 when the question demands working.
- **Follow through** means an early mistake does not lose all the later marks — always carry on.
- Where a mark scheme lists a **range** (e.g. for a graph reading), the answer must lie inside it.
- "**cao**" answers must be in exactly the right form — e.g. a fraction in its simplest form.

## Checklist while marking

For each lost mark, record in the error log:

| Q | Topic | Marks lost | Type of error | What I should have done |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7b | Reverse percentages | 2 | Method | 68 ÷ 0.85, not 68 × 1.15 |
| 12 | Algebraic fractions | 1 | Careless | Sign error: −3(x − 2) = −3x + 6 |
| 15c | Circle theorems | 2 | Knowledge | Tangent ⟂ radius — learn reasons |

Use four error types so you can see patterns:
- **Knowledge** – didn't know the fact/method → revise the topic.
- **Method** – knew the topic but used the wrong approach → redo similar questions.
- **Careless** – arithmetic, sign, copying, misread → slow down; check.
- **Time / not attempted** – ran out of time or skipped → practise speed; answer first parts.

Also check:
- Did you lose marks for **missing working** on questions you got right in your head?
- Did you lose marks for the **form** of the answer (simplest form, standard form, exact value, units)?
- Were any non-calculator arithmetic errors repeated (e.g. decimals in division)?

Then **redo** every question you lost marks on, without looking at the mark scheme, and check again. A question only leaves the error log when you can do it correctly on your own.

## Quick-fire revision

Points examiners' reports for Paper 2 regularly mention:
- not simplifying fractions or giving decimals where an exact answer is needed;
- estimating without rounding to 1 significant figure;
- confusing HCF and LCM;
- sign errors when expanding brackets with a negative in front;
- angle questions without geometrical reasons when reasons are asked for;
- measuring diagrams that are not drawn to scale.
