# Past paper · Paper 4 timed (1/2)

> **Goal:** Do the first hour of a full Extended Paper 4 under exam conditions, with your calculator, and mark clearly where you stop.

## Before you start

- **Paper:** a Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 **Paper 4 (Extended)** from the 2025 syllabus onwards — e.g. May/June 2026, Oct/Nov 2025 or May/June 2025 (variant 41, 42 or 43), or the 2025 specimen Paper 4. Choose one you have **not** done, ideally from the same series as your Paper 2 so you can compare.
- Older Paper 4s (before 2025) were 2 h 30 min and 130 marks. If you must use one, allow about 1.2 minutes per mark and expect it to take longer than two sessions.
- **Mark scheme:** downloaded but closed until session 6.
- **Calculator:** your scientific calculator (the one you will use in the exam), in **degree** mode. Check: sin 30 = 0.5.
- **Equipment:** black pen, pencil, ruler, protractor, compasses, eraser, tracing paper, timer.
- Quiet room, phone away.

## Exam format reminder

| | Paper 4 (Extended) |
|---|---|
| Duration | 2 hours |
| Marks | 100 (50% of the qualification) |
| Calculator | **Required** |
| Questions | structured questions, each with several parts; answer all of them |
| Pace | about **1.2 minutes per mark** |
| Accuracy | non-exact answers to **3 significant figures**, angles in degrees to **1 decimal place**, unless the question says otherwise |
| π | use your calculator value or 3.142 |

Questions are longer than on Paper 2: a typical question is set in one context (a journey, a business, a shape, a data set) and combines several topics. A graph-drawing question and a statistics question appear in most papers.

## How to do it

This paper is split over two sessions. Today you work for **60 minutes**.

1. **0:00 – 0:02** Write the start time. Read the cover instructions and the formula list.
2. **0:02 – 0:58** Work through the questions in order.
   - Time checks: about mark 20 at 25 minutes, about mark 40 at 50 minutes.
   - If a part takes far longer than its marks deserve, star it and move to the next part (later parts often do not depend on it, or you can use a given value).
3. **0:58 – 1:00** Stop. Draw a line under your last working and write "STOP – session 1 – 60 min". List any starred parts.
4. Put the paper away. Do not check answers or revise those questions before session 2.

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Write down calculator working.** Show the expression you typed, e.g. "5000 × 1.032⁶ = 6040.156…". If the final answer is wrong, the method mark can still be given.
- **Keep full accuracy.** Store intermediate answers in the calculator memory (or write at least 4–5 significant figures) and only round the final answer. Premature rounding is one of the most common reasons for losing A marks on Paper 4.
- **3 significant figures:** write the full display first, then round: 0.045 618 → 0.0456; 12 345.6 → 12 300. Money to the nearest cent.
- **"Show that" with a given value** (e.g. "show that the angle is 37.6° correct to 1 d.p."): give a more accurate value (37.62…) before the rounded one.
- **Graph questions:** use brackets for negative x in the table; plot carefully; smooth curve; draw lines needed to solve equations; read values to the accuracy of the scale.
- **Trigonometry:** label the triangle, choose SOH CAH TOA, sine rule or cosine rule, and write the rule with the values substituted. Bearings are three figures, measured clockwise from north.
- **Statistics:** estimate of the mean from a grouped table uses mid-values: Σfx ÷ Σf. Cumulative frequency is plotted at the upper end of each class.
- **Units:** check what units the answer line has (cm² vs m², minutes vs hours).
- **Reverse percentages and compound interest** come up often in the first context question — see your number notes.

## Checklist while marking

Marking is in session 6. Before you finish today:
- answers on the answer lines, with the accuracy asked for;
- starred parts listed on the cover;
- stopping point clearly marked.

## Quick-fire revision

Calculator checks before you begin:
- degree mode (sin 90 = 1);
- fraction key and the S⇔D key to switch between fraction and decimal;
- the ×10ˣ key for standard form: (3.2 × 10⁴) entered as 3.2 ×10ˣ 4;
- the memory or Ans key, to avoid rounding;
- brackets round numerators and denominators: (7 + √73) ÷ (2 × 3).
