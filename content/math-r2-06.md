# Mark Paper 4 with the mark scheme

> **Goal:** Mark your Paper 4 strictly with the official mark scheme, compare it with your Paper 2 result, and add every lost mark to your error log.

## Before you start

- Your completed Paper 4 (both sessions).
- The **mark scheme** for the same series and variant (check the code, e.g. 0580/42 Oct/Nov 2025).
- The **examiner report** and **grade thresholds** for that series.
- Red pen, calculator, your error log (with the Paper 2 entries from last time).

## Exam format reminder

Paper 4 (Extended): 2 hours, 100 marks, calculator required, 50% of the final grade. Answers to 3 significant figures (angles 1 decimal place) unless the question says otherwise. Together with Paper 2 (another 100 marks) it gives your total out of 200.

## How to do it

1. **0:00 – 0:03** Reread the abbreviations at the start of the mark scheme (M, A, B, SC, ft, oe, cao, isw, dep — see the Paper 2 marking session).
2. **0:03 – 0:38** Mark question by question.
   - Check accuracy carefully: the mark scheme often accepts a range of answers or writes, e.g., "12.3 or 12.30…". An answer that is wrong because you rounded too early **loses the A mark**.
   - Graphs: mark scheme tolerances are usually stated (e.g. a point must be within half a small square).
   - Look for method marks you earned even when the final answer is wrong.
3. **0:38 – 0:43** Total out of 100. Add your Paper 2 total for a score out of 200 and compare with the grade thresholds for that series (use the thresholds table, not a guessed percentage).
4. **0:43 – 0:57** Error log entries for every lost mark.
5. **0:57 – 1:00** Update your list of three weakest topics, taking both papers into account.

## Exam technique for this paper

What marking Paper 4 usually shows — and how to fix it in the real exam:

- **Premature rounding.** If your answer is 0.1 or 0.2 away from the mark scheme value, you probably rounded in the middle. Fix: use the Ans key or memory; write 5+ significant figures for intermediate values.
- **Wrong accuracy in the final answer.** 3 s.f. means 3 significant figures, not 3 decimal places. 0.04562 → 0.0456, not 0.046.
- **Missing working.** Calculator answers with no written expression score nothing if wrong. Always write the calculation first.
- **Misreading the context.** "Find the interest" vs "find the total"; "the percentage profit" vs "the profit". Underline the question's final request.
- **Not using given values.** "Show that x = 7.3" followed by "use x = 7.3 to find…" — use the given value even if you got something else.
- **Calculator mode.** If all your trigonometry answers are wrong in the same way, check degree mode.

## Checklist while marking

For each lost mark add a row to the error log:

| Paper | Q | Topic | Marks lost | Error type | Correct method / fact |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P4 | 3c | Bounds | 1 | Method | upper bound of a ÷ b = a_max ÷ b_min |
| P4 | 6b | Sine rule | 2 | Careless | rounded side to 4.6 too early |
| P4 | 9a | Histogram | 2 | Knowledge | frequency density = frequency ÷ width |

Error types: **Knowledge** (didn't know), **Method** (wrong approach), **Careless** (slip, misread, rounding, units), **Time** (not reached).

Then compare the two papers:
- Which **topics** appear in the log for both papers? These are the top priority.
- Which **error type** is most common? If "Careless" dominates, plan 5 minutes of checking at the end of each paper. If "Time" dominates, practise with strict timing per question.
- Did you score a higher percentage on Paper 2 or Paper 4? Note it at the top of the log.

Finally, redo every question where you lost marks, without the mark scheme, then check.

## Quick-fire revision

Points examiners' reports for Paper 4 regularly mention:
- premature rounding in multi-step calculations;
- sine rule used for a non-matching pair; cosine rule rearranged wrongly for an angle;
- histograms drawn with frequency instead of frequency density;
- transformations described incompletely (missing centre, angle or direction);
- compound interest calculated as simple interest;
- graph curves drawn as straight-line segments or with points misplotted.
