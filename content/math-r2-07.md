# Weak topics and formulae

> **Goal:** Fix your three weakest topics from the error log and make sure you know by heart every formula that is not printed in the exam.

## Before you start

- Your **error log** from marking Paper 2 and Paper 4, with your three weakest topics chosen.
- The topic study pages for those topics (the Round 1 maths pages) and your summary cards.
- A blank sheet for a formula sheet, plus flashcards.
- The **formula list** page from the front of the past paper you used (to see exactly what is given).
- One or two extra past papers (not yet done) to take questions on your weak topics from, and their mark schemes.

## Exam format reminder

| | Paper 2 | Paper 4 |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | 2 hours | 2 hours |
| Marks | 100 | 100 |
| Calculator | not allowed | required |
| Weight | 50% | 50% |

Both papers include a list of formulae. For the Extended papers it covers areas and volumes (e.g. trapezium, circle, curved surface area and volume of a cylinder, cone and sphere, volume of a prism and pyramid) and, for Extended only, the quadratic formula, the sine rule, the cosine rule and the area of a triangle ½ab sin C. **Check the list in your own past paper** — anything not on it must be learnt.

## How to do it

1. **0:00 – 0:05** Look at the error log. Sort entries by topic and count marks lost. Confirm your top three topics (most marks lost, appearing on both papers).
2. **0:05 – 0:35** Weak topics, 10 minutes each:
   - 3 min: reread the notes on the topic and write the key method on a card;
   - 6 min: redo the question you got wrong, then one more question on the same topic from another past paper;
   - 1 min: mark it. If still wrong, mark the topic for another session.
3. **0:35 – 0:50** Formula sheet: write the formulae below **from memory** on a blank sheet. Then compare with this list and highlight what you missed.
4. **0:50 – 0:57** Make flashcards of the highlighted formulae (formula on one side, a quick example on the other).
5. **0:57 – 1:00** Update the error log: tick off topics now fixed; mark what still needs work.

## Exam technique for this paper

- Write formulae down **before** substituting — the formula with values in it usually earns the method mark.
- If a formula is on the list, use the list; don't rely on memory for those.
- For each weak topic, ask "what kind of error was it?" A knowledge gap needs notes and examples; a method error needs several similar questions; careless errors need a checking routine.
- Mix your weak topics into short timed sets (e.g. 3 questions in 12 minutes) in later sessions — spaced practice works better than one long block.

## Checklist while marking

When you mark today's retry questions:
- Did you get the method right first time (M marks)? If yes but the answer was wrong, it is a careless error — note what slipped.
- Is the answer in the right form and accuracy?
- Move a topic off the "weak" list only after **two** correct attempts on different days.

## Quick-fire revision

**Formulae and facts not given on the formula list — learn these.**

Number
- Percentage change = (change ÷ original) × 100; reverse percentage: original = new ÷ multiplier.
- Simple interest I = Prn/100; compound interest/growth: P(1 + r/100)ⁿ; decay: P(1 − r/100)ⁿ.
- Bounds: half a unit either side; a ÷ b max = a_max ÷ b_min; a − b max = a_max − b_min.
- Index laws: aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ, aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ, (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ, a⁰ = 1, a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ, a^(m/n) = (ⁿ√a)ᵐ.

Algebra and graphs
- Difference of two squares: a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b).
- Completed square: x² + bx + c = (x + b/2)² − (b/2)² + c; turning point of (x + p)² + q is (−p, q).
- nth term of a linear sequence: dn + (a − d); quadratic: a = second difference ÷ 2; geometric: a × rⁿ⁻¹.
- Variation: y = kx, y = kx², y = k/x, y = k/x², etc.
- Gradient m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁); y = mx + c; perpendicular gradients: m₁ × m₂ = −1.
- Midpoint ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2); length √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²).
- Differentiation: axⁿ → naxⁿ⁻¹; turning points where dy/dx = 0.
- Speed = distance ÷ time; acceleration = gradient of speed–time graph; distance = area under it.

Geometry and mensuration
- Pythagoras a² + b² = c²; SOH CAH TOA.
- Angle sum of a polygon (n − 2) × 180°; exterior angles of a polygon add to 360°.
- Circle area πr² and circumference 2πr are on the list, but know them anyway — you need them for arcs and sectors.
- Arc length (θ/360) × 2πr; sector area (θ/360) × πr².
- Similar shapes: area factor k², volume factor k³.
- Density = mass ÷ volume.
- Exact values: sin 30° = ½, cos 60° = ½, tan 45° = 1, sin 60° = cos 30° = √3/2, sin 45° = cos 45° = √2/2, tan 60° = √3, tan 30° = 1/√3.
- Circle theorems with their reasons (angle at centre = 2 × angle at circumference, angle in a semicircle = 90°, angles in the same segment are equal, opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°, tangent ⟂ radius, alternate segment theorem).

Statistics and probability
- Mean from a frequency table: Σfx ÷ Σf (mid-values for grouped data).
- Frequency density = frequency ÷ class width.
- Interquartile range = upper quartile − lower quartile.
- P(not A) = 1 − P(A); independent events P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B); mutually exclusive P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B).
- Conditional probability: P(A given B) = P(A and B) ÷ P(B).
