# Elements: harmony, tonality and cadences

> **In the exam:** Harmony and tonality questions appear on the Appraising paper (1 h 45 min, 80 marks) as 1–4 mark questions: identify a **cadence** (1 mark), say whether music is **major or minor** (1 mark), name a **modulation** (1–2 marks), describe harmony ("slow chord changes", "dissonant", "pedal note") for 2–4 marks, and use these ideas in the 12-mark comparison. Also useful for Composing, where chord choice earns marks.

## What you need to know

- **Triads** and chord numbers (I, IV, V, vi), **primary chords**, **chord inversions**, **seventh chords** (V7).
- **Tonality**: major, minor, **modal**, **atonal**, **tonic** and **dominant**.
- **Cadences**: perfect, imperfect, plagal, interrupted; Tierce de Picardie.
- **Modulation** (key change) and common destinations.
- **Consonance and dissonance**, suspension, pedal note, drone, chromatic harmony.
- Harmonic features of the set works: ground bass and **basso continuo** (Purcell, Bach), **12-bar blues** (pop/jazz), chord loops in pop.

## Notes

### Chords

A **triad** is three notes: a root, a 3rd above and a 5th above. A **chord** is two or more notes played together; an **arpeggio** or **broken chord** plays them one at a time.

The chords are numbered from each note of the scale using **Roman numerals**. In C major:

| Number | Name | Chord | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | **Tonic** | C–E–G | home chord, start and finish |
| II | Supertonic | D–F–A | leads to V |
| IV | **Subdominant** | F–A–C | moves to I or V |
| V | **Dominant** | G–B–D | creates tension, wants to go to I |
| VI | Submediant | A–C–E | relative minor chord |
| V7 | Dominant seventh | G–B–D–F | stronger pull to I |

**I, IV and V are the primary chords** and can harmonise most simple melodies. Major keys: I, IV, V are major; in minor keys, i and iv are minor and V is major (it needs the raised 7th).

**Inversions**: root position has the root as the lowest note. First inversion (marked "b") has the 3rd in the bass; second inversion ("c") has the 5th in the bass. Inversions make a smoother bass line.

### Tonality

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Major** | bright; based on the major scale |
| **Minor** | darker; lowered 3rd (and often 6th, 7th) |
| **Modal** | based on a mode such as Dorian or Mixolydian (rock, folk, film) |
| **Tonal** | has a clear key centre |
| **Atonal** | no key centre; no tonic (20th-century music) |
| **Chromatic** | uses many notes from outside the key; tense and unstable |
| **Diatonic** | uses only notes from the key; stable |

### Consonance and dissonance

- **Consonant**: sounds stable and pleasant (octaves, 3rds, 5ths).
- **Dissonant**: sounds tense and clashing (2nds, 7ths, tritone), wanting to resolve.
- **Suspension**: a note from the previous chord is held over a chord change, creates a dissonance, then falls by step to resolve.
- **Pedal note**: a long held or repeated note (usually in the bass) while chords change above it. **Dominant pedal** builds tension; **tonic pedal** gives stability.
- **Drone**: a sustained note or fifth, typical of bagpipes, folk and Indian music.

### Cadences

A **cadence** is two chords that end a phrase, like punctuation.

| Cadence | Chords | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Perfect** | V → I (or V7 → I) | full stop, finished, strong |
| **Imperfect** | any chord → V (often I–V or IV–V) | comma, unfinished, a question |
| **Plagal** | IV → I | the "Amen" cadence, gentler |
| **Interrupted** | V → VI | surprise, avoids the expected ending |

**Tierce de Picardie**: a minor-key piece (or section) ends on a **major tonic chord** (raised 3rd). Common in Baroque music.

How to hear them: perfect and plagal both feel finished. Perfect has a strong "pull" from dominant; plagal is calmer. Imperfect feels unfinished. Interrupted sounds like a surprise.

### Modulation

**Modulation** is a change of key within a piece. The most common destinations from a **major** key: the **dominant** (e.g. C major to G major) and the **relative minor**. From a **minor** key: the **relative major** (A minor to C major) and the dominant.

You can often hear it as a new accidental that becomes part of the music (for example F♯ appearing for a move to G major). A **pop key change** (modulation up a tone or semitone near the end, e.g. in *Defying Gravity* or many ballads) gives the final chorus a lift.

In Classical sonata form (Beethoven), the **exposition** moves from the tonic to a contrasting key (for a minor-key piece, the **relative major**), and the **recapitulation** brings the music back to the tonic.

### Harmonic rhythm and progressions

- **Harmonic rhythm**: how often the chords change. Slow = calm; fast = busy.
- **Chord loops**: pop and rock repeat 3 or 4 chords (e.g. I–V–vi–IV).
- **12-bar blues**: 12 bars built on I, IV and V (I I I I | IV IV I I | V IV I I/V).
- **Ground bass** (Purcell): a bass line repeated over and over while the music above changes. **Basso continuo**: bass instrument (cello, bass viol) plus a chord instrument (harpsichord, organ, lute) playing the figured bass; the harmony is "realised" from the numbers under the bass.
- **Circle of fifths progression**: chords whose roots fall by fifths, typical of Baroque sequences.

### How to describe harmony in the exam

Good phrases: "slow harmonic rhythm with sustained chords"; "uses a **perfect cadence** to end the phrase"; "modulates to the dominant"; "dissonant, chromatic chords create tension"; "**tonic pedal** under changing chords"; "chord loop of four chords repeated through the verse".

## Key terms and definitions

- **Triad**: a chord of three notes: root, third and fifth.
- **Primary chords**: chords I, IV and V.
- **Cadence**: a chord progression that ends a phrase.
- **Perfect cadence**: chord V followed by chord I.
- **Imperfect cadence**: a cadence ending on chord V.
- **Plagal cadence**: chord IV followed by chord I.
- **Interrupted cadence**: chord V followed by chord VI.
- **Modulation**: a change of key within a piece.
- **Tonality**: the key of a piece of music; the way it uses a tonic centre.
- **Pedal note**: a long sustained or repeated note, usually in the bass, while the harmony changes above.
- **Dissonance**: a combination of notes that sounds tense and wants to resolve.
- **Tierce de Picardie**: a major chord at the end of a piece in a minor key.

## Common mistakes

- Calling any ending a "perfect cadence": it must be **V to I**.
- Saying a cadence is "major" or "minor": say perfect, imperfect, plagal or interrupted.
- Confusing **key change (modulation)** with a change of chord.
- Saying "dissonant" and "minor" mean the same. Minor can be consonant; dissonance is about clashing notes.
- Forgetting that minor-key pieces often end on a major chord (**Tierce de Picardie**).
- Confusing **pedal** (held note) with **drone** (usually part of folk/Indian music; the drone is permanent).
