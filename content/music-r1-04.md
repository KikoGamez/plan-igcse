# Elements: rhythm, metre, tempo and dynamics

> **In the exam:** Rhythm, metre, tempo and dynamics come up in almost every listening question on the Appraising paper (1 h 45 min, 80 marks): name a time signature or tempo marking (1 mark), describe a rhythm ("syncopated", "dotted") for 2–3 marks, explain a change in tempo or dynamics (1–2 marks), and **rhythm dictation** (completing a rhythm you hear). Italian terms appear in scores and as vocabulary questions.

## What you need to know

- Note and rest values, dotted notes, ties, triplets.
- **Time signatures**: simple (2/4, 3/4, 4/4), compound (6/8, 9/8, 12/8) and irregular (5/4, 7/8); beats per bar.
- **Rhythmic devices**: syncopation, anacrusis, hemiola, polyrhythm, cross-rhythm, swing, rubato.
- **Tempo**: metronome marks (bpm), Italian terms, changes of speed.
- **Dynamics and articulation**: symbols and Italian terms.

## Notes

### Note values (British names)

| Note | Name (UK) | US name | Beats (crotchet = 1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 𝅝 | semibreve | whole note | 4 |
| 𝅗𝅥 | minim | half note | 2 |
| ♩ | crotchet | quarter note | 1 |
| ♪ | quaver | eighth note | ½ |
| 𝅘𝅥𝅯 | semiquaver | sixteenth note | ¼ |

A **dot** adds half the note's value: dotted crotchet = 1½ beats. A **tie** joins two notes of the same pitch into one longer note. A **triplet** squeezes 3 notes into the time of 2. **Rests** are silences with the same names (semibreve rest, etc.).

### Time signatures

The **top number** says how many beats in a bar; the **bottom number** says what kind of beat (4 = crotchet, 8 = quaver).

| Type | Time signature | Beats and feel | Example feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple duple | 2/4 | 2 crotchet beats, each splits into 2 | march |
| Simple triple | 3/4 | 3 crotchet beats | waltz |
| Simple quadruple | 4/4 | 4 crotchet beats | most pop and rock |
| Compound duple | **6/8** | **2** dotted-crotchet beats, each splits into 3 | jig, lilting feel |
| Compound triple / quadruple | 9/8, 12/8 | 3 / 4 dotted-crotchet beats | slow blues, ballads |
| Irregular | 5/4, 7/8 | groups like 3+2 or 2+2+3 | Balkan and modern music |

**Simple time** beats divide into 2; **compound time** beats divide into 3. To count 6/8 say "ONE-two-three TWO-two-three". Do not call 6/8 "six beats": it has two main beats.

### Rhythmic features

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Syncopation** | accents on weak beats or off the beat; notes tied across the beat. Typical of jazz, funk, Latin music, samba (Spalding) |
| **Anacrusis (upbeat)** | one or more notes before the first full bar |
| **Dotted rhythm** | long-short pattern; typical of Baroque overtures, marches |
| **Hemiola** | in 3/4 or 6/8, two groups of 3 felt as three groups of 2, giving a feel of cross-rhythm; used at Baroque cadences |
| **Polyrhythm** | two or more different rhythms at the same time (West African drumming, *Release*) |
| **Cross-rhythm** | one rhythm against another with a different pulse |
| **Swing** | pairs of quavers played long-short (like triplets), a jazz feature |
| **Rubato** | "robbed time", slight speeding up and slowing down for expression |
| **Pulse** | the steady beat. **Free time** has no steady pulse |
| **Ostinato** | repeated rhythmic or melodic pattern |
| **Backbeat** | accent on beats 2 and 4 (snare drum in rock) |

### Tempo

Tempo is the speed of the beat. It is shown by **metronome marks** (♩ = 120 means 120 crotchet beats per minute) or by Italian terms.

| Term | Meaning | Approx. bpm |
|---|---|---|
| Grave | very slow, solemn | 40 |
| Largo | slow, broad | 45–60 |
| Adagio | slow, at ease | 66–76 |
| Andante | walking pace | 76–108 |
| Moderato | moderate | 108–120 |
| Allegro | fast, lively | 120–156 |
| Vivace | lively, quick | 156–176 |
| Presto | very fast | 168–200 |

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| accelerando (accel.) | getting faster |
| rallentando (rall.), ritardando (rit.) | gradually slowing down |
| a tempo | back to the original speed |
| rubato | flexible tempo |
| fermata (pause) | hold the note longer |

Beethoven's Pathétique begins with a slow **Grave** introduction before the fast **Allegro di molto e con brio** (very fast with spirit).

### Dynamics

| Symbol | Italian | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **pp** | pianissimo | very quiet |
| **p** | piano | quiet |
| **mp** | mezzo piano | moderately quiet |
| **mf** | mezzo forte | moderately loud |
| **f** | forte | loud |
| **ff** | fortissimo | very loud |
| **cresc.** (hairpin <) | crescendo | getting louder |
| **dim.** / **decresc.** (hairpin >) | diminuendo | getting quieter |
| **sfz** | sforzando | a sudden strong accent |

**Terraced dynamics** (Baroque) are sudden changes between loud and quiet, rather than gradual ones, because of the harpsichord and organ. The piano was developed in the Classical era because it could play both **piano** and **forte** and make gradual changes. This is how the "piano-forte" got its name.

### Articulation

**Legato** (smooth, joined; shown with a slur), **staccato** (short, detached; dot), **accent** (>), **tenuto** (hold full value), **marcato** (marked, emphasised), **pizzicato** (plucked strings), **sostenuto** (sustained).

### Describing rhythm and tempo well

- "The rhythm is **syncopated**, with accents on the off-beat."
- "Allegro in **4/4** with a steady **backbeat** on beats 2 and 4."
- "Tempo is slow and expressive with **rubato**, and ends with a **rallentando**."
- "A **crescendo** builds from piano to forte over four bars."

## Key terms and definitions

- **Pulse**: the regular beat in music.
- **Metre**: the grouping of beats into bars, shown by the time signature.
- **Time signature**: two numbers at the start; top = beats per bar, bottom = type of beat.
- **Simple time**: each beat divides into two.
- **Compound time**: each beat divides into three.
- **Syncopation**: accenting weak beats, or notes off the beat.
- **Anacrusis**: an upbeat; the note(s) before the first strong beat of the first full bar.
- **Polyrhythm**: two or more contrasting rhythms played at the same time.
- **Tempo**: the speed of the music.
- **Rubato**: flexibility of tempo, speeding up and slowing down for expression.
- **Crescendo / diminuendo**: gradually getting louder / quieter.

## Worked examples

**1. How many beats are in a bar of 6/8, and what type of time is it? [2]**
Two main beats (dotted crotchets) (1); compound duple (1).

**2. Describe the rhythm of the main melody of this extract (syncopated funk riff). [3]**
"Syncopated (1) with notes tied over the beat / accents on off-beats (1) in a steady 4/4 groove with a regular backbeat (1)."

**3. The tempo marking is Andante and the music slows at the end. Give the terms. [2]**
Andante = at a walking pace (1); rallentando or ritardando (1).

## Common mistakes

- Treating **6/8** as six beats: it is two compound beats.
- Mixing up **tempo** (speed) with **dynamics** (volume) and **rhythm** (pattern of note lengths).
- Writing "syncopated" for any fast rhythm: syncopation is accents on weak beats or off the beat.
- Mixing crotchet and quaver (quaver = half a beat, crotchet = 1; US eighth = quaver).
- Forgetting that a dot adds half the value, not one beat.
- Using "pianissimo" for "piano": pp is very quiet, p is quiet.
