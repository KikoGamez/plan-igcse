# Elements: rhythm, metre, tempo and dynamics

## What you need to know

- All note and rest values including **dotted rhythms** and **triplets**.
- Metre: duple, triple, quadruple, irregular; simple and compound time signatures.
- Rhythmic devices: syncopation, hemiola, anacrusis, cross-rhythm, polyrhythm, habanera, marcato, forward and reverse clave; tāla and īqā'āt.
- Tempo: Italian terms (allegro, andante, adagio…), changes in tempo (accelerando, rallentando, rubato), BPM.
- Dynamics: pp, p, mp, mf, f, ff, crescendo, diminuendo, terraced dynamics.
- Articulation: legato, staccato, accent, sforzando, slur.

## Notes

### 1. Note and rest values

| Name (UK) | US | Beats (crotchet = 1) |
|---|---|---|
| Semibreve | whole | 4 |
| Minim | half | 2 |
| Crotchet | quarter | 1 |
| Quaver | eighth | ½ |
| Semiquaver | sixteenth | ¼ |

Each note has a matching **rest** (silence) of the same length.

- A **dot** adds half the note's value: a dotted crotchet = 1½ beats; a dotted minim = 3 beats. A dotted rhythm (long–short, e.g. dotted quaver + semiquaver) gives a jerky, march-like or "swinging" feel.
- A **triplet** squeezes three notes into the time of two (written with a 3 above/below): three triplet quavers = one crotchet beat.
- A **tie** joins two notes of the same pitch into one long note.

### 2. Metre and time signatures

**Metre** is the pattern of strong and weak beats. The **time signature** shows it: the top number = beats per bar; the bottom = the type of note counted (4 = crotchet, 8 = quaver).

| Type | Time signatures | Beat is… | Feel / examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Simple duple** | 2/4 | crotchet, 2 per bar | march, polka |
| **Simple triple** | 3/4 | crotchet, 3 per bar | waltz, minuet |
| **Simple quadruple** | 4/4 (common time, **C**) | crotchet, 4 per bar | rock, pop |
| **Compound duple** | 6/8 | dotted crotchet, 2 per bar, each split into 3 quavers | jig, lilting "ONE-and-a TWO-and-a" |
| **Compound triple** | 9/8 | 3 dotted crotchets | |
| **Compound quadruple** | 12/8 | 4 dotted crotchets | slow blues, ballads |
| **Irregular** | 5/4, 7/8 | unequal groups (3+2, 2+2+3…) | restless, unusual feel |

In **simple time** each beat divides into **two**; in **compound time** each beat divides into **three**. A quick test: tap along. If beats feel like "1 and 2 and", it is simple; if "1 2 3, 4 5 6" in two big beats, it is 6/8.

### 3. Rhythmic features

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Syncopation** | Stressing weak beats or off-beats by accents or ties, so the rhythm goes against the pulse. Jazz, funk, Latin music |
| **Hemiola** | Two bars of 3 felt as three groups of 2 (3/4 sounding like 3/2); creates a slowing, cadential "broadening" |
| **Anacrusis** | One or more unstressed notes **before the first bar line** (an upbeat) |
| **Cross-rhythm** | Two or more conflicting rhythms or accent patterns played together (e.g. 3 against 2) |
| **Polyrhythm** | Several different rhythms sounded at once (typical of African and Latin music) |
| **Ostinato** | Persistent repeated rhythm or pattern (see topic 5) |
| **Rubato** | "Stolen time": flexibility in the tempo for expression |

### 4. Latin and world rhythms (areas 5 and 6)

- **Habanera:** a dotted-quaver, semiquaver, quaver, quaver pattern in 2/4 (one bar = 2 beats): a Cuban-born rhythm that underlies tango and related Latin styles; it has an elastic, swaying feel.
- **Marcado:** strongly marked, accented beats in tango, a "stomping" four-in-the-bar that gives the music its stern, dramatic character.
- **Clave:** a five-note rhythmic key pattern in salsa and other Afro-Cuban music, played on two wooden sticks. In 4/4 it has **three** hits in one bar and **two** in the other. **Forward clave** (3–2) starts with the three-hit bar; **reverse clave** (2–3) starts with the two-hit bar. All other parts fit around the clave. It is a **cross-rhythm** against the steady beat.
- **Tāla** (Hindustani): a repeating rhythmic cycle of beats (e.g. teental, 16 beats in 4 groups of 4), played on the tabla. The first beat of the cycle, **sam**, is the point of arrival.
- **Īqā'āt** (singular **īqā'**): rhythmic modes in Arab music, cycles of strong ("dum") and weak ("tak") strokes on percussion such as the riq or darbuka.

### 5. Tempo

| Italian | Meaning | BPM (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| Largo / Lento | very slow, broad | 40–60 |
| Adagio | slow | 66–76 |
| Andante | walking pace | 76–108 |
| Moderato | moderate | 108–120 |
| Allegro | fast, lively | 120–156 |
| Vivace / Presto | very fast | 156+ |

**BPM** = beats per minute (e.g. 120 BPM = 2 beats per second); EDM tempos are given in BPM (typically 120 to 150).

**Changes of tempo:** **accelerando** (gradually faster), **rallentando** / **ritardando** (gradually slower, often at the end of a piece), **rubato** (flexible), **a tempo** (back to the original speed).

### 6. Dynamics and articulation

| Sign | Italian | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **pp** | pianissimo | very quiet |
| **p** | piano | quiet |
| **mp** | mezzo piano | moderately quiet |
| **mf** | mezzo forte | moderately loud |
| **f** | forte | loud |
| **ff** | fortissimo | very loud |
| **cresc.** | crescendo | gradually louder |
| **dim.** | diminuendo | gradually quieter |
| **sf** | sforzando | a sudden strong accent |

**Terraced dynamics** are sudden steps from loud to quiet (no gradual change). They are typical of **Baroque** music (organ, harpsichord, concerto grosso tutti vs concertino).

**Articulation:** **legato** (smooth, joined; shown by a **slur**), **staccato** (short and detached; dot above/below), **accent** (>: emphasised).

## Key terms and definitions

- **Pulse:** the steady beat in music.
- **Metre:** the grouping of beats into regular strong and weak patterns.
- **Simple time:** each beat divides into two (e.g. 2/4, 3/4, 4/4).
- **Compound time:** each beat divides into three (e.g. 6/8, 9/8, 12/8).
- **Syncopation:** placing emphasis on weak beats or off-beats.
- **Hemiola:** a rhythmic device in which two bars of triple time are felt as three beats of duple time.
- **Anacrusis:** the note(s) before the first full bar; an upbeat.
- **Tempo:** the speed of the music.
- **Dynamics:** the volume of the music.
- **Terraced dynamics:** sudden changes between loud and quiet with no gradual change.

## Worked examples

**1. How many crotchet beats are in a dotted minim followed by a quaver rest and two quavers? [2]**
Dotted minim = 3 beats [1]. Quaver rest + two quavers = 3 × ½ = 1½ beats, so the total is **4½ beats** [1]. Always check a bar total against the time signature (this would not fit one bar of 4/4).

**2. An extract in 6/8 has two strong beats per bar and each beat divides into three. Name the time type. [2]**
**Compound duple** time [2] (1 mark for compound, 1 for duple).

**3. Describe the rhythm and tempo of a salsa extract. [4]**
"The **clave** pattern is played throughout [1] (forward clave, 3–2) [1] with **syncopated** rhythms in the bass and piano [1] and a fast, steady tempo of about 180 BPM [1]."

## Common mistakes

- Treating 6/8 as "six beats". It is two beats, each divided in three.
- Forgetting that a dot adds half again.
- Mixing up **syncopation** (off-beat emphasis) with **anacrusis** (upbeat before bar 1).
- Using "fast" or "loud" with no term. Write allegro, vivace, ff, etc.
- Writing "crescendo" for a sudden loud passage. Crescendo is gradual; sudden = sforzando or terraced.
- Mixing up forward clave (3–2) with reverse clave (2–3).
