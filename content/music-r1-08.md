# Western music: Romantic and 20th century

> **In the exam:** Paper 1 (Listening, c.1 h 15 min, 70 marks) always has an extract from Area 3 (Romantic music), usually an orchestral extract with questions on instruments, texture, melody, key, tempo and "how does the music suggest...?". Extracts from Area 7 (stage and screen, early 20th century to now) also use orchestral music and ask how music creates atmosphere. Marks per extract run from 6 to 16, so expect a mix of 1-mark multiple choice, 2–4 mark "describe/explain" questions, and sometimes a printed score to follow.

## What you need to know

- **Area 3: Romantic music.** Focus on single-movement works for orchestra. Focus work: **Smetana, *Vltava* from *Má vlast***. Key trends: the **expansion of the orchestra**, the importance of **programme music**, and **nationalism**.
- Wider listening (not prescribed): Mendelssohn *A Midsummer Night's Dream* overture; Emilie Mayer *Faust* overture; Borodin String Quartet No. 2; Clara Schumann Piano Concerto in A minor; Chopin Étude in E, Op. 10 No. 3.
- Features of Romantic music: expressive melody, chromatic harmony, wide dynamic and tempo range, rubato, large orchestra.
- Early 20th century to now (Areas 4–7 repertoire): how composers use rhythm, harmony and orchestration in music for stage and screen, e.g. Stravinsky *The Rite of Spring*, John Williams *Star Wars*. See also the topics on World Focus and on world music.
- Terms: *leitmotif, programme music, symphonic poem, overture, nationalism, chromatic, rubato, tremolo, pizzicato, ostinato, pedal, polyrhythm, irregular metre, dissonance.*

## Notes

### Romantic music (c.1820–1900) in six points

| Feature | What you hear |
|---|---|
| **Expressive melody** | Long, lyrical, emotional tunes with wide range and big leaps; **rubato** (stretching the time) |
| **Chromatic harmony** | More notes from outside the key; richer chords, **dissonance** and surprising modulations to keep tension |
| **Large orchestra** | More instruments: more brass (trombones, tuba), piccolo, harp, extra percussion. Bigger sound and many **colours** |
| **Wide dynamics and tempi** | Very soft (pp or lower) to very loud (ff), many **crescendos**, frequent tempo changes (accelerando, rallentando) |
| **Programme music** | Music with a story or picture: **symphonic poems**, programme symphonies, concert overtures |
| **Nationalism** | Composers use folk tunes, dances and stories of their country (Smetana: Bohemia/Czech lands) |

Other Romantic genres: **concerto** for virtuoso soloist (e.g. Clara Schumann), solo **piano pieces/étude** (Chopin), **art song (lied)**, **string quartet** (Borodin), **concert overture** (Mendelssohn, Mayer).

### Focus work: Smetana, *Vltava* (1874)

*Vltava* ("The Moldau") is the second of six symphonic poems in the cycle ***Má vlast*** ("My Homeland"). A **symphonic poem** is a single-movement orchestral piece telling a story. *Vltava* follows the river Vltava from its **source** to Prague. It is a good example of **programme music** and **nationalism**, and of the expanded Romantic orchestra (piccolo, harp, brass, percussion are all used).

Typical way of working through the piece (check this against your recording and class notes):

| Section | What the music depicts | Musical features to describe |
|---|---|---|
| **The two sources** | Two small springs that join | **Flutes** (then clarinets) with flowing, rippling quavers over **pizzicato strings**: light, quiet, a gentle texture |
| **The river theme** | The wide river | **Long, lyrical melody** on high strings and woodwind, in a **minor key (E minor)** in compound time (6/8); it is heard many times, with changes of orchestration, as the main **recurring** idea (a kind of **theme transformation**) |
| **The hunt** | A forest hunt | **Horns** with hunting calls / fanfares; faster, lively, major key, brass-led |
| **Peasant wedding** | A village dance | A **polka**: bouncy, strong rhythm, in a major key; reflects Czech folk dance (nationalism) |
| **Moonlight and nymphs** | Night, water spirits | **Muted strings**, flutes, a soft, floating, calmer texture |
| **St John's Rapids** | Fast, rough water | **Fast, loud, dramatic;** cymbals and percussion, piccolo, rushing figures, **minor key**; big dynamic changes |
| **River widens** | Arrives in Prague | The river theme returns **in the major**, fuller and more triumphant; a **brass theme** suggests the Vyšehrad castle; the piece ends with two short, loud chords |

Check your own recording for exact instruments and keys; if a question quotes a printed extract, use what you see and hear in the extract.

Things to say when you describe *Vltava*:
- The **river theme** is transformed (different keys, instruments, dynamics) and keeps the work together.
- Orchestration paints the picture: flutes = water, horns = hunt, polka = dance, rapids = percussion and speed.
- **Nationalism**: the subject (a Czech river), folk-style dance and national pride.

### How to describe a Romantic extract

Cover: instruments (and any unusual ones), melody (lyrical, wide range), harmony (chromatic notes, dissonance), dynamics (crescendo to a climax), tempo (rubato, accelerando), texture (melody with accompaniment; sometimes polyphonic), **mood** and what it might depict. Always say how the music does it.

### 20th-century music: what changed

From the early 1900s composers broke away from the Romantic idea that music must always sound beautiful and smooth. The **syllabus covers early 20th-century to present-day music** in Areas 4–7. For Area 7 (stage and screen), study how orchestral music supports action.

| Feature | Example/effect |
|---|---|
| **Rhythm becomes powerful** | **Ostinato**, irregular and changing metre, strong accents and **polyrhythm**: Stravinsky, *The Rite of Spring* (ballet, 1913) |
| **Dissonance and new harmony** | Clashing chords and **cluster chords** create tension and shock |
| **Large orchestra with strong colour** | Extreme registers, brass and percussion used for impact |
| **Leitmotif for characters** | A short theme represents a character or idea (John Williams, *Star Wars*) |
| **Technology** | Sampling, synthesisers and electronic production in modern film, games and dance music |

**Film music tools:** **leitmotif**, fanfare (brass), tremolo and dissonant chords for tension, a rising sequence to build excitement, a slow theme on strings for emotion, a change of instrumentation for a change of mood. **Video game music:** loops that repeat without gaps and change with the action (Yoko Shimomura, *Gran Saga*).

## Key terms and definitions

- **Programme music:** instrumental music that tells a story or paints a picture.
- **Symphonic poem:** a single-movement orchestral work based on a story, poem or scene.
- **Nationalism (in music):** using the folk music, dances, stories or landscapes of one's own country to express national identity.
- **Chromatic harmony:** harmony using many notes from outside the key.
- **Rubato:** flexible tempo, "robbed time", speeding up and slowing down expressively.
- **Leitmotif:** a short recurring musical idea linked to a person, object or idea.
- **Ostinato:** a short pattern repeated many times.
- **Overture:** an orchestral piece played before a drama or opera; a concert overture is a stand-alone orchestral piece.
- **Tremolo:** very fast repetition of one note (or quick alternation between two), on strings made by rapid bow movement.
- **Pizzicato:** plucked strings.
- **Polyrhythm:** two or more different rhythms played at the same time.

## Worked examples

**1. Give two ways in which Smetana's *Vltava* shows features of Romantic music. [2]**
*Model answer:* "It is programme music because it follows the course of a river from its source to Prague (1). It uses a large orchestra with harp, piccolo and brass for different colours (1)." (also acceptable: nationalism through a polka and a Czech subject; wide dynamic contrasts.)

**2. Describe how the music of the extract creates the impression of flowing water. [3]**
*Model answer:* "Flutes play rippling, running quavers (1) over plucked, pizzicato strings (1). The dynamics are quiet and the texture is light and delicate (1)."
*Why it earns marks:* three precise musical details, each linked to the effect.

**3. Explain why the river theme is important in *Vltava*. [3]**
*Model answer:* "It is the main recurring melody (1) and returns many times as the river flows on (1). It is changed in key (minor to major), instrument and dynamic, so the piece is unified but varied (1)."

**4. Name two ways film composers create tension. [2]**
*Model answer:* "Tremolo strings (1) and dissonant chords/cluster chords (1)." Also: ostinato, rising sequence, crescendo, low brass.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing up periods: **Romantic** is not "romantic love songs". It is a period of music, c.1820–1900.
- Saying the whole of *Vltava* is in a minor key. It begins in the minor but changes mood and key.
- Saying "loud, fast, dramatic" without any musical language. Name the instrument or technique.
- Calling every orchestral piece with a story an "opera". An opera has singers and acting; a symphonic poem has none.
- Forgetting "how": "The horn imitates a hunt" earns a mark only when you say **horn calls/fanfares**.
- Using "leitmotif" for any repeated tune. A leitmotif represents a person or idea.
