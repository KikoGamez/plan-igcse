# Past paper · Listening paper timed (1/2)

> **Goal:** Sit the first half of a real Appraising paper under exam conditions, with the audio, so you know how it feels and where you lose marks.

## Before you start

- **Paper to use:** a real Pearson Edexcel **GCSE Music 1MU0/03 (Appraising)** past paper or the Sample Assessment Material. Pearson publishes past papers on its website (June series, e.g. 2018, 2019, 2022, 2023, depending on what is available) with mark schemes. The **audio** is only available through your teacher: ask for the CD/mp3 files. **Check with your teacher which paper and series the school uses for mocks.**
- Have ready: the question paper and printed extracts, a pencil and rubber, a pen, headphones or speakers, a timer, and your set-work fact sheets **closed** (no notes).
- Use the audio's built-in pauses; do not rewind.
- If you do not have the audio, sit the written questions you can and use the practice questions at the end of this file.

## Exam format reminder

- **Appraising paper (Paper 3, code 1MU0/03 in the Pearson Edexcel GCSE (9–1) Music spec):** 1 hour 45 minutes, **80 marks**, 40% of the course. Check with your teacher that this is the paper your school sits.
- **Section A (68 marks):** six questions based on the **eight set works** (two per area of study), using extracts from recordings and the printed score extracts. It includes short answers, listening for features, and a **melody/rhythm dictation** question where you complete notation.
- **Section B (12 marks):** one extended-response question comparing a set work with an unfamiliar piece, using musical vocabulary.
- You hear each extract several times, with set pauses; the paper is "audio-led", so the recording runs the exam.

## How to do it

1. **0–5 min** – Set up: quiet room, phone away, timer for 50 minutes. Read the front page.
2. **5–55 min** – Do **the first half of the paper** (about Questions 1–3, roughly 40 marks) with the audio playing. During pauses, write notes immediately.
3. **55–60 min** – Stop. Do not mark. Write on the paper where you were guessing. Put the paper away until the next session.

**Time rule:** about one minute per mark for written questions, plus the listening time.

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Read the question before the music starts.** In the pause before each play, underline the **command word** and the number of marks.
- **Marks = points.** A 2-mark question needs **two distinct points**; a 4-mark question needs four, each with evidence.
- Use **musical vocabulary**: instead of "the music gets louder", write "crescendo"; instead of "going up", write "ascending/sequence".
- **Name the instrument** you hear (e.g. "harpsichord"), not "keyboard".
- On **dictation** questions, write the rhythm first, then the pitch contour.
- If you miss something, **move on**; there are repeated plays.
- Write "from the set work" evidence where asked: key, structure, dynamics.

## Checklist while marking

(Do this in the next session; it is here so you know what to track now.)

- [ ] Did I read every question before the extract?
- [ ] Did I use technical words?
- [ ] Did I identify instruments precisely?
- [ ] Did I give the right number of points per mark?
- [ ] Which questions were about set works, and which were unfamiliar?

## Quick-fire revision

- Baroque: harpsichord, continuo, terraced dynamics, polyphony.
- Classical: balanced phrases, Alberti bass, sonata form.
- Romantic: chromatic, rubato, big orchestra, programme music.
- Ground bass = repeated bass (Purcell); ritornello = returning theme (Bach).
- Sonata form: exposition, development, recapitulation (Beethoven).
- Leitmotif = theme linked to a character (Star Wars).
- Fusion = blend of traditions (Release, Samba Em Prelúdio).
- Multi-tracking, panning, reverb = production terms (Queen).
- Monophonic, homophonic, polyphonic.
- Tempo/dynamic terms: allegro, grave, crescendo, sforzando.
- Cadences: perfect (V–I), imperfect (ends on V), plagal (IV–I), interrupted (V–vi).
- Rhythm terms: syncopation, triplet, dotted, 6/8 vs 3/4.
