# Past paper · Listening timed (1/2)

> **Goal:** Sit the first half of a Cambridge Paper 1 (extracts 1–4) under exam conditions, with the real audio, and see how well you can listen, follow a score and answer in correct terms before the mock.

## Before you start

- **Paper:** the **2026 specimen Paper 1** (question paper, insert, confidential instructions and mark scheme are published on the Cambridge International website, with audio from your teacher), or a **June or November 2026 Paper 1** variant (11, 12 or 13) if your teacher can give you the audio and mark scheme. Papers from before 2026 followed an earlier syllabus: they are fine for practice, but skip questions on set works or areas that are no longer in the syllabus. Ask your teacher which paper and audio to use: the audio is held on Cambridge's teacher site, so a school copy is needed.
- **Audio:** play the extracts with the **right number of repeats** (3–5 plays for each, as the paper says). Use a laptop or speaker, not headphones if you may be disturbed. Ask a parent to play the track so you don't press pause.
- **Mark scheme:** keep it closed; you use it in the next sessions.
- **Timer** and **pencil, black pen, rubber**; no keyboard or other instrument, as in the exam.
- Quiet desk, phone away, notes closed. Have a **blank page** for your error log.
- Print the insert if there is one.

## Exam format reminder

- **Paper 1 Listening** (Cambridge IGCSE Music 0410, syllabus for 2026–2028): about **1 hour 15 minutes**, **70 marks**, **40%** of the IGCSE. All questions test AO1 (listening). Externally marked.
- **Eight short recorded extracts**, with at least one from each of the seven areas of study (Baroque, Classical, Romantic, Music and words, Music for dance, Music for small ensemble, Music for stage and screen). The paper tells you the area and, where suitable, the tradition or genre.
- Seven extracts have **multiple choice and short answer** questions; **one** has a **short descriptive question**. Each extract is worth **6 to 16 marks**.
- Each extract is played **three to five times**, depending on the questions. Some extracts come with **printed music** (treble, alto and/or bass clef); longer passages are in a separate **insert**. Words are printed for vocal extracts, with a translation or summary if not in English.
- Question types: identify instruments, voices, features, composers (multiple choice); describe or comment; compare passages; follow a score; name the key, intervals, chords and cadences; fill in missing melody or rhythm (multiple choice); transpose short passages; identify structure with letters (e.g. AABA).
- Command words: **identify** = name; **give** = recall; **describe** = state features; **explain** = say why/how with evidence; **compare** = similarities and/or differences; **comment** = informed opinion; **suggest** = apply knowledge, several answers valid.

## How to do it

| Time | What to do |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | Read the instructions. Read the **whole of the first extract's questions** before the music starts. Underline command words and mark counts. |
| 0:03–0:12 | Extract 1 (play 3–5 times as set). During the first play, listen for the overall sound; on later plays, tackle each question. |
| 0:12–0:21 | Extract 2. |
| 0:21–0:30 | Extract 3. |
| 0:30–0:40 | Extract 4. |
| 0:40–0:45 | Check that every question is answered: multiple choice is never blank. |
| 0:45–1:00 | **Review (open book):** note the extracts where you were unsure; write for each: area of study, the style, and two features you missed. Start your error log. |

Rules: no replaying extracts. If the paper allows time between plays, use it to write, not to stare at the page. If you finish early, return to the 2–4 mark questions and add a feature.

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Read the questions first.** Know what to listen for before the first play (instrument? key? structure?).
- **Use the plays:** play 1 = overall impression and the easy questions; later plays = detail (cadence, rhythm, who enters when).
- **Multiple choice:** eliminate the answers you know are wrong; never leave blank.
- **Printed score:** follow with a finger. Use bar numbers. For a key question: key signature, then accidentals, then final chord.
- **Describe [2–4 marks]:** use **musical terms** (e.g. pizzicato, imitation, sequence, drone). The number of marks = the number of separate points.
- **Explain:** give a feature *and* the effect/reason ("tremolo strings create tension").
- **Compare:** write one sentence that includes **both** items ("The first is homophonic, whereas the second is polyphonic").
- **Context questions:** name the tradition, instrument and purpose (dance, ritual, film).
- **Transposition and intervals:** write the answer and show the quick working.
- **Longer descriptive question:** plan with the elements list: instruments, melody, harmony, rhythm, texture, structure, dynamics and tempo.

## Quick-fire revision

- Perfect cadence = V–I; imperfect = ends on V; interrupted = V–VI.
- Ripieno = full group; concertino = small solo group.
- Basso continuo = bass line plus harpsichord/organ chords.
- Sonata form: exposition (tonic then dominant), development, recapitulation (all tonic).
- Alberti bass = broken chords in left hand; Classical.
- Terraced dynamics = sudden changes; Baroque.
- Programme music = music that tells a story; Smetana's Vltava.
- Leitmotif = recurring theme for a person or idea.
- Clave = five-note rhythm pattern in salsa (3+2 or 2+3).
- Heterophonic = same tune in different decorated versions at once.
- Tanpura = drone in Hindustani music; ālāp → jōr → jhālā → gat.
- Maqām (Arab) includes quarter tones; takht includes oud, qanun, nay.
- EDM: build up → drop → breakdown.
- Syllabic = one note per syllable; melismatic = many.
- B flat clarinet sounds a major 2nd lower than written.
