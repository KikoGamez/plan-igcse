# Past paper · Listening timed (2/2)

> **Goal:** Finish the paper: sit the second half (extracts 5–8, including the descriptive question), under the same exam conditions, and then see how your listening stamina and your long answer hold up.

## Before you start

- **Paper:** the **2026 specimen Paper 1** (question paper, insert, confidential instructions and mark scheme are published on the Cambridge International website, with audio from your teacher), or a **June or November 2026 Paper 1** variant (11, 12 or 13) if your teacher can give you the audio and mark scheme. Papers from before 2026 followed an earlier syllabus: they are fine for practice, but skip questions on set works or areas that are no longer in the syllabus. Ask your teacher which paper and audio to use: the audio is held on Cambridge's teacher site, so a school copy is needed.
- Use the **same paper** as in session 1. If the paper has a separate **insert** for longer passages, have it ready.
- **Audio** set up for extracts 5–8 with the correct number of repeats.
- Timer, pencil, black pen, rubber. Notes closed. Mark scheme closed.
- Your list of **weak areas** from session 1 on a sticky note: remember it, but do not use notes during the test.

## Exam format reminder

- **Paper 1 Listening** (Cambridge IGCSE Music 0410, syllabus for 2026–2028): about **1 hour 15 minutes**, **70 marks**, **40%** of the IGCSE. All questions test AO1 (listening). Externally marked.
- **Eight short recorded extracts**, with at least one from each of the seven areas of study (Baroque, Classical, Romantic, Music and words, Music for dance, Music for small ensemble, Music for stage and screen). The paper tells you the area and, where suitable, the tradition or genre.
- Seven extracts have **multiple choice and short answer** questions; **one** has a **short descriptive question**. Each extract is worth **6 to 16 marks**.
- Each extract is played **three to five times**, depending on the questions. Some extracts come with **printed music** (treble, alto and/or bass clef); longer passages are in a separate **insert**. Words are printed for vocal extracts, with a translation or summary if not in English.
- Question types: identify instruments, voices, features, composers (multiple choice); describe or comment; compare passages; follow a score; name the key, intervals, chords and cadences; fill in missing melody or rhythm (multiple choice); transpose short passages; identify structure with letters (e.g. AABA).
- Command words: **identify** = name; **give** = recall; **describe** = state features; **explain** = say why/how with evidence; **compare** = similarities and/or differences; **comment** = informed opinion; **suggest** = apply knowledge, several answers valid.

## Exam technique for this paper

- **The descriptive extract** (the one question that asks you to describe): plan before writing, use the elements: **instruments, melody, harmony, rhythm and tempo, texture, structure, dynamics**, then **context**. Write in short, precise points; each fact is a mark. Do not waste time on narrative.
- **Order your points** by time: "At the start... then... at the end..."
- **Tradition recognition:** sound-world clues: tabla/sitar = Hindustani; oud/qanun = Arab; erhu/dizi = silk and bamboo; congas/clave = salsa; bandoneón = tango; synth drop = EDM.
- **Word-setting questions:** syllabic or melismatic? word-painting? strophic or through-composed? verse–chorus?
- **Stage and screen:** leitmotif, fanfare, tremolo, ostinato, dissonance, loops: say the technique and its effect.
- **Don't change a correct MCQ answer** unless you are sure.
- **Don't leave blanks:** in a "describe" question an attempt can earn a mark; a blank earns none.
- **Do not name pieces** unless asked: an extract is likely **not** a focus work; use what you can hear.

## Checklist while marking

- [ ] Have I answered every item in extracts 5–8?
- [ ] Is my long answer organised by elements (instruments, texture, etc.)?
- [ ] Did I use at least four named musical terms in the long answer?
- [ ] Which tradition clues did I miss?
- [ ] Was I out of time? Which question took the most time?
- [ ] For printed music: did I check the clef (alto clef for viola)?
- [ ] Add to the error log all misheard instruments and misread keys.

## Quick-fire revision

- Listening plan: question first, overall sound, detail, check.
- Elements list: instruments, melody, harmony, rhythm, texture, structure, dynamics, tempo, context.
- Texture words: monophonic, unison, homophonic, polyphonic, imitative, heterophonic, antiphonal, tutti, drone.
- Structure words: binary, ternary, rondo, ritornello, theme and variations, strophic, through-composed, verse–chorus.
- Ornaments: trill, mordent, turn, appoggiatura, acciaccatura.
- Articulation: legato, staccato, accent, sforzando, slur.
- Dynamics: pp, p, mp, mf, f, ff, crescendo, diminuendo.
- Italian tempo: allegro (fast), andante (walking), adagio (slow); accelerando, rallentando, rubato.
- Intervals: count from the lower note; major, minor, perfect.
- Minor key clue: raised 7th note (leading note) in the melody or harmony.
- Baroque clues: harpsichord, continuo, terraced dynamics, ornaments.
- Romantic clues: large orchestra, chromatic harmony, rubato, programme music.
- Dance clues: tango = marcado/habanera; salsa = clave; EDM = loops and drops.
- Alto clef = viola; transposing instruments need moving to concert pitch.
