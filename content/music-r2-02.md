# Past paper · Listening paper timed (2/2)

> **Goal:** Finish the paper under the same timed conditions, including the extended-response question, so you have a complete paper ready to mark.

## Before you start

- **Paper to use:** the **same** Pearson Edexcel 1MU0/03 paper as in session 1, with the same audio. Keep the pages from session 1 together.
- Have ready: the paper, pencil, pen, headphones, timer. No notes. Choose a quiet space.
- Do **not** look up any answers yet.

## Exam format reminder

- **Appraising paper (Paper 3, code 1MU0/03 in the Pearson Edexcel GCSE (9–1) Music spec):** 1 hour 45 minutes, **80 marks**, 40% of the course. Check with your teacher that this is the paper your school sits.
- **Section A (68 marks):** six questions based on the **eight set works** (two per area of study), using extracts from recordings and the printed score extracts. It includes short answers, listening for features, and a **melody/rhythm dictation** question where you complete notation.
- **Section B (12 marks):** one extended-response question comparing a set work with an unfamiliar piece, using musical vocabulary.
- You hear each extract several times, with set pauses; the paper is "audio-led", so the recording runs the exam.

## How to do it

1. **0–5 min** – Set up, timer for 50 minutes.
2. **5–40 min** – Questions 4–6 (the rest of Section A) with audio. Treat the dictation carefully.
3. **40–55 min** – **Section B** (extended response, 12 marks). Spend about **5 minutes planning**: write 3 points on the set work, 3 on the unfamiliar piece, 2 similarities, 2 differences. Then write about 10 minutes.
4. **55–60 min** – Check: did you answer everything? Fill any blanks (any answer earns a chance of a mark).

## Exam technique for this paper

- For the **12-mark comparison**, use a structure: *Introduction → similarities → differences → conclusion*. Use the elements: melody, harmony, rhythm, texture, timbre, structure, dynamics.
- Give **specific** evidence ("the harpsichord plays a basso continuo", "the strings play tremolo"), not general ("it is Baroque").
- Use **comparison words**: "whereas", "in contrast", "both".
- Use correct terminology and spelling (e.g. "ostinato", "syncopation", "ritornello").
- Do not retell the whole piece; **answer the question** (e.g. about texture).
- On the dictation: notate **rhythm correctly first**; then the pitches; the stems and bar lines matter.

## Quick-fire revision

- Texture words: monophonic, homophonic, polyphonic, unison, canon.
- Structure words: binary, ternary, rondo, verse-chorus, ritornello, sonata form.
- Dynamics: pp, p, mp, mf, f, ff, crescendo, diminuendo, sforzando.
- Tempo: grave, adagio, andante, allegro, presto, rubato, accelerando, ritardando.
- Articulation: staccato, legato, accent, tenuto.
- Instruments by family: strings, woodwind, brass, percussion, keyboard.
- Voices: soprano, alto, tenor, bass, countertenor; syllabic, melismatic.
- Production: reverb, delay, distortion, phasing, panning, multi-tracking.
- World: raga, tala, polyrhythm, call and response, heterophony.
- Set works: Bach, Beethoven, Purcell, Queen, Schwartz, Williams, Afro Celt Sound System, Spalding: one fact each.
