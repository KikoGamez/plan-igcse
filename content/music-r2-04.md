# Set works and vocabulary: weak points

> **Goal:** Build one-page fact sheets for the set works you are weakest on, and fix the vocabulary that cost you marks in the past paper.

## Before you start

- **You need:** your error log from the last session, your set-work list and booklet (from your teacher), recordings of the eight set works (if you have them), blank paper or index cards, a timer.
- **Check your set-work list.** The notes in this app follow the Pearson Edexcel GCSE (9–1) Music list: Bach (Brandenburg 5, mvt 3), Beethoven (Pathétique, mvt 1), Purcell (Music for a While), Queen (Killer Queen), Schwartz (Defying Gravity), Williams (Star Wars Main Title), Afro Celt Sound System (Release), Spalding (Samba Em Prelúdio).

## Exam format reminder

- **Appraising paper (Paper 3, code 1MU0/03 in the Pearson Edexcel GCSE (9–1) Music spec):** 1 hour 45 minutes, **80 marks**, 40% of the course. Check with your teacher that this is the paper your school sits.
- **Section A (68 marks):** six questions based on the **eight set works** (two per area of study), using extracts from recordings and the printed score extracts. It includes short answers, listening for features, and a **melody/rhythm dictation** question where you complete notation.
- **Section B (12 marks):** one extended-response question comparing a set work with an unfamiliar piece, using musical vocabulary.
- You hear each extract several times, with set pauses; the paper is "audio-led", so the recording runs the exam.

## How to do it

1. **0–5 min** – Look at your error log. Pick **three set works** or **ten terms** that cost most marks.
2. **5–30 min** – For each of the three works make a **one-page fact sheet** using the **DR SMITH** grid:
   - **D**ynamics, **R**hythm/tempo/metre, **S**tructure, **M**elody, **I**nstruments/timbre, **T**exture, **H**armony/tonality.
   - Add **context** (composer, date, genre) and **two special features** (e.g. Bach: harpsichord solo; Williams: leitmotif).
3. **30–45 min** – Vocabulary: write the ten terms you got wrong on cards, definition on the back. Test yourself, then a friend or parent. Say each aloud with a set-work example.
4. **45–55 min** – Listen to one extract from each of your three works (2–3 minutes each) with your sheet. Tick each feature you can hear.
5. **55–60 min** – Close your sheets and write three facts per work from memory. Check and correct.

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Pair the term with the evidence:** "ground bass (repeated bass line under the voice)".
- When asked about a set work, give **specific** features, not "it has instruments".
- Use at least **one fact on context** (composer, date, style) when asked about style or period.
- For comparison, prepare **similarity/difference pairs** between two set works (e.g. Purcell's ground bass vs Queen's riff).
- Spell the technical terms right; the examiner will not guess.
- Stay on the question: if it asks for texture, do not talk about instruments.

## Checklist while marking

- [ ] I have a fact sheet for each weak set work.
- [ ] Each sheet has all seven DR SMITH headings.
- [ ] I can say each key term's definition from memory.
- [ ] I linked each term to a set-work example.
- [ ] I tested myself without looking.
- [ ] I wrote down the facts I still forget for next time.

## Quick-fire revision

- Bach: concerto grosso, ritornello form, flute + violin + harpsichord soloists, D major.
- Beethoven: sonata form, C minor, Grave introduction returns, dramatic dynamics.
- Purcell: ground bass, solo voice, harpsichord, melisma on "beguile".
- Queen: Mercury, 1974, multi-tracked guitars and vocals, E♭ major.
- Schwartz: Wicked, Act 1 finale, belting, key changes, ostinato.
- Williams: LSO, brass fanfare, leitmotif, late-Romantic style.
- Afro Celt Sound System: Irish + West African + electronic dance; 1999.
- Spalding: samba/bossa nova + jazz; double bass and voice; Portuguese lyrics.
- Ground bass = repeated bass; ostinato = any repeated pattern.
- Leitmotif; diegetic; fusion; polyrhythm; heterophony.
- Texture: monophonic, homophonic, polyphonic.
- Cadences: perfect, plagal, imperfect, interrupted.
