# The skeletal system

> **In the exam:** Paper 1 (fitness and body systems). Expect a mix of 1-mark multiple-choice (name a bone, name a joint type, name a movement), 2–3 mark short answers (functions of the skeleton linked to a sporting example, describe the movement at a joint) and a place in longer 4–6 mark questions that join anatomy to a skill. You will often be given a sporting picture or situation and asked to name the bone, joint or movement involved.

## What you need to know

- The **functions** of the skeleton: support/shape, protection, movement (levers), production of blood cells, storage of minerals.
- The names and locations of the main bones, including the five regions of the vertebral column.
- The structure of a **synovial joint**: cartilage, synovial fluid and membrane, ligaments, tendons, joint capsule.
- The two joint types you must know: **hinge** and **ball and socket**, with examples.
- The movements at joints: **flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, rotation, circumduction, plantar flexion, dorsi flexion**, and which joint does each in a sporting action.
- How bones, ligaments and tendons work together (ligaments: bone to bone; tendons: muscle to bone).

## Notes

### Functions of the skeleton (link every one to sport)

| Function | What it means | Sporting example |
|---|---|---|
| **Support / shape** | The skeleton holds the body up and gives it its shape; muscles attach to it | The spine and legs support the body in a handstand or while standing in goal |
| **Protection** | Bones cover vital organs | The **cranium** protects the brain (boxing, rugby); **ribs** protect heart and lungs; **vertebral column** protects the spinal cord |
| **Movement** | Muscles pull on bones across joints; bones act as **levers** | The femur and tibia are pulled by the quadriceps to kick a ball |
| **Blood cell production** | Red and white blood cells are made in the **bone marrow** (e.g. in the femur, sternum, ribs) | Red cells carry oxygen to working muscles in a marathon |
| **Storage of minerals** | Calcium and phosphorus are stored in bone and released when needed | Calcium is needed for muscle contraction; strong bones resist impact in running |

**Tip:** in a 2-mark answer, give the function and a sporting example. "Protection – the ribs protect the heart and lungs from a tackle in rugby" earns both marks.

### The main bones

Learn the bone, where it is, and one sporting use.

| Region | Bones |
|---|---|
| Head | **Cranium** (skull) |
| Shoulder girdle | **Clavicle** (collarbone), **scapula** (shoulder blade) |
| Chest | **Sternum** (breastbone), **ribs** |
| Arm | **Humerus** (upper arm), **radius** (thumb side of forearm), **ulna** (little-finger side) |
| Hand | **Carpals** (wrist), **metacarpals** (palm), **phalanges** (fingers and toes) |
| Trunk | **Vertebral column**, **pelvis** |
| Leg | **Femur** (thigh – the longest bone), **patella** (kneecap), **tibia** (shin, the larger lower-leg bone), **fibula** (thinner, outer lower-leg bone) |
| Foot | **Tarsals** (ankle), **metatarsals** (foot), **phalanges** (toes) |

**The vertebral column** has 33 small bones (vertebrae) in five regions, from top to bottom. Learn the order:

1. **Cervical** (neck, 7)
2. **Thoracic** (upper back, 12; ribs attach here)
3. **Lumbar** (lower back, 5)
4. **Sacrum** (fused, joins the pelvis)
5. **Coccyx** (tail bone, fused)

The spinal cord runs through the column. Discs of cartilage between vertebrae absorb shock and allow bending.

### Joints

A **joint** is where two or more bones meet. In sport we focus on **synovial joints**, which are freely movable.

**Parts of a synovial joint**

| Part | Job |
|---|---|
| **Joint capsule** | Fibrous sleeve that surrounds and holds the joint together |
| **Synovial membrane** | Lines the capsule and produces synovial fluid |
| **Synovial fluid** | Lubricates the joint, reducing friction; also nourishes cartilage |
| **Articular (hyaline) cartilage** | Smooth, slippery covering on bone ends; reduces friction and absorbs shock |
| **Ligaments** | Tough, slightly elastic bands that join **bone to bone**; stabilise the joint and prevent dislocation |
| **Tendons** | Join **muscle to bone**; transmit the muscle's pull to move the bone |

**Hinge joint**: allows movement in **one plane only**, like a door hinge: bending (flexion) and straightening (extension). Examples: **elbow, knee, ankle** (ankle: dorsi/plantar flexion).

**Ball and socket joint**: the rounded head of one bone fits into the cup of another. Allows movement in **all directions** (the greatest range). Examples: **shoulder** (humerus into scapula) and **hip** (femur into pelvis).

Which to choose: the hip and shoulder allow rotation and circumduction (a bowler's arm, a swimmer's arm); the knee and elbow only bend and straighten, which suits the power of a kick or a push.

### Movements at joints

| Movement | Meaning | Sporting example |
|---|---|---|
| **Flexion** | Angle at the joint **decreases** | Bending the elbow in the upward phase of a biceps curl; bending the knee when running |
| **Extension** | Angle at the joint **increases** (straightening) | Straightening the knee at the kick of a football; straightening the elbow in a basketball shot |
| **Abduction** | Movement **away** from the midline of the body | Raising the arms sideways in a star jump; legs apart in a star jump |
| **Adduction** | Movement **towards** the midline | Pulling the arms back down in a star jump |
| **Rotation** | A bone turns around its own long axis | Turning the arm in a tennis serve; turning the upper body in a golf swing (shoulder/hip) |
| **Circumduction** | Circular movement; the limb traces a cone | Arm action in a cricket bowl or overarm swimming recovery (shoulder) |
| **Plantar flexion** | Pointing the toes **down** (ankle extends) | Pushing off the floor in a sprint start; tiptoe in ballet |
| **Dorsi flexion** | Pulling the toes **up** towards the shin | Heel strike in running; foot position when landing |

**Midline trick:** **AB**duction = **AB**ducted, taken **AB**way. **AD**duction = **AD**ded to the body.

### Bones, ligaments and tendons in action

When you kick a ball, the quadriceps muscle contracts. Its **tendon** pulls on the tibia. The knee (a hinge joint) straightens (**extension**). The knee's **ligaments** stop the joint moving sideways or too far. If the ligaments are overstretched, you get a sprain; if a tendon is damaged, it is a strain (injuries are covered in a later topic).

### Skeleton and exercise (long term)

Weight-bearing exercise (running, jumping, weights) makes bone **denser and stronger**, lowering the risk of osteoporosis later in life. Cartilage thickens a little and ligaments and tendons become stronger.

## Key terms and definitions

- **Skeleton**: the framework of 206 bones in the adult body.
- **Joint**: where two or more bones meet.
- **Synovial joint**: a freely movable joint containing synovial fluid.
- **Ligament**: strong connective tissue joining bone to bone.
- **Tendon**: strong connective tissue joining muscle to bone.
- **Cartilage**: smooth tissue covering the ends of bones that reduces friction and absorbs shock.
- **Synovial fluid**: fluid that lubricates the joint.
- **Flexion**: movement that decreases the angle at a joint.
- **Extension**: movement that increases the angle at a joint.
- **Abduction**: movement away from the midline of the body.
- **Adduction**: movement towards the midline of the body.
- **Circumduction**: circular movement of a limb at a ball and socket joint.
- **Bone marrow**: soft tissue inside bones where blood cells are made.

## Worked examples

**1. Explain two functions of the skeleton, using a sporting example for each. [4 marks]**

*Model answer:* Protection (1): the cranium protects the brain, e.g. when a rugby player is tackled / a cyclist falls (1). Movement (1): muscles pull on bones, which act as levers, so a footballer can kick the ball (1).

*Why it earns marks:* each function is named (1) and linked to a specific sport situation (1). "Protects organs" alone is only 1 mark.

**2. A basketball player shoots. Name the joint type at the elbow and name the movement at the elbow as the arm straightens. [2 marks]**

*Model answer:* Hinge joint (1); extension (1).

**3. Describe how a ligament and a tendon differ in function, and say why a synovial joint needs synovial fluid. [3 marks]**

*Model answer:* Ligaments join bone to bone and stabilise the joint (1); tendons join muscle to bone and transmit the force so the bone moves (1). Synovial fluid lubricates the joint to reduce friction (1).

**4. A cricketer bowls overarm with a full circle of the arm. Name the type of joint at the shoulder and the movement. [2 marks]**

*Model answer:* Ball and socket (1); circumduction (1).

## Common mistakes

- Mixing up **ligaments and tendons** (ligaments: bone to bone; tendons: muscle to bone).
- Writing "tibia and fibula" the wrong way round: the **tibia** is the big shin bone at the front.
- Saying "the knee is a ball and socket" — it is a **hinge**.
- Confusing abduction and adduction; always say "away from / towards the **midline**".
- Mixing up flexion and extension for the knee in running: bending the leg back is **flexion**; straightening it is **extension**.
- Giving a function without a sporting example when the question says "using an example".
- Forgetting that **bone marrow** makes blood cells, so the skeleton has a link to the cardiovascular system.
