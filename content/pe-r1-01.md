# The skeletal system

> **In the exam:** Paper 1 (Theory, 1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks) is all short and structured questions, and Section 1 "Anatomy and physiology" is one of its four areas. Skeleton questions are usually 1–4 marks: "Name the bone labelled X" [1], "State two functions of the skeleton" [2], "Name the type of joint at the knee" [1], "Name the movement at the shoulder" [1], and "Describe the structure of a synovial joint / explain the function of cartilage" [2–3]. Questions are almost always set in a sporting context (a gymnast, a bowler, a goalkeeper), so link the bone or joint to the action.

## What you need to know

- Four functions of the skeleton: shape and support, muscle attachment for movement, protection, red blood cell production.
- Location and function of these bones: cranium, clavicle, scapula, humerus, radius, ulna, carpals, metacarpals, phalanges, ribs, pelvis, femur, tibia, fibula, patella, talus, tarsals, metatarsals, phalanges.
- Classify bones as long, short or flat.
- Joint types: fixed (fibrous), slightly movable (cartilaginous), freely movable (synovial: ball and socket, hinge), with an example of each.
- Structure and function of a synovial joint: synovial membrane, synovial fluid, joint (fibrous) capsule, cartilage, ligaments.
- Movement at joints: flexion/extension, abduction/adduction, rotation, plantar flexion/dorsiflexion, used to describe movements in sport.
- Compare range of movement and stability of ball and socket joints with hinge joints.

## Notes

### Functions of the skeleton

| Function | What it means | Sporting example |
|---|---|---|
| **Shape and support** | The skeleton is the framework that holds the body up and gives it its shape | The spine and legs let a runner stand upright |
| **Muscle attachment for movement** | Muscles pull on bones (through tendons) to make levers move | The biceps pulls on the radius to bend the arm when throwing |
| **Protection** | Bones surround and shield soft organs | The cranium protects the brain when heading a ball; ribs protect heart and lungs in a tackle |
| **Red blood cell production** | Red blood cells are made in the bone marrow of long bones, ribs and the pelvis | More red cells carry more oxygen to the muscles of an endurance athlete |

(Bones also store calcium, but the four above are the ones the syllabus lists.)

### Bones you must locate

| Bone | Location | Function / sport link | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cranium** | Skull, round the brain | Protects the brain | Flat |
| **Clavicle** | Collarbone, between the top of the sternum and the scapula | Holds the shoulder in position; attachment for muscles | Long |
| **Scapula** | Shoulder blade, upper back | Attachment for shoulder muscles; forms the shoulder socket | Flat |
| **Humerus** | Upper arm | Muscle attachment; moves at the shoulder and elbow (bowling, throwing) | Long |
| **Radius** and **ulna** | Forearm (radius on the thumb side) | Muscle attachment; allow bending and rotation of forearm and wrist | Long |
| **Carpals** | Wrist (8 small bones) | Give a small amount of movement at the wrist | Short |
| **Metacarpals** | Palm of the hand | Form the hand; muscle attachment for gripping | Long |
| **Phalanges** | Fingers and toes | Gripping, balance and pushing off | Long |
| **Ribs** | Chest | Protect heart and lungs; move during breathing | Flat |
| **Pelvis** | Hips | Protects organs in the lower abdomen; strong attachment for leg and trunk muscles; transfers weight to the legs | Flat |
| **Femur** | Thigh | Longest and strongest bone; muscle attachment for running and kicking | Long |
| **Patella** | Kneecap, front of the knee | Protects the knee joint and improves the pull of the quadriceps | Short (sesamoid) |
| **Tibia** | Shin, the larger lower-leg bone | Bears body weight; muscle attachment | Long |
| **Fibula** | Thin bone beside the tibia | Muscle attachment; stabilises the ankle | Long |
| **Talus** | Ankle, between the tibia and the heel bone | Passes weight and movement from the leg to the foot | Short |
| **Tarsals** | Ankle and heel (7 bones) | Support body weight; absorb impact when landing | Short |
| **Metatarsals** | Mid-foot | Form the arch; help push off when sprinting | Long |

**Bone classification**
- **Long bones**: longer than they are wide; act as levers and contain marrow (humerus, femur, tibia, phalanges).
- **Short bones**: about as long as wide; give strength and small, fine movements (carpals, tarsals, talus).
- **Flat bones**: thin and often curved; protect organs and give a large surface for muscle attachment (cranium, scapula, ribs, pelvis).

### Types of joint

A **joint** is a place where two or more bones meet.

| Type | Movement | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed (fibrous / immovable)** | None | Joints between the bones of the cranium |
| **Slightly movable (cartilaginous)** | A small amount | Between the vertebrae of the spine |
| **Freely movable (synovial)** | Large range | Shoulder, hip (ball and socket); elbow, knee, ankle (hinge) |

### Structure of a synovial joint

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| **Cartilage** | Smooth, rubbery layer on the ends of the bones; reduces friction and absorbs shock |
| **Synovial membrane** | Lines the joint capsule and produces synovial fluid |
| **Synovial fluid** | Lubricates the joint so the bones move smoothly, and nourishes the cartilage |
| **Joint (fibrous) capsule** | Tough sleeve around the joint that holds it together and keeps the fluid in |
| **Ligaments** | Strong, slightly elastic bands joining **bone to bone**; stabilise the joint and prevent dislocation |

Do not mix up **ligaments (bone to bone)** with **tendons (muscle to bone)**.

### Ball and socket vs hinge

| | Ball and socket | Hinge |
|---|---|---|
| Examples | Shoulder, hip | Elbow, knee, ankle |
| Movements | Flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, rotation (all directions) | Flexion and extension only (one direction, like a door) |
| Range of movement | **Large** | **Smaller** |
| Stability | **Less stable** (more dislocations) | **More stable** |

Sport link: a bowler's shoulder needs a big range to swing the arm round; a knee needs stability to take body weight when landing.

### Types of movement

| Movement | Definition | Sporting example |
|---|---|---|
| **Flexion** | Decreases the angle at a joint | Bending the knee as you prepare to kick; the upward phase of a bicep curl |
| **Extension** | Increases the angle at a joint | Straightening the knee at the point of kicking |
| **Abduction** | Movement **away** from the midline of the body | Raising the arms sideways in a star jump |
| **Adduction** | Movement **towards** the midline | Bringing the legs together at the end of a star jump |
| **Rotation** | Turning a bone around its own axis | The shoulder in a tennis serve or discus throw |
| **Plantar flexion** | Pointing the toes down (ankle extends) | Pushing off in a sprint, a ballet dancer on tiptoe |
| **Dorsiflexion** | Pulling the toes up towards the shin | Heel strike when running, the toes pulled up as the foot lands |

Join joint + movement + action: "flexion at the **elbow** as the arm bends to catch the ball".

## Key terms and definitions

- **Joint**: a place where two or more bones meet.
- **Synovial joint**: a freely movable joint with a joint capsule filled with synovial fluid.
- **Ligament**: strong, elastic tissue that joins bone to bone and stabilises a joint.
- **Tendon**: strong, non-elastic tissue that attaches muscle to bone.
- **Cartilage**: smooth, rubbery tissue on the ends of bones that reduces friction and absorbs shock.
- **Flexion**: movement that decreases the angle at a joint.
- **Extension**: movement that increases the angle at a joint.
- **Abduction**: movement away from the midline of the body.
- **Adduction**: movement towards the midline of the body.
- **Plantar flexion**: pointing the toes away from the shin (extending the ankle).
- **Dorsiflexion**: pulling the toes up towards the shin.

## Worked examples

**Q1. A goalkeeper dives to save a shot, landing on the ground. State two functions of the skeleton that help her. [2]**

- Protection (1): the ribs and cranium protect the heart, lungs and brain if she lands hard.
- Muscle attachment for movement (1): her muscles pull on her bones so her arms and legs can move quickly to reach the ball.

*Tip: two functions earn the 2 marks; the sporting link makes sure the mark is safe if the function is worded loosely.*

**Q2. Name the type of joint at the shoulder and give two reasons why it is less stable than the knee. [3]**

- Ball and socket joint (1).
- The socket is shallow, so the head of the humerus is not held tightly (1).
- It has a large range of movement in all directions, and more range means less stability; the hinge at the knee only moves in one plane, so it is more stable (1).

**Q3. Describe the movement at the ankle and the knee of a sprinter pushing off from the blocks. [4]**

- Ankle: plantar flexion (1), the toes point down / foot pushes away from the shin (1).
- Knee: extension (1), the angle at the knee increases as the leg straightens to drive the body forward (1).

**Q4. Explain the function of synovial fluid and cartilage in a netball player's knee. [4]**

- Synovial fluid lubricates the joint (1), so the bones move smoothly with little friction when she jumps and lands (1).
- Cartilage covers the ends of the bones (1) and absorbs shock when she lands, protecting the bone from wear (1).

## Common mistakes

- Saying "ligaments join muscle to bone". That is tendons. **Ligaments: bone to bone.**
- Writing "radious" or confusing the **ulna** and **radius** (the radius is on the thumb side). Spell all bones correctly; examiners will not guess.
- Giving "the skeleton makes you strong" as a function. Use the four listed functions in the mark-scheme wording.
- Saying the knee or ankle is "ball and socket". Only the **shoulder and hip** are.
- Confusing **abduction** (away) with **adduction** (towards). Trick: **ab**duct = take **ab**way.
- Naming a movement without the joint, or the joint without a sporting action, when the question says "in a physical activity".
- Mixing up plantar flexion and dorsiflexion. Plantar = sole of foot pushes down (toes pointed).

## Practice questions

1. State **two** functions of the skeleton. [2]
2. Name the bone in the upper arm and the bone commonly called the shoulder blade. [2]
3. Classify the femur, the carpals and the ribs as long, short or flat. [3]
4. Name the type of joint at the elbow and state the movements it allows. [2]
5. Describe the function of ligaments and of synovial membrane. [2]
6. A basketball player jumps for a rebound. Name the movement at the ankle as she takes off, and the movement at the hip as she lifts her knee. [2]
7. Compare the range of movement and stability of the hip with those of the knee. [3]
8. Explain how the skeleton helps a rugby player who is tackled. [3]
