# The muscular system

> **In the exam:** Paper 1 (Theory, 1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks; short and structured questions). Typical items: "Name the muscle labelled A" [1], "Name the antagonist to the quadriceps" [1], "Explain how the biceps and triceps work as an antagonistic pair when throwing" [3–4], "Name the type of contraction in the quadriceps as she lowers into a squat" [1], "Which muscle fibre type is best for a marathon runner and why?" [2–3]. Questions are set in a sporting context, so name the muscle and the action.

## What you need to know

- Location and role of: latissimus dorsi, trapezius, deltoid, pectorals, biceps, triceps, abdominals, gluteals, hip flexors, hamstring group, quadriceps group, gastrocnemius, tibialis anterior (individual hamstring and quadriceps names are not required).
- The role of tendons.
- Antagonistic muscle action at the shoulder, elbow, hip, knee and ankle: agonists (prime movers) and antagonists.
- Isotonic (concentric and eccentric) and isometric contractions.
- Muscle fibre types, slow and fast twitch, limited to: force created, fatigue tolerance, aerobic/anaerobic energy supply.

## Notes

### Muscles you must know

| Muscle | Location | Role (what it does) | Sporting example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trapezius** | Upper back and neck, triangle shape | Pulls the shoulder blades together and raises the shoulders; holds the head up | Shrugging; carrying; rowing |
| **Deltoid** | Rounded top of the shoulder | Raises the arm forwards and sideways (flexion, abduction), rotates the arm | Lifting the arm in a tennis serve; shoulder press |
| **Latissimus dorsi** | Wide, large muscle across the lower back to the upper arm | Pulls the arm down and back (adduction, extension) and rotates it inward | Pull-ups; the swimming pull-through |
| **Pectorals** | Chest | Pull the arm across the front of the body (horizontal adduction) | Bench press; throwing; push-ups |
| **Biceps** | Front of the upper arm | Bends (flexes) the elbow | Pull-up; bicep curl |
| **Triceps** | Back of the upper arm | Straightens (extends) the elbow | Push-up; shot put |
| **Abdominals** | Front of the stomach ("abs") | Bend the trunk forwards; hold the trunk steady and keep posture | Sit-up; core stability in gymnastics |
| **Gluteals** | Buttocks | Extend the hip (drive the leg back); powerful in running and jumping | Sprinting; squats; jumping |
| **Hip flexors** | Front of the hip, top of the thigh | Raise (flex) the knee / thigh towards the body | Lifting the knee when sprinting |

| **Hamstrings** (group) | Back of the thigh | Bend (flex) the knee | Heel flick in sprinting; hamstring curl |
| **Gastrocnemius** | Calf, back of the lower leg | Points the foot down (plantar flexion) | Standing on tiptoes; push-off in a sprint |

### Tendons

**Tendons** attach muscle to bone. They are strong and do not stretch. When a muscle contracts it pulls on the tendon, which pulls the bone, so the joint moves. (Ligaments join bone to bone.)

### Antagonistic pairs

Muscles can only **pull** (by contracting); they cannot push. So muscles work in pairs at a joint:
- The **agonist (prime mover)** is the muscle that **contracts** to cause the movement.
- The **antagonist** **relaxes** (lengthens) to allow the movement.
- When the movement is reversed, the roles swap.

| Joint | Movement | Agonist | Antagonist |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Elbow** | Flexion | Biceps | Triceps |
| | Extension | Triceps | Biceps |
| **Knee** | Flexion | Hamstrings | Quadriceps |
| | Extension | Quadriceps | Hamstrings |
| **Hip** | Flexion | Hip flexors | Gluteals |
| | Extension | Gluteals | Hip flexors |
| **Ankle** | Plantar flexion | Gastrocnemius | Tibialis anterior |
| | Dorsiflexion | Tibialis anterior | Gastrocnemius |
| **Shoulder** | Flexion / abduction (raising the arm) | Deltoid | Latissimus dorsi |
| | Extension / adduction (pulling the arm down and back) | Latissimus dorsi | Deltoid |

**Example — kicking a football:** to straighten the knee, the quadriceps (agonist) contract while the hamstrings (antagonist) relax.

### Types of muscle contraction

| Type | What happens | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Isotonic: concentric** | The muscle **shortens** while it contracts; the joint moves | Biceps shortening during the upward phase of a bicep curl |
| **Isotonic: eccentric** | The muscle **lengthens** while it contracts, controlling the movement | Biceps lengthening as you lower the weight slowly; quadriceps when you lower into a squat or land from a jump |
| **Isometric** | The muscle contracts but **stays the same length**; no movement | Holding a plank; holding a handstand; pushing against a wall; holding a gymnast's balance |

"Isotonic" means constant tension with movement. Eccentric contractions act as a brake and are linked to muscle soreness (DOMS).

### Muscle fibre types

| | Slow twitch (Type I) | Fast twitch (Type II) |
|---|---|---|
| Force created | Low | **High** |
| Fatigue tolerance | **High** (fatigue slowly) | Low (fatigue quickly) |
| Energy supply | **Aerobic** (uses oxygen) | **Anaerobic** (short bursts, no oxygen needed) |
| Colour / blood supply | Red, rich blood supply | Whiter, fewer capillaries |
| Best for | Marathon, long-distance cycling, swimming 1500 m | 100 m sprint, weightlifting, shot put, high jump |

Most people have a mix. The ratio is mostly genetic, so elite sprinters tend to have many fast-twitch fibres and elite distance runners many slow-twitch fibres.

## Key terms and definitions

- **Agonist (prime mover)**: the muscle that contracts to produce the movement.
- **Antagonist**: the muscle that relaxes to allow the agonist to move the joint.
- **Antagonistic pair**: two muscles that work in opposition at a joint, one contracting while the other relaxes.
- **Tendon**: strong, inelastic tissue that attaches muscle to bone.
- **Isotonic contraction**: the muscle changes length while contracting (concentric = shortens, eccentric = lengthens).
- **Isometric contraction**: the muscle contracts without changing length, so there is no movement.
- **Slow twitch fibres**: fibres that produce low force, resist fatigue and use aerobic energy.
- **Fast twitch fibres**: fibres that produce high force, fatigue quickly and use anaerobic energy.

## Worked examples

**Q1. Name the agonist and antagonist when a sprinter drives the knee up (flexes the hip) in the sprint stride. [2]**

Agonist: hip flexors (1). Antagonist: gluteals (1). The hip flexors contract (shorten) while the gluteals relax and lengthen.

**Q2. A gymnast holds a handstand. Name the type of muscle contraction in the deltoids and explain your answer. [2]**

Isometric (1) because the muscle contracts to hold the position, but its length does not change and there is no movement (1).

**Q3. Explain why a marathon runner needs mainly slow twitch muscle fibres. [3]**

- Slow twitch fibres resist fatigue (1), so the runner can keep contracting for over two hours without tiring (1).
- They use aerobic energy, and the marathon is low-intensity over a long time so it needs oxygen-based energy (1).

**Q4. Describe how the biceps and triceps work as an antagonistic pair when a basketball player brings the ball up to shoot (elbow flexion). [4]**

- Biceps are the agonist / prime mover (1).
- The biceps contract (shorten, concentric) (1) and pull on the radius through the tendon (1).
- The triceps are the antagonist and relax (lengthen) to allow the elbow to bend (1).

## Common mistakes

- Saying the **antagonist "does the opposite" or "pushes"**. Muscles only pull; the antagonist **relaxes**.
- Mixing up which muscle is agonist when the direction reverses. Always ask: which muscle is shortening **right now**?
- Using "biceps/triceps" for every joint. Learn all five pairs (shoulder, elbow, hip, knee, ankle).
- Saying "eccentric means the muscle relaxes". It is **contracting while lengthening**.
- Calling a muscle "the thigh muscle" or "the calf". Use the exact name: quadriceps, hamstrings, gastrocnemius.
- Writing "fast twitch is better" without saying for which activity.
- Confusing tendons with ligaments.

## Practice questions

1. Name the muscle that is found on the front of the upper arm and the muscle on the back of the thigh group. [2]
2. State the function of a tendon. [1]
3. Name the agonist when the knee extends as a footballer kicks the ball. [1]
4. Name the antagonist to the gastrocnemius. [1]
5. A weightlifter slowly lowers a barbell to the chest with the pectorals and triceps controlling the weight. Name this type of muscle contraction. [1]
6. Give the difference between an isotonic and an isometric contraction, with an example of each. [4]
7. Explain why a 100 m sprinter has more fast twitch muscle fibres than slow twitch. [3]
8. Name the muscle used for each: (a) a sit-up, (b) the push-off from tiptoes, (c) pulling the arm down in the swimming pull. [3]

1. Biceps (1); hamstrings (1).
2. Attaches muscle to bone (1).
3. Quadriceps (1).
4. Tibialis anterior (1).
5. Isotonic, eccentric (1) (accept eccentric).
6. Isotonic: the muscle changes length as it contracts (1), e.g. biceps shortening in a bicep curl (1). Isometric: the muscle contracts but stays the same length with no movement (1), e.g. holding a plank (1).
