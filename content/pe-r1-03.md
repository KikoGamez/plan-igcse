# Movement analysis: forces and levers

> **In the exam:** Paper 1 (Theory, 1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks; short and structured questions). The Cambridge syllabus puts this under "Simple biomechanics": **force** and **levers**. Typical items: "Draw and label a first-class lever" [3], "State the class of lever at the elbow / ankle" [1], "Identify the fulcrum, effort and resistance in..." [3], "Name the forces acting on a sprinter in the blocks" [2–3], "Explain what force = mass × acceleration means" [2]. **Planes and axes of movement are not on the Cambridge IGCSE syllabus**, so this hour covers force, applications of force and lever classes instead; do not spend time on planes and axes.

## What you need to know

- Force: a push or a pull; force = mass × acceleration; increases/decreases in force cause acceleration/deceleration.
- Forces acting on: a moving performer (gravity, air resistance, muscular force), a sprinter in the blocks (gravity, ground reaction force, air resistance), an object flying through the air (force applied at release, air resistance, gravity).
- Identify and draw the three classes of lever (first, second, third); identify the fulcrum, resistance (load) and effort; state a body example of each.

## Notes

### Force

A **force** is a **push or a pull**. It is measured in **newtons (N)**.

**Force = mass × acceleration (F = m × a)**
- Mass in kg, acceleration in m/s², force in N.
- A bigger force on the same mass gives more acceleration; the same force on a smaller mass also gives more acceleration (a light sprinter accelerates faster than a heavy one with the same leg force).
- A force can make something **speed up (accelerate)**, **slow down (decelerate)**, change direction or change shape.

Worked calculation: a 60 kg sprinter accelerates at 5 m/s². Force = 60 × 5 = **300 N**. A rugby ball (0.44 kg) kicked with 220 N: acceleration = 220 ÷ 0.44 = 500 m/s².

### Forces acting in sport

| Situation | Forces | What each does |
|---|---|---|
| **A moving performer** (runner, cyclist, swimmer) | **Gravity** (weight) | Pulls the performer down towards the ground |
| | **Air resistance** (drag) | Pushes against the direction of movement, slowing the performer |
| | **Muscular force** | Produced by the performer to move forward |
| **A sprinter in the blocks** | **Gravity** | Pulls her down |
| | **Ground reaction force** | The ground pushes back up and forward on her feet when she pushes on the blocks (Newton: every action has an equal and opposite reaction) |
| | **Air resistance** | Small at the start; grows as she speeds up |
| **An object flying through the air** (javelin, basketball, football) | **Force applied at release** | The throw/kick sends it into the air; no more force from the thrower after release |
| | **Air resistance** | Slows the object down |
| | **Gravity** | Pulls it down, producing the curved flight path |

Ways to reduce air resistance: streamlined shape (cycling helmet, tight kit, crouching), smooth surfaces.

### Levers

The body moves by **levers**: a **bone (rigid bar)** pivots about a **joint (fulcrum)**, with muscles supplying the **effort**.

- **Fulcrum**: the pivot point (the joint).
- **Effort**: the force applied (muscle contraction).
- **Resistance (load)**: what is being moved or resisted (body part, weight, ball).

**Class of lever depends on which part is in the middle.** Remember **1-2-3 = F-R-E**, i.e. the item in the middle is:
- **First class: Fulcrum** in the middle
- **Second class: Resistance** in the middle
- **Third class: Effort** in the middle

| Class | Order | Diagram (E = effort, F = fulcrum, R = resistance) | Body example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1st class** | E – **F** – R | `E ——▲—— R` | **Neck**: nodding the head (muscles at the back of the neck pull down, the joint between the skull and spine is the fulcrum, the head's weight is the resistance). Also the triceps extending the elbow |
| **2nd class** | F – **R** – E | `F —— R —— E` | **Ankle**: rising onto the toes. The ball of the foot is the fulcrum, body weight (resistance) is through the ankle, the calf muscle (gastrocnemius) pulls up on the heel (effort) |
| **3rd class** | F – **E** – R | `F —— E —— R` | **Elbow**: bicep curl. The elbow is the fulcrum, the biceps pull in between (effort), the weight in the hand is at the end (resistance). **Most levers in the body are 3rd class** |

How to draw one in the exam: draw a straight line (the bone), a triangle under it (the fulcrum), an arrow pointing up or down for effort and an arrow for resistance, and **label all three**. Check the order matches the class.

Why levers matter: a third-class lever lets the limb move **fast and through a large range** (good for throwing, kicking), although it needs a **large effort** from the muscle. Second-class levers give force advantage (a small effort moves a large load) but a small range. (Mechanical advantage calculations are not required.)

## Key terms and definitions

- **Force**: a push or a pull, measured in newtons.
- **Acceleration**: the rate of change of speed (speeding up or slowing down).
- **Gravity**: the force that pulls objects towards the ground.
- **Air resistance**: a force that opposes the motion of a performer or object through the air.
- **Ground reaction force**: the force the ground exerts back on the performer when they push on it.
- **Lever**: a rigid bar (bone) that pivots about a fixed point (joint) when a force is applied.
- **Fulcrum**: the pivot point of a lever (the joint).
- **Effort**: the force applied to a lever (by the muscle).
- **Resistance (load)**: the weight or force that the lever has to overcome.

## Worked examples

**Q1. A footballer of mass 70 kg accelerates at 4 m/s². Calculate the force. [2]**

Force = mass × acceleration (1) = 70 × 4 = 280 N (1). *Include the unit.*

**Q2. Name three forces acting on a sprinter in the starting blocks. [3]**

Gravity (1), ground reaction force (1), air resistance (1).

**Q3. Draw a labelled diagram of a third-class lever and give an example from the body. [4]**

- Straight line for the bone with a triangle at one end for the fulcrum (1).
- Effort arrow placed **between** fulcrum and resistance (1).
- Resistance arrow at the opposite end from the fulcrum (1).
- Example: biceps flexing the elbow (fulcrum = elbow, effort = biceps, resistance = weight of forearm/hand) (1).

**Q4. Explain why a javelin travels in a curved path after release. [3]**

- After release, no further force from the thrower acts on the javelin (1).
- Gravity pulls it downwards (1) and air resistance slows it, so it follows a curved path and eventually lands (1).

## Common mistakes

- Writing vague answers such as "friction" or "pushing" when the syllabus wants **gravity, ground reaction force, air resistance, muscular force and force at release**.
- Writing **"force applied by the thrower"** as acting on the javelin in the air. It acts only **at release**.
- Putting the effort in the wrong place in a lever diagram. For a third-class lever the **effort is in the middle**.
- Saying "the muscle is the fulcrum". The fulcrum is the **joint**.
- Forgetting units: newtons (N).
- Wasting time on planes and axes. They are not on the Cambridge syllabus.
- Saying "F = m × a" without explaining: more force gives more acceleration, or more mass gives less acceleration for the same force.

## Practice questions

1. Define "force". [1]
2. State the equation linking force, mass and acceleration. [1]
3. A 50 kg gymnast accelerates at 6 m/s². Calculate the force. [2]
4. Name the forces acting on a cyclist riding along a flat road. [3]
5. Name the three forces involved when a basketball is shot and travels through the air. [3]
6. State the class of lever at (a) the elbow in a bicep curl, (b) the ankle when rising onto the toes, (c) the neck when nodding. [3]
7. For the bicep curl, identify the fulcrum, the effort and the resistance. [3]
8. Explain why most levers in the body are third class. [2]

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. A push or a pull (1).
2. Force = mass × acceleration (1).
3. 50 × 6 (1) = 300 N (1).
4. Gravity (1), air resistance (1), muscular force (1).
5. Force applied at release (the throw) (1), air resistance (1), gravity (1).
6. (a) Third class (1); (b) second class (1); (c) first class (1).
7. Fulcrum: elbow joint (1); effort: biceps muscle (1); resistance: weight of the forearm and the dumbbell (1).
8. They give a large range of movement and speed at the end of the limb (1) which suits throwing, kicking and running (1).
