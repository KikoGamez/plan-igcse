# Movement analysis: levers, planes and axes

> **In the exam:** Paper 1. Expect questions that give you a sporting action or a diagram and ask you to: name the **class of lever** (1 mark) and label load, effort and fulcrum (up to 3 marks); state or explain **mechanical advantage** (2–3 marks); name the **plane** and **axis** of a movement (2 marks for a pair, often from a picture such as a somersault, cartwheel or twist). Sometimes combined with joint movements in a 4–6 mark question.

## What you need to know

- How the body uses **levers** (bone = lever, joint = fulcrum, muscle = effort).
- The three classes of lever (**first, second, third**) with sporting examples.
- **Mechanical advantage**: what it is, how to calculate it, and what a high or low value means for a body lever.
- The three **planes of movement** (sagittal, frontal, transverse) and three **axes of rotation** (frontal, sagittal, longitudinal), and which go together.

## Notes

### Levers

A **lever** has three parts:

- **Fulcrum (pivot)**: the point the lever turns around, here a **joint**.
- **Effort**: the force applied, here from a **muscle** pulling through a tendon.
- **Load (resistance)**: the weight to be moved, such as the body part, a ball or a weight.

In the body: **bones are the levers, joints are the fulcrums, muscles provide the effort**. Levers magnify either **force** or **speed/range of movement**.

**The class is decided by which part is in the middle.**

| Class | In the middle | Order | Body example | Sport example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **First** | **Fulcrum** (F) | Effort – **Fulcrum** – Load | Neck: the skull pivots on the top of the spine, with neck muscles pulling at the back (head extension). Also triceps extending the elbow | Tilting the head back to watch a high ball; extending the elbow in a triceps push-down |
| **Second** | **Load** (L) | Fulcrum – **Load** – Effort | Standing on tiptoe (plantar flexion): the ball of the foot is the fulcrum, body weight acts through the ankle (load) and the calf muscle (gastrocnemius) pulls on the heel (effort) | Rising onto the toes in a sprint start or a calf raise |
| **Third** | **Effort** (E) | Fulcrum – **Effort** – Load | **Elbow** during flexion: elbow joint is the fulcrum, biceps attaches near the elbow, the load is in the hand | Bicep curl; kicking a ball (hip fulcrum, quadriceps effort); swinging a bat |

The easiest way to remember: **1-2-3 = F-L-E** (**F**ulcrum, **L**oad, **E**ffort in the middle).

**Most levers in the human body are third class**, because muscles attach close to the joint. Second-class levers are rare in the body. First-class levers are also uncommon.

### Mechanical advantage (MA)

**Mechanical advantage = effort arm ÷ load arm**

- **Effort arm**: distance from the **fulcrum to where the effort** is applied (the muscle attachment).
- **Load arm**: distance from the **fulcrum to the load**.

| Mechanical advantage | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **MA > 1** (effort arm longer) | The lever is a **force multiplier**: a small effort moves a larger load. Less effort needed, but a smaller range and speed. Typical of **second-class** levers. |
| **MA < 1** (effort arm shorter) | The lever sacrifices force for **speed and range of movement**. A big effort is needed but the load moves fast and far. Typical of **third-class** levers. |
| **MA = 1** | Effort and load arm equal; no gain in force or speed. Possible in first-class levers. |

**Calculation example:** effort arm = 4 cm, load arm = 32 cm. MA = 4 ÷ 32 = **0.125**. This is below 1, so it needs a large effort but gives speed and range of movement, e.g. throwing a ball.

**Why it matters in sport:** a **longer lever** (e.g. a tennis racket or a bowler's straight arm) increases the load arm, so the end of the lever travels faster. But it needs more force from the muscles. This is why a long-armed bowler can bowl fast, but must be very strong.

### Planes of movement

A plane is an imaginary flat sheet cutting through the body. Movement happens **in a plane**.

| Plane | Divides the body into | Movements in this plane | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sagittal** | **Left and right** halves | **Flexion and extension** (forwards/backwards movements) | Running, a forward roll, a somersault, bicep curl, a kick |
| **Frontal** | **Front and back** halves | **Abduction and adduction** (sideways movements) | Cartwheel, star jump, side-stepping, raising the arms sideways |
| **Transverse** | **Top and bottom** halves | **Rotation** (twisting movements) | Spinning on ice, a discus throw, a golf swing, a pirouette |

### Axes of rotation

An axis is an imaginary line **through the body** around which the body turns. Each axis is at **right angles** to its plane.

| Axis | Runs | Goes with plane | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Frontal axis** | Side to side (left–right) | **Sagittal plane** | Somersault, forward roll, bicep curl |
| **Sagittal axis** | Front to back | **Frontal plane** | Cartwheel, star jump (abduction) |
| **Longitudinal axis** | Head to toe (vertical) | **Transverse plane** | Twist or spin, ice-skater's pirouette, discus turn |

**Pairs to learn (the exam's favourite):**

- **Sagittal plane + frontal axis** → flexion/extension, somersaults
- **Frontal plane + sagittal axis** → abduction/adduction, cartwheels
- **Transverse plane + longitudinal axis** → rotation, twists, spins

**Trick:** the plane and its axis are never the same word. Sagittal plane goes with the frontal axis; frontal plane goes with the sagittal axis; transverse plane goes with the longitudinal axis.

### Putting it together: example analysis

**Biceps curl:** movement is flexion at the elbow, in the **sagittal plane**, about the **frontal axis**. Lever class: **third** (the biceps attaches between the fulcrum, the elbow, and the load, in the hand).

**Calf raise (standing on tiptoes):** plantar flexion at the ankle, **sagittal plane**, **frontal axis**, **second-class** lever.

**Cartwheel:** the body moves sideways in the **frontal plane** about the **sagittal axis**.

**Spin on one foot in ice skating:** **transverse plane**, **longitudinal axis**.

## Key terms and definitions

- **Lever**: a rigid bar (bone) that turns around a fixed point (the fulcrum / joint) when a force (effort) moves a load.
- **Fulcrum**: the point around which a lever turns (the joint).
- **Effort**: the force applied to the lever (by the muscle).
- **Load**: the resistance or weight that the lever moves.
- **First-class lever**: the fulcrum is between the effort and the load.
- **Second-class lever**: the load is between the fulcrum and the effort.
- **Third-class lever**: the effort is between the fulcrum and the load.
- **Mechanical advantage**: effort arm ÷ load arm; how much a lever multiplies force.
- **Plane of movement**: an imaginary flat surface that divides the body; movement happens within it.
- **Axis of rotation**: an imaginary line through the body about which movement rotates.

## Worked examples

**1. Identify the class of lever used when the elbow flexes in a bicep curl and justify your answer. [2 marks]**

*Model answer:* Third-class lever (1) because the effort (biceps attachment) is between the fulcrum (elbow joint) and the load (weight in the hand) (1).

**2. A lever has an effort arm of 5 cm and a load arm of 25 cm. Calculate its mechanical advantage and state what it tells you. [3 marks]**

*Model answer:* MA = effort arm ÷ load arm = 5 ÷ 25 (1) = 0.2 (1). Because MA is less than 1, more effort is needed than the load, but the lever gives speed and range of movement (1).

**3. A gymnast performs a forward somersault. Name the plane and axis of movement. [2 marks]**

*Model answer:* Sagittal plane (1); frontal axis (1).

**4. Explain why second-class levers are more efficient at moving heavy loads than third-class levers. [2 marks]**

*Model answer:* A second-class lever has an effort arm longer than the load arm, so MA > 1 (1); a small effort can move a larger load (1).

## Common mistakes

- Mixing the **load arm** and **effort arm** in the formula; the MA formula has **effort arm on top**.
- Calling the **elbow flexion** a first- or second-class lever. It is **third class** (and so are most levers in the body).
- Giving the same name for plane and axis (e.g. "sagittal plane, sagittal axis"). They never match.
- Forgetting a pair for rotation: **transverse plane, longitudinal axis**.
- Saying the **fulcrum is the muscle**; the fulcrum is the **joint**, the effort comes from the muscle.
- Writing an MA of "5:1" with no meaning. State that MA > 1 means force is multiplied; < 1 means speed/range is gained.
- Confusing planes by thinking "frontal = forwards". The **sagittal** plane is for forwards/backwards actions.
