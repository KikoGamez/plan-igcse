# Cardiovascular and respiratory systems

> **In the exam:** Paper 1. Typical questions: label or name parts of the heart or the route of blood (1–3 marks), calculate **cardiac output** from heart rate and stroke volume (2 marks), describe the structure and function of blood vessels (2–3 marks), explain **gas exchange** at the alveoli (3 marks), describe the mechanics of breathing (2–3 marks), and interpret data on heart rate or lung volumes. Often linked to exercise in a 4–6 mark extended question.

## What you need to know

- The structure of the **heart**: atria, ventricles, valves, septum, and the main vessels.
- The path of blood through the heart, lungs and body (**double circulatory system**).
- **Blood vessels**: arteries, veins, capillaries; structure and function.
- The components and functions of **blood**.
- **Heart rate, stroke volume, cardiac output** (Q = HR × SV), and typical resting values.
- The **respiratory system** and the pathway of air; **inspiration and expiration** (diaphragm, intercostal muscles).
- **Gas exchange** at the alveoli and at the muscles (diffusion); lung volumes (tidal volume, vital capacity, minute ventilation).
- How the systems respond to exercise: **vascular shunting**, and increases in heart rate and breathing rate.

## Notes

### The heart

The heart is a muscular pump, about the size of a fist, with **two sides separated by the septum**. Each side has an **atrium** (receives blood) above a **ventricle** (pumps blood out).

| Side | Carries | Pumps to |
|---|---|---|
| **Right side** | **Deoxygenated** blood (low oxygen) | The lungs, through the pulmonary artery |
| **Left side** | **Oxygenated** blood (high oxygen) | The whole body, through the aorta |

The **left ventricle** has the thickest muscular wall, because it must pump blood around the whole body.

**Valves** (between atria and ventricles, and at the exits of the ventricles) stop the blood flowing backwards.

**Main vessels attached to the heart:**

| Vessel | Carries blood | To / from |
|---|---|---|
| **Vena cava** | Deoxygenated | Body to the **right atrium** |
| **Pulmonary artery** | Deoxygenated | Right ventricle to the **lungs** |
| **Pulmonary vein** | Oxygenated | Lungs to the **left atrium** |
| **Aorta** | Oxygenated | Left ventricle to the **body** |

(Memory tip: **A**rteries carry blood **A**way from the heart; **V**eins bring it back. The only exception to "arteries = oxygenated" is the pulmonary artery, which is deoxygenated.)

### Path of blood (double circulation)

**Body → vena cava → right atrium → (valve) → right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs (gas exchange) → pulmonary vein → left atrium → (valve) → left ventricle → aorta → body.**

It is **double** because blood passes through the heart **twice** in one full circuit: once on the way to the lungs, once on the way to the body.

### Blood vessels

| Vessel | Structure | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Arteries** | **Thick, muscular, elastic walls**; narrow lumen; blood under high pressure | Carry blood **away from the heart**; the walls can widen and narrow (vasodilation/vasoconstriction) to control blood flow |
| **Veins** | **Thinner walls**; wide lumen; **valves** to stop backflow | Carry blood **to the heart** at low pressure; skeletal muscles squeeze veins (the **muscle pump**) |
| **Capillaries** | **One cell thick** walls; very small | Allow **gas exchange** and exchange of nutrients and waste with the tissues |

### Blood

| Component | Function |
|---|---|
| **Red blood cells** | Carry oxygen (bound to **haemoglobin**) to muscles |
| **White blood cells** | Fight infection (immune system) |
| **Platelets** | Help blood to clot at a wound |
| **Plasma** | Liquid part: carries nutrients, hormones, waste (CO₂) and heat |

### Heart rate, stroke volume and cardiac output

- **Heart rate (HR)**: number of beats per minute (bpm). Resting average about **60–80 bpm**; maximum about **220 − age**.
- **Stroke volume (SV)**: volume of blood pumped by the **left ventricle in one beat** (ml). Resting average about **70 ml**.
- **Cardiac output (Q)**: volume of blood pumped by the heart per minute.

**Q = HR × SV**

| | Resting (untrained) | Trained athlete (resting) |
|---|---|---|
| HR | ~72 bpm | **Lower**, e.g. 50 bpm |
| SV | ~70 ml | **Higher**, e.g. 100 ml |
| Q | ~5 litres/min | Similar at rest (5000 ml) |

**Calculation example:** HR = 75 bpm, SV = 80 ml. Q = 75 × 80 = **6000 ml/min** = **6 litres/min**. Keep units: ml/min or litres/min (1000 ml = 1 litre).

During exercise, HR, SV and Q all **increase**. Trained athletes can reach a much higher maximum Q, which supplies more oxygen to the muscles.

### Vascular shunting

During exercise the body redirects blood: **vasodilation** of arteries supplying the working muscles (more blood and oxygen) and **vasoconstriction** to the digestive system and other organs not needed. This is **vascular shunting**.

### The respiratory system

**Path of air:** nose/mouth → **trachea** → **bronchi** (one to each lung) → **bronchioles** → **alveoli**.

**Alveoli** are tiny air sacs at the ends of the bronchioles, with very thin walls and surrounded by **capillaries**.

### Breathing mechanism

| | Inspiration (breathing in) | Expiration (breathing out) |
|---|---|---|
| **Diaphragm** | Contracts and **flattens** | Relaxes and **domes up** |
| **Intercostal muscles** (between ribs) | Contract, ribs move **up and out** | Relax, ribs move **down and in** |
| **Chest volume** | **Increases** | **Decreases** |
| **Pressure in lungs** | Decreases, air flows **in** | Increases, air flows **out** |

During hard exercise, expiration becomes active (extra muscles such as the abdominals help force air out).

### Gas exchange (diffusion)

Gases move by **diffusion**: from a region of **high concentration to low concentration**.

- **At the alveoli:** the alveoli have **high O₂** and low CO₂ compared with the blood in the capillaries, so **oxygen diffuses into the blood** (bound to haemoglobin) and **carbon dioxide diffuses out** to be exhaled.
- **At the muscles:** blood has high O₂ and the muscle cells have low O₂ (used up), so **oxygen diffuses into the muscle**, and CO₂ (waste from respiration) **diffuses into the blood**.

**Why the alveoli are good for gas exchange:** a huge surface area (millions of alveoli), walls only **one cell thick**, a rich capillary blood supply, and moist walls. Short diffusion distance and a steep concentration gradient (kept by breathing and blood flow).

### Lung volumes

| Term | Meaning | Effect of exercise |
|---|---|---|
| **Tidal volume (TV)** | Volume of air breathed in or out in **one breath** (about 500 ml at rest) | **Increases** |
| **Breathing rate / frequency** | Breaths per minute (about 12–15 at rest) | **Increases** |
| **Minute ventilation (VE)** | Volume of air breathed in **per minute** = tidal volume × breathing rate | **Increases** |
| **Vital capacity** | Maximum volume you can breathe **out after a maximum breath in** | Unchanged in the short term; may rise with training |
| **Residual volume** | Air left in the lungs after a maximum breath out | Stays the same |

**Calculation:** TV = 500 ml, breathing rate = 14 breaths/min. VE = 500 × 14 = 7000 ml/min = 7 litres/min.

## Key terms and definitions

- **Heart rate**: the number of times the heart beats per minute.
- **Stroke volume**: the volume of blood pumped out of the left ventricle with each beat.
- **Cardiac output**: the volume of blood pumped out of the heart per minute (HR × SV).
- **Artery**: a blood vessel that carries blood away from the heart.
- **Vein**: a blood vessel that carries blood towards the heart.
- **Capillary**: a very small blood vessel with walls one cell thick where gas exchange takes place.
- **Vasoconstriction / vasodilation**: narrowing / widening of blood vessels.
- **Vascular shunting**: redirecting blood flow to the working muscles during exercise.
- **Alveoli**: tiny air sacs in the lungs where gas exchange takes place.
- **Diffusion**: movement of a gas from an area of high concentration to an area of low concentration.
- **Tidal volume**: the volume of air breathed in or out in one breath.
- **Minute ventilation**: the volume of air breathed in or out per minute.
- **Vital capacity**: the maximum volume of air that can be breathed out after a maximum breath in.
- **Haemoglobin**: the protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen.

## Worked examples

**1. A swimmer has a resting heart rate of 55 bpm and a stroke volume of 95 ml. Calculate her cardiac output in litres per minute. [2 marks]**

*Model answer:* Q = HR × SV = 55 × 95 (1) = 5225 ml/min = 5.2 (5.225) litres/min (1).

*Marks:* 1 for the method, 1 for the answer with units.

**2. Describe how oxygen moves from the alveoli to the blood. [3 marks]**

*Model answer:* Oxygen concentration is higher in the alveoli than in the blood in the capillaries (1), so oxygen diffuses across the thin alveolar and capillary walls (1) into the blood, where it combines with haemoglobin in red blood cells (1).

**3. Explain how the structure of an artery suits its function. [3 marks]**

*Model answer:* Arteries carry blood away from the heart under high pressure (1). They have thick, muscular, elastic walls to withstand the pressure (1) and a narrow lumen to keep pressure high (1).

**4. Describe what happens to the diaphragm and ribs during inspiration. [2 marks]**

*Model answer:* The diaphragm contracts and flattens (1) and the intercostal muscles contract, moving the ribs up and out, increasing chest volume so air flows in (1).

## Common mistakes

- Saying the **right side** of the heart carries **oxygenated** blood. The **right** side has **deoxygenated** blood going to the lungs.
- Writing "all arteries carry oxygenated blood": the **pulmonary artery** carries deoxygenated blood, and the **pulmonary vein** carries oxygenated blood.
- Forgetting **units** in cardiac output (ml/min or litres/min) or mixing up ml and litres.
- Mixing up **breathing** (moving air in and out) with **respiration** (releasing energy in cells), and **gas exchange** (diffusion).
- Saying gases "move because the body needs it". Say they move by **diffusion down a concentration gradient**.
- Mixing stroke volume (per beat) and cardiac output (per minute).
- Describing the diaphragm as "relaxing and flattening" on inspiration; it **contracts** to flatten.
