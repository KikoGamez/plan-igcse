# Cardiovascular and respiratory systems

> **In the exam:** Paper 1 (Theory, 1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks; short and structured questions). This is a heavily tested area. Expect "Name the structure labelled A on a heart diagram" [1–3], "State the pathway of air to the alveoli" [2–3], "Explain how the alveoli are adapted for gaseous exchange" [3], "State the function of red blood cells / platelets" [1], "Compare arteries and veins" [3], "Calculate cardiac output when stroke volume is 70 ml and heart rate is 60 bpm" [2], "Describe the effect of exercise on tidal volume" [2].

## What you need to know

- Respiratory: pathway of air (mouth/nasal passage, trachea, bronchi, bronchioles, alveoli); gaseous exchange at the alveoli and their characteristics; function of the diaphragm and intercostal muscles in normal breathing; tidal volume, vital capacity, residual volume, minute ventilation and the effect of exercise.
- Circulatory: function of plasma, red blood cells, white blood cells, platelets; role of haemoglobin; structure and function of arteries, capillaries and veins.
- Heart: function and location of atria, ventricles and valves; pathway of blood through the heart (aorta, vena cava, pulmonary artery, pulmonary vein).
- Cardiac output = stroke volume × heart rate; the effect of exercise on the heart.

## Notes

### The respiratory system

**Pathway of air:** mouth / nasal passage → **trachea** → **bronchi** (left and right) → **bronchioles** → **alveoli**.

**Mechanics of breathing**

| | Inhaling (breathing in) | Exhaling (breathing out) at rest |
|---|---|---|
| **Diaphragm** | Contracts and flattens | Relaxes and moves up (dome shape) |
| **Intercostal muscles** (between the ribs) | Contract; ribs move up and out | Relax; ribs move down and in |
| **Chest volume** | Increases | Decreases |
| **Air pressure in lungs** | Falls below atmospheric, so air flows in | Rises, so air is pushed out |

(During hard exercise, exhaling also becomes active, but the syllabus asks for normal breathing.)

**Gaseous exchange at the alveoli.** The alveoli are tiny air sacs at the ends of the bronchioles, surrounded by capillaries. Oxygen moves from the alveoli into the blood and carbon dioxide moves from the blood into the alveoli by **diffusion** (from high to low concentration).

Alveoli are adapted for fast exchange because they have:
- **A very large surface area** (millions of alveoli)
- **Walls one cell thick** (a short diffusion distance)
- **A rich network of capillaries** (blood carries oxygen away, keeping the gradient steep)
- **Moist walls**, so gases dissolve and pass through
- **Constant ventilation**: breathing keeps bringing fresh oxygen in, so the concentration gradient stays steep

**Lung volumes**

| Term | Meaning | At rest (approx.) | Effect of exercise |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tidal volume** | Air breathed in or out in one **normal breath** | ~500 ml | **Increases** (deeper breaths) |
| **Vital capacity** | The **maximum** air you can breathe out after a maximum breath in | ~4,800 ml (varies) | Stays about the same (may rise slightly with training) |
| **Residual volume** | Air **left in the lungs** after the biggest exhalation (the lungs never empty) | ~1,200 ml | Stays about the same |
| **Minute ventilation** | Volume of air breathed **in one minute** = tidal volume × breathing rate | ~6,000 ml (500 × 12) | **Increases a lot** (both factors rise) |

### The circulatory system

**Components of blood**

| Component | Function |
|---|---|
| **Plasma** | Liquid part (about 55%); carries blood cells, nutrients, hormones, waste (CO₂) and heat around the body |
| **Red blood cells** | Carry oxygen (and some CO₂) using **haemoglobin** |
| **White blood cells** | Fight infection (immune system) |
| **Platelets** | Clump together to **clot** blood and seal cuts |

**Haemoglobin** is the protein in red blood cells. It combines with **oxygen** in the lungs (forming oxyhaemoglobin), carries it to the muscles and releases it there; it also helps carry **carbon dioxide** back to the lungs. More haemoglobin means more oxygen can be carried, which is a benefit for endurance.

**Blood vessels**

| | Arteries | Capillaries | Veins |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direction | **Away** from the heart | Between arteries and veins; link to the tissues | **Towards** the heart |
| Wall | **Thick**, muscular and elastic | **One cell thick** | Thin |
| Lumen (hole) | Small | Very small | **Large** |
| Valves | No | No | **Yes**, to stop backflow |
| Blood | Usually oxygenated (not the pulmonary artery), high pressure | Where gas, nutrient and waste exchange happens | Usually deoxygenated (not the pulmonary vein), low pressure |

Why: arteries must withstand high pressure from the heart; capillary walls are thin so oxygen can pass through; veins have valves because the blood is at low pressure and could flow backwards.

### The heart

The heart is a double pump in the centre of the chest, with four chambers:
- **Atria** (right and left): the **top** chambers; receive blood coming into the heart.
- **Ventricles** (right and left): the **bottom** chambers; pump blood out of the heart. The **left ventricle** has the thickest wall, because it pumps blood all around the body.
- **Valves**: stop blood flowing backwards; between atria and ventricles, and at the exits from the ventricles. (You do not need valve names.)

**Pathway of blood (trace it with a finger):**

Body → **vena cava** → right atrium → right ventricle → **pulmonary artery** → lungs (picks up oxygen) → **pulmonary vein** → left atrium → left ventricle → **aorta** → body.

Remember: the right side carries **deoxygenated** blood to the lungs; the left side carries **oxygenated** blood to the body. The pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood (the one artery with this); the pulmonary vein carries oxygenated blood.

### Cardiac output

- **Heart rate (HR)**: number of heartbeats per minute (bpm).
- **Stroke volume (SV)**: the volume of blood pumped out of the left ventricle **per beat** (ml).
- **Cardiac output (Q)**: the volume of blood pumped out of the heart **per minute**.

**Cardiac output = stroke volume × heart rate** (ml/min or l/min)

Example: SV 70 ml × HR 72 bpm = 5,040 ml/min (about 5 l/min).

**Effect of exercise**
- Short term: heart rate, stroke volume and so cardiac output all **increase** to send more oxygen to the working muscles. Maximum heart rate is roughly **220 − age**.
- Long term (training): the heart muscle gets bigger and stronger (**hypertrophy**), stroke volume rises at rest and in exercise, so the **resting heart rate falls** (bradycardia, under 60 bpm). The heart pumps the same cardiac output with fewer beats.

## Key terms and definitions

- **Gaseous exchange**: the movement of oxygen from the alveoli into the blood and carbon dioxide from the blood into the alveoli, by diffusion.
- **Tidal volume**: the volume of air breathed in or out during one normal breath.
- **Vital capacity**: the maximum volume of air that can be forced out after a maximum inhalation.
- **Residual volume**: the volume of air that stays in the lungs after a maximum exhalation.
- **Minute ventilation**: the volume of air breathed in (or out) in one minute (tidal volume × breathing rate).
- **Haemoglobin**: the protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen (and some carbon dioxide).
- **Heart rate**: the number of times the heart beats in one minute.
- **Stroke volume**: the volume of blood pumped out of the heart (left ventricle) in one beat.
- **Cardiac output**: the volume of blood pumped out of the heart in one minute (stroke volume × heart rate).

## Worked examples

**Q1. Explain two ways the alveoli are adapted for gaseous exchange. [4]**

- Walls are only one cell thick (1) so the diffusion distance is short and oxygen passes quickly into the blood (1).
- Surrounded by a network of capillaries (1) which carry oxygen away and maintain a concentration gradient (1).

**Q2. A swimmer has a stroke volume of 85 ml and a heart rate of 60 bpm at rest. Calculate her cardiac output and explain why her stroke volume is higher than an untrained person's. [4]**

- Cardiac output = 85 × 60 (1) = 5,100 ml/min (1) (or 5.1 l/min).
- Training makes the heart muscle bigger and stronger (hypertrophy) (1), so more blood is pumped out each beat (1).

**Q3. Describe the pathway of blood from the body to the lungs. [3]**

Vena cava (1) → right atrium → right ventricle (1) → pulmonary artery → lungs (1).

**Q4. Describe two differences between arteries and veins. [2]**

- Arteries have thick, muscular walls, veins have thin walls (1).
- Veins have valves, arteries do not / veins have a larger lumen (1). (Or arteries carry blood away from the heart at high pressure; veins carry it back at low pressure.)

## Common mistakes

- Saying "the pulmonary vein carries deoxygenated blood". It carries **oxygenated** blood from the lungs. Think "**veins go to the heart**", whatever the oxygen content.
- Mixing up atria (top, receive) and ventricles (bottom, pump).
- Writing "the heart is on the left". It is in the **middle**, tilted left; the left ventricle is the strongest.
- Confusing **tidal volume** (normal breath) with **vital capacity** (maximum breath).
- Saying "red blood cells carry oxygen" without **haemoglobin**.
- Leaving cardiac output without **units** (ml/min or l/min).
- Saying exercise makes "residual volume" bigger. It stays about the same, and **tidal volume** and **minute ventilation** go up.
- Saying we breathe out only carbon dioxide. Exhaled air has **less oxygen and more CO₂** than inhaled air, but still contains oxygen.

## Practice questions

2. Describe what happens to the diaphragm and intercostal muscles during inhalation. [2]
3. State the function of (a) platelets, (b) plasma. [2]
4. Define tidal volume and state the effect of exercise on it. [2]
5. A footballer's tidal volume is 600 ml and breathing rate 15 breaths/min. Calculate minute ventilation. [2]
6. Name the blood vessel that carries oxygenated blood from the lungs to the heart. [1]
7. Calculate cardiac output when stroke volume = 75 ml and heart rate = 68 bpm. [2]
8. Explain the long-term effects of endurance training on the heart. [3]
