# Components of fitness and fitness testing

> **In the exam:** Paper 1 (Theory, 1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks; short and structured questions). Typical items: "Define cardiovascular endurance" [1], "Name the component of fitness needed for a goalkeeper to dive and save a penalty" [1–2], "Describe how to carry out the Sit and Reach Test / Ruler Drop Test" [3–4], "Name a test for muscular endurance" [1], "Give two reasons for fitness testing" [2], "Using the results table, compare two athletes' scores and say what the performer should do next" [3–4], "Explain what is meant by VO₂ max and two factors that affect it" [3]. Always link the component to a named sport action.

## What you need to know

- The components of fitness (health-related and skill-related): agility, balance (static and dynamic), cardiovascular endurance/stamina, coordination, flexibility, muscular endurance, power, reaction time, speed, strength; link each to performance in physical activities.
- How to carry out the named tests: MSFT or 12-Minute Cooper Run, Sit and Reach, Multi-Stage Abdominal Curl Conditioning Test, Vertical Jump, 30-Metre Sprint, 1 Rep Max or Hand Grip Dynamometer, Illinois Agility Test, Standing Stork Test, Anderson Wall Toss Coordination Test, Ruler Drop Test.
- Reasons for fitness testing.
- VO₂ max: meaning, importance as a measure of cardiovascular endurance, factors that affect it (age, gender, genetics, lifestyle, training).
- Fitness definition and its link to health.

## Notes

### Fitness and health

**Fitness** is the ability to cope with (or meet) the demands of the environment. **Health** is a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity (WHO). You can be healthy but not fit for a sport, or fit but ill. Fitness has components, and each sport needs a different mix.

### The ten components

| Component | Definition | Sporting example | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cardiovascular endurance (stamina)** | Ability of the heart and lungs to supply oxygen to the working muscles for a long time | Marathon, 90-minute football match | Health-related |
| **Muscular endurance** | Ability of a muscle or muscle group to keep contracting repeatedly without tiring | Rowing, press-ups, cycling uphill | Health-related |
| **Strength** | The maximum force a muscle can exert in one contraction | Weightlifting, scrummaging in rugby | Health-related |
| **Flexibility** | The range of movement possible at a joint | Gymnast's splits, hurdler | Health-related |
| **Speed** | The ability to move the body, or part of it, quickly over a distance (distance ÷ time) | 100 m sprint | Skill-related |
| **Power** | Strength × speed: the ability to produce force quickly | Vertical jump, shot put, a smash in volleyball | Skill-related |
| **Agility** | The ability to change direction quickly while keeping control | Dodging a defender in basketball | Skill-related |
| **Balance** | Keeping the centre of mass over the base of support. **Static** = still; **dynamic** = while moving | Gymnast on the beam (both), a handstand (static) | Skill-related |
| **Coordination** | Using two or more body parts together smoothly and accurately | Hand–eye in tennis, dribbling a football | Skill-related |
| **Reaction time** | The time between a stimulus and the start of the response | Sprinter reacting to the starting gun | Skill-related |

### Fitness tests: how to carry them out

| Component | Test | Protocol (how to do it) | Result measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cardiovascular endurance** | **Multi-Stage Fitness Test (bleep test)** | Run 20 m shuttles between two lines in time with bleeps that get faster; stop when you cannot reach the line in time twice | Level and shuttle reached |
| | **12-Minute Cooper Run** | Run as far as possible in 12 minutes around a track | Distance (m) |
| **Flexibility** | **Sit and Reach** | Sit with legs straight, feet flat against a box; slowly reach forward as far as possible along the ruler, keeping the legs straight; hold for 2 seconds | Distance (cm) reached |
| **Muscular endurance** | **Multi-Stage Abdominal Curl Conditioning Test** | Lie with knees bent; do curl-ups in time with a recording that speeds up, moving the hands along a marked strip; stop when you cannot keep to the beat | Number of curl-ups |
| **Power** | **Vertical Jump** | Stand side-on to a wall, reach up and mark the highest point; jump as high as possible and mark again | Difference in cm |
| **Speed** | **30-Metre Sprint** | Sprint 30 m from a standing or set start; timed with a stopwatch or timing gates | Time (s) |
| **Strength** | **1 Repetition Maximum (1RM)** | Lift the heaviest weight you can lift once with correct technique (with a spotter) | Weight (kg) |
| | **Hand Grip Dynamometer** | Squeeze the dynamometer as hard as possible with the dominant hand, arm by your side | Force (kg) |
| **Agility** | **Illinois Agility Test** | Run a set course of cones (lying face down at the start, weaving through four cones) as fast as possible | Time (s) |
| **Balance** | **Standing Stork Test** (static) | Stand on one leg, other foot against the knee, hands on hips; raise the heel; time until you lose balance | Time (s) |
| **Coordination** | **Anderson Wall Toss** | Stand 2 m from a wall; throw a ball with one hand and catch with the other for 30 seconds | Number of catches |
| **Reaction time** | **Ruler Drop Test** | A partner holds a ruler vertically between your open fingers and drops it without warning; catch it as fast as you can | Distance (cm) caught |

For all tests: do a warm-up, use the same conditions each time, and follow the protocol exactly so that results are **reliable** (you can repeat them) and **valid** (they measure the right component).

### Reasons for fitness testing

- To check **suitability** for a sport or position (e.g. does she have the stamina for a midfielder?).
- To identify **strengths and weaknesses**.
- To **monitor improvement** by testing before and after a programme.
- To **compare** with others or with national averages (norms).
- To **inform the design of a training programme** (train the weak areas).
- For **motivation**: seeing progress or setting targets.

### VO₂ max

**VO₂ max** is the **maximum volume of oxygen** the body can take in, transport and use per minute during exercise (usually in ml per kg of body weight per minute, ml/kg/min). It is the best measure of **cardiovascular endurance**: a high VO₂ max means the heart and lungs deliver lots of oxygen so the performer can work aerobically at a high level for longer. Elite endurance athletes have VO₂ max values of 70+, untrained people around 30–40.

**Factors that affect VO₂ max**
- **Age**: decreases as you get older.
- **Gender**: males tend to be higher (larger heart/lungs, more haemoglobin, less body fat).
- **Genetics**: some people inherit a bigger capacity.
- **Lifestyle**: smoking, poor diet and inactivity lower it.
- **Training**: aerobic training raises it.

### Interpreting fitness test data

Example: Sit and Reach (cm), 14-year-old girls (compare with a norms table).

| | Before 8-week programme | After |
|---|---|---|
| Ana | 18 cm | 24 cm |
| Lucía | 30 cm | 31 cm |

- Say what the numbers show: Ana improved by 6 cm, Lucía by 1 cm.
- Compare with the norm (e.g. average 25 cm): Ana was well below average before and is now just below it; Lucía was already above average.
- Give a reason or next step: Ana's flexibility is increasing, so keep stretching; Lucía is already good and can maintain it.
- Note limits: one test only; effort, warm-up and time of day affect results.

## Key terms and definitions

- **Fitness**: the ability to cope with (or meet) the demands of the environment.
- **Cardiovascular endurance (stamina)**: the ability of the heart and lungs to supply oxygen to the muscles for a long period.
- **Muscular endurance**: the ability of a muscle or muscle group to contract repeatedly without fatiguing.
- **Strength**: the maximum force a muscle or muscle group can exert.
- **Flexibility**: the range of movement at a joint.
- **Speed**: the ability to move quickly; distance ÷ time.
- **Power**: strength × speed; the ability to exert force quickly.
- **Agility**: the ability to change direction quickly while staying in control.
- **Balance**: the ability to keep the centre of mass over the base of support (static or dynamic).
- **Coordination**: the ability to use two or more body parts together smoothly and efficiently.
- **Reaction time**: the time between a stimulus and the start of a response.
- **VO₂ max**: the maximum volume of oxygen that can be taken in, transported and used by the muscles per minute.

## Worked examples

**Q1. Name the component of fitness being tested by the Ruler Drop Test and give a sporting example where it is important. [2]**

Reaction time (1); e.g. a sprinter reacting to the starting gun / a goalkeeper reacting to a penalty (1).

**Q2. Describe how to carry out the Sit and Reach test. [4]**

- Sit on the floor with legs straight and feet flat against the box (1).
- Reach forward slowly with arms outstretched, keeping legs straight / no bouncing (1).
- Push the marker along the ruler as far as possible and hold for about 2 seconds (1).
- Record the distance (cm) reached (1).

**Q3. A hockey player's 30-metre sprint time improves from 4.9 s to 4.6 s after a six-week plyometric programme. Explain two reasons why she carried out the fitness test. [4]**

- To monitor improvement (1): the second test shows whether the plyometric programme worked, i.e. faster by 0.3 s (1).
- To identify strengths and weaknesses / compare with others (1): she could see whether her speed is good enough for her position (1).

**Q4. Explain two factors that can affect a person's VO₂ max. [4]**

- Training (1): aerobic training raises VO₂ max because the heart and lungs improve at delivering oxygen (1).
- Age (1): VO₂ max falls with age because the heart and lungs work less well (1). (Also acceptable: gender, genetics, lifestyle.)

## Common mistakes

- Confusing **strength** (maximum force once) with **muscular endurance** (repeated contractions) and **power** (strength × speed).
- Describing agility as "being fast". Agility is **changing direction quickly with control**.
- Naming a test without saying what it measures, or giving the **wrong component** (the vertical jump tests **power**, not strength).
- Forgetting to give the **unit** or the measurement in a protocol (cm, seconds, kg, level reached).
- Giving vague reasons for testing ("to see how fit you are"). Use: strengths/weaknesses, monitor improvement, compare, plan training, motivation.
- Writing that VO₂ max is "how much oxygen you breathe". It is the **maximum oxygen the body can take in, transport and use**.
- In data questions: not using numbers from the table to support your statement.
