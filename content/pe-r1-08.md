# Injury prevention and performance-enhancing drugs

> **In the exam:** Paper 1 (Theory, 1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks). Typical questions: "Describe a suitable warm-up for a netball player" [3–4], "Give two reasons for a cool-down" [2], "Identify two ways to reduce the risk of injury in a swimming pool" [2], "Describe the RICE method" [3–4], "Name a type of performance-enhancing drug and state its effect" [2], "Give two disadvantages of using drugs" [2], and "Explain how blood doping can improve performance" [3]. Use only activities from the Cambridge list as examples.

## What you need to know

- Reasons for, and phases of, a **warm-up** and **cool-down**, with an activity-specific example.
- **Risk:** real v perceived; risks in different environments; ways to reduce risk and injury.
- Minor injuries: **winding, cuts/grazes, blisters**; causes of **bruises, muscle, tendon and ligament injuries**; **RICE**.
- **PEDs:** why performers use them; anabolic steroids, beta blockers, stimulants, diuretics; effects, activities that benefit; disadvantages; role of organising bodies.
- **Blood doping:** why, how, effects, side effects.

## Notes

### Warm-up

A warm-up has three phases, each with a purpose:

| Phase | What to do | Example (hockey) |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Pulse raiser** | Gentle activity that raises heart rate and body temperature | Jogging, skipping |
| **2. Stretching** | Dynamic stretches of the muscles and joints to be used | Leg swings, lunges, arm circles |
| **3. Skill-related / familiarisation** | Movements and drills from the activity, building up to full speed | Passing in pairs, short sprints, shooting |

**Physiological reasons:** raises muscle temperature (muscles contract faster and more efficiently), increases blood flow and oxygen to muscles, increases range of movement at joints, reduces the risk of muscle/tendon injury. **Psychological reasons:** focus and concentration, mental rehearsal of skills, reduces nerves/anxiety.

### Cool-down

| Phase | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Gradual decrease in pulse** | Light jogging/walking for 5–10 minutes to bring heart rate and breathing down slowly |
| **Stretching** | Static stretches (hold 15–30 s) for the muscles used |

**Reasons:** removes waste products such as lactic acid, helps prevent blood pooling and dizziness (keeps blood flowing back to the heart), reduces muscle stiffness/soreness, helps the body return to its resting state. **Psychological:** time to relax and reflect on the performance.

### Risk

- **Real risk:** a danger that is actually likely to cause harm (e.g. a wet floor in a sports hall, a rugby tackle with poor technique, rough water in sailing).
- **Perceived risk:** a danger that someone *thinks* exists whether or not it is likely (e.g. a beginner fears falling during a trampolining routine even with a spotter and safe set-up).

**Risk assessment** means identifying hazards, deciding how likely and how serious they are, and acting to reduce them. Typical hazards:

| Environment | Examples |
|---|---|
| Indoor sports hall | Slippery floor, equipment not stored safely, poor lighting |
| Playing field | Holes, uneven or wet ground, broken glass, goalposts not secured |
| Swimming pool | Wet surround, deep water, no lifeguard, running on poolside |
| Artificial surfaces | Friction burns/grazes, wrong footwear, hard surface causing joint stress |

**Strategies to reduce risk/injury:**
- **Protective clothing and equipment:** shin pads, gum shield, helmet.
- **Appropriate clothing and footwear:** correct studs/trainers, remove jewellery.
- **Lifting and carrying equipment safely:** bend the knees, team lifts.
- **Maintaining hydration.**
- **Warm-up and cool-down.**
- **Following the rules** (e.g. no high tackles) and officials' decisions.
- **Suitable level of competition:** matched by age, size, skill and gender where appropriate (e.g. weight categories in judo).

### Injuries

**Minor injuries**

| Injury | Cause | Simple treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Winding** | A blow to the stomach that knocks the air out | Rest, loosen tight clothing, breathe slowly and steadily |
| **Cut / graze** | Falls, contact with the ground or equipment | Clean with water, apply pressure to stop bleeding, cover with a plaster/dressing |
| **Blister** | Friction from poorly fitting shoes/socks | Do not burst; cover with a blister plaster; prevent with well-fitting footwear and socks |

**Soft tissue injuries**

| Injury | Cause |
|---|---|
| **Bruise** | A direct blow ruptures small blood vessels under the skin (blood collects) |
| **Muscle injury (strain / pulled muscle)** | Muscle is overstretched or torn, e.g. sprinting without a warm-up |
| **Tendon injury** | Overuse or sudden force (e.g. Achilles tendon) |
| **Ligament injury (sprain)** | Joint is twisted beyond its normal range, e.g. rolling an ankle |

**RICE** treatment for soft tissue injuries:
- **R**est: stop playing, avoid putting weight on the injury.
- **I**ce: apply a cold pack (wrapped in cloth) for about 10–20 minutes; reduces swelling and pain.
- **C**ompression: bandage to limit swelling.
- **E**levation: raise the injury above heart level to reduce swelling.

Serious injury (e.g. suspected fracture, head injury, unconsciousness): stop, do not move the performer, and get medical help.

### Performance-enhancing drugs (PEDs)

**Why do performers use them?** To improve performance, to keep up with (or beat) competitors who may be using them, and for fame and increased wealth (prize money, sponsorship).

| Drug | Effect | Example of an activity where it helps | Possible health risks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Anabolic steroids** | Build muscle mass and strength, speed recovery | Weightlifting, sprinting, rugby | Liver damage, heart disease, high blood pressure, aggression, hormone problems |
| **Beta blockers** | Slow heart rate and reduce anxiety and muscle tremor | Archery, shooting, snooker, golf | Very low heart rate, tiredness, low blood pressure |
| **Stimulants** | Increase alertness, reaction time and reduce tiredness | Football, cycling, boxing | Heart problems, addiction, anxiety, high blood pressure |
| **Diuretics** | Remove fluid: weight loss, can hide other drugs | Boxing, judo, horse-racing jockeys (weight categories) | Dehydration, cramps, dizziness, kidney problems |

**Disadvantages of PEDs (general):** health risks (some fatal); cheating (unfair on other competitors); **disqualification or a ban**; fines and loss of sponsorship/medals (**financial penalty**); **public humiliation** and damaged reputation; drug scandals reduce public trust in the sport.

**Role of organising bodies** (e.g. the World Anti-Doping Agency and national governing bodies): publish a list of **banned substances**; carry out **testing** (urine and blood samples, in-competition and out-of-competition/random tests); punish offenders. Drugs are banned because they are **unfair**, **harm health**, and set a bad example to young people.

### Blood doping

Increases the number of red blood cells so more oxygen is carried to muscles.

- **How:** (1) blood is removed from the athlete weeks before the event, the body replaces it; (2) the stored blood is re-injected before competition (**transfusion**). EPO (a hormone injection) can also increase red blood cell production.
- **Effect:** higher oxygen-carrying capacity → better **endurance** (delay of fatigue). Used in cycling, long-distance running, cross-country skiing.
- **Side effects:** thicker blood (higher risk of blood clots, heart attack, stroke), infection from needles/transfused blood, allergic reaction.

## Key terms and definitions

- **Warm-up:** preparation before exercise that gradually raises heart rate and muscle temperature and prepares the body and mind.
- **Cool-down:** gradually lowering heart rate and stretching after exercise to help recovery.
- **Real risk:** a danger that is likely to cause harm.
- **Perceived risk:** a danger that a person believes to exist, whether or not it does.
- **RICE:** Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation: treatment for soft tissue injuries.
- **Performance-enhancing drug:** a substance taken to improve sporting performance, usually banned.
- **Blood doping:** increasing the number of red blood cells (e.g. by transfusion) to improve oxygen transport.

## Worked examples

**1. Describe a suitable warm-up for a basketball player. [4]**

*Model answer:* Start with a pulse raiser such as jogging around the court to increase heart rate and body temperature [1]. Then do dynamic stretches of the muscles to be used, e.g. leg swings and arm circles [1]. Then skill-related activities, such as dribbling and passing in pairs, increasing in speed [1]. Link to a reason, e.g. this reduces the risk of muscle strain [1].

**2. Explain why a boxer might take diuretics. [2]**

*Model answer:* Diuretics remove water from the body, so the boxer loses weight [1] and can make the weight limit for a lower category (or hide other drugs) [1].

**3. Describe how RICE would be used on a player who has twisted her ankle. [4]**

*Model answer:* Rest: stop playing and do not put weight on it [1]. Ice: apply a cold pack wrapped in a cloth to reduce swelling [1]. Compression: bandage the ankle to limit swelling [1]. Elevation: raise the ankle above the heart to reduce blood flow and swelling [1].

## Common mistakes

- Saying a warm-up "stops injuries" without a reason (e.g. warmer muscles are more elastic and less likely to tear).
- Writing a cool-down that is only stretching: include the **gradual decrease in pulse**.
- Mixing up real and perceived risk.
- Listing beta blockers as increasing performance in endurance sports: they **calm** the performer, so they help in precision sports.
- Saying steroids "make you stronger" without muscle mass; and saying diuretics build muscle (they cause weight loss).
- Writing "ice" for RICE without saying what it does (reduces swelling and pain).
- Using only "it is cheating" as a disadvantage of drugs when the question asks for health, financial or reputation effects.
