# Health, fitness, well-being and lifestyle

> **In the exam:** Appears on the theory papers as definitions (1–2 marks: health, fitness, well-being), short “give an example” questions (1 mark) and 3–6 mark explain/evaluate questions on the benefits of exercise or the effects of an inactive or unhealthy lifestyle. Examples must be clearly physical, mental or social. Check with your teacher which paper this sits on.

## What you need to know

- Definitions of **health, fitness and well-being**, and how they are linked.
- The **physical, mental and social** benefits of regular exercise.
- The effects of a **sedentary lifestyle** and of **inactivity**: obesity, coronary heart disease, type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, osteoporosis, poor mental health.
- **Lifestyle choices**: activity level, diet, sleep, smoking, alcohol, drugs, stress.
- **Body types (somatotypes)** and **weight-related terms**: overweight, obese, underweight, BMI.
- Recommended **activity guidelines** for young people and adults.

## Notes

### Health, fitness and well-being

- **Health**: a state of complete **physical, mental and social well-being**, and not merely the absence of disease or illness (World Health Organization).
- **Fitness**: the ability to meet the demands of the environment (to cope with the physical demands of daily life and sport without undue fatigue).
- **Well-being**: a person’s overall state of feeling healthy, happy and satisfied with life.

They are linked: being fit helps health, and good health supports well-being. A person can be fit but not healthy (e.g. an athlete with an infection) or healthy but not fit.

### The three parts of health

| Type | Meaning | Examples of how exercise helps |
|---|---|---|
| **Physical** | The body works properly | Stronger heart and lungs, stronger bones and muscles, healthy weight, better posture, lower risk of disease |
| **Mental** (emotional) | Feeling positive, coping with stress | Releases **endorphins** (feel-good chemicals), reduces stress and anxiety, improves mood, builds self-confidence and self-esteem, improves sleep |
| **Social** | Relationships and belonging | Make friends, teamwork, cooperation, sense of belonging, learning leadership and communication |

Exam tip: always say which of the three you are talking about. “It makes you happy” is mental; “makes friends” is social.

### Benefits of regular exercise (physical)

- Stronger heart (lower resting heart rate, bigger stroke volume) and improved cardiovascular fitness.
- Lower blood pressure and cholesterol.
- Stronger bones and muscles, better joint health, better posture.
- Helps control weight (energy balance).
- Better sleep and more energy.
- Lower risk of CHD, type 2 diabetes, some cancers, osteoporosis.

### Effects of a sedentary lifestyle

A **sedentary lifestyle** involves little physical activity and a lot of sitting (e.g. long hours on screens, travelling by car).

| Problem | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Obesity** | Body stores extra energy as fat. Raises risk of CHD, diabetes and joint problems |
| **Coronary heart disease (CHD)** | Fatty deposits narrow arteries supplying the heart muscle, causing angina or heart attack |
| **Type 2 diabetes** | Body does not use insulin properly, so blood sugar is too high. Linked to inactivity and weight |
| **High blood pressure** | Strains the heart and blood vessels; risk of stroke |
| **Osteoporosis** | Bones lose density and become weak and brittle; weight-bearing exercise helps build bone |
| **Poor mental health** | Higher stress, low mood, low self-esteem |
| **Low fitness** | Tire easily, weaker muscles, poor posture |

### Lifestyle factors

- **Activity level**: regular exercise improves health; inactivity harms it.
- **Diet**: balanced diet supports health; too much fat, sugar and salt harms it.
- **Sleep**: teenagers need around 8–10 hours. Sleep is when the body repairs and recovers.
- **Smoking**: damages lungs and blood vessels, reduces oxygen carried in the blood (carbon monoxide), lowers endurance, causes cancer and heart disease.
- **Alcohol**: damages liver, affects judgement and coordination, adds empty calories, dehydrates.
- **Recreational drugs**: harm health and performance.
- **Stress**: long-term stress affects sleep, immune system and mood. Exercise and relaxation help.

### Activity guidelines

- **Children and young people (5–18)** are recommended to do an average of **at least 60 minutes of moderate to vigorous physical activity a day**, including activities that strengthen muscles and bones on at least 3 days a week, and to reduce sedentary time.
- **Adults**: at least **150 minutes** of moderate activity (or 75 minutes of vigorous activity) a week, plus strength work on 2 days.

### Body composition and body types

**Overweight** and **obese** mean having more body weight or fat than is healthy for your height; **obesity** is a more severe level. **Underweight** is below a healthy weight. One measure is **BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height² (m²)**.

| BMI | Category |
|---|---|
| under 18.5 | underweight |
| 18.5–24.9 | healthy |
| 25–29.9 | overweight |
| 30 or more | obese |

BMI does not separate muscle from fat, so a muscular athlete may show as “overweight”. BMI is less accurate for athletes and growing teenagers.

**Somatotypes (body types):**

| Type | Build | Suited to |
|---|---|---|
| **Endomorph** | Wide hips, rounded, stores fat easily | Sumo wrestling, props in rugby, weightlifting (heavy) |
| **Mesomorph** | Wide shoulders, narrow hips, muscular | Sprinting, gymnastics, rugby, swimming |
| **Ectomorph** | Narrow shoulders and hips, thin, little muscle | Distance running, high jump, basketball |

Few people are a pure type; most are a mix.

### Energy balance

**Energy in (food) = energy out (activity + metabolism)** keeps weight stable. More in than out leads to weight gain; less in than out leads to weight loss.

## Key terms and definitions

- **Health:** a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity.
- **Fitness:** the ability to meet the demands of the environment.
- **Well-being:** feeling healthy, happy and content with life.
- **Sedentary lifestyle:** a way of living with little physical activity.
- **Obesity:** having an excessive amount of body fat (BMI of 30 or more).
- **Coronary heart disease:** narrowing of the coronary arteries by fatty deposits.
- **Osteoporosis:** a condition where bones lose density and become fragile.
- **Endorphins:** chemicals released during exercise that improve mood.
- **BMI:** body mass index; weight in kg ÷ height in m squared.
- **Somatotype:** body type (endomorph, mesomorph, ectomorph).

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** Give one physical, one mental and one social benefit of regular exercise. [3]

*Model answer:* **Physical:** stronger heart / lower risk of CHD / helps control weight (1). **Mental:** reduces stress or anxiety / improves self-esteem (1). **Social:** makes new friends or improves teamwork (1).

**Example 2.** Calculate the BMI of a person weighing 70 kg and 1.75 m tall, and state their BMI category. [3]

*Model answer:* BMI = 70 ÷ 1.75² (1) = 70 ÷ 3.0625 = **22.9** (1). This is in the healthy range (18.5–24.9) (1).

**Example 3.** Explain how a sedentary lifestyle can lead to coronary heart disease. [3]

*Model answer:* Inactivity can cause weight gain and raised cholesterol (1). Fatty deposits build up and narrow the coronary arteries (1), so less oxygenated blood reaches the heart muscle, which can cause angina or a heart attack (1).

## Common mistakes

- Defining health as “not being ill”. You must include **physical, mental and social** well-being.
- Putting a mental benefit as a physical one (e.g. “feels confident”). Self-confidence is mental.
- Confusing **fitness** and **health**.
- Using BMI for athletes without comment: BMI cannot tell muscle from fat.
- Mixing up somatotypes: ectomorph = thin, endomorph = rounded, mesomorph = muscular.
- Writing “it helps you lose weight” for every answer. Give a different reason each time.
- Writing that obesity “causes” type 2 diabetes with no link. Say that excess fat reduces how well the body uses insulin.

## Practice questions

2. Give two social benefits of playing team sport. [2]
3. State two ways exercise improves mental health. [2]
4. Explain what is meant by a sedentary lifestyle. [1]
5. Name two health problems linked to inactivity. [2]
6. Calculate BMI for 60 kg and 1.6 m height. [2]
7. Which somatotype is best suited to distance running and why? [2]
8. Explain how smoking reduces performance in an endurance event. [3]
