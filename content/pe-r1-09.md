# Health, fitness, well-being and lifestyle

> **In the exam:** Paper 1 (Theory, 1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks). Short and structured questions: "Give the WHO definition of health" [2], "Describe one mental and one social benefit of exercise" [2–4], "Define fitness" [1], "Explain how a person can be fit but not healthy" [2–3], "Describe two lifestyle choices that affect health" [2–4]. Often linked to a named performer or to the components of fitness.

## What you need to know

- The **WHO definition of health**; physical, mental and social health and well-being.
- The definition of **fitness** and the **relationship between health and fitness**.
- The need for a healthy, active lifestyle, balanced diet and maintaining fitness.
- Positive effects of exercise on **physical, mental and social** health.
- Components of fitness and how they link to activities (the full list is in topic 6; summary below).
- Effects of an inactive or unhealthy lifestyle (obesity, heart disease, etc.).

## Notes

### Health and well-being

The **World Health Organization (WHO)** defines health as "a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity". So health is more than not being ill.

| Type | What it means | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Physical** | All body systems working well; free from illness, injury and disease; able to carry out everyday tasks | Healthy heart and lungs, a healthy weight, being able to climb stairs without getting exhausted |
| **Mental (psychological)** | Able to cope with stress; can control emotions; feeling good about yourself (self-esteem) | Handling exam pressure, feeling confident |
| **Social** | Essential human needs are met; friendship and support; having value within society; able to mix with other people | Being part of a team, friends who support you |

### Fitness

**Fitness** = the ability to cope with (or meet) the demands of the environment. A footballer needs different fitness than a snooker player. There are health-related and skill-related components:

| Health-related | Skill-related |
|---|---|
| Cardiovascular endurance (stamina), muscular endurance, strength, flexibility | Agility, balance (static, dynamic), coordination, power, reaction time, speed |

(Body composition is not in the Cambridge list: you can still mention it briefly as part of health, but the exam uses the list above.)

### The relationship between health and fitness

They are linked but **not the same**.
- **Ill health can reduce fitness:** a performer with a long illness cannot train, so fitness falls.
- **Fitness can exist despite ill health:** someone who has asthma or a poor diet (or smokes) can still be fit enough to train and perform well in some activities, so a person can be fit but not healthy.
- **Fitness helps health:** regular fitness helps prevent illness, lowers the risk of heart disease and obesity.

**To stay healthy:** live an active lifestyle, eat a balanced diet, maintain fitness, get enough sleep, avoid smoking and alcohol/drugs, and manage stress.

### Benefits of exercise

| Type | Benefits |
|---|---|
| **Physical** | Stronger heart and lungs, lower risk of heart disease and obesity, stronger bones and muscles, better posture, more energy, better sleep |
| **Mental** | Reduces stress and anxiety, releases "feel-good" chemicals, improves self-esteem and body image, better concentration |
| **Social** | Make friends, team work, a sense of belonging, learning leadership and communication, sharing experiences with family and community |

### Effects of inactivity and unhealthy habits

- **Sedentary lifestyle:** low energy use means energy is stored as fat → **obesity**, which increases the risk of coronary heart disease, type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure and joint problems.
- **Weak muscles and bones:** reduced fitness, poor posture.
- **Mental health:** higher risk of stress, anxiety and low self-esteem; social isolation.
- **Smoking:** damages lungs and reduces oxygen intake; **alcohol:** liver damage, slower reactions; **poor diet:** too much sugar/fat leads to weight gain; too little energy leads to tiredness and weak bones.
- **Lack of sleep:** slower recovery and poor concentration.

### Applying to physical activities

Exam answers get marks for **linking** fitness to a sport: "A gymnast needs flexibility to perform splits", "A 1500 m runner needs cardiovascular endurance to keep running at pace", "A sprinter needs speed and power", "A goalkeeper needs reaction time". Choose the component, then the reason.

## Key terms and definitions

- **Health:** a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity (WHO).
- **Fitness:** the ability to cope with (or meet) the demands of the environment.
- **Physical well-being:** all body systems working well; free from illness, injury and disease; able to do everyday tasks.
- **Mental well-being:** being able to cope with stress, control emotions and feel good about yourself.
- **Social well-being:** having friends and support, having your essential needs met and being able to mix with others.
- **Sedentary lifestyle:** a lifestyle with very little physical activity.
- **Obesity:** being very overweight because of excess stored fat, which damages health.

## Worked examples

**1. Explain how a person can be fit but not healthy. [2]**

*Model answer:* Fitness is the ability to meet the demands of an activity, but health also includes mental and social well-being and being free from disease [1]. For example, a sprinter could be fit for racing but smoke or suffer stress or illness, so is not healthy overall [1].

**2. Describe one mental and one social benefit of taking part in team sports. [4]**

*Model answer:* Mental: exercise reduces stress and anxiety [1] because it releases chemicals that make you feel good, improving self-esteem [1]. Social: you make friends and feel part of a group [1], supported by your team-mates, which improves social well-being [1].

**3. Explain why a performer might become less fit if they are unwell. [2]**

*Model answer:* Illness can prevent the performer from training [1], so fitness is lost (reversibility), e.g. less cardiovascular endurance [1].

## Common mistakes

- Defining health as "not being ill": you need physical, mental **and** social.
- Defining fitness as "being healthy". Use "the ability to cope with the demands of the environment".
- Writing "exercise is good for you" without saying how (type of benefit + explanation).
- Giving the same benefit twice under different headings.
- Using examples (e.g. a sport) not on the Cambridge activity list.
- Forgetting that unhealthy people can still be fit, and fit people can be unhealthy.

## Practice questions

2. Define fitness. [1]
3. Give two characteristics of social health and well-being. [2]
4. Describe two ways in which exercise improves mental health. [2]
5. Explain how ill health can lead to lower fitness. [2]
6. Give two health problems that can be caused by a sedentary lifestyle. [2]
7. Explain why flexibility is important for a gymnast. [2]
8. A person smokes but plays football every week. Explain whether they are fit and whether they are healthy. [3]
