# Diet and nutrition

> **In the exam:** Paper 1 (Theory, 1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks). Short questions inside the health, fitness and training section: "State the function of protein" [1], "Give two sources of carbohydrate" [2], "Explain why a marathon runner eats a high-carbohydrate meal" [2–3], "Explain what happens to unused energy" [2], "Compare the energy needs of a teenage male and a sedentary adult" [2–3], "Describe the role of water" [2]. Linked to aerobic respiration and glycogen in the energy topic.

## What you need to know

- The function and food sources of **carbohydrates, fats, proteins and water**.
- A **balanced diet** and the **energy balance** for physical activity.
- Different **energy needs**: male v female, teenager v child, active v sedentary.
- Unused energy is stored as **fat** (can lead to **obesity**).
- Energy from food: **glucose** used in respiration; glucose stored as **glycogen** in muscles and liver.
- Hydration (water) for performers.

## Notes

### Nutrients

| Nutrient | Function | Sources | Used by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Carbohydrates** | Main source of **energy** (broken down to glucose); stored as glycogen | Pasta, rice, bread, potatoes, fruit, cereals | Endurance athletes (e.g. marathon, cycling) |
| **Fats** | **Energy store** (long-term energy source at lower intensity), insulation, protects organs | Oils, butter, nuts, oily fish, cheese | Long, low-intensity exercise |
| **Proteins** | **Growth and repair** of muscles and tissues | Meat, fish, eggs, beans, dairy, tofu | Strength athletes, for muscle repair after training |
| **Water** | Prevents **dehydration**; carries nutrients, regulates temperature (sweat), part of blood plasma | Drinks, fruit, vegetables | All performers |

(Vitamins, minerals and fibre help the body work well, but the Cambridge list only names carbohydrates, fats, proteins and water. If a question mentions fibre: it helps digestion.)

### Balanced diet

A **balanced diet** contains the right amounts of all the nutrients (and enough water, vitamins, minerals and fibre) to meet the body's needs. For a healthy performer, roughly: **mostly carbohydrate**, a moderate amount of protein, and less fat (mostly unsaturated), with plenty of fruit and vegetables.

### Energy balance

- **Energy in** (food and drink) compared to **energy out** (basic body functions plus exercise).
- **Equal** → weight stays the same. **More in than out** → the extra energy is **stored as fat** and weight increases (risk of **obesity**). **More out than in** → weight loss, tiredness and poor performance.
- Active people need more energy. Males usually need more than females (more muscle, larger body size); teenagers need more than younger children (growth); active people need more than sedentary people.

### How energy is released

- Food is broken down to **glucose**, which muscle cells use in respiration to release energy.
- Some glucose is converted to **glycogen** and stored in muscles and the liver as an energy reserve. When you exercise, glycogen is changed back into glucose.
- **Carbohydrate loading** (high-carbohydrate meals before an endurance event) increases glycogen stores, delaying fatigue.

### Diet for performance

| Performer | Diet advice |
|---|---|
| Marathon runner | High carbohydrate (pasta) before the race; keep hydrated; carbohydrate drinks during |
| Weightlifter / rugby player | Higher protein for muscle growth and repair; enough carbohydrate for energy |
| Gymnast (weight control) | Lower in fat/energy but still balanced so energy is not too low |
| All performers | Eat a meal 2–3 hours before; a snack with carbohydrate and protein after exercise to recover |

### Hydration

Water is lost through **sweat**, breath and urine. **Dehydration** reduces blood volume, raises heart rate, causes fatigue, headaches, cramp, dizziness and reduces concentration. So performers drink water before, during and after exercise, and more in hot weather or long events. Sports drinks replace fluid and carbohydrate in long events.

## Key terms and definitions

- **Balanced diet:** a diet that gives the right proportions of all nutrients the body needs.
- **Carbohydrate:** the body's main source of energy, broken down to glucose.
- **Protein:** a nutrient needed for growth and repair of tissues.
- **Fat:** a nutrient used as an energy store, for insulation and protection.
- **Glycogen:** the stored form of glucose, kept in the muscles and liver.
- **Energy balance:** the relationship between energy taken in (food) and energy used (activity).
- **Obesity:** a health condition of excess body fat caused by taking in more energy than is used.
- **Dehydration:** loss of too much water from the body, reducing performance.

## Worked examples

**1. Explain why a marathon runner eats a high-carbohydrate meal the night before a race. [3]**

*Model answer:* Carbohydrate is broken down to glucose, the body's main energy source [1]. Extra glucose is stored as glycogen in the muscles and liver [1]. This provides a larger energy store so the runner can continue aerobic respiration for longer and delay fatigue [1].

**2. Explain what happens to energy that is taken in but not used. [2]**

*Model answer:* The unused energy is stored as fat [1], which over time can lead to weight gain and obesity [1].

**3. Compare the energy needs of a sedentary adult and an active teenager. [2]**

*Model answer:* The active teenager needs more energy because they use more for exercise and for growth [1], while the sedentary adult uses less so needs fewer calories (otherwise energy is stored as fat) [1].

## Common mistakes

- Saying "protein gives energy" as the main role (its key function is growth and repair).
- Saying "fats are bad": they are a necessary energy store and insulation.
- Writing glucose and glycogen as the same thing.
- Forgetting the effect on performance: always link to a sport (e.g. dehydration slows a runner).
- Mixing up water's roles: it prevents dehydration and controls body temperature through sweating, but it does not give energy.
- Naming a food but not the nutrient (e.g. "pasta" instead of "carbohydrate").
