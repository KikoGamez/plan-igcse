# Sport psychology: skill, goals, guidance and feedback

> **In the exam:** Appears on the theory papers as short recall (1–2 marks: define skill, classify a skill, name a type of guidance or feedback), plus 3–6 mark questions where you place a skill on a continuum with a reason, set SMART goals for a performer, or explain which guidance/feedback a coach should use. Always use the sport given in the question. Check with your teacher which paper this sits on.

## What you need to know

- **Skill** and how it differs from **ability**; **skill classification** using continua: open–closed, simple–complex, gross–fine, self-paced–externally paced, low–high organisation.
- **Types of practice**: massed, distributed, fixed, variable.
- **Goal setting**: **SMART** targets, and why they improve motivation and performance.
- **Types of guidance**: visual, verbal, manual, mechanical.
- **Types of feedback**: intrinsic, extrinsic, positive, negative, knowledge of results and of performance.
- **Motivation** (intrinsic and extrinsic), **arousal** and the **inverted-U theory**, and **mental preparation** (imagery, mental rehearsal, selective attention).

## Notes

### What is skill?

A **skill** is a learned, consistent ability to bring about a planned outcome with maximum certainty and minimum effort or time. Skills are **learned** (ability is inherited). Features of skilful play: **efficient, fluent, accurate, consistent, aesthetic** and goal-directed.

### Classifying skills: continua

Skills are not simply one or the other; they sit on a **continuum**. Write the position and give a reason related to the skill.

| Continuum | One end | Other end |
|---|---|---|
| **Open–closed** | **Open:** environment unpredictable, you must adapt (a pass in football) | **Closed:** environment stable and predictable (a golf putt, a gymnastics vault, a free throw) |
| **Simple–complex** | **Simple:** few decisions or parts (a sprint start) | **Complex:** many decisions, lots of information (a tactical pass in netball) |
| **Gross–fine** | **Gross:** large muscle groups (a shot put, a sprint) | **Fine:** small, precise movements (a snooker shot, darts throw) |
| **Self-paced–externally paced** | **Self-paced:** performer controls the timing (a tennis serve, a penalty kick) | **Externally paced:** timing set by others or the environment (returning a serve, a tackle) |
| **Low–high organisation** | **Low:** sub-routines easily separated (a swimming stroke) | **High:** parts closely linked, cannot easily be separated (a golf swing, a cartwheel) |

Example: a **tennis serve** is closed (the performer decides when and how), self-paced, fine/gross mix, and fairly high in organisation.

### Types of practice

| Practice | Meaning | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Massed** | Practice with no or very short rests | Simple skills, fit and motivated performers, experts |
| **Distributed** | Practice with rest or changes of activity between | Complex or dangerous skills, beginners, tiring tasks |
| **Fixed** | Repeating the same skill in the same conditions | Closed skills (a serve, a free throw) |
| **Variable** | Practising a skill in changing conditions | Open skills (passing against defenders) |

### Goal setting: SMART targets

Goals give direction, raise motivation, increase effort and show progress. Short-term goals build towards long-term goals. A SMART target is:

- **S**pecific: exactly what you will do (e.g. “improve my 1500 m time”, not “get fitter”).
- **M**easurable: has a number so you can check it (e.g. 20 seconds faster).
- **A**chievable: realistic for your ability.
- **R**ecorded (or **R**ealistic): written down so you can track progress. Some versions use “Realistic”; accept both but be ready for either.
- **T**ime-bound: a deadline (e.g. within 8 weeks).

Example: “I will reduce my 100 m time from 14.5 s to 14.0 s within 8 weeks by training three times a week.”

Benefits: increase motivation, focus attention, improve confidence when goals are reached, help plan training. If goals are too hard they can demotivate; too easy and they give no challenge.

### Guidance

| Type | How it is given | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Visual** | Demonstrations, videos, diagrams, coaching boards | Beginners learning a movement; shows what the skill looks like |
| **Verbal** | Spoken instructions or comments | Experts (short, clear cues) or to add to a demonstration; keep it short for beginners |
| **Manual** | Coach physically moves the performer’s body | Beginners, gymnasts (spotting), dangerous skills; builds confidence |
| **Mechanical** | Equipment helps (floats, harness, ball machine, trampoline belt) | Dangerous skills, or to build confidence and technique |

Beginners need clear visual guidance and limited verbal information; experts can handle more detail.

### Feedback

Feedback is information about how a performance went.

- **Intrinsic:** from the performer’s own senses (how the shot felt).
- **Extrinsic:** from outside: coach, spectators, video, scoreboard.
- **Positive:** tells you what was correct, building confidence and motivation.
- **Negative:** tells you what was wrong so you can correct it. Best when it also says how to improve.
- **Knowledge of results (KR):** the outcome (the ball went in, time was 12.1 s).
- **Knowledge of performance (KP):** how the skill was done (the elbow dropped during the shot).

Good feedback is quick, specific and balanced. Beginners need positive and simple feedback; experts can use detailed negative feedback and mostly rely on intrinsic.

### Motivation

- **Intrinsic:** from within (enjoyment, satisfaction, personal challenge).
- **Extrinsic:** from outside: **tangible** (trophies, money, medals) and **intangible** (praise, status, applause).
- Extrinsic rewards can raise motivation, but intrinsic motivation is stronger and lasts longer.

### Arousal

**Arousal** is the level of alertness and readiness to perform, from deep sleep to extreme excitement. The **inverted-U theory**: performance rises as arousal increases up to an **optimum level**, then falls if arousal gets too high (over-arousal, anxiety).

- **Low arousal** suits fine, precise or complex skills (snooker, golf putt, penalties) so performers must stay calm.
- **High arousal** suits gross or simple skills needing strength and power (weightlifting, rugby tackle, shot put).
- Too little arousal: unfocused, slow. Too much: nervous, muscle tension, mistakes.
- **Controlling arousal:** deep breathing, relaxation, positive self-talk; or a team talk/loud music to raise it.

### Mental preparation

- **Mental rehearsal / imagery:** picturing yourself performing the skill perfectly, using all senses.
- **Selective attention:** focusing on the important cues and ignoring distractions (crowd, noise).
- **Positive self-talk:** encouraging yourself to build confidence.
- **Relaxation / deep breathing:** lowers anxiety before pressure moments.

## Key terms and definitions

- **Skill:** a learned ability to bring about the result you want with maximum certainty and minimum outlay of energy or time.
- **Ability:** an inherited, stable trait that underlies skills.
- **Open skill:** a skill affected by the environment, and which has to be adapted.
- **Closed skill:** a skill unaffected by the environment, performed the same way each time.
- **SMART targets:** specific, measurable, achievable, recorded (or realistic), time-bound.
- **Guidance:** help given to a performer to learn or improve a skill.
- **Feedback:** information the performer receives about a performance.
- **Intrinsic motivation:** drive that comes from within the performer.
- **Extrinsic motivation:** drive from external rewards.
- **Arousal:** the physical and mental readiness to perform.
- **Mental rehearsal:** going through a performance in your head.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** Classify a golf putt on the open–closed continuum and give a reason. [2]

*Model answer:* It is a **closed** skill (1), because the environment is stable and the performer controls what happens: the ball is stationary and the action is the same each time (1).

**Example 2.** A coach wants to improve a Year 10 student’s 800 m time (currently 3 min 10 s). Write a SMART target. [3]

*Model answer:* “I will lower my 800 m time from 3:10 to 3:00 (specific, measurable) (1) by training three times a week (achievable) (1) within 10 weeks, recording times in a log (time-bound / recorded) (1).”

**Example 3.** Explain why manual guidance is useful for a beginner learning a back somersault. [2]

*Model answer:* The coach physically supports the performer (1), giving confidence and safety while they feel the correct movement (1).

## Common mistakes

- Treating skills as only open **or** closed. Show you know it is a continuum, and **give a reason**.
- Writing “achievable” goals that are not measurable: always include a number and a date.
- Mixing up **guidance** (before/during practice) and **feedback** (after).
- Calling a coach’s comment intrinsic. Intrinsic comes from **the performer’s own senses**.
- Saying more arousal is always better. Link the right level to the skill (fine skills need lower arousal).
- Confusing **KR** (result) and **KP** (technique).
- Saying “motivated” without saying whether it is intrinsic or extrinsic.
