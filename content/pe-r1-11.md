## Notes

### Skill and ability

- **Skill:** a learned action with a specific outcome, which can be improved with practice (e.g. a tennis serve).
- **Ability:** an **inherited**, natural trait that supports the learning of many skills (e.g. coordination, balance, speed).
- **Factors affecting skill level:** age/maturity, culture, motivation, anxiety, arousal, facilities, environment, teaching and coaching.
- **Skilled performance is:** fluent, aesthetically pleasing, consistent, accurate, goal-directed, coordinated.

### Skill classification continua

| Continuum | One end | Other end |
|---|---|---|
| **Basic ↔ complex** | **Basic:** simple, few decisions, little information to process (running) | **Complex:** many decisions, lots of information (a gymnastics vault with a twist) |
| **Fine ↔ gross** | **Fine:** small, precise movements (darts, a snooker shot) | **Gross:** large muscle movements (a sprint, a shot put) | |
| **Open ↔ closed** | **Open:** environment unpredictable; decisions needed (a pass in hockey, a tackle) | **Closed:** environment predictable; same movement each time (a golf drive, a free throw) |

To "justify" a placing give a reason: "A hockey pass is **open** because defenders and team-mates move so the player must decide where and when to pass."

### Information processing

**Input → Decision-making → Output → Feedback** (and back to input).

| Stage | Role | Example (tennis return) |
|---|---|---|
| **Input** | Information from the environment through the senses | See the ball and the opponent's position |
| **Decision-making** | Brain uses memory to select a response | Decide to play a forehand cross-court |
| **Output** | Muscles carry out the movement | Hit the shot |
| **Feedback** | Information about the performance (from senses or others) | Feel the shot, see it land out |

- **Short-term memory:** holds information for a short time (seconds); limited capacity. **Long-term memory:** stores learned skills and information for a long time; practice moves information here.
- **Limited channel capacity / single-channel hypothesis:** the brain can deal with only **one** decision at a time, so a second stimulus must wait (why a "dummy" works: the defender processes the fake first).

### Stages of learning

| Stage | Performer |
|---|---|
| **Cognitive** (beginner) | Has to think about each part; many mistakes; needs clear instructions, demonstrations and lots of feedback; movement jerky |
| **Associative** (practising) | Mistakes reduce; movements smoother; can use feedback; practises to refine, can start to think about tactics |
| **Autonomous** (expert) | Skill is automatic and consistent; can think about tactics/opponents; can detect own errors |

### Feedback

| Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Intrinsic** | Comes from the performer's own senses ("feel") | Feeling the ball leave the racket well |
| **Extrinsic** | Comes from outside: coach, video, scoreboard | Coach says "keep your elbow up" |
| **Knowledge of performance (KP)** | Information about **how** the movement was done | "Your knee was straight on the landing" |
| **Knowledge of results (KR)** | Information about the **outcome** | "The ball went in", the time on the stopwatch |

**Link to stages:** cognitive performers need extrinsic feedback and KR (simple, positive); autonomous performers use intrinsic feedback and KP. Feedback is important because it helps correct errors, motivates the performer and shows progress.

### Guidance

| Type | Meaning | Example | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Visual** | Seeing the skill | Demonstration, video, diagrams | Beginners |
| **Verbal** | Being told | Coaching points, instructions | All stages, but keep it brief for beginners |
| **Manual / mechanical** | Physical support or aids | Coach holds the arm; floats/armbands, harness in trampolining | Beginners and dangerous skills (safety) |

### Goal setting: SMARTER

**S**pecific, **M**easurable, **A**greed (with coach), **R**ealistic, **T**ime-phased (deadline), **E**xciting, **R**ecorded. Example: "Improve my 100 m freestyle time from 1:20 to 1:15 in 8 weeks, agreed with my coach, recorded in a training diary." Goals increase motivation and can **control anxiety** by focusing on the task rather than the outcome.

### Motivation

- **Intrinsic:** from within (enjoyment, pride, personal satisfaction). Lasts longer.
- **Extrinsic:** from outside: **tangible** (trophies, money) or **intangible** (praise, applause). Can boost effort but may stop if the reward is removed; too much can reduce enjoyment.

### Arousal and the inverted-U theory

**Arousal** = how alert and ready the performer is (a physical and mental state of readiness from very low to very high).

**Inverted-U (Yerkes–Dodson law):** performance increases as arousal rises up to an **optimal point**, then falls as arousal gets too high. Draw a hill shape with arousal on the x-axis (low → high) and performance on the y-axis (low → high); the peak is the optimum.

- **Underarousal:** not ready, bored, slow reactions (e.g. a goalkeeper not concentrating).
- **Overarousal:** too anxious, muscles tense, poor decisions, mistakes.
- **Optimal arousal depends on the skill:** **fine** skills (snooker, archery) need **lower** arousal; **gross** skills (rugby tackle, weight lift) need **higher** arousal.

### Anxiety

| Type | Meaning | Symptoms |
|---|---|---|
| **Cognitive** | Mental worry | Negative thoughts, poor concentration, fear of failure |
| **Somatic** | Physical effects | Sweating, fast heart rate, butterflies, tense muscles |

**Causes:** importance of the event, fear of failure, crowd/audience, opponent, lack of confidence or experience, pressure from others.

**Relaxation techniques** (combine them): **mental rehearsal** (go through the skill in your head), **visualisation** (picture a successful performance), **deep breathing**. They help by increasing concentration, controlling breathing and **reducing heart rate**.

### Personality

- **Introvert:** quiet, reserved, prefers to be alone or in small groups, needs low stimulation; often prefers individual/precision activities (archery, long-distance running, gymnastics).
- **Extrovert:** outgoing, sociable, seeks excitement, enjoys large groups; often prefers team/contact sports (rugby, football, basketball).

## Key terms and definitions

- **Skill:** a learned action with a specific outcome that can be improved with practice.
- **Ability:** an inherited, stable trait that determines potential to learn skills.
- **Open skill:** a skill carried out in an unpredictable environment.
- **Closed skill:** a skill carried out in a predictable environment.
- **Intrinsic feedback:** feedback from the performer's own senses.
- **Extrinsic feedback:** feedback from an external source such as a coach.
- **Arousal:** the physical and mental state of alertness/readiness of a performer.
- **Cognitive anxiety:** the mental (thinking) part of anxiety: worry.
- **Somatic anxiety:** the physical part of anxiety: sweating, fast heart rate.
- **Motivation:** the drive to do something.

## Worked examples

**1. Place a netball shot on the open–closed continuum and justify your choice. [3]**

*Model answer:* A shot from the goal circle is towards the **closed** end [1] because the performer is taking the shot in a fairly stable environment with the same technique each time [1], but a defender can move so it is not at the extreme end (it is somewhat open) [1].
*Tip: either end can be accepted if justified, but name the end and give a reason.*

**2. Describe the characteristics of a performer in the cognitive stage of learning. [3]**

*Model answer:* The performer has to think about every part of the skill [1], makes many errors and movements are jerky [1], needs clear demonstrations and extrinsic feedback from a coach [1].

**3. Explain the inverted-U theory using an example. [4]**

*Model answer:* As arousal increases, performance improves up to an optimum point [1]. After this point, too much arousal (overarousal) causes performance to fall [1]. Too little arousal (underarousal) also leads to poor performance [1]. The optimal level depends on the skill: a fine skill like a snooker shot needs low arousal, a gross skill such as a rugby tackle needs higher [1].

## Common mistakes

- Defining ability as "being good at something". It is **inherited**.
- Saying a skill is "open" because it is outdoors. Open = unpredictable environment and decisions.
- Mixing up intrinsic/extrinsic motivation and intrinsic/extrinsic feedback.
- Mixing up KP (how) and KR (what happened).
- Writing "SMART" only: the Cambridge syllabus uses **SMARTER**.
- Drawing the inverted-U without labelling axes (arousal on x, performance on y) or without the optimum.
- Giving physical symptoms for cognitive anxiety (cognitive = thoughts, somatic = body).
