# Socio-cultural influences and data in sport

> **In the exam:** Appears on the theory papers as short answers (1–2 marks: name a factor affecting participation, define sponsorship), 3–6 mark “explain/discuss” questions (positive and negative effects of media or commercialisation, ethics and deviance) and data questions (read a table or graph, calculate a mean, percentage or range, describe a trend, 2–4 marks). Always give a **reason** and an **example**. Check with your teacher which paper this sits on.

## What you need to know

- **Factors affecting participation**: age, gender, disability, socio-economic group, ethnicity, access and facilities, time, cost, family, peers, education, role models, media.
- **Commercialisation**: sponsorship, media and the **golden triangle**; **advantages and disadvantages** for sport, performers, sponsors and spectators.
- **Ethics and deviance**: sportsmanship, gamesmanship, drugs, violence, hooliganism, cheating.
- Ways of **increasing participation** and the role of governing bodies.
- **Using data**: reading tables, bar charts and line graphs; calculating mean, range, percentages; describing patterns and trends; qualitative versus quantitative data; reliability and validity.

## Notes

### Participation: what affects whether people take part?

| Factor | Examples of effects |
|---|---|
| **Age** | Activity falls in the teenage years and adulthood; older people have less access to suitable sessions |
| **Gender** | Fewer girls and women take part; less media coverage, fewer role models, lower prize money, stereotypes; some sports seen as “male” |
| **Disability** | Fewer accessible facilities, specialist equipment may be expensive, lack of coaches; Paralympic and disability sport raises awareness |
| **Socio-economic group** | Cost of kit, membership and travel can exclude lower-income groups; leisure time varies |
| **Ethnicity and culture** | Religious dress, practice or beliefs may limit access; racism and lack of role models; some groups under-represented |
| **Access and facilities** | Distance to facilities, transport, and opening times; rural areas have fewer options |
| **Time** | Work, school and family commitments reduce free time |
| **Family and peers** | Active parents and friends encourage participation; lack of support discourages |
| **Education** | PE and extra-curricular clubs introduce sports; poor school experiences put people off |
| **Role models** | Visible successful athletes inspire participation (e.g. London 2012 effect) |
| **Media** | Coverage raises interest and awareness; but unbalanced coverage can reduce visibility of some sports and groups |

Exam tip: give the **group**, the **barrier** and the **reason**. “Girls may not take part because there are fewer female role models shown on television” scores better than “girls don’t like sport”.

### Increasing participation

- Provide **free or low-cost** access, more local facilities and transport.
- Offer a wider range of activities, women-only or disability sessions.
- Run **campaigns** (e.g. “This Girl Can”), use role models and schools programmes.
- Governing bodies provide coaching courses and funding.

### Commercialisation and the media

**Commercialisation** is when sport is treated as a business to make money. The **golden triangle** links **sport, sponsorship and media**: each depends on the others. Sport gives sponsors exposure; sponsors give sport money; media pays for broadcasting rights and gives sponsors an audience.

**Sponsorship** is financial or other support given by a business in return for advertising.

| For | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| **Sport / performers** | Money for facilities, training and prize money; allows full-time athletes; wider coverage | Pressure to win; performers may lose control (kick-off times, rule changes); sponsor can withdraw; may favour “popular” sports |
| **Sponsors** | Advertising, improved image, increased sales | Cost is high; bad behaviour by a sponsored athlete harms the brand |
| **Spectators** | Can watch more sport on TV, better stadiums and entertainment | Cost of subscriptions and tickets, over-commercial events, schedule changes |
| **Media** | Large audiences and advertising income | Overexposure, only major sports get coverage |

**Positive effects of media on sport:** raises awareness and participation, role models, provides income, educates (rules and tactics). **Negative effects:** too much coverage of a few sports, pressure on performers and their privacy, unrealistic body images, changes to rules and timings for TV.

### Ethics and deviance

- **Sportsmanship (fair play):** playing within the rules and spirit of the game, being respectful (e.g. shaking hands, kicking the ball out when an opponent is injured).
- **Gamesmanship:** bending the rules without breaking them, to gain an advantage (e.g. time-wasting, slowing the game when ahead).
- **Deviance:** behaviour that breaks the rules or norms. Types include **positive deviance** (overconformity, such as playing on while injured) and **negative deviance** (cheating, drug use, violence, bribery).
- **Drugs:** cheating and harmful (see PEDs topic).
- **Violence and hooliganism:** by players (reckless fouls) or by spectators (fighting, abuse); caused by high stakes, alcohol, rivalry and frustration. Controls: stewards, CCTV, bans, stadium rules, fines, red cards.
- **Match-fixing and bribery:** deliberately losing for money.

Why people cheat: money, status, pressure, win-at-all-costs attitude. Consequences: bans, fines, loss of reputation and sponsors.

### Data in sport

**Types of data:** **quantitative** (numbers: times, scores, heart rates) and **qualitative** (descriptions, opinions: how nervous you felt). **Primary** data is collected yourself; **secondary** data comes from others.

**Skills you must show:**
- **Mean** = total of the values ÷ number of values.
- **Range** = highest − lowest.
- **Percentage** = (part ÷ whole) × 100.
- **Percentage change** = (change ÷ original) × 100.
- **Reading graphs:** use the axes and units; quote values from the graph; describe the trend (rising, falling, levelling off) and any anomaly.
- **Describing a trend:** give figures: “Participation rose from 30% in 2015 to 45% in 2022, an increase of 15 percentage points.”

**Evaluating data:**
- **Reliable:** consistent results if repeated (same method, same conditions, large sample).
- **Valid:** measures what it is supposed to measure (e.g. a sit-and-reach test is valid for flexibility but not for strength).
- **Sample:** a bigger and more representative sample gives more reliable results.
- Check **units**, **scale** and the **source** (is it biased?).

Example table:

| Day | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Minutes exercised | 30 | 45 | 0 | 60 | 45 |

Mean = (30 + 45 + 0 + 60 + 45) ÷ 5 = 180 ÷ 5 = **36 minutes**. Range = 60 − 0 = **60 minutes**.

## Key terms and definitions

- **Participation:** taking part in sport or physical activity.
- **Commercialisation:** the process of running sport as a business to make a profit.
- **Sponsorship:** financial or other support given by a business in return for advertising.
- **Golden triangle:** the relationship between sport, sponsors and the media.
- **Sportsmanship:** playing fairly and respecting opponents and officials.
- **Gamesmanship:** using unsporting tactics that stay within the rules to gain an advantage.
- **Deviance:** behaviour that goes against the rules or accepted norms.
- **Hooliganism:** violent or destructive behaviour by spectators.
- **Quantitative data:** numerical data. **Qualitative data:** descriptive data.
- **Reliable:** gives consistent results when repeated. **Valid:** measures what it claims to measure.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** Explain why fewer teenage girls than boys take part in some sports. [3]

*Model answer:* There are fewer female role models shown by the media (1), so girls may see some sports as “for boys” (1). Peer pressure, body image concerns or fewer female-only sessions or teams in school may also reduce participation (1).

**Example 2.** Give one advantage and one disadvantage of sponsorship for an elite athlete. [2]

*Model answer:* Advantage: money allows full-time training / better equipment (1). Disadvantage: pressure to win and perform for the sponsor / may lose the sponsor if form falls (1).

**Example 3.** A table shows the number of students attending PE clubs each term: 20, 26, 32, 30. Calculate the mean and describe the trend. [3]

*Model answer:* Mean = (20 + 26 + 32 + 30) ÷ 4 = 108 ÷ 4 = **27** (2). Attendance rose from 20 to 32 then fell slightly to 30 (1).

## Common mistakes

- Giving one-word answers such as “money” or “cost”. Say **who** is affected and **why**.
- Describing the golden triangle without naming all three: **sport, sponsorship, media**.
- Writing only the positive or only the negative side in a “discuss” question.
- Confusing **gamesmanship** (legal, but unsporting) with **cheating** (breaking the rules).
- Forgetting **units** in data answers, or reading the wrong axis.
- Writing “the graph goes up” without figures. Always quote numbers.
- Using stereotypes. Base the answer on barriers such as access, role models and cost.
- Mixing up **reliable** and **valid**.
