# Social, cultural and ethical influences

> **In the exam:** Paper 1 (Theory, 1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks). Short and structured questions that often ask you to "describe", "explain" or "suggest": "Give two advantages of sponsorship to a performer" [2], "Describe one disadvantage of media coverage for the sport" [2], "Suggest two ways to increase participation among teenage girls" [2–4], "Explain the term gamesmanship" [2], "Give two benefits to a country of hosting the Olympic Games" [2], "Explain one positive and one negative impact of technology" [2–4]. Examples must come from the Cambridge activity list.

## What you need to know

- **Leisure, recreation, play, sport**; factors that influence leisure activities; growth in leisure.
- The **sports development pyramid**: foundation, participation, performance, elite.
- **Sponsorship** and **media**: types, advantages and disadvantages.
- **Global events** and being a host nation.
- **Professional v amateur**; **technology** in sport.
- Factors affecting **access** and **participation**; strategies to increase participation.
- **Sportsmanship and gamesmanship** (and drugs are covered in topic 8).

## Notes

### Leisure, recreation, play and sport

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Leisure time** | Free time when you are not working, studying or doing essential tasks |
| **(Physical) recreation** | Activity done in leisure time for enjoyment, e.g. a walk, a casual game of badminton |
| **Play** | Spontaneous, enjoyable, with few rules, for fun (e.g. children kicking a ball) |
| **Sport** | Organised, competitive physical activity with rules, often with winners/losers |

**Factors influencing what people do in leisure time:** age, interests, social circumstances (money, time), family and peer influences, facilities available, and where you live (geography, culture, tradition, e.g. skiing in mountain regions).

**Growth in leisure activities:** more leisure time (shorter working hours), advances in technology, improvements in healthcare, better health awareness, more leisure facilities, cheaper equipment, better travel and wider media coverage.

### Sports development pyramid

| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| **Elite** (top) | The best performers, competing internationally/professionally, e.g. Olympic athletes |
| **Performance** | Skilled performers in clubs/regional or national competitions, seeking to improve, often with coaching |
| **Participation** | People who take part regularly for enjoyment, fitness and social reasons, e.g. a local league |
| **Foundation** (base) | Basic skills learned in school PE or early years, the base for everything above |

A wide base of participation helps produce elite performers; success at the top inspires more people to join the foundation.

### Sponsorship

**Types:** financial support; clothing/footwear/equipment; provision of specialist facilities.

| Who | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| **Performer / team** | Money to train full-time, free equipment, fame, better facilities | Pressure to perform and wear/promote the brand; may lose the sponsor after poor results or scandal |
| **Sponsor** | Advertising and brand recognition, positive image, increased sales | Cost; negative publicity if the performer misbehaves or loses |
| **Sport / event** | Money for facilities, prizes, coaching and development; more publicity | Sponsor may influence the timing/rules; sponsors from unhealthy products may harm image |
| **Audience** | Cheaper tickets, better events | More advertising; some sponsors seen as unhealthy (fast food, gambling) |

### Media

**Types:** television, internet and social media, print (newspapers, magazines), radio.

| Who | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| **Performer** | Fame, role model, sponsorship, income | Loss of privacy, intense pressure, criticism and online abuse |
| **Sport / event** | More publicity, interest, funding (TV rights), new participants | Rules or timings may be changed to suit TV; coverage concentrated on popular sports so minority sports ignored |
| **Audience** | Can watch from home, expert analysis, replays, information, access to more sport | Can reduce live attendance, pay-per-view cost, may stay inactive watching rather than playing |

### Global events and hosting

Hosting events such as the Olympic Games or a World Cup: **advantages** include new stadia and training facilities, **home advantage**, national pride, more tourism, more jobs (employment), better transport and **infrastructure**, and a **legacy** (facilities and participation inspired for years). **Disadvantages** (useful for evaluation): very high cost, unused stadiums afterwards, security risks and disruption.

### Professional and amateur

| | Professional | Amateur |
|---|---|---|
| Traditional | Paid to take part; sport is their job | Takes part for enjoyment, not paid |
| Today | The line is **blurred**: professionals and amateurs both compete at the Olympic Games | Some amateurs receive sponsorship or funding |

### Technology in sport

| Use | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Decision-making by officials** | Hawk-Eye in tennis, VAR in football, TMO in rugby |
| **Recording time and distance** | Electronic timing and photo-finish, laser distance measurement in athletics |
| **Enhancing performance** | Aerodynamic helmets and bikes in cycling, lightweight kit, swimsuits |

**Positive:** more accurate decisions, fairer results, improved safety and performance, more interesting for spectators. **Negative:** can slow down the game, interrupts the flow, expensive (richer teams benefit), reduces the role of officials' judgement, gives an unfair advantage to those who can afford it.

### Access and participation

**Access factors:** age, gender, disability, social and cultural influences (e.g. religious dress rules, traditions).
**Participation factors:** access, discrimination, education, environment and climate, family, financial considerations, media coverage, role models, time and work commitments.

**Strategies to increase participation (the 3 Ps: promotion, provision, access):**
- **Promotion:** campaigns, role models, media coverage of under-represented groups (e.g. women's sport), taster days.
- **Provision:** build facilities, subsidised or free sessions, adapted equipment and disability sports, women-only sessions, coaching.
- **Access:** transport, ramps and accessible changing rooms, flexible opening hours, reduced costs.

### Sportsmanship and gamesmanship

- **Sportsmanship:** playing fairly, following the rules and showing respect for officials, opponents and team-mates, e.g. shaking hands, helping an injured opponent, kicking the ball out when someone is injured, accepting the umpire's decision.
- **Gamesmanship:** bending the rules (without actually breaking them) to gain an advantage, e.g. wasting time in football, "sledging" in cricket, delaying a serve in tennis, shouting during an opponent's putt.

## Key terms and definitions

- **Leisure time:** free time available once work and essential tasks are completed.
- **Recreation:** activity chosen in leisure time for enjoyment.
- **Sponsorship:** financial or other support given to a performer, team or event in return for advertising.
- **Role model:** a person whose behaviour inspires others.
- **Host nation:** the country that stages a major sporting event.
- **Professional:** a performer who is paid to take part in sport.
- **Amateur:** a performer who takes part for enjoyment and is not paid.
- **Sportsmanship:** fair play and respect for others in sport.
- **Gamesmanship:** using tactics (not necessarily illegal) to gain an unfair advantage.
- **Legacy:** the long-term benefits left after a major event.

## Worked examples

**1. Explain two advantages of sponsorship to a performer. [4]**

*Model answer:* Financial support means they can afford to train full-time and pay for coaching [1+1]. Free equipment/kit reduces costs and lets them use the best quality [1+1]. (Also: fame and recognition.)
*Each answer gets a point for the advantage and a point for the explanation.*

**2. Suggest two ways of increasing participation among teenage girls in sport. [4]**

*Model answer:* Promote female role models/media coverage of women's sport [1] so girls see it as normal and are inspired [1]. Provide girl-only sessions/more clubs at a time and price they can afford [1] which removes barriers such as lack of confidence and cost [1].

**3. Describe one positive and one negative effect of technology on spectators. [2]**

*Model answer:* Positive: replays and video decisions (e.g. VAR) mean fairer outcomes [1]. Negative: reviews can slow the game and reduce the excitement [1].

## Common mistakes

- Writing about the advantage to the wrong group: always check **who** the question asks about (performer, sponsor, sport, audience).
- Saying "media gives more money": say what type (TV rights, sponsorship).
- Confusing gamesmanship (bending rules) with cheating (breaking rules) or drug use.
- One-word answers like "facilities" for hosting: say what (new stadia and training venues).
- Describing professionals only as "better": it is about being **paid**.
- Giving only a barrier to participation and no solution when asked for strategies.
