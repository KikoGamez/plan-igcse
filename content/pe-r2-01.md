# Past paper · Paper 1 timed (1/2)

> **Goal:** Sit the first half of a real Cambridge IGCSE PE Paper 1 under exam conditions, to practise timing, command words and writing answers that earn marks.

## Before you start

- **Paper:** a Cambridge IGCSE Physical Education (0413) **Paper 1 Theory**, e.g. May/June 2024 Paper 12 or Oct/Nov 2023 Paper 12 (any recent series and variant works; use one you have not seen). Ask your teacher for papers and mark schemes, or use the past papers section of the Cambridge International website or your school's Cambridge support hub. Keep the **mark scheme closed** until the next session.
- Have ready: black pen, pencil, ruler, calculator, water, a watch or timer, and a blank sheet for rough working.
- Phone away. Sit at a table. This session is the **first half of the paper**; the next session finishes it.
- Set a timer for **30 minutes**.

## Exam format reminder

- **Paper 1 Theory:** 1 hour 45 minutes, **100 marks**, 50% of the IGCSE. The other 50% is coursework (four practical activities).
- Short-answer and structured questions; **answer every question**. Questions test knowledge (AO1) and applying it to physical activities (AO2), including analysis and evaluation.
- Topics: anatomy and physiology; health, fitness and training; skill acquisition and psychology; social, cultural and ethical influences.
- Use only activities from the Cambridge list as examples.
- **Timing:** about 1 minute per mark (about 63 minutes for 60 marks), leaving 15–20 minutes at the end to check.

## How to do it

1. **0–2 min:** Look at the paper: how many pages and questions, where the longer answers are. Write the time you will start each half (e.g. "half-way = question 10").
2. **2–32 min (timer on):** Work through the **first half of the paper in order**, about 1 minute per mark. If stuck for more than 90 seconds, put a ring round the question number and move on.
3. **32–40 min:** Return to ringed questions. If you still have no idea, write something: no marks are lost for a wrong answer.
4. **40–50 min:** Read your answers back. Add a second point where a 2-mark question has only one; check calculations; check labels on diagrams/graphs.
5. **50–60 min:** Break. Do not mark yet. Write down in your error log which topics felt weak (e.g. "joints, arousal"). Keep the paper for the next session.

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Read the command word.** *State/Give/Name/Identify* = short, no explanation. *Describe* = say what happens or what it is like. *Explain* = give a reason ("because..."). *Outline* = main points briefly. *Suggest* = apply your knowledge to the situation. *Compare* = similarities **and** differences. *Evaluate/Discuss* = advantages and disadvantages, then a conclusion.
- **Marks = points.** A 3-mark question needs three separate points. Do not repeat the same idea in different words.
- **Use the context.** If the question names a sport, your answer must use it ("a gymnast needs flexibility to ...").
- **Calculations:** show working (e.g. 220 − 16 = 204; 60% of 204 = 122). Add units (bpm, litres).
- **Use the term in the mark scheme style:** "range of movement", "extrinsic feedback", "aerobic respiration", "lactic acid".
- **Don't mix up similar terms:** stroke volume and cardiac output; intrinsic/extrinsic; cognitive/somatic; ligament and tendon.
- For **graph/data** questions: quote values from the table or graph.

## Quick-fire revision

- Functions of the skeleton: shape/support, muscle attachment, protection, red blood cell production.
- Synovial joint parts: synovial membrane, synovial fluid, capsule, cartilage, ligaments.
- Ball and socket (shoulder, hip) = large range of movement but less stable; hinge (elbow, knee) = movement in one direction, more stable.
- Antagonistic pairs: biceps/triceps, quadriceps/hamstrings, gastrocnemius/tibialis anterior.
- Cardiac output = stroke volume × heart rate.
- Aerobic: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water. Anaerobic: glucose → lactic acid.
- Long-term effects of exercise: hypertrophy of the heart, lower resting pulse (bradycardia), higher stroke volume.
- SPORT: Specificity, Progression, Overload, Reversibility, Tedium. FITT: Frequency, Intensity, Time, Type.
- Aerobic training zone: 60–80% of (220 − age).
- Fitness tests: MSFT/Cooper (endurance), sit and reach, vertical jump, 30 m sprint, hand grip, ruler drop.
- First-class lever: fulcrum in the middle; second-class: resistance in the middle; third-class: effort in the middle.
