# Past paper · Theory paper timed (1/2)

> **Goal:** Sit the first half of a full theory paper under real exam conditions, so you practise the timing, the command words and writing answers that earn marks.

## Before you start

- **Paper:** use a real **Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Physical Education (4PE1)** theory paper if you can. Ask your teacher for the mock paper, or download a past paper or sample assessment from the Pearson qualifications website (search “International GCSE Physical Education” and open the past papers section). Use the **mark scheme** for marking in the next session; do not look at it now.
- If there is more than one theory paper, do **Paper 1** now (or whichever the teacher assigns), and Paper 2 in a later mock.
- **Have ready:** black pen, pencil, ruler, calculator, a bottle of water. Phone away. A timer.
- Find a quiet table. No notes, no textbook, no music.
- Check the front page of the paper: total time and total marks. Work out your pace: **minutes ÷ marks**.

## Exam format reminder

- Written theory papers, made up of short answer, calculation and extended-answer questions on the whole course (body systems, movement, training, injury and drugs, health and diet, psychology, socio-cultural influences, using data).
- Command words matter: state, name, describe, explain, evaluate. Extended answers (4–6 marks) are marked on the **quality of explanation** and on applying knowledge to the context given.
- Some marks are for calculating and reading tables and graphs.
- Check the cover page of your paper for the exact timing and marks, because your school may use a shortened version for mocks.

## How to do it

1. **0–3 min** — Set up. Read the front page. Write the timing plan on scrap paper: about one minute per mark, and 5 minutes at the end to check.
2. **3–50 min** — Work through the paper **in order**, as far as the first half of the questions (or the first half of the marks if you are splitting a single paper). Underline command words and the sport named. Write answers in full sentences.
3. **50–57 min** — Check: calculation units, missing parts (a), (b), (c), and one-word answers that need a reason.
4. **57–60 min** — Mark in the margin the questions you guessed or skipped (a star). Stop and put down your pen. **Do not open the mark scheme.** You finish the paper next session.

If you complete the whole paper early, stop at the point your teacher set, and write the question number you reached.

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Read the command word:** *state / name / give* = short, no explanation. *Describe* = what happens. *Explain* = say **why** (use “because”, “so”, “which means”). *Evaluate / discuss* = both sides, then a judgement.
- **Marks tell you the length:** 1 mark = one point; 2 marks = two points or one point developed; 4–6 marks = several developed points.
- **Apply to the context.** If the question names a sport or performer (a 400 m runner, an archer), your answer must mention it. Generic answers lose marks.
- **Calculations:** show the working (max HR = 220 − age; BMI = kg ÷ m²; mean = total ÷ number). Units every time.
- **Use key terms exactly:** sprain (ligament) vs strain (muscle/tendon); sets vs reps; intrinsic vs extrinsic; reliable vs valid.
- **Graphs and tables:** quote figures from the data, with units; describe a trend then give an exact change.
- **Do not leave blanks.** For multiple choice, remove two options and guess.
- **Check the time:** if stuck on a question for more than its marks in minutes, move on.

## Checklist while marking

(You mark in session 3; for now, note these as you write.)

- Star any question where you guessed.
- Circle any term you could not remember (put it on the error log later).
- Note questions where you ran out of time.
- Did you answer every part of every question? Did every “explain” have a “because”?
- Did every calculation show working and units?

## Quick-fire revision

- Max HR = 220 − age; aerobic zone about 60–80%, anaerobic about 80–90%.
- SPORT: specificity, progressive overload, reversibility, tedium. FITT: frequency, intensity, time, type.
- Sprain = ligament; strain = muscle/tendon; PRICE for soft tissue.
- Warm-up: pulse raiser, stretch, skill practice. Cool-down: light exercise and stretch.
- Health = complete physical, mental and social well-being, not just no disease.
- BMI = kg ÷ m²; healthy range 18.5–24.9.
- Carbohydrate is stored as glycogen; protein grows and repairs; iron makes haemoglobin.
- Open skill = changing environment; closed = predictable. Gross = large muscles; fine = precise.
- SMART: specific, measurable, achievable, recorded/realistic, time-bound.
- Intrinsic feedback = own senses; extrinsic = from outside; KR = result, KP = technique.
- Golden triangle = sport, sponsorship, media.
- Mean = total ÷ number; range = highest − lowest.
