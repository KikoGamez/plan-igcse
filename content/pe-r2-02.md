# Past paper · Theory paper timed (2/2)

> **Goal:** Finish the paper you started, still under exam conditions, with special care over the long (4–6 mark) answers.

## Before you start

- **Paper:** the **same paper** you used in session 1 (Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Physical Education 4PE1 past paper or the one your teacher gave you). Continue from the question number you reached.
- **Have ready:** black pen, calculator, water, timer, and the question number where you stopped last time. Do **not** open the mark scheme.
- Quiet room, no notes, no phone.
- Do not look at the answers you wrote last time. Move forward.

## Exam format reminder

- The second half of the theory paper usually contains more extended answers and context questions, often on training, health, psychology, socio-cultural influences and using data, as well as the body-system questions.
- Extended answers (4–6 marks) are marked on how many developed, accurate points you give **linked to the performer or sport in the question**.
- Check the front page for total time and marks, and confirm how many minutes you have left in this sitting.

## How to do it

1. **0–3 min** — Set up. Write on scrap paper: the number of marks left and a minute-per-mark plan.
2. **3–50 min** — Complete the remaining questions in order. For each long answer spend 30 seconds planning: write three or four key terms from the topic, then write them out as sentences.
3. **50–57 min** — Check. Have you answered every part? Do all calculations have units and working? Do all “explain” answers include a reason?
4. **57–60 min** — Star any question you guessed. Put the whole paper together, name and date on the front. Do not mark yet: marking is the next session.

**Plan for a 6-mark answer:** point 1 (term + explanation + link to sport); point 2 (the same); point 3; point 4; short conclusion if the command word is *evaluate*.

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Read the command word:** *state / name / give* = short; *describe* = what happens; *explain* = why; *evaluate / discuss* = both sides plus a judgement.
- **Use the marks:** one distinct point per mark; do not repeat yourself.
- **For a “discuss” question** (e.g. effects of commercialisation): give two advantages, two disadvantages, and a short conclusion.
- **For a programme question:** aim, method, FITT, progressive overload, rest, tedium, link to the sport.
- **For drug questions:** name the drug, give the effect, link it to a sport, and give a health risk.
- **For data questions:** quote figures, give units, show the calculation, describe the trend.
- **For injury questions:** name the injury, tissue type, cause and treatment (PRICE).
- **Keep to time:** do not spend 10 minutes on a 2-mark question.

## Quick-fire revision

- Training methods: continuous, fartlek, interval, circuit, plyometrics, weights, flexibility.
- Weights: strength = heavy, few reps; endurance = light, many reps.
- Drugs: steroids (muscle), beta blockers (steady), diuretics (weight loss/masking), stimulants (alert), EPO (red blood cells), narcotics (hide pain).
- Cool-down removes lactic acid by keeping blood flowing.
- Sedentary lifestyle → obesity, CHD, type 2 diabetes, osteoporosis, low mood.
- Dehydration: thicker blood, higher heart rate, cramp, fatigue.
- Carbohydrate loading increases glycogen stores before endurance events.
- Massed = no rests; distributed = rests; fixed = same conditions; variable = changing.
- Manual guidance for safety; visual for beginners.
- Inverted-U: too much arousal harms fine skills.
- Gamesmanship bends rules; deviance breaks them.
- Reliable = consistent; valid = measures what it claims.
