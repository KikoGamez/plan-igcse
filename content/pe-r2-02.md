# Past paper · Paper 1 timed (2/2)

> **Goal:** Finish the same Cambridge IGCSE PE Paper 1 under timed conditions, then check your answers once before you mark them.

## Before you start

- **Paper:** the **same** Cambridge IGCSE Physical Education (0413) Paper 1 Theory as the previous session (e.g. May/June 2024 Paper 12, or whichever series and variant you used). Keep the **mark scheme closed**.
- Have ready: pens, pencil, calculator, ruler, water, timer.
- Set a timer for **50 minutes** for the second half. In the real exam you have 1 hour 45 minutes in total, so the second half should take about 50–55 minutes, with 10 minutes at the end for checking.
- Put your phone out of reach.

## Exam format reminder

- **Paper 1:** 1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks, 50% of the qualification; short and structured questions; answer all.
- Later questions often cover **skill acquisition and psychology** and **social, cultural and ethical influences** or longer structured questions on training and health. AO1 (knowledge) and AO2 (applying and evaluating) each earn half the marks.
- No question is optional: leave **no blanks**.

## How to do it

1. **0–3 min:** Start the timer. Read the next question carefully; underline command words and the sport/context.
2. **3–45 min:** Complete the remaining questions in order at about 1 minute per mark. Ring any you skip.
3. **45–50 min:** Finish ringed questions. Fill every blank with your best guess.
4. **50–55 min:** Check: command words, number of points against marks, units on calculations, spelling of key terms (e.g. "aerobic", "ligament", "hypertrophy").
5. **55–60 min:** Count what you completed. Write a short note: which topics were easy, which hard, and where time ran out. Do **not** mark yet.

## Exam technique for this paper

- **"Explain" questions:** use because / so / which means: point → reason. e.g. "Increases heart rate, **so** more oxygen is delivered to muscles."
- **"Describe a warm-up/training programme":** phases or FITT, each with a specific example.
- **"Evaluate":** at least one advantage and one disadvantage, then a short conclusion in the context of the performer.
- **Psychology:** name the type (e.g. intrinsic v extrinsic), define it, then apply it to a performer.
- **Social/ethical:** write about the right group (performer, sponsor, sport, spectators) and give advantages **and** disadvantages when asked.
- **Don't repeat the question** in your answer: start with the point.
- **Don't use brand-name examples** unless a context requires them; use activities from the Cambridge list.
- If a question has a table/graph, quote data and use units.

## Checklist while marking

(For the next session.) You will mark each answer against the mark scheme: tick each mark point, write the missing point in a different colour, and note the reason you lost the mark.

## Quick-fire revision

- WHO health: complete physical, mental and social well-being, not merely the absence of disease or infirmity.
- Fitness: the ability to cope with the demands of the environment.
- Warm-up: pulse raiser, stretching, skill-related. Cool-down: gradual decrease in pulse, stretching.
- RICE: Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation.
- PEDs: steroids (muscle), beta blockers (anxiety), stimulants (alertness), diuretics (weight loss).
- Stages of learning: cognitive, associative, autonomous.
- Feedback: intrinsic, extrinsic, KP, KR. Guidance: visual, verbal, manual/mechanical.
- Inverted-U: performance peaks at optimum arousal; fine skills need lower arousal.
- SMARTER: Specific, Measurable, Agreed, Realistic, Time-phased, Exciting, Recorded.
- Pyramid: foundation, participation, performance, elite.
- Hosting an event: facilities, tourism, jobs, pride, legacy.
- Gamesmanship bends the rules; sportsmanship is fair play.
