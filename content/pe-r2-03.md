# Mark the paper with the mark scheme

> **Goal:** Mark your paper honestly with the official mark scheme, find where marks were lost, and write every error in your error log.

## Before you start

- **You need:** your completed paper, the official **mark scheme** (Pearson mark scheme for the same paper; if you do not have it, ask your teacher), a **green or red pen** (different from the pen you used in the exam), a notebook or document for the **error log**, and a calculator.
- Work through the paper in order. Do not rewrite your answers; add notes next to them.
- Set a timer: you have 60 minutes for marking and the error log.

## Exam format reminder

- Mark schemes list **marking points**; each correct point earns a mark, up to the maximum. Extended answers use **levels**: look at the descriptor, decide on the level, then choose the mark within it.
- Marks are given for correct **key terms**, and for answers that are linked to the context. Alternatives are listed with “/”; “do not accept” (DNA) lists wrong answers.
- Be strict: if the idea is there but the key term is missing or too vague (“it gets you fit”), do not award the mark unless the scheme says so.

## How to do it

1. **0–5 min** — Total up the marks available. Sort your pens and open the mark scheme.
2. **5–35 min** — Mark every question. For each lost mark write a tiny note: **K** = knowledge gap, **T** = used the wrong key term / too vague, **A** = did not apply to the sport, **C** = command word misread (e.g. described instead of explaining), **M** = maths or units, **R** = ran out of time.
3. **35–40 min** — Add up. Write the total and the percentage. Calculate marks lost by topic.
4. **40–55 min** — Fill in the **error log**: one line per lost mark (or group of marks): topic, question number, what I wrote, what the mark scheme wanted, the rule to remember.
5. **55–60 min** — Choose the **three** topics that lost the most marks; this is the plan for the next session.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Match the mark scheme’s wording:** note the key terms they used and learn them.
- **Learn from long answers:** read the top-level example and notice (1) the number of points, (2) the key terms, (3) the link to the sport.
- **If you lost marks for “not enough detail”**, practise adding a **reason** or **consequence** to each point.
- **If you lost marks on calculations**, redo the calculation now, and make sure you show working.
- **If you lost marks for misreading,** underline the command word in your head and ask “is this describe or explain?”.
- **If time was the problem,** work out which questions took the longest and set a time limit for them.

## Checklist while marking

- Be harsh and fair: give a mark only when the mark scheme point is clearly there.
- Did you spot pattern errors (e.g. mixing sprain and strain more than once)?
- Are there questions you got right by luck (starred and correct)? Add them to the log as “unsure”.
- Did you lose marks on definitions? Write the exact definition into the log.
- Did you lose marks on applying to the sport? Write a model answer in your own words.
- Total the marks lost for each topic: body systems, movement, training, injury/drugs, health, diet, psychology, socio-cultural, data.
- Count how many marks you lost from each cause (K/T/A/C/M/R). The biggest group tells you what to change first.

## Quick-fire revision

- Sprain = ligament; strain = muscle/tendon.
- PRICE: protection, rest, ice, compression, elevation.
- Max HR = 220 − age. Zones: aerobic 60–80%, anaerobic 80–90%.
- Principles: specificity, progressive overload, reversibility, tedium.
- Health = physical + mental + social well-being.
- BMI = kg ÷ m².
- Protein = growth and repair; carbohydrate = energy (glycogen); iron = haemoglobin; calcium = bones.
- Closed = stable; open = adapt. Self-paced = performer controls timing.
- SMART = specific, measurable, achievable, recorded, time-bound.
- Guidance: visual, verbal, manual, mechanical.
- Sportsmanship vs gamesmanship vs deviance.
- Mean = total ÷ number; percentage change = change ÷ original × 100.
