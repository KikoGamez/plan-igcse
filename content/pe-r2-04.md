# Weak topics and key terms

> **Goal:** Use your error log to fix the topics where you lost the most marks, then test yourself on key terms so you can write exact definitions in the exam.

## Before you start

- **Material:** your error log from the previous session, your marked Paper 1, your notes for the topics you lost most marks on, and the flashcards in the app (use the practice cards for those topics).
- Paper or index cards, a pen, a timer for 60 minutes.
- Choose your **three weakest topics** (most marks lost with reason code K or V). If marks were lost on application (A), add one practice question for each.

## Exam format reminder

- Paper 1 (1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks): short and structured questions on anatomy and physiology; health, fitness and training; skill acquisition and psychology; and social, cultural and ethical influences.
- A lot of marks come from **precise terms and definitions** and from **applying** them to a Cambridge-list activity.

## How to do it

1. **0–5 min:** Pick your three weakest topics from the log. Write them at the top of a page.
2. **5–35 min (10 minutes per topic):** For each topic:
   - Re-read the relevant notes for 4 minutes, focusing on the points you lost.
   - Close the notes and write everything you remember (a "brain dump") for 3 minutes.
   - Compare with the notes and add what you missed in a different colour for 3 minutes.
3. **35–50 min:** Flashcards. Use the app's cards for the same topics and test yourself. Put any card you get wrong in a "again" pile and repeat until the pile is empty. Write the definitions you struggle with on index cards.
4. **50–58 min:** Answer **two exam questions per weak topic** from the practice questions or the past paper (a 3-mark and a 4-mark). Mark with the mark scheme or the notes.
5. **58–60 min:** Update the error log: tick the ones you can now do. Choose the next topic for tomorrow.

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Definitions:** learn them word for word where the syllabus gives exact wording (WHO health, fitness definition, aerobic and anaerobic equations).
- **Use key terms** exactly: "synovial fluid", "hypertrophy", "bradycardia", "EPOC", "somatic anxiety", "limited channel capacity".
- When a topic gives a **list** (SPORT, FITT, SMARTER, RICE), learn it as a list and be able to apply each item.
- **Link the theory to a sport** every time.
- If you could not do a question, write a model answer for it after checking the mark scheme: writing it is practice.

## Checklist while marking

- Can I define each of the key terms I lost marks on?
- Can I list each acronym (SPORT, FITT, RICE, SMARTER) and explain each letter?
- Can I draw/label (inverted-U, information processing model, synovial joint, lever types)?
- Can I give one advantage and one disadvantage for each training method?
- Can I name each PED, its effect, and a sport?
- Can I explain real v perceived risk with examples?
- Did I answer the re-test questions with the right number of points?

## Quick-fire revision

- Skeleton functions: shape/support, movement (muscle attachment), protection, red blood cell production.
- Muscle fibres: slow twitch (fatigue-resistant, aerobic), fast twitch (more force, fatigue quickly).
- Isotonic = concentric/eccentric; isometric = no change in length.
- Long-term effects of exercise: heart hypertrophy, bradycardia, higher stroke volume, better lactic acid tolerance.
- VO2 max: maximum volume of oxygen the body can use per minute; factors: age, gender, genetics, lifestyle, training.
- Altitude training: more red blood cells, helps endurance.
- Balanced diet: carbohydrates (energy), fats (energy store), proteins (growth/repair), water.
- Skill: learned; ability: inherited. Open v closed; fine v gross; basic v complex.
- Arousal: inverted-U; fine skills lower arousal.
- Introvert (quiet, individual sports), extrovert (outgoing, team sports).
- Strategies to increase participation: promotion, provision, access.
- Technology: officials, timing, equipment: positive and negative.
