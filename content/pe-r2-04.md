# Weak topics and key terms

> **Goal:** Use your error log to fix your three weakest topics, and memorise the key terms that cost you marks, using flashcards and self-testing.

## Before you start

- **You need:** the error log from the previous session, your notes or the topic notes in this app (check the topic sessions for the topics you lost marks in), the **Practice** flashcards in the app for those topics, scrap paper, a timer.
- Pick the **three topics** with the most lost marks. Write them at the top of the page.
- If you have time, ask a parent or friend to quiz you from the flashcards.

## Exam format reminder

- Many of the marks lost in PE are **key-term and definition** marks, and **application** marks (linking to a sport). Both can be fixed in an hour of focused revision.
- The theory papers test the whole course: body systems and movement; fitness and training; injury and drugs; health, diet and lifestyle; psychology; socio-cultural influences; and data. Weak spots are usually spread across these areas.
- Check the cover page of your paper for exact timing and marks.

## How to do it

1. **0–5 min** — Read your error log. Group the errors by topic and by cause (knowledge gap, key term, application, command word, maths, time). Pick three topics.
2. **5–25 min** — **Topic 1 and 2** (10 minutes each): open the notes. Close them. Write from memory everything you can on the topic in a mind map or list. Open the notes again and add what you missed in a different colour.
3. **25–35 min** — **Topic 3** the same way (10 minutes).
4. **35–50 min** — **Flashcards.** Use the app’s cards for the three topics (or make cards from your log). Go through each; put cards you got wrong in a pile and repeat until the pile is gone. Aim for three clean runs.
5. **50–57 min** — **Redo** two questions you got wrong in the paper, without looking at the mark scheme. Compare with the mark scheme.
6. **57–60 min** — Write your **three golden rules** for the exam (one sentence each) from your most common errors, e.g. “Always link the answer to the sport named”.

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Key terms:** use the exact term from the specification (ligament, tendon, glycogen, haemoglobin, intrinsic, extrinsic, reliability, validity).
- **Explain questions:** point + because + link to the sport.
- **Compare questions:** use words such as “whereas” or “but”, and cover both sides.
- **Discuss questions:** advantages and disadvantages, then a judgement.
- **Calculations:** write the formula, substitute, answer with units.
- **Long answers:** spend 30 seconds on a plan with 3–4 key terms.
- **Checks in the last 5 minutes:** every part answered; every “explain” has a “because”; units given.

## Checklist while marking

When you test yourself:

- Say the answer aloud or write it before turning a card over.
- Mark honestly: only fully correct answers count.
- Note cards missed twice: those go on a “hard” list to retest tomorrow and again in three days.
- For a redone question, tick each mark-scheme point you included, and underline the ones you missed.
- Check your three golden rules appear in the answer you redo.

## Quick-fire revision

- Principles of training: SPORT; FITT; progressive overload means gradually harder.
- Interval = work and rest; fartlek = unplanned speed changes; circuit = stations; plyometrics = power.
- Max HR = 220 − age; zones 60–80% aerobic, 80–90% anaerobic.
- Warm-up: pulse raiser, stretch, skill practice; cool-down removes waste.
- Ligament = bone to bone (sprain); tendon = muscle to bone (strain).
- Drugs: effect + risk + sport (beta blockers: archery; EPO: endurance; diuretics: weight categories).
- Health: physical, mental and social; sedentary lifestyle effects.
- Nutrients: carbohydrate = energy; protein = repair; fat = energy store; iron and calcium roles.
- Hydration: dehydration thickens blood and raises heart rate.
- Skill classification continua with a reason each time.
- SMART targets with numbers and a date.
- Golden triangle; sportsmanship, gamesmanship, deviance; reliable vs valid.
