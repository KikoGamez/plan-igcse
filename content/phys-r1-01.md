# Motion and graphs

> **In the exam:** In Co-ordinated Sciences 0654 (Extended) Physics questions sit alongside Biology and Chemistry in every paper. Paper 2 (Multiple Choice, 45 min, 40 questions across all three sciences): expect 1–3 items on motion (reading distance–time or speed–time graphs, scalar vs vector, average speed). Paper 4 (Theory, 2 h, 120 marks): a motion part often opens a Physics question, typically 4–8 marks (speed–time graph, gradient = acceleration, area = distance, explaining a falling object with drag). Paper 6 (Alternative to Practical, 1 h 30 min, 60 marks): timing motion or oscillations (pendulums, rolling balls), measuring multiples, plotting graphs and finding gradients.

## What you need to know

Syllabus sections P1.1 and P1.2 (Core, then *Supplement* for Extended).

- Describe the use of rulers and measuring cylinders to find a length or a volume; measure time intervals with clocks and digital timers.
- Determine an average value for a small distance or a short interval of time by measuring multiples (including the period of oscillation of a pendulum).
- *Supplement:* a scalar has magnitude (size) only; a vector has magnitude and direction. Scalars: distance, speed, time, mass, energy, temperature. Vectors: force, weight, velocity, acceleration, gravitational field strength.
- Define speed as distance travelled per unit time; v = s ÷ t; average speed = total distance travelled ÷ total time taken.
- Sketch, plot and interpret distance–time and speed–time graphs; increasing speed = accelerating, decreasing speed = decelerating.
- Determine qualitatively from the shape of a distance–time or speed–time graph when an object is at rest, moving at constant speed, accelerating or decelerating.
- Calculate speed from the gradient of a straight-line section of a distance–time graph; calculate distance from the area under a speed–time graph (constant speed and constant acceleration).
- *Supplement:* define velocity as speed in a given direction; define acceleration as change in velocity per unit time, a = Δv ÷ Δt.
- *Supplement:* determine from data or the shape of a speed–time graph when an object has constant or changing acceleration; calculate acceleration from the gradient of a straight-line section of a speed–time graph.
- *Supplement:* deceleration is a negative acceleration (use this in calculations); the acceleration of free fall g near the Earth's surface is approximately constant, about 9.8 m/s².

**Not in 0654** (it is in Physics 0625): finding the resultant of two vectors at right angles. Skip it.

## Notes

### Equations (no formula sheet — learn these)

| Equation | Symbols and units |
|---|---|
| v = s ÷ t | v speed (m/s), s distance (m), t time (s) |
| average speed = total distance travelled ÷ total time taken | m/s |
| a = Δv ÷ Δt *(Supplement)* | a acceleration (m/s²), Δv change in velocity (m/s), Δt time taken (s) |
| Area of triangle = ½ × base × height; area of trapezium = ½(a + b) × h | for distance under speed–time graphs |

Unit conversion you must be fast at: 1 km/h = 1000 m ÷ 3600 s, so **÷ 3.6** to go from km/h to m/s (72 km/h = 20 m/s). The front cover of Papers 2 and 4 reminds you that g = 9.8 m/s² (weight of 1.0 kg = 9.8 N).

### Scalars and vectors (Supplement)

A **scalar** has magnitude only; a **vector** has magnitude and direction. Distance and speed are scalars; velocity is a vector. A car going round a roundabout at constant speed has a changing velocity (its direction changes), so it is accelerating.

### Distance–time graphs

| Shape | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Horizontal line | at rest |
| Straight sloping line | constant speed |
| Curve getting steeper | accelerating |
| Curve getting less steep | decelerating |

**Gradient = speed.** Use a straight-line section: speed = change in distance ÷ change in time.

### Speed–time graphs

| Shape | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Horizontal line | constant speed |
| Straight line sloping up | constant acceleration |
| Straight line sloping down | constant deceleration |
| Curve with gradient decreasing | changing (decreasing) acceleration *(Supplement)* |
| Line on the time axis | at rest |

- **Gradient = acceleration** (Supplement). A negative gradient is a deceleration (negative acceleration).
- **Area under the line = distance travelled.** Split into rectangles and triangles. Check the units on the axes (minutes? km/h?).

### Falling objects and drag (applying forces to motion)

- Without air resistance, objects near the Earth fall with the same constant acceleration g ≈ 9.8 m/s², whatever their mass.
- Drag (friction in a gas or liquid) increases with speed. A skydiver or a ball bearing in oil:
  1. At the start, speed is zero so drag is zero; resultant force = weight, so the object accelerates.
  2. As speed increases, drag increases; the resultant force (weight − drag) decreases, so the acceleration decreases (speed–time graph gets less steep — changing acceleration).
  3. When drag = weight, the resultant force is zero, so acceleration is zero: constant (terminal) speed.
- This uses P1.2 (changing acceleration) together with P1.5 (drag, resultant force). "Terminal velocity" is not a named 0654 objective, but explaining this sequence is a typical application question.

### Paper 6 skills for this topic

- **Measuring multiples:** time 20 oscillations of a pendulum and divide by 20; measure the thickness of 100 sheets of paper and divide by 100. This reduces the effect of reaction time / ruler precision on the result.
- **Timing:** stop-watches read to 0.01 s but human reaction time is about 0.1–0.2 s, so record times sensibly and repeat (average the repeats).
- **Pendulum period:** measure length to the centre of the bob; start timing as the bob passes the centre of the swing or use a fiducial marker; use small amplitude.
- **Rolling ball / trolley down a ramp:** distance with a metre rule, time with a stop-watch; repeat; keep the release point the same; release, don't push.
- **Tables:** column headings as quantity / unit (e.g. t / s); same number of decimal places in a column; no units in the body.
- **Graphs:** label axes with quantity and unit, scale using more than half the grid, plot to half a small square, single thin best-fit line, gradient from a large triangle shown on the graph.

## Worked examples

**1.** A car accelerates uniformly from 4.0 m/s to 18 m/s in 7.0 s. (a) Calculate its acceleration. [2] (b) Calculate the distance travelled in this time. [2]

- (a) a = Δv ÷ Δt = (18 − 4.0) ÷ 7.0 (C1) = **2.0 m/s²** (A1)
- (b) distance = area under speed–time graph = ½ × (4.0 + 18) × 7.0 (C1) = **77 m** (A1)

**2.** A runner runs 120 m in 15 s, rests for 10 s, then runs 80 m in 20 s. (a) Calculate the speed during the first section. [1] (b) Calculate the average speed for the whole journey. [2]

- (a) 120 ÷ 15 = **8.0 m/s** (B1)
- (b) total distance = 200 m, total time = 45 s (C1); average speed = 200 ÷ 45 = **4.4 m/s** (A1). (Include the 10 s rest in the total time.)

**3.** A skydiver jumps from a plane. Explain why her speed eventually becomes constant. [4]

- At first only weight acts / air resistance is zero, so she accelerates. (B1)
- As her speed increases, air resistance increases. (B1)
- The resultant force (weight − air resistance) decreases, so her acceleration decreases. (B1)
- When air resistance equals weight, resultant force is zero, so acceleration is zero and speed is constant. (B1)

**4.** A cyclist starts from rest and accelerates uniformly to 6.0 m/s in 4.0 s, rides at 6.0 m/s for 10 s, then decelerates uniformly to rest in 3.0 s. (a) Calculate the total distance travelled. [3] (b) Calculate the deceleration. [2]

- (a) areas: ½ × 4.0 × 6.0 = 12 m; 10 × 6.0 = 60 m; ½ × 3.0 × 6.0 = 9 m (C1 for any two areas correct, C1 for the third); total = **81 m** (A1)
- (b) a = (0 − 6.0) ÷ 3.0 (C1) = −2.0 m/s², so deceleration = **2.0 m/s²** (A1)

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting to convert units: minutes to seconds, km/h to m/s, cm to m.
- Leaving out the time spent at rest when calculating average speed.
- Reading the gradient of a speed–time graph as speed, or treating the area under a distance–time graph as meaningful (it is not).
- Writing "its speed becomes constant because forces are balanced" without explaining how drag changes with speed — examiners want the sequence: speed ↑ → drag ↑ → resultant force ↓ → acceleration ↓.
- Saying "the forces are balanced so it stops" — zero resultant force means constant velocity, not necessarily rest.
- Calling velocity or acceleration a scalar.
- Taking a gradient from a tiny triangle or from data points not on the line.

## Practice questions

1. Which list contains only vector quantities? A distance, force, mass B velocity, acceleration, weight C speed, weight, energy D temperature, velocity, time [1]
2. A train travelling at 25 m/s decelerates uniformly to rest in 50 s. (a) Calculate its acceleration. [2] (b) Calculate the distance it travels while stopping. [2]
3. A student times 20 complete oscillations of a pendulum as 32.4 s. (a) Calculate the period. [1] (b) Explain why she times 20 oscillations instead of one. [2]
4. A stone is dropped from rest and falls for 2.5 s. Air resistance is negligible. Calculate (a) its speed after 2.5 s, [2] (b) the distance fallen. [2]
5. A car travels at 72 km/h. Calculate the time it takes to travel 1.5 km. [3]
6. A car's speed increases from 12 m/s to 30 m/s with a constant acceleration of 1.5 m/s². Calculate (a) the time taken, [2] (b) the distance travelled in this time. [2]
7. Sketch a speed–time graph for a ball bearing released at the surface of a tall tube of oil, and state what the gradient shows at the start and at the end. [3]

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. **B**
2. (a) a = (0 − 25) ÷ 50 = **−0.50 m/s²** (deceleration 0.50 m/s²). (b) area = ½ × 25 × 50 = **625 m** (accept 630 m).
3. (a) 32.4 ÷ 20 = **1.62 s**. (b) Reaction-time error is about the same for one or 20 oscillations, so dividing by 20 makes it a much smaller fraction of the measurement; more accurate period.
4. (a) v = g × t = 9.8 × 2.5 = **24.5 m/s** (accept 25 m/s). (b) area = ½ × 24.5 × 2.5 = **30.6 m** (accept 31 m).
5. 72 ÷ 3.6 = 20 m/s; 1.5 km = 1500 m; t = 1500 ÷ 20 = **75 s**.
6. (a) t = Δv ÷ a = (30 − 12) ÷ 1.5 = **12 s**. (b) area = ½ × (12 + 30) × 12 = **252 m** (accept 250 m).
7. Curve starting at origin, steep at first, gradient decreasing, levelling off to a horizontal line. Start: gradient is greatest (maximum acceleration, drag zero). End: gradient zero — acceleration zero, constant speed.
