# Mass, weight, density and forces

> **In the exam:** Paper 2: 3–5 items (mass vs weight, density and floating, resultant force, spring graphs). Paper 4: regularly a 6–10 mark question combining W = mg, F = ma and resultant forces, or a load–extension graph. Paper 6: density by displacement and the spring (load–extension) experiment are among the most common practical contexts.

## What you need to know

- Mass is a measure of the quantity of matter in an object at rest relative to the observer; weight is a gravitational force on an object that has mass.
- Gravitational field strength g is force per unit mass, g = W ÷ m; it is equivalent to the acceleration of free fall. Weights (and masses) may be compared using a balance.
- *Extended:* weight is the effect of a gravitational field on a mass.
- Density is mass per unit volume, ρ = m ÷ V; describe how to find the density of a liquid, a regular solid and an irregular solid that sinks (displacement); decide whether an object floats from density data. *Extended:* whether one liquid floats on another (liquids that do not mix).
- Forces may change the size and shape of an object; load–extension graphs for an elastic solid and the experiment.
- *Extended:* spring constant k = F ÷ x; limit of proportionality (elastic limit not required).
- Resultant of forces along the same straight line; an object stays at rest or at constant speed in a straight line unless a resultant force acts; a resultant force can change speed or direction.
- *Extended:* F = ma (force and acceleration are in the same direction).
- Friction between surfaces impedes motion and produces heating; drag in liquids and gases (air resistance).
- *Extended:* describe circular motion qualitatively (force perpendicular to the motion). F = mv²/r is **not** required.

## Notes

### Equations

| Equation | Symbols and units |
|---|---|
| g = W ÷ m (so W = mg) | W weight (N), m mass (kg), g gravitational field strength (N/kg) = 9.8 N/kg on Earth |
| ρ = m ÷ V | ρ density (kg/m³ or g/cm³), m mass (kg or g), V volume (m³ or cm³) |
| k = F ÷ x *(Extended)* | k spring constant (N/m or N/cm), F load/force (N), x extension (m or cm) |
| F = ma *(Extended)* | F resultant force (N), m mass (kg), a acceleration (m/s²) |
| Volume of block = l × w × h; volume of cylinder = πr²h | from the maths requirements |

Conversions: 1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³; 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³; 1 cm³ = 1 ml. Water ≈ 1.0 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³.

### Mass and weight

| Mass | Weight |
|---|---|
| Quantity of matter | Gravitational force on a mass |
| kg; scalar | N; vector (acts towards the centre of the planet) |
| Same everywhere | Changes with g (Moon g ≈ 1.6 N/kg) |
| Measured with a balance (compares weights in the same field) | Measured with a force meter (newton meter) |

g = 9.8 N/kg is the same number as the acceleration of free fall 9.8 m/s² — the two are equivalent.

### Density

- **Regular solid:** measure length, width, height with a ruler (or diameter with a ruler/calipers), calculate volume; mass on a balance; ρ = m ÷ V.
- **Irregular solid that sinks:** mass on a balance; volume by displacement — read the measuring cylinder before and after lowering the object in (V = V₂ − V₁), or use a displacement (Eureka) can and collect the overflow in a measuring cylinder.
- **Liquid:** mass of empty measuring cylinder, then with liquid; mass of liquid = difference; volume read from the cylinder.
- **Floating:** an object floats if its density is less than the liquid's density. *Extended:* a liquid with lower density floats on a denser liquid if they do not mix (oil floats on water).

### Forces and resultant force

- Forces along one line: add those in one direction, subtract those in the other. Resultant zero → object at rest **or** moving at constant velocity.
- A resultant force changes velocity: speed up, slow down, or change direction.
- Friction acts between surfaces, opposes motion and causes heating; drag acts in liquids and gases (air resistance) and increases with speed.
- **F = ma (Extended):** always use the **resultant** force. Acceleration is in the direction of the resultant force.

### Springs and load–extension graphs

- Extension x = stretched length − original (unstretched) length.
- Graph of load (or extension) against the other: straight line through the origin while extension is proportional to load (Hooke's law behaviour).
- *Extended:* **limit of proportionality** — the point beyond which the graph is no longer a straight line (load no longer proportional to extension). Spring constant k = F ÷ x = gradient of load–extension graph (load on y-axis). A stiffer spring has a larger k.

### Circular motion (Extended, qualitative only)

An object moving in a circle at constant speed has a changing velocity, so there must be a resultant force acting **perpendicular to its motion, towards the centre**. For a satellite this is gravity; for a ball on a string it is tension; for a car on a bend it is friction.
- If the force increases (mass and radius constant), the speed increases.
- If the force increases (mass and speed constant), the radius decreases.
- A bigger mass needs a bigger force to keep the same speed and radius.
- If the force is removed the object moves off in a straight line along the tangent.

### Paper 6 skills for this topic

- **Spring experiment:** measure original length with a metre rule held vertically; read at eye level to avoid parallax; add masses in equal steps (e.g. 100 g = 0.98 N ≈ 1.0 N); record length, calculate extension; check the spring returns to its original length; repeat while unloading. Safety: goggles, keep feet away / sand tray below.
- **Measuring cylinder:** read the bottom of the meniscus at eye level; choose a cylinder with small divisions that the object fits in.
- **Displacement:** lower the object gently on a thread so no water splashes out; remove air bubbles.
- Typical P6 tasks: calculate density with correct units and significant figures; comment on whether two results are equal "within the limits of experimental accuracy" (±10%); suggest improvements (repeat, use a smaller-division instrument).

## Key terms and definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Mass | A measure of the quantity of matter in an object at rest relative to the observer |
| Weight | The gravitational force on an object that has mass |
| Gravitational field strength | Force per unit mass |
| Density | Mass per unit volume |
| Spring constant | Force per unit extension |
| Limit of proportionality | The point beyond which extension is no longer proportional to load (the graph stops being a straight line) |
| Resultant force | The single force that has the same effect as all the forces acting on the object |
| Friction | Force between two surfaces that may impede motion and produce heating |

## Worked examples

**1.** A stone of mass 156 g is lowered into a measuring cylinder; the water level rises from 40.0 cm³ to 100.0 cm³. Calculate the density of the stone in g/cm³ and kg/m³. [3]

- V = 100.0 − 40.0 = 60.0 cm³ (C1)
- ρ = 156 ÷ 60.0 = **2.6 g/cm³** (A1) = **2600 kg/m³** (B1)

**2.** A spring is 4.0 cm long when unloaded and 10.0 cm long with a load of 3.0 N. (a) Calculate the spring constant. [2] (b) Calculate the length of the spring with a 4.5 N load, assuming the limit of proportionality is not exceeded. [2]

- (a) x = 10.0 − 4.0 = 6.0 cm (C1); k = 3.0 ÷ 6.0 = **0.50 N/cm** (= 50 N/m) (A1)
- (b) x = 4.5 ÷ 0.50 = 9.0 cm (C1); length = 4.0 + 9.0 = **13.0 cm** (A1)

**3.** A car of mass 1200 kg has a driving force of 3000 N; resistive forces total 600 N. Calculate its acceleration. [3]

- resultant force = 3000 − 600 = 2400 N (C1)
- a = F ÷ m = 2400 ÷ 1200 (C1) = **2.0 m/s²** (A1)

**4.** A ball on a string is whirled in a horizontal circle at constant speed. (a) Explain why the ball is accelerating. [2] (b) State what happens to the tension needed if the speed is increased with the radius unchanged. [1]

- (a) Its direction of motion / velocity is continuously changing (B1); so there is a resultant force (tension) towards the centre (B1).
- (b) Tension must increase. (B1)

## Common mistakes

- Giving weight in kg or mass in N. Weight is a force: newtons.
- Using the total length instead of the extension in k = F ÷ x.
- Using one force (e.g. the driving force) in F = ma instead of the **resultant** force.
- Mixing units in density: grams with m³. Convert first.
- Saying a constant-speed object has "no forces" — it has forces that are balanced.
- Stating that an object on a circular path is pushed outwards ("centrifugal force"). The resultant force is towards the centre.
- Confusing limit of proportionality with elastic limit (only limit of proportionality is required).

## Practice questions

1. An astronaut has a mass of 70 kg. Calculate her weight on Earth and on the Moon (g = 1.6 N/kg). State her mass on the Moon. [3]
2. A metal block measures 2.0 cm × 3.0 cm × 5.0 cm and has a mass of 81 g. Calculate its density. [2]
3. Liquid X has density 0.80 g/cm³ and liquid Y has density 1.0 g/cm³; they do not mix. A plastic bead has density 0.90 g/cm³. Describe where the bead ends up when both liquids and the bead are poured into a beaker. [2]
4. A box of mass 5.0 kg is pulled along the floor with a force of 30 N. Friction is 12 N. Calculate the acceleration. [3]
5. A lift of mass 800 kg is pulled upwards by a cable with tension 9000 N. Calculate (a) the weight of the lift, [1] (b) its acceleration. [3]
6. Describe how to find the density of an irregular stone that sinks in water. [4]
7. Describe how the load–extension graph identifies the limit of proportionality. [2]

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Earth: 70 × 9.8 = **686 N** (accept 690 N); Moon: 70 × 1.6 = **112 N** (accept 110 N); mass still **70 kg**.
2. V = 30 cm³; ρ = 81 ÷ 30 = **2.7 g/cm³**.
3. X (0.80) floats on Y (1.0) so X forms the top layer; the bead (0.90) sinks in X but floats on Y, so it rests **at the boundary between the two liquids**.
4. Resultant = 30 − 12 = 18 N; a = 18 ÷ 5.0 = **3.6 m/s²**.
5. (a) W = 800 × 9.8 = **7840 N**. (b) resultant = 9000 − 7840 = 1160 N upwards; a = 1160 ÷ 800 = **1.45 m/s²** (accept 1.5 m/s²) upwards.
6. Measure mass on a balance; partly fill a measuring cylinder and record volume; lower stone in on a thread, record new volume; volume of stone = difference; ρ = m ÷ V.
7. The graph is a straight line (through the origin) up to a point; the limit of proportionality is the point where the line starts to curve.

</details>
