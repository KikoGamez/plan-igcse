# Moments, momentum and pressure

> **In the exam:** Paper 2: 3–5 items (balancing beams, stability, pressure in liquids, conservation of momentum). Paper 4: moments and momentum each appear very often, typically 5–9 marks each (principle of moments with several forces, collisions, impulse, Δp = ρgΔh). Paper 6: balancing a metre rule on a pivot (principle of moments) is a classic context; also centre of gravity with a plumb line.

## What you need to know

- Moment of a force is a measure of its turning effect; moment = force × perpendicular distance from the pivot; everyday examples.
- Apply the principle of moments with one force each side of the pivot (including balancing a beam). *Extended:* more than one force each side.
- With no resultant force and no resultant moment an object is in equilibrium. *Extended:* describe an experiment to show there is no resultant moment on an object in equilibrium.
- State what is meant by centre of gravity; describe an experiment to find the centre of gravity of an irregular plane lamina; describe the effect of the position of the centre of gravity on stability.
- *Extended:* momentum p = mv; impulse = FΔt = Δ(mv); conservation of momentum in one dimension; resultant force = change in momentum per unit time, F = Δp ÷ Δt.
- Pressure = force per unit area, p = F ÷ A; how pressure varies with force and area in everyday examples.
- Pressure beneath a liquid surface increases with depth and density. *Extended:* Δp = ρgΔh.

## Notes

### Equations

| Equation | Symbols and units |
|---|---|
| moment = F × d | F force (N), d perpendicular distance from pivot (m or cm); moment in N m (or N cm) |
| Principle of moments: sum of clockwise moments = sum of anticlockwise moments (about the same pivot) | |
| p = mv *(Extended)* | p momentum (kg m/s), m mass (kg), v velocity (m/s) |
| impulse = FΔt = Δ(mv) *(Extended)* | impulse (N s), F force (N), Δt time (s) |
| F = Δp ÷ Δt *(Extended)* | resultant force (N) = change in momentum (kg m/s) ÷ time (s) |
| p = F ÷ A | p pressure (Pa = N/m², or N/cm²), F force (N), A area (m² or cm²) |
| Δp = ρgΔh *(Extended)* | Δp change in pressure (Pa), ρ density of liquid (kg/m³), g = 9.8 N/kg, Δh change in depth (m) |

Note: 1 N s = 1 kg m/s. 1 m² = 10 000 cm², so 1 N/cm² = 10 000 Pa.

### Moments

- Perpendicular distance: measured from the pivot at right angles to the line of action of the force. Longer spanner → bigger moment for the same force.
- Equilibrium: (1) no resultant force (upward forces = downward forces) and (2) no resultant moment.
- A uniform beam's weight acts at its centre — include it as a force when the pivot is not at the centre.
- The pivot itself pushes up with a force equal to the total downward force (if all others are vertical). Taking moments about the pivot removes this unknown force.
- *Extended experiment:* hang weights on both sides of a metre rule balanced on a pivot (or supported by force meters); adjust until it balances; record forces and distances; show Σ(F × d) clockwise = Σ(F × d) anticlockwise.

### Centre of gravity and stability

- **Centre of gravity:** the point through which the weight of an object appears to act.
- **Experiment (irregular lamina):** make three small holes near the edge; hang the lamina from a pin through one hole so it swings freely; hang a plumb line from the same pin; when still, mark the line on the lamina; repeat for the other holes; the centre of gravity is where the lines cross. (Three lines check accuracy.)
- **Stability:** an object topples when the vertical line through its centre of gravity falls outside its base. More stable: **low centre of gravity and wide base** (racing cars, bus test, Bunsen burner base).

### Momentum (Extended)

- Momentum is a vector: choose a positive direction; velocities the other way are negative.
- **Conservation of momentum:** in a collision or explosion, total momentum before = total momentum after (if no external resultant force).
- Explosions/recoil: total momentum before = 0, so the two parts move in opposite directions with equal-sized momenta.
- Kinetic energy is usually **not** conserved in collisions (some becomes internal/thermal and sound energy) — but momentum always is.
- **Impulse and safety:** for a given change in momentum, F = Δp ÷ Δt, so a longer collision time means a smaller force. Crumple zones, airbags, seat belts, helmets, crash mats and bending knees on landing all increase Δt.

### Pressure

- Large area → small pressure: snowshoes, wide tractor tyres, foundations. Small area → large pressure: knives, nails, stiletto heels.
- **Liquids:** pressure increases with depth and with density; it acts in all directions; it does not depend on the shape of the container. A dam is thicker at the bottom.
- *Extended:* Δp = ρgΔh gives the **extra** pressure due to the liquid. Total pressure on a diver = atmospheric pressure + ρgh (atmospheric pressure ≈ 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa, given if needed).

### Paper 6 skills for this topic

- **Balancing a metre rule:** find the balance point of the rule alone first (it may not be exactly 50.0 cm); read distances from the centre of each mass/load, not its edge; move loads until the rule is horizontal and balanced; repeat for several positions; plot e.g. load against 1/d or d against load and comment on proportionality.
- Sources of error: hard to judge exact balance; mass positions difficult to read; the masses' values may not be exact. Improvements: use a sharper pivot, read scale at eye level, repeat and average.
- **Centre of gravity:** use a plumb line, check the lamina swings freely, use three suspension points.

## Key terms and definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Moment of a force | Force × perpendicular distance from the pivot |
| Principle of moments | For an object in equilibrium, the sum of clockwise moments about a point = the sum of anticlockwise moments about the same point |
| Equilibrium | No resultant force and no resultant moment |
| Centre of gravity | The point through which the weight of an object (appears to) act |
| Momentum | Mass × velocity |
| Impulse | Force × time for which the force acts (= change in momentum) |
| Resultant force (momentum form) | The change in momentum per unit time |
| Pressure | Force per unit area |

## Worked examples

**1.** A uniform metre rule of weight 1.2 N is pivoted at its 30.0 cm mark. Calculate the load that must hang at the 5.0 cm mark to balance it. [3]

- Weight of rule acts at 50.0 cm, 20.0 cm from the pivot: clockwise moment = 1.2 × 20.0 = 24 N cm (C1)
- Load is 25.0 cm from the pivot: F × 25.0 = 24 (C1)
- F = **0.96 N** (A1)

**2.** Trolley A (2.0 kg) moving at 3.0 m/s collides with stationary trolley B (1.0 kg); they stick together. (a) Calculate their velocity after the collision. [3] (b) Show that kinetic energy is not conserved. [2]

- (a) momentum before = 2.0 × 3.0 = 6.0 kg m/s (C1); after: 3.0 × v = 6.0 (C1); v = **2.0 m/s** (A1)
- (b) KE before = ½ × 2.0 × 3.0² = 9.0 J; KE after = ½ × 3.0 × 2.0² = 6.0 J (B1); 6.0 J < 9.0 J so KE is not conserved (3.0 J transferred to thermal/sound) (B1)

**3.** A ball of mass 0.15 kg hits a wall at 20 m/s and rebounds at 15 m/s. Contact time is 0.050 s. Calculate (a) the impulse, [2] (b) the average force on the ball. [2]

- (a) Δp = 0.15 × 15 − 0.15 × (−20) = 0.15 × 35 (C1) = **5.25 N s** (A1)
- (b) F = Δp ÷ Δt = 5.25 ÷ 0.050 (C1) = **105 N** (A1)

**4.** A diver is 12 m below the surface of sea water (density 1030 kg/m³). Calculate the pressure due to the water. [2]

- Δp = ρgΔh = 1030 × 9.8 × 12 (C1) = **1.2 × 10⁵ Pa** (121 000 Pa) (A1)

## Common mistakes

- Using the distance along a slanting beam or from the end of the rule instead of the perpendicular distance from the pivot.
- Forgetting the weight of the beam itself when it is not pivoted at its centre.
- Ignoring the sign (direction) of velocity in rebound questions — change in velocity is 35 m/s, not 5 m/s.
- Assuming kinetic energy is conserved in collisions.
- Not converting cm² to m² (divide by 10 000) before calculating pressure in Pa.
- Explaining airbags as "reducing the momentum" — they increase the time taken for the change in momentum, so the force is smaller.
- Describing stability without mentioning the vertical line through the centre of gravity relative to the base.

## Practice questions

1. A child of weight 300 N sits 2.0 m from the pivot of a see-saw. Where must a child of weight 400 N sit to balance it? [2]
2. An elephant of weight 50 000 N stands on four feet, each of area 0.10 m². Calculate the pressure on the ground. [2]
3. A rifle of mass 4.0 kg fires a bullet of mass 0.010 kg at 400 m/s. Calculate the recoil velocity of the rifle. [3]
4. A car of mass 1000 kg travelling at 15 m/s is stopped in a crash in 0.30 s. (a) Calculate the average force. [2] (b) Explain how a crumple zone reduces injuries. [2]
5. Describe an experiment to find the centre of gravity of a thin irregular card. [4]
6. Explain why a bus with passengers on the top deck is less stable than when they sit downstairs. [2]
7. Calculate the pressure difference between the top and bottom of a water tank 2.5 m deep (density 1000 kg/m³). [2]

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. 300 × 2.0 = 400 × d; d = **1.5 m** on the other side.
2. A = 4 × 0.10 = 0.40 m²; p = 50 000 ÷ 0.40 = **125 000 Pa** (1.25 × 10⁵ Pa).
3. Momentum before = 0; 0.010 × 400 = 4.0 kg m/s; 4.0 × v = 4.0; v = **1.0 m/s** in the opposite direction to the bullet.
4. (a) Δp = 1000 × 15 = 15 000 kg m/s; F = 15 000 ÷ 0.30 = **50 000 N**. (b) It crumples so the car takes longer to stop / increases the collision time; same change in momentum so smaller force (on occupants).
5. Hang card from a pin through a hole near the edge so it swings freely; hang plumb line from the pin; mark the vertical line; repeat with two other holes; centre of gravity where lines cross.
6. The centre of gravity is higher; it tilts less before the vertical line through the centre of gravity passes outside the base (wheels), so it topples more easily.
7. Δp = 1000 × 9.8 × 2.5 = **24 500 Pa** (2.45 × 10⁴ Pa).
