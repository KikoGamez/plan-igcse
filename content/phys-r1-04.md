# Energy, work and power

> **In the exam:** Paper 2: 3–4 items (energy stores, efficiency, power, energy resources). Paper 4: nearly every paper has a question using Ek = ½mv² and ΔEp = mgΔh together (falling objects, roller coasters, ramps), plus efficiency/power; energy resources appear as describe/explain questions (4–6 marks). Paper 6: power or efficiency from measured data, sometimes ramps or bouncing balls.

## What you need to know

- Energy may be stored as kinetic, gravitational potential, chemical, elastic (strain), nuclear, electrostatic and internal (thermal).
- Energy is transferred between stores by forces (mechanical work done), electrical currents (electrical work done), heating, and by electromagnetic, sound and other waves.
- Conservation of energy, simple flow diagrams. *Extended:* complex, multi-stage examples and Sankey diagrams.
- *Extended:* Ek = ½mv²; ΔEp = mgΔh.
- Work done = energy transferred; W = Fd = ΔE.
- Energy resources: fossil fuels, biofuels, water (waves, tides, hydroelectric dams), geothermal, nuclear fuel, solar cells, solar panels, wind — including boiler, turbine and generator; advantages and disadvantages (renewability, availability, reliability, scale, environmental impact).
- Efficiency qualitatively. *Extended:* efficiency = useful energy output ÷ total energy input (× 100%) and the power version.
- *Extended:* the Sun's radiation is the main source of all our energy resources except geothermal, nuclear and tidal; energy is released by nuclear fusion in the Sun; research into fusion for large-scale electricity.
- Power is work done per unit time and energy transferred per unit time: P = W ÷ t, P = ΔE ÷ t.

## Notes

### Equations

| Equation | Symbols and units |
|---|---|
| Ek = ½mv² *(Extended)* | Ek kinetic energy (J), m mass (kg), v speed (m/s) |
| ΔEp = mgΔh *(Extended)* | ΔEp change in gravitational potential energy (J), g = 9.8 N/kg, Δh change in height (m) |
| W = Fd = ΔE | W work done (J), F force (N), d distance moved in the direction of the force (m), ΔE energy transferred (J) |
| P = W ÷ t and P = ΔE ÷ t | P power (W), t time (s) |
| efficiency = useful energy output ÷ total energy input (× 100%) *(Extended)* | no unit (or %) |
| efficiency = useful power output ÷ total power input (× 100%) *(Extended)* | no unit (or %) |

Prefixes: 1 kJ = 10³ J, 1 MJ = 10⁶ J; 1 kW = 10³ W, 1 MW = 10⁶ W; 1 J = 1 N m; 1 W = 1 J/s.

### Energy stores and transfers

Describe events as **store → transfer → store**. Examples:
- Ball falling: gravitational potential → kinetic (and some to internal/thermal of the air by air resistance).
- Battery-powered torch: chemical → (electrical work) → light (EM waves) + internal of surroundings.
- Car braking: kinetic → internal (thermal) of brakes and surroundings (work done by friction).
- Stretched catapult: elastic (strain) → kinetic.

**Conservation of energy:** energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred between stores. Total energy is constant; "wasted" energy usually ends up as internal (thermal) energy of the surroundings.

**Sankey diagrams (Extended):** arrow width proportional to energy; input on the left, useful output continues straight, wasted output bends away. Input = sum of outputs.

### Using Ek and Ep together (Extended)

If air resistance/friction is negligible, loss in Ep = gain in Ek, so mgΔh = ½mv² and v = √(2gΔh) — mass cancels. If friction acts, work done against friction = loss in Ep − gain in Ek.

### Work and power

- Work is done only when a force moves its point of application in the direction of the force. Holding a heavy box still: no work done on the box.
- Lifting at constant speed: force = weight, so W = mgh.
- Power = rate of doing work. A more powerful motor does the same work in less time.

### Efficiency

- No device is 100% efficient (friction, heating of wires, sound). Efficiency can never be greater than 1 (100%).
- Wasted energy = total input − useful output.

### Energy resources

Most power stations: energy source heats water in a **boiler** → steam → turns a **turbine** → turns a **generator** → electricity.

| Resource | How it works | Renewable? | Main advantages | Main disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fossil fuels (coal, oil, gas) | burned, heat water → steam → turbine | No | reliable, large scale, on demand | CO₂ (climate change), SO₂ (acid rain), will run out |
| Biofuels (wood, ethanol, biogas) | burned like fossil fuels | Yes | renewable, "carbon neutral" if replanted | needs large land area, still emits CO₂ when burned |
| Nuclear (uranium) | fission heats water → steam | No | no CO₂ in operation, large scale, reliable | radioactive waste, risk of accidents, expensive to build/decommission |
| Hydroelectric | water stored behind a dam (Ep) flows through turbines | Yes | reliable, on demand, no fuel cost | floods valleys, habitats lost, few suitable sites |
| Tidal | tides fill/empty a barrage through turbines | Yes | predictable | few sites, affects estuary habitats, varies over day |
| Waves | wave motion drives generators | Yes | no fuel | unreliable, small scale, hazard to shipping |
| Geothermal | hot rocks heat water → steam | Yes | reliable | only in volcanic/thin-crust areas |
| Wind | wind turns turbine directly (no boiler) | Yes | no fuel or emissions | unreliable (wind varies), visual/noise impact, small output per turbine |
| Solar cells | light → electricity directly | Yes | no emissions, good for remote places | only in daytime, depends on weather, large area for large output |
| Solar panels | infrared heats water directly | Yes | cheap hot water | only heats water, needs sunshine |

*Extended:* The Sun's radiation is the original source for fossil fuels, biofuels, hydroelectric (evaporation → rain), waves and wind (uneven heating of the atmosphere) and solar. **Not** from the Sun: geothermal, nuclear, tidal (mainly the Moon's gravity). The Sun releases energy by **nuclear fusion** of hydrogen into helium; research continues into fusion reactors for large-scale electricity.

### Paper 6 skills for this topic

- **Power of a student climbing stairs:** measure mass (scales), vertical height of stairs (count steps × height of one step, measured with a ruler), time with a stop-watch; P = mgh ÷ t.
- **Bouncing ball:** drop from measured heights, measure rebound height with a metre rule held vertically; view at eye level; repeat; efficiency ≈ rebound height ÷ drop height (since Ep ∝ h).
- **Trolley/ball on a ramp:** measure height h and speed v; compare mgh with ½mv².

## Key terms and definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Work done | Force × distance moved in the direction of the force; equal to the energy transferred |
| Power | Work done per unit time / energy transferred per unit time |
| Efficiency | Useful energy output ÷ total energy input (or useful power output ÷ total power input) |
| Conservation of energy | Energy cannot be created or destroyed; it is transferred from one store to another, and the total remains constant |
| Renewable resource | A resource that is replaced (by natural processes) as it is used / will not run out |
| Nuclear fusion | Joining of light nuclei to form a heavier nucleus, releasing energy (powers the Sun) |

## Worked examples

**1.** A roller-coaster car of mass 500 kg starts from rest at the top of a slope 20 m high. Ignore friction. (a) Calculate the loss of gravitational potential energy when it reaches the bottom. [2] (b) Calculate its speed at the bottom. [3]

- (a) ΔEp = mgΔh = 500 × 9.8 × 20 (C1) = **98 000 J** (A1)
- (b) Ek = 98 000 J (C1); ½ × 500 × v² = 98 000 so v² = 392 (C1); v = **19.8 m/s** (A1)

**2.** An electric motor lifts a 30 kg load through 4.0 m in 6.0 s. The electrical power input is 400 W. Calculate (a) the useful power output, [3] (b) the efficiency. [2]

- (a) work = mgh = 30 × 9.8 × 4.0 = 1176 J (C1); P = 1176 ÷ 6.0 (C1) = **196 W** (A1)
- (b) efficiency = 196 ÷ 400 (C1) = **0.49 or 49%** (A1)

**3.** A car of mass 900 kg moving at 20 m/s brakes and stops in 40 m. Calculate the average braking force. [3]

- Ek = ½ × 900 × 20² = 180 000 J (C1)
- work done by brakes = Ek, so F × 40 = 180 000 (C1)
- F = **4500 N** (A1)

**4.** Compare wind turbines and a gas-fired power station as ways of generating electricity. [4]

- Wind is renewable; gas is non-renewable / will run out. (B1)
- Wind produces no CO₂/greenhouse gas while generating; gas releases CO₂ contributing to climate change. (B1)
- Gas is reliable / available on demand; wind output depends on wind speed. (B1)
- Gas stations generate on a large scale from a small site; many wind turbines are needed / visual impact. (B1)

## Common mistakes

- Squaring the whole of ½mv instead of only v; forgetting to square-root at the end when finding v.
- Using mass instead of weight in W = Fd for lifting (force is mg).
- Giving efficiency greater than 100% (useful and total swapped) or with a unit.
- Writing "energy is lost" — say "transferred to the internal (thermal) energy of the surroundings".
- Calling nuclear power renewable; saying tidal energy comes from the Sun.
- Vague comparisons ("it's bad for the environment") — name the specific effect (CO₂ → climate change, flooding of habitats).
- Forgetting to convert kW to W or minutes to seconds in power calculations.

## Practice questions

1. A lamp has an input of 60 J each second; 12 J is emitted as light. Calculate its efficiency and state what happens to the rest of the energy. [3]
2. A ball is dropped from a height of 5.0 m. Ignoring air resistance, calculate its speed just before it hits the ground. [3]
3. A crane motor has a power of 2.0 kW. Calculate the energy it transfers in 30 s. [2]
4. A cyclist pedals 3.0 km against an average resistive force of 25 N at constant speed. Calculate the work done. [2]
5. A power station is 40% efficient and its electrical output is 600 MW. Calculate the total power input and the wasted power. [3]
6. Name three energy resources that do **not** have the Sun as their original source. [2]
7. Describe the energy transfers in a hydroelectric power station. [3]

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. 12 ÷ 60 = **0.20 (20%)**; 48 J each second is transferred to the internal (thermal) energy of the surroundings (heating).
2. v = √(2 × 9.8 × 5.0) = √98 = **9.9 m/s**.
3. E = P × t = 2000 × 30 = **60 000 J** (60 kJ).
4. W = 25 × 3000 = **75 000 J** (75 kJ).
5. input = 600 ÷ 0.40 = **1500 MW**; wasted = 1500 − 600 = **900 MW**.
6. **Geothermal, nuclear, tidal** (2 marks for all three, 1 mark for two).
7. Gravitational potential energy of water stored behind the dam → kinetic energy as water flows down → turbine (kinetic) → generator → electrical energy (some wasted as internal energy/sound).
