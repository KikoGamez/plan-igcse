# Light and the electromagnetic spectrum

> **In the exam:** In Co-ordinated Sciences 0654 (Extended) Physics questions appear alongside Biology and Chemistry. Paper 2 (Multiple Choice, 45 min, 40 questions across all three sciences): typically 1–3 items here (plane mirror images, refraction diagrams, lens images, order and uses of the EM spectrum, dispersion). Paper 4 (Theory, 2 h, 120 marks): a common Physics part of 4–8 marks (converging-lens ray diagram, n = sin i ÷ sin r, total internal reflection and optical fibres, EM uses and dangers). Paper 6 (Alternative to Practical, 1 h 30 min, 60 marks): "optics experiments using optics pins, mirrors, prisms, lenses, glass or Perspex blocks" is a named practical context — expect ray tracing and angle measuring.

## What you need to know

Syllabus sections P3.2 and P3.3 (Core, then *Supplement* for Extended).

- Use ray diagrams to define normal, angle of incidence and angle of reflection; angle of incidence = angle of reflection.
- Describe the image in a vertical plane mirror: same size, same distance from the mirror, laterally inverted. *Supplement:* explain why it is virtual; use simple diagrams, measurements and calculations for reflection by plane mirrors.
- Define refraction as the change in direction of a light ray passing from one medium to another; use normal, angle of incidence, angle of refraction; describe light passing through a transparent material (boundaries between two media only).
- *Supplement:* define refractive index n as the ratio of the speeds of a wave in two different regions; recall and use n = sin i ÷ sin r.
- *Supplement:* describe total internal reflection with ray diagrams; define the critical angle as the angle of incidence at which the angle of refraction is 90° and above which all light is totally internally reflected; describe total internal reflection in optical fibres and state some common applications.
- Thin converging lens: action on a parallel beam (rays from a distant object are parallel); principal axis, principal focus (focal point), focal length; ray diagrams for real images; describe images as enlarged / same size / diminished and upright / inverted.
- *Supplement:* ray diagrams for a virtual image; describe images as real / virtual; use of a single lens as a magnifying glass.
- Dispersion of white light by a glass prism; the seven colours (red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet) in order of frequency and of wavelength.
- Main regions of the EM spectrum in order of frequency and of wavelength; all EM waves travel at the same high speed in a vacuum. *Supplement:* this speed is 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s, approximately the same in air.
- Applications: radio (radio and TV transmissions, radar); microwaves (satellite TV, mobile phones, microwave ovens); infrared (TV remote controls, thermal imaging); visible (vision, photography); ultraviolet (detecting fake bank notes); X-rays (medical scanning, security scanners); gamma rays (detection of cancer and its treatment).
- Harmful effects of excessive exposure: ultraviolet — damage to surface cells and eyes, leading to skin cancer and eye conditions; X-rays and gamma rays — mutation or damage to cells in the body.

**Not in 0654** (they are in Physics 0625): n = 1 ÷ sin c, diverging lenses, correcting long and short sight, monochromatic light, digital and analogue signals, Bluetooth/wireless internet and satellite-phone details. Skip them.

## Notes

### Equations

| Equation | Symbols and units |
|---|---|
| angle of incidence = angle of reflection | degrees (°), measured from the normal |
| n = sin i ÷ sin r *(Supplement)* | n refractive index (no unit), i angle in air, r angle in the material |
| n = speed in air (vacuum) ÷ speed in the material *(Supplement — the definition as a ratio of speeds)* | m/s ÷ m/s, no unit |
| v = fλ, with v = 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s for all EM waves in a vacuum *(Supplement value)* | f (Hz), λ (m) |

### Reflection

- Angles are always measured from the **normal** (line at 90° to the surface).
- Plane mirror image: **same size, same distance behind the mirror as the object is in front, laterally inverted, upright**.
- *Supplement — virtual:* the reflected rays only appear to come from a point behind the mirror; no light actually reaches that point, so the image cannot be formed on a screen.
- Constructing the image: mark the image point the same perpendicular distance behind the mirror; draw rays from the image point to the eye — real rays only in front of the mirror (solid), extensions behind it dashed.

### Refraction

- Light slows down entering glass/water: bends **towards** the normal. Speeds up leaving: bends **away** from the normal. Along the normal: no change of direction (speed still changes).
- Rectangular block: emerging ray is parallel to the incident ray (displaced sideways).
- *Supplement — refractive index:* n = speed in air ÷ speed in the material = sin i ÷ sin r (i in air). Larger n → light slowed more → bends more.
- *Supplement — critical angle c:* the angle of incidence (inside the glass/water) for which the angle of refraction is 90°.
- *Supplement — total internal reflection (TIR):* when light inside the denser material (glass, water) hits the boundary with air at an angle **greater than the critical angle**, all of it is reflected. Both conditions: travelling from glass/water towards air **and** i > c.
- Demonstration: semicircular block — aim the ray at the centre of the flat face through the curved side (so it enters along a normal); increase the angle until the refracted ray runs along the surface (i = c), then TIR.
- *Supplement — optical fibres:* light enters one end and undergoes repeated total internal reflection along the fibre, so very little escapes. Applications: telecommunications (telephone, cable TV, broadband internet) and endoscopes for seeing inside the body.

### Thin converging lens

- Parallel rays (e.g. from a distant object) refract and meet at the **principal focus F** on the principal axis. **Focal length** = distance from the centre of the lens to F.
- **Ray diagram rules:**
  1. Ray parallel to the principal axis → passes through F on the far side.
  2. Ray through the centre of the lens → straight on, undeviated.
  3. Ray through F on the near side → leaves parallel to the axis.
- **Images:**

| Object position | Image | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Beyond 2F | real, inverted, diminished, between F and 2F | camera, eye |
| At 2F | real, inverted, same size, at 2F | — |
| Between F and 2F | real, inverted, enlarged, beyond 2F | projector |
| Between F and the lens *(Supplement)* | virtual, upright, enlarged, same side as object | magnifying glass |

- *Supplement:* a **real** image is formed where rays actually meet and can be formed on a screen. A **virtual** image is where rays only appear to come from (trace the refracted rays back with dashed lines) and cannot be formed on a screen.
- *Supplement — magnifying glass:* place the object closer to the lens than the focal length; look through the lens to see an upright, enlarged, virtual image.

### Dispersion

White light through a glass prism splits into a spectrum: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet. Violet is refracted most and has the highest frequency and shortest wavelength; red is refracted least and has the lowest frequency and longest wavelength.

### The electromagnetic spectrum

Order of **increasing frequency** (decreasing wavelength): **radio → microwaves → infrared → visible → ultraviolet → X-rays → gamma rays.** All are transverse and travel at the same speed in a vacuum (3.0 × 10⁸ m/s, Supplement).

| Region | Uses (syllabus list) | Harmful effects (syllabus list) |
|---|---|---|
| Radio | radio and TV transmissions, radar | — |
| Microwaves | satellite TV, mobile (cell) phones, microwave ovens | — |
| Infrared | TV remote controllers, thermal imaging | — |
| Visible | vision, photography | — |
| Ultraviolet | detecting fake bank notes | damage to surface cells and eyes → skin cancer and eye conditions |
| X-rays | medical scanning, security scanners | mutation or damage to cells in the body |
| Gamma | detection of cancer and its treatment | mutation or damage to cells in the body |

### Paper 6 skills for this topic

- **Ray tracing with pins:** pins at least 5 cm apart (further apart → more accurate line); view the bases of the pins; draw normals with a protractor/set square; measure angles to the nearest degree; thin lines with a sharp pencil.
- **Glass block:** trace the outline, mark the incident ray with two pins, look through the block to line up two more pins with the images of the first two; join up; measure i and r; plot sin i against sin r — gradient = n (Supplement).
- **Lens:** focus an image of a distant window or an illuminated object onto a screen; measure object distance and image distance from the centre of the lens; repeat. Difficulty: judging the sharpest image — move the screen back and forth and take the midpoint; darken the room.
- **Mirror:** check that angle of incidence = angle of reflection "within the limits of experimental accuracy" (±10%).
- **Transmission of light:** classify materials as transparent, translucent or opaque (named in the practical list).

## Key terms and definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Normal | Line at right angles to a surface at the point where the ray meets it |
| Angle of incidence / reflection / refraction | Angle between the incident / reflected / refracted ray and the normal |
| Refraction | Change in direction of a light ray passing from one medium to another |
| Refractive index *(S)* | Ratio of the speeds of a wave in two different regions |
| Critical angle *(S)* | Angle of incidence at which the angle of refraction is 90°, and above which all light is totally internally reflected |
| Principal focus (focal point) | Point where rays parallel to the principal axis meet after passing through a converging lens |
| Focal length | Distance from the centre of the lens to the principal focus |
| Real image *(S)* | Image formed where rays actually meet; can be formed on a screen |
| Virtual image *(S)* | Image formed where rays only appear to come from; cannot be formed on a screen |
| Dispersion | Splitting of white light into its colours by refraction (e.g. by a glass prism) |

## Worked examples

**1.** A ray enters a glass block with angle of incidence 40°; the angle of refraction is 25°. (a) Calculate the refractive index. [2] (b) Calculate the speed of light in the glass. [2]

- (a) n = sin 40° ÷ sin 25° = 0.643 ÷ 0.423 (C1) = **1.52** (A1)
- (b) speed = 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 1.52 (C1) = **2.0 × 10⁸ m/s** (A1)

**2.** An object is placed 30 cm from a converging lens of focal length 10 cm. A ray diagram drawn to scale shows the image 15 cm from the lens. Describe the image. [3]

- real (B1), inverted (B1), diminished (B1)

**3.** Explain how light travels along a curved optical fibre, and give one use of optical fibres. [3]

- Light hits the inside surface of the fibre at an angle of incidence greater than the critical angle. (B1)
- It is totally internally reflected, again and again along the fibre (very little light escapes). (B1)
- Use: telecommunications / broadband / cable TV / endoscope. (B1)

**4.** State the region of the EM spectrum used for each: (a) thermal imaging, (b) security scanners at airports, (c) radar. [3]

- (a) infrared (B1) (b) X-rays (B1) (c) radio waves (B1)

## Common mistakes

- Measuring angles from the surface instead of from the normal.
- Drawing refraction the wrong way (light entering glass bends towards the normal).
- Giving only one condition for TIR — you need both: from glass/water towards air **and** angle of incidence > critical angle.
- Rays in lens diagrams without arrows, or not bending at the lens line; virtual rays drawn solid instead of dashed.
- Describing an image with only one word — give all three: real/virtual, upright/inverted, enlarged/diminished/same size.
- Wrong order of the EM spectrum, or saying different EM waves travel at different speeds in a vacuum.
- Giving dangers vaguely ("UV is dangerous") instead of "damage to surface cells and eyes, leading to skin cancer".

## Practice questions

1. A wifi router emits microwaves of frequency 2.4 GHz. Calculate the wavelength. [2]
2. Light in air enters water with an angle of incidence of 50°. The refractive index of water is 1.33. Calculate the angle of refraction. [3]
3. A girl stands 2.0 m in front of a plane mirror. How far is she from her image? [1]
4. Light travels from water into air. The angle of incidence in water is 30° and n for water is 1.33. Calculate the angle of refraction in air. [3]
5. State which region of the EM spectrum is used for (a) treating cancer, (b) detecting fake bank notes, (c) TV remote controls. [3]
6. List the seven colours of the visible spectrum in order of decreasing wavelength, and state which colour is refracted most by a prism. [2]
7. Describe the image produced by a magnifying glass and state where the object must be placed. [3]

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. λ = 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 2.4 × 10⁹ = **0.125 m** (12.5 cm).
2. sin r = sin 50° ÷ 1.33 = 0.766 ÷ 1.33 = 0.576; r = **35°** (35.2°).
3. **4.0 m** (image is 2.0 m behind the mirror).
4. Here light goes from water to air, so n = sin(angle in air) ÷ sin(angle in water): sin r = 1.33 × sin 30° = 0.665; r = **41.7°** (accept 42°).
5. (a) gamma rays (b) ultraviolet (c) infrared.
6. Red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet; **violet** is refracted most.
7. Virtual, upright, enlarged; object placed between the lens and its principal focus (closer than the focal length).
