# Light and the electromagnetic spectrum

> **In the exam:** Paper 2: 4–6 items (mirror images, refraction diagrams, critical angle, lens images, order and uses of the EM spectrum). Paper 4: light appears in almost every paper — ray diagrams for a converging lens, n = sin i/sin r, critical angle and total internal reflection, optical fibres, EM uses/dangers and digital signals (typically 7–10 marks). Paper 6: optics experiments with pins, glass blocks, mirrors and lenses are very common (often a whole question).

## What you need to know

- Normal, angle of incidence, angle of reflection; angle of incidence = angle of reflection; image in a plane mirror: same size, same distance from the mirror, virtual. *Extended:* constructions, measurements and calculations for plane mirrors.
- Normal, angle of refraction; experiments with transparent blocks; passage of light across a boundary; critical angle; internal and total internal reflection.
- *Extended:* refractive index n = ratio of speeds of a wave in two regions; n = sin i ÷ sin r; n = 1 ÷ sin c; optical fibres in telecommunications.
- Thin converging and diverging lenses on a parallel beam; focal length, principal axis, principal focus; ray diagrams for a real image (converging lens); image descriptions (enlarged/same size/diminished, upright/inverted, real/virtual); a virtual image is formed when diverging rays are extrapolated backwards and cannot be projected onto a screen.
- *Extended:* ray diagrams for a virtual image (magnifying glass); correcting long and short sight with converging and diverging lenses.
- Dispersion of white light by a prism; seven colours in order of frequency and wavelength. *Extended:* monochromatic light = single frequency.
- EM spectrum regions in order; same speed in a vacuum (*Extended:* 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s, about the same in air); uses and harmful effects; satellite communication by microwaves.
- *Extended:* mobile phones, wireless internet, Bluetooth, optical fibres; digital vs analogue signals; sound as either; benefits of digital signalling.

## Notes

### Equations

| Equation | Symbols and units |
|---|---|
| angle of incidence = angle of reflection | degrees (°), measured from the normal |
| n = sin i ÷ sin r *(Extended)* | n refractive index (no unit), i angle in air, r angle in the material |
| n = 1 ÷ sin c *(Extended)* | c critical angle (°) |
| n = speed of light in air (vacuum) ÷ speed in material *(Extended — definition as ratio of speeds)* | m/s ÷ m/s |
| v = fλ, with v = 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s for all EM waves in a vacuum | f (Hz), λ (m) |

### Reflection

- Angles are always measured from the **normal** (line at 90° to the surface).
- Plane mirror image: **virtual, upright, same size, same distance behind the mirror as the object is in front, laterally inverted**.
- Constructing the image: draw the image point the same perpendicular distance behind the mirror; draw rays from the image point to the eye — the real rays exist only in front of the mirror; dashed lines behind.

### Refraction

- Light slows down entering glass/water: bends **towards** the normal. Speeds up leaving: bends **away** from the normal. Along the normal: no change of direction (speed still changes).
- Rectangular block: emerging ray is parallel to the incident ray (displaced sideways).
- **Critical angle c:** angle of incidence (inside the denser material) for which the angle of refraction is 90°.
- **Total internal reflection (TIR):** if light in the denser material hits the boundary at an angle **greater than the critical angle**, all of it is reflected. Conditions: travelling from denser to less dense medium (e.g. glass → air) AND angle of incidence > c.
- Experiments: semicircular block — aim ray at the centre of the flat face from the curved side so it enters the curved surface along a normal; increase the angle until refracted ray lies along the surface (c) and then TIR.
- Examples: optical fibres, prisms in periscopes/binoculars, sparkle of diamonds.
- *Extended — optical fibres:* light (visible or infrared) enters one end and undergoes repeated TIR at the core boundary; carries data for cable TV, high-speed broadband, telephone; also endoscopes in medicine.

### Lenses

- **Converging (convex):** parallel rays refract and meet at the **principal focus F**. **Focal length f** = distance from the centre of the lens to F.
- **Diverging (concave):** parallel rays spread out as if coming from a principal focus on the same side as the light comes from.
- **Ray diagram rules (converging lens):**
  1. Ray parallel to the principal axis → passes through F on the far side.
  2. Ray through the centre of the lens → straight on, undeviated.
  3. Ray through F on the near side → leaves parallel to the axis.
- **Images:**

| Object position | Image | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Beyond 2F | real, inverted, diminished, between F and 2F | camera, eye |
| At 2F | real, inverted, same size, at 2F | — |
| Between F and 2F | real, inverted, enlarged, beyond 2F | projector |
| Inside F *(Extended)* | virtual, upright, enlarged, same side as object | magnifying glass |

- A **real** image is formed where rays actually meet — can be projected on a screen. A **virtual** image is where rays only appear to come from (traced back with dashed lines) — cannot be projected.
- *Extended — sight:* **short sight**: distant objects focus in front of the retina (eyeball too long/lens too strong) → corrected with a **diverging** lens. **Long sight**: near objects focus behind the retina → corrected with a **converging** lens.

### Dispersion

White light through a prism splits into a spectrum: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet. Violet is refracted most (highest frequency, shortest wavelength); red least (lowest frequency, longest wavelength). Different colours travel at different speeds in glass. *Extended:* light of a single frequency is **monochromatic**.

### The electromagnetic spectrum

Order of **increasing frequency** (decreasing wavelength): **radio → microwaves → infrared → visible → ultraviolet → X-rays → gamma rays.** All are transverse, travel at 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s in a vacuum, and can be reflected and refracted.

| Region | Uses (syllabus list) | Harmful effects of excessive exposure |
|---|---|---|
| Radio | radio and TV transmission, astronomy, RFID; *Bluetooth* | — |
| Microwaves | satellite TV, mobile phones, microwave ovens; *wireless internet* | internal heating of body cells |
| Infrared | electric grills, remote controls, intruder alarms, thermal imaging, optical fibres | skin burns |
| Visible | vision, photography, illumination | — |
| Ultraviolet | security marking, detecting fake bank notes, sterilising water | damage to surface cells and eyes → skin cancer and eye conditions |
| X-rays | medical scanning, security scanners | mutation or damage to cells |
| Gamma | sterilising food and medical equipment, detection and treatment of cancer | mutation or damage to cells |

- Satellite communication is mainly by microwaves: some satellite phones use low-orbit satellites; some satellite phones and direct-broadcast satellite TV use geostationary satellites.
- *Extended:* mobile phones and wireless internet use microwaves because microwaves can penetrate some walls and need only a short aerial; Bluetooth uses radio waves because they pass through walls (but the signal is weakened); optical fibres use visible/infrared because glass is transparent to them and they can carry high rates of data.

### Digital and analogue (Extended)

- **Analogue** signal: varies continuously, can take any value. **Digital** signal: only two values (on/off, 1/0).
- Sound can be sent as either.
- Benefits of digital: noise picked up along the way can be removed when the signal is **regenerated accurately**, so greater range; **higher rate of transmission of data**.

### Paper 6 skills for this topic

- **Ray tracing with pins:** place pins at least 5 cm apart (further apart → more accurate line); view the bases of the pins (they may be bent); draw normals with a protractor/set square; measure angles to the nearest degree; draw thin lines with a sharp pencil.
- **Glass block:** trace the outline, mark incident ray with two pins, look through the block to line up two more pins with the images of the first two; join up; measure i and r; plot sin i against sin r — gradient = n (Extended).
- **Lens:** focus an image of a distant window or an illuminated object onto a screen; measure object distance u and image distance v from the centre of the lens; repeat for different u. Difficulties: hard to judge sharpest image — move the screen back and forth and take the midpoint; room should be darkened.
- **Mirror:** check that angle of incidence = angle of reflection "within the limits of experimental accuracy".

## Key terms and definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Normal | Line at right angles to a surface at the point where the ray meets it |
| Angle of incidence / reflection / refraction | Angle between the incident / reflected / refracted ray and the normal |
| Refractive index | Ratio of the speeds of a wave in two different regions (speed in air ÷ speed in the material) |
| Critical angle | Angle of incidence in the denser medium for which the angle of refraction is 90° |
| Total internal reflection | Complete reflection of light inside a denser medium when the angle of incidence is greater than the critical angle |
| Principal focus (focal point) | Point where rays parallel to the principal axis converge (converging lens) or appear to diverge from (diverging lens) |
| Focal length | Distance from the centre of the lens to the principal focus |
| Real image | Image formed where rays actually meet; can be formed on a screen |
| Virtual image | Image formed where rays only appear to come from (extrapolated backwards); cannot be formed on a screen |
| Dispersion | Splitting of white light into its colours by refraction (e.g. by a prism) |
| Monochromatic | Light of a single frequency |

## Worked examples

**1.** A ray enters a glass block with angle of incidence 40°; the angle of refraction is 25°. (a) Calculate the refractive index. [2] (b) Calculate the critical angle. [2]

- (a) n = sin 40° ÷ sin 25° = 0.643 ÷ 0.423 (C1) = **1.52** (A1)
- (b) sin c = 1 ÷ 1.52 (C1); c = **41°** (A1)

**2.** Calculate the speed of light in glass of refractive index 1.5. [2]

- v = 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 1.5 (C1) = **2.0 × 10⁸ m/s** (A1)

**3.** An object is placed 30 cm from a converging lens of focal length 10 cm. By drawing a ray diagram to scale, the image is found 15 cm from the lens. Describe the image. [3]

- real (B1), inverted (B1), diminished (B1)

**4.** Explain why mobile phones use microwaves. [2]

- Microwaves can penetrate (some) walls. (B1)
- They only need a short aerial (to transmit and receive). (B1)

## Common mistakes

- Measuring angles from the surface instead of from the normal.
- Drawing refraction the wrong way (light entering glass must bend towards the normal).
- Stating only one condition for TIR — you need both: from denser to less dense **and** i > c.
- Rays in lens diagrams without arrows, or not bending at the lens centre line; virtual rays drawn solid instead of dashed.
- Describing an image with only one word — give all three: real/virtual, upright/inverted, enlarged/diminished/same size.
- Mixing up which lens corrects which defect (short sight → diverging).
- Wrong order of the EM spectrum (learn: Radio, Micro, IR, Visible, UV, X, Gamma) or saying different EM waves travel at different speeds in a vacuum.
- Giving dangers vaguely ("UV is dangerous") instead of "damage to surface cells and eyes, leading to skin cancer".

## Practice questions

1. A wifi router emits microwaves of frequency 2.4 GHz. Calculate the wavelength. [2]
2. Diamond has refractive index 2.42. Calculate its critical angle. [2]
3. A girl stands 2.0 m in front of a plane mirror. How far is she from her image? [1]
4. Light travels from water into air. The angle of incidence in water is 30° and n for water is 1.33. Calculate the angle of refraction in air. [3]
5. State which region of the EM spectrum is used for (a) sterilising medical equipment, (b) detecting fake bank notes, (c) TV remote controls. [3]
6. State two advantages of digital signals over analogue signals. [2]
7. Describe the image produced by a magnifying glass and state where the object must be placed. [3]

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. λ = 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 2.4 × 10⁹ = **0.125 m** (12.5 cm).
2. sin c = 1 ÷ 2.42 = 0.413; c = **24.4°**.
3. **4.0 m** (image is 2.0 m behind the mirror).
4. Here light goes from water to air, so n = sin(angle in air) ÷ sin(angle in water): sin r = 1.33 × sin 30° = 0.665; r = **41.7°** (accept 42°).
5. (a) gamma rays (b) ultraviolet (c) infrared.
6. Can be regenerated accurately, removing noise, so longer range; higher rate of data transmission.
7. Virtual, upright, enlarged; object placed between the lens and its principal focus (closer than f).
