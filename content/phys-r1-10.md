# Magnetism and the motor effect

> **In the exam:** Paper 2: 3–5 items (field patterns, induced magnetism, soft iron vs steel, direction of force with the left-hand rule, ways to increase a motor's turning effect). Paper 4: usually a 5–9 mark question on field patterns of wires and solenoids, relays or loudspeakers, the d.c. motor and the commutator, or deflection of a charged particle beam. Paper 6: plotting field lines with a compass is possible but less common; mostly diagram and description skills.

## What you need to know

- Forces between magnetic poles (attraction, repulsion) and between magnets and magnetic materials; induced magnetism; temporary magnets (soft iron) vs permanent magnets (steel); magnetic vs non-magnetic materials.
- A magnetic field is a region in which a magnetic pole experiences a force; direction of field = direction of the force on the N pole of a magnet; draw the field around a bar magnet; plotting with a compass or iron filings.
- Uses of permanent magnets and electromagnets.
- *Extended:* magnetic forces are due to interactions between magnetic fields; relative strength of a field is shown by the spacing of field lines.
- Field pattern and direction around a straight wire and a solenoid; experiment to show it; use of the magnetic effect of a current in relays and loudspeakers.
- *Extended:* how the field strength varies around wires and solenoids; effect of changing the size and direction of the current.
- Experiment to show a force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field, including reversing the current and the field. *Extended:* relative directions of force, field and current; direction of force on beams of charged particles.
- A current-carrying coil in a magnetic field experiences a turning effect, increased by more turns, larger current, stronger field. *Extended:* operation of a d.c. motor, including split-ring commutator and brushes.

## Notes

### Equations

There are no calculation equations in this topic. You must know the **rules for directions**:

| Rule | What it gives |
|---|---|
| Field lines go from N to S outside a magnet | direction of field (force on a N pole) |
| Right-hand grip rule: thumb along the conventional current, fingers curl in the field direction | field around a wire; for a solenoid, fingers along the current in the turns → thumb points to the N pole |
| Fleming's left-hand rule *(Extended)*: **F**irst finger = **F**ield (N→S), se**C**ond finger = **C**urrent (conventional, + to −), thu**M**b = **M**otion/force | direction of force on a current-carrying conductor or charged-particle beam |

### Magnets and magnetic materials

- Like poles repel, unlike poles attract. A magnet attracts unmagnetised magnetic materials (iron, steel, nickel, cobalt) — only **repulsion** proves something is a magnet.
- **Magnetic materials:** iron, steel, nickel, cobalt. **Non-magnetic:** copper, aluminium, plastic, wood, glass.
- **Induced magnetism:** a magnetic material placed near a magnet becomes a magnet itself; the end nearest a N pole becomes a S pole (so it is attracted). Chains of paper clips hanging from a magnet.
- **Soft iron:** easily magnetised and easily loses its magnetism → **temporary magnet** (electromagnet cores, transformer cores, relays).
- **Steel:** harder to magnetise but keeps its magnetism → **permanent magnet** (fridge magnets, compasses, loudspeakers, motors).

### Magnetic fields

- Bar magnet: lines leave N, curve round, enter S; never cross; closest together (strongest) at the poles.
- *Extended:* closer lines → stronger field. Between two opposite poles facing each other: nearly uniform, parallel field. Between two like poles: a neutral point where fields cancel.
- **Plotting compass method:** place magnet on paper; put compass near the N pole; mark where the compass needle points; move the compass so its tail is at the last dot; repeat to form a line; the needle shows direction. Iron filings show the pattern but not the direction.
- Uses: permanent magnets — compasses, door catches, loudspeakers, motors, generators. Electromagnets — scrap-yard cranes, relays, electric bells, MRI scanners; advantage: can be switched off and strength adjusted.

### Magnetic effect of a current

- **Straight wire:** concentric circles around the wire; direction by the right-hand grip rule. *Extended:* field is strongest close to the wire and gets weaker further away (circles drawn further apart); larger current → stronger field; reversing the current reverses the field direction.
- **Solenoid (coil):** field inside is strong and uniform (parallel lines); outside it looks like a bar magnet's field. *Extended:* stronger with larger current, more turns per unit length, and a soft-iron core; reversing the current reverses the poles.
- Experiment: wire passes vertically through a horizontal card; sprinkle iron filings, tap the card (pattern) / place plotting compasses around the wire (direction); reverse the current and see compasses reverse.

### Relays and loudspeakers

- **Relay:** a small current in a coil magnetises a soft-iron core; it attracts an iron armature, which pivots and closes (or opens) contacts in a second circuit. A small, safe current (e.g. from a sensor circuit) can switch a large current (e.g. a car starter motor, a heater, a mains lamp).
- **Loudspeaker:** an alternating current in a coil attached to a cone sits in the field of a permanent magnet; the coil experiences a force that reverses as the current reverses; the coil and cone vibrate at the frequency of the current, producing sound. Larger current → larger amplitude → louder.

### Force on a current-carrying conductor (the motor effect)

- A wire carrying a current across a magnetic field experiences a force (the wire's field interacts with the magnet's field).
- Experiment: a loose wire (or a copper rod on two rails) between the poles of a U-shaped magnet, connected to a d.c. supply; it moves (jumps or rolls) when current flows.
- Reverse the current **or** reverse the field → force reverses. Reverse both → force unchanged.
- No force if the current is parallel to the field.
- Force is larger with larger current and stronger field.
- *Extended — charged particle beams:* a beam of moving charged particles is a current. For **electrons**, the conventional current is in the **opposite** direction to their motion — apply the left-hand rule with the second finger pointing opposite to the electron motion. The force is perpendicular to the motion, so the beam follows a curved path.

### The d.c. motor

- A coil carrying current sits between magnetic poles. Forces on the two sides of the coil are in opposite directions (current in opposite directions) → a turning effect (moment) → the coil rotates.
- Turning effect increased by: **more turns, larger current, stronger magnetic field** (also a soft-iron core in the coil).
- *Extended — split-ring commutator and brushes:*
  - Brushes (carbon) press against the split ring and connect the rotating coil to the supply.
  - Every half turn (when the coil is vertical, perpendicular to the field) the commutator **reverses the current in the coil**.
  - This keeps the force on each side of the coil in the same direction as it passes each pole, so the coil keeps rotating in the same direction.
  - The coil's momentum carries it past the vertical position, where the forces have no turning effect.
- Reversing the current or the field reverses the direction of rotation.

### Paper 6 skills for this topic

- Use a plotting compass to trace field lines; mark dots with a sharp pencil; keep other magnetic materials away.
- Describe how to test whether a metal bar is a magnet (bring each end towards both poles of a known magnet; repulsion shows it is a magnet).

## Key terms and definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Magnetic field | A region in which a magnetic pole experiences a force |
| Direction of a magnetic field | The direction of the force on the N pole of a magnet at that point |
| Magnetic material | A material that is attracted by a magnet and can be magnetised (e.g. iron, steel, nickel, cobalt) |
| Induced magnetism | Magnetism produced in a magnetic material when it is placed in a magnetic field |
| Temporary magnet | Magnet that loses its magnetism easily (soft iron) |
| Permanent magnet | Magnet that keeps its magnetism (steel) |
| Electromagnet | Coil of wire (usually with soft-iron core) that is magnetic only when a current flows |
| Split-ring commutator *(Ext)* | Device that reverses the current in a motor coil every half turn so it keeps rotating the same way |

## Worked examples

**1.** State two differences between soft iron and steel as magnetic materials and give one use of each. [4]

- Soft iron is easily magnetised; steel is harder to magnetise. (B1)
- Soft iron loses its magnetism easily (temporary); steel keeps it (permanent). (B1)
- Soft iron: core of an electromagnet / relay / transformer. (B1)
- Steel: permanent magnet, e.g. compass needle / loudspeaker magnet. (B1)

**2.** Explain how a relay allows a small current to switch on a large motor. [3]

- Small current in the relay coil magnetises the (soft-iron) core. (B1)
- The core attracts the iron armature, which moves/pivots. (B1)
- This closes the contacts / switch in the motor circuit so the large current flows. (B1)

**3.** Describe the function of the split-ring commutator in a d.c. motor. [3]

- It reverses the current in the coil (B1) every half turn / when the coil is vertical (B1).
- So the forces on each side of the coil keep turning it in the same direction. (B1)

**4.** A beam of electrons travels from left to right across the page. A magnetic field is directed into the page. State the direction of the force on the electrons. [2]

- Conventional current is from right to left (opposite to electron flow). (B1)
- Left-hand rule: field into page, current to the left → force is **down the page** (towards the bottom). (B1)

## Common mistakes

- Saying a magnet attracts all metals (copper and aluminium are not magnetic).
- Saying attraction proves an object is a magnet — only repulsion does.
- Field lines that cross, start in mid-air or have no arrows; arrows going S to N outside a magnet.
- Forgetting that electrons move opposite to conventional current in beam questions.
- Saying the commutator "changes the direction of the coil" or "makes it a.c." — it reverses the **current in the coil**.
- Answering "how can you make the motor turn faster?" with "more power" — give the three syllabus factors.
- Confusing the motor effect (current + field → force) with induction (movement + field → e.m.f.).

## Practice questions

1. Which material is used for the core of an electromagnet? A copper B steel C soft iron D aluminium [1]
2. Draw the magnetic field pattern around a straight vertical wire carrying current upwards out of the paper (viewed from above). [2]
3. State three ways of increasing the turning effect on a motor coil. [3]
4. Explain how a loudspeaker produces sound. [4]
5. A wire between the poles of a magnet moves upwards when a current flows. State what happens when (a) the current is reversed, (b) both the current and field are reversed. [2]
6. Describe how to use a plotting compass to show the field lines around a bar magnet. [3]
7. Describe how the strength of the magnetic field of a solenoid can be increased. [3]

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. **C**
2. Concentric circles centred on the wire, spaced further apart further out; arrows anticlockwise (current out of the paper, right-hand grip).
3. More turns on the coil; larger current; stronger magnet/field (any three; soft-iron core also accepted).
4. Alternating current passes through the coil; the coil is in the field of a permanent magnet so experiences a force; the force reverses direction as the current reverses; the coil and cone vibrate, making pressure waves (sound) in the air at the same frequency.
5. (a) moves downwards (b) moves upwards (unchanged).
6. Place compass near one pole; mark the position of the needle's ends with dots; move compass so its tail is on the last dot; repeat; join dots, add arrows in the direction the needle points (N to S).
7. Increase the current; more turns (per unit length); put a soft-iron core inside.
