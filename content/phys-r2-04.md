# Past paper · Paper 6

> **Goal:** Sit a full Alternative to Practical paper in 1 hour, then mark it quickly against the mark scheme, focusing on the practical-skill marks (tables, graphs, precision, evaluation, planning).

## Before you start

- **Paper:** Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625 **Paper 6 (Alternative to Practical)** — component 61, 62 or 63 — from the most recent series you have not done (e.g. May/June 2025 or Oct/Nov 2024; Feb/March 2025 has variant 62 only).
- The **mark scheme** for that paper (and the examiner report if you have time later).
- **Timer** set to 60 minutes. This session will run to about 75–80 minutes including marking; if you only have 60 minutes, mark the next day.
- **Equipment:** sharp HB pencil, ruler (30 cm, mm scale), protractor, set square, rubber, scientific calculator, black pen. Graphs and ray diagrams must be done in sharp pencil.

## Exam format reminder

- **1 hour, 40 marks**, 20% of the grade. All questions compulsory; no experiments are done.
- Usually **four questions**, each based on an experiment described with diagrams: e.g. a mechanics/timing experiment, a thermal experiment, an electrical circuit, an optics experiment with pins, lenses or blocks. One question is usually a **planning** question (describe how you would carry out an investigation).
- Timing: about **14 minutes per question**, with 4 minutes to check.

## How to do it

1. **0–60 min — sit the paper.** Read each experiment description fully before answering. Take readings from diagrams with your ruler/protractor (diagrams may be to scale — the question tells you).
2. **60–75 min — mark.** Use the mark scheme; Paper 6 schemes often have tolerance ranges for readings and graph points (e.g. "± half a small square"). Be strict about units and headings.
3. **75–80 min — error log.** Record lost marks by **skill** (not just topic): table, graph, reading, calculation, conclusion, evaluation, planning.

## Exam technique for this paper

**Readings and tables**
- Read scales to the nearest half division (e.g. thermometer with 1 °C divisions → 0.5 °C); avoid parallax (eye level, perpendicular to the scale).
- Column headings: quantity **/** unit, e.g. *V / V*, *t / s*, *θ / °C*. No units in the body of the table.
- Record all readings to the same precision (e.g. 23.0, 24.5 — not 23, 24.5).
- Calculated values: same number of significant figures as the least precise data (usually 2–3).

**Graphs**
- Label axes with quantity and unit, the right way round (the variable named first in "plot a graph of y against x" goes on the y-axis).
- Choose a scale so points fill **more than half** the grid in both directions, using 1, 2 or 5 units per 2 cm; don't use awkward scales such as 3 or 7.
- Plot points accurately (± half a small square) with small crosses or dots in circles.
- **Best-fit line:** one thin, smooth line or curve; roughly equal points on either side; ignore clear anomalies; it does not have to pass through the origin unless the data show that.
- **Gradient:** draw a large triangle (hypotenuse at least half the length of the line), show it on the graph, use values read from the line (not data points), give 2–3 s.f.

**Conclusions and evaluation**
- "Does the data support the suggestion that y is proportional to x?" → look for a **straight line through the origin**; quote evidence (e.g. "the line passes through the origin" or "the ratio y/x is constant within the limits of experimental accuracy").
- Results are "the same within the limits of experimental accuracy" if they differ by less than about **10%** — show the comparison.
- Sources of error must be **specific to the experiment**: "difficult to judge when the rule is exactly balanced", "reaction time when starting the stop-watch", "thermometer bulb not fully covered", "heat loss to surroundings", "wire heats up so resistance changes". Avoid generic "human error".
- Improvements: repeat and average; take more readings over a wider range; use a fiducial marker; time more oscillations; use a lid/insulation; switch off between readings; view at eye level.

**Precautions** (often 1 mark): avoid parallax, stir the water, check meter zero, switch off between readings, keep pins vertical and far apart, darken the room for lens experiments.

**Planning question** — cover each heading (marks are usually one per point):
- Apparatus list (including measuring instruments).
- Method: what you change (independent variable), how you measure the dependent variable, step-by-step.
- Control variables — name at least two specific ones.
- Range and number of values: e.g. "at least five values of length from 20 cm to 100 cm".
- Table headings with units.
- How to analyse: what graph to plot and what it should show (e.g. straight line through origin), or what to calculate.
- Safety precaution if relevant (hot water, heavy masses, high current).

## Checklist while marking

- [ ] Readings within the mark scheme tolerance? If not — was it parallax, wrong scale division, or wrong units?
- [ ] Table headings with quantity / unit; consistent decimal places.
- [ ] Graph: axes labelled with units, sensible scale, points accurate, thin best-fit line, gradient triangle shown.
- [ ] Calculations: correct equation, unit, sensible significant figures.
- [ ] Conclusions supported with evidence from data.
- [ ] Evaluation points specific to the experiment, not generic.
- [ ] Planning: apparatus, method, control variables, range, table, analysis all present.
- Error-log columns: Q/part · skill · marks lost · what the mark scheme wanted · action.

## Quick-fire revision

- Pendulum: time 20 oscillations, divide by 20; time from centre of swing; small amplitude.
- Spring: extension = length − original length; read at eye level; check return to original length.
- Density: volume by displacement; read bottom of meniscus.
- Moments: measure distances to the centre of each mass from the pivot; check rule balances first.
- Specific heat/cooling: stir, read thermometer at eye level to 0.5 °C, insulate, same starting temperature.
- Electricity: ammeter in series, voltmeter in parallel; R = V/I; switch off between readings.
- Optics: angles from the normal; pins ≥ 5 cm apart, view bases; lens image — focus sharply, measure from lens centre.
- Light/sound speed and timing: large distances or many repeats reduce the effect of reaction time.
- Accuracy rule: results within ±10% are "equal within the limits of experimental accuracy".
