# Histograms and standardised scores

> **In the exam:** Histograms appear in most series (5–8 marks: complete the frequency density column, draw bars, read frequencies back off the graph, estimate a frequency over part of a class). Standardised scores appear regularly as a 3–5 mark question comparing performances in two different tests or events; a common final part asks you to work backwards to a mean or standard deviation.

## What you need to know

- Draw and interpret histograms with equal and unequal class widths, using frequency density.
- Understand that in a histogram the area of each bar is proportional to the frequency.
- Estimate frequencies within part of a class from a histogram.
- Calculate and interpret standardised scores: standardised score = (value − mean) ÷ standard deviation.
- Use standardised scores to compare values from different distributions.

## Notes

### Histograms

**Frequency density = frequency ÷ class width**

- The vertical axis is labelled **frequency density**; the horizontal axis is a continuous scale (no gaps between bars).
- The **area** of a bar represents the frequency: frequency = frequency density × class width.
- Use the true class boundaries, e.g. 10 < a ≤ 15 has width 5.
- Unequal widths are the reason for frequency density: a wide class with many values should not look artificially tall.

Method for drawing:
1. Add columns for class width and frequency density.
2. Choose a sensible scale for frequency density.
3. Draw bars from boundary to boundary with heights = frequency density.

**Working out the scale when the axis is unlabelled:** use one bar whose frequency you know. Example: a bar of width 10 representing 15 people is 3 cm tall → frequency density 1.5 is 3 cm → 1 cm represents 0.5.

**Estimating part of a class:** assume values are spread evenly. Frequency between 12 and 15 in class 10–15 = 3 × frequency density of that class.

**Modal class** in a histogram = the class with the highest frequency **density** (tallest bar), not the most frequent class.

### Standardised scores

**Standardised score = (value − mean) ÷ standard deviation**

- It tells you how many standard deviations a value is above (positive) or below (negative) the mean.
- A score of 0 means exactly at the mean.
- To compare performances in different tests, compare standardised scores: the higher standardised score is the better **relative** performance.
- For times in a race (where lower is better), a more negative standardised score is the better performance — read the context.
- Rearranging: value = mean + standardised score × standard deviation.

## Key terms and definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Frequency density | Frequency ÷ class width |
| Histogram | A diagram for continuous grouped data in which the area of each bar is proportional to the frequency |
| Standardised score | (Value − mean) ÷ standard deviation; the number of standard deviations a value is from the mean |

## Worked examples

**1.** The ages of 185 people at a concert are shown.

| Age (a years) | 0 < a ≤ 10 | 10 < a ≤ 15 | 15 < a ≤ 20 | 20 < a ≤ 30 | 30 < a ≤ 50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 20 | 30 | 45 | 50 | 40 |

(a) Calculate the frequency densities. [2]
(b) Estimate how many people were aged between 12 and 25. [3]

(a) 20 ÷ 10 = **2**; 30 ÷ 5 = **6**; 45 ÷ 5 = **9**; 50 ÷ 10 = **5**; 40 ÷ 20 = **2** B2 (B1 for 3 correct)
(b) 12 to 15: 3 × 6 = 18 M1; 15 to 20: 45; 20 to 25: 5 × 5 = 25 M1; total **88** A1

**2.** Amir scored 74 in a Maths test (mean 62, standard deviation 8) and 63 in an English test (mean 55, standard deviation 5). In which test did he do better compared with the other students? [3]

Maths: (74 − 62) ÷ 8 = **1.5** M1; English: (63 − 55) ÷ 5 = **1.6** A1
English, because his standardised score is higher A1

**3.** In a test with mean 50 and standard deviation 12, Lucia's standardised score was −0.75. Find her mark. [2]

50 + (−0.75) × 12 M1 = **41** A1

## Common mistakes

- Plotting frequency instead of frequency density on the vertical axis.
- Using the wrong class width (e.g. width 4 for 10 < a ≤ 15).
- Leaving gaps between bars, or drawing bars at midpoints.
- Reading frequency straight off the vertical axis instead of multiplying by the width.
- Choosing the modal class by frequency when widths are unequal.
- Comparing raw marks or raw differences from the mean instead of standardised scores.
- Getting the order wrong: (mean − value) instead of (value − mean), which gives the wrong sign.
- In "lower is better" contexts (race times), choosing the higher standardised score as better.

## Practice questions

1. A class 26 < x ≤ 30 has frequency 18. Find its frequency density. [1]
2. A histogram bar for the class 50 < x ≤ 75 has frequency density 1.6. How many values are in this class? [1]
3. In a histogram, the class 20 < t ≤ 30, frequency 15, is drawn with height 3 cm. Calculate the height of the bar for the class 30 < t ≤ 35, which has frequency 12. [3]
4. Sara scored 48 in History (mean 40, standard deviation 6) and 71 in Physics (mean 65, standard deviation 5). Use standardised scores to decide in which subject she did better. [3]
5. Heights in a group have mean 160 cm and standard deviation 7.5 cm. Find the height with standardised score 2. [2]
6. A value of 90 has a standardised score of 1.25 in a distribution with mean 75. Find the standard deviation. [2]
7. In a test, a mark of 70 has standardised score 1 and a mark of 52 has standardised score −0.5. Find the mean and standard deviation of the marks. [4]
8. Two runners: Kai ran 100 m in 12.1 s (mean 12.8 s, sd 0.5 s); Lea ran 400 m in 58.0 s (mean 61.0 s, sd 2.0 s). Who performed better relative to their event? [3]

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. 18 ÷ 4 = **4.5**
2. 1.6 × 25 = **40**
3. FD = 15 ÷ 10 = 1.5 ↔ 3 cm, so 1 unit = 2 cm; new FD = 12 ÷ 5 = 2.4 → **4.8 cm**
4. History: 8 ÷ 6 = **1.33**; Physics: 6 ÷ 5 = **1.2** → **History**
5. 160 + 2 × 7.5 = **175 cm**
6. (90 − 75) ÷ σ = 1.25 → σ = 15 ÷ 1.25 = **12**
7. μ + σ = 70 and μ − 0.5σ = 52 → 1.5σ = 18 → **σ = 12**, **mean = 58**
8. Kai: (12.1 − 12.8) ÷ 0.5 = −1.4; Lea: (58.0 − 61.0) ÷ 2.0 = −1.5. Lower times are better, so the more negative score is better: **Lea**.
