# Past paper · Paper 1 timed (1/2)

> **Goal:** Start a complete Statistics Paper 1 under real exam conditions and work steadily for 60 minutes, so you practise pacing across the whole 1 h 45 paper.

## Before you start

- **Paper:** a Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Statistics (4ST1) **Paper 1** from the most recent series you have **not** done (e.g. June 2025 or June 2024). Download the question paper and the mark scheme from the Pearson Qualifications website, but put the mark scheme away — do not look at it today.
- **Timer:** set for 60 minutes. You will finish the paper in the next session (45 minutes), so the total is the full 1 h 45.
- **Calculator:** a scientific calculator is allowed. Check it is in normal mode and that you know how to use statistics mode (mean, Σx, Σx², standard deviation) — but always write down the working.
- **Equipment:** black pen, pencil, ruler, protractor, compasses, eraser, graph paper is printed in the paper. Phone in another room.
- **Space:** a clear desk, no notes, no music. Sit the paper as if it were the mock on 11 January.

## Exam format reminder

- **Two papers**, Paper 1 and Paper 2, each **1 hour 45 minutes**, each worth **50%** of the qualification. Both cover the whole specification and a calculator may be used.
- All questions are compulsory and answered in the spaces on the paper. There are no optional sections.
- Questions are a mix of short items (1–3 marks) and longer structured questions on one context (8–15 marks), roughly increasing in difficulty through the paper.
- The total number of marks is printed on the front cover. Divide 105 minutes by the total to get your **minutes per mark** (around 1 minute per mark) and use it to pace yourself.
- Diagrams such as cumulative frequency graphs, histograms, box plots and scatter diagrams are drawn on grids printed in the paper.

## How to do it

1. **0–2 min** – Read the front cover. Write the total marks and your minutes-per-mark rate at the top of a scrap sheet. Note the time you should reach the halfway point.
2. **2–60 min** – Work through the paper in order. For each question, glance at the marks and spend about that many minutes on it.
 - If you are stuck for more than 2 minutes, put a star in the margin and move on.
 - Write every step of working, even with a calculator: method marks are awarded for working.
 - Draw graphs in pencil, accurately, with a ruler.
3. **At 60 minutes** – Stop. Draw a line under the last thing you wrote and write "60 min". Note which question you reached and how many marks you have attempted. This tells you whether you are on pace.
4. Do **not** look at the mark scheme or your notes between sessions. Keep the paper somewhere safe for Part 2.

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Command words.** "Write down" / "state" = no working needed, one quick answer. "Calculate" / "work out" = show method. "Describe" = say what the data show, with figures. "Compare" = two comparisons (a measure of location and a measure of spread) in context. "Interpret" = explain what the value means in the context of the question. "Give a reason" = one clear reason linked to the context.
- **Context is essential.** "Strong positive correlation" alone often earns only part of the mark; add "students who revised longer tended to score higher".
- **Method marks (M)** are given for a correct method even if the final answer is wrong; **accuracy marks (A)** depend on a correct method; **B marks** are independent. So always write the calculation you are doing, e.g. "rs = 1 − 6 × 22 ÷ (8 × 63)".
- **Graphs:** plot cumulative frequency at upper class boundaries, moving averages at the middle of their period, and the mean point on scatter diagrams. Lines of best fit must pass through the mean point.
- **Accuracy:** give answers to 3 significant figures unless the question says otherwise, and do not round in the middle of a calculation.
- **Sampling and questionnaire questions:** be specific: "Use a random number generator to select 50 different numbers from 1 to 800, ignoring repeats".
- **Diagram criticism:** name the problem (axis not starting at zero, overlapping classes, no key) and say why it misleads.

## Checklist while marking

You will mark the whole paper in session 3 (*Mark with the mark scheme*). For now, check only:
- Did you reach about the 57–60% point of the marks in 60 minutes? If you were well behind, note which question took too long.
- Did you leave any question completely blank? Mark it with a star — in Part 2 you will try it first after finishing the paper.
- Did you show working on every calculation?

## Quick-fire revision

- Stratified sample from a group = (group size ÷ population) × sample size.
- Systematic sample: k = population ÷ sample size; random start from 1 to k.
- Estimated mean = Σfx ÷ Σf (midpoints). Standard deviation = √(Σfx² ÷ Σf − (Σfx ÷ Σf)²).
- Quartiles: (n + 1)/4 for lists; n/4 for cumulative frequency graphs.
- Outliers: below Q₁ − 1.5 × IQR or above Q₃ + 1.5 × IQR.
- Frequency density = frequency ÷ class width.
- Standardised score = (value − mean) ÷ standard deviation.
- rs = 1 − 6Σd² ÷ (n(n² − 1)).
- Seasonal variation = actual − trend; prediction = trend + mean seasonal variation.
- Index = (value ÷ base value) × 100; chain base uses the previous year.
- P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B); independent if P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B); P(A | B) = P(A ∩ B) ÷ P(B).
