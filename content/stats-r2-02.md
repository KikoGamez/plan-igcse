# Past paper · Paper 1 timed (2/2)

> **Goal:** Finish the Paper 1 you started last session in the remaining 45 minutes of exam time, then use the last 15 minutes to check your answers the way you will in the real exam.

## Before you start

- **Paper:** the same Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Statistics (4ST1) Paper 1 you started in Part 1, with the line marking where you stopped at 60 minutes. The mark scheme stays closed until next session.
- **Timer:** 45 minutes for the paper, then 15 minutes for checking.
- **Calculator** (scientific, normal mode), black pen, pencil, ruler, protractor, compasses, eraser.
- **Conditions:** no notes, no phone, quiet desk. Do not look back at notes from the last session.

## Exam format reminder

- Paper 1 and Paper 2: **1 hour 45 minutes each**, **50% each**, calculator allowed, all questions compulsory, written on the question paper.
- Questions are a mixture of short items and longer structured questions on a single context. Later questions are usually longer and more demanding, so the last 45 minutes often carries a large share of the marks.
- Pace: about 1 minute per mark (check the total on the cover). The final 45 minutes should cover the remaining ~43% of the marks.

## How to do it

1. **0–1 min** – Re-read your note from Part 1: which question you reached and any starred questions.
2. **1–45 min** – Continue from where you stopped. Keep to your minutes-per-mark rate.
 - If you reach the end with time left, go back to starred questions.
 - For long questions, read the whole question first: later parts often tell you what earlier answers should look like (e.g. "Show that the mean is 22.4").
3. **At 45 minutes** – Stop writing new answers. Write "105 min" under your last line. This is the end of the real exam time.
4. **45–60 min – Checking (outside exam time, but practise the habit):**
 - Reread every question: did you answer what was asked (units, accuracy, "give a reason", "in context")?
 - Check every calculation quickly by a second method or estimate (e.g. is the mean between the smallest and largest values? Is rs between −1 and 1? Do pie chart angles add to 360°?).
 - Check graphs: labels, scales, plotting positions, lines through the mean point.
 - Complete any starred questions you skipped, in a different colour so you can see what you did after time.
5. Write a quick self-estimate of your score out of the total; you will compare it with the real mark next session.

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Long context questions** (10–15 marks) often run: calculate → draw → interpret → evaluate. Do not skip the interpretation parts — they are often the easiest marks.
- **"Show that" parts:** write the calculation in full and give the answer to more figures than the value shown.
- **Using a given value:** if you can't do part (a), use the value given in part (b) or a sensible value and carry on — later marks are still available.
- **Comparisons:** two statements, one about location (mean or median), one about spread (standard deviation, IQR or range), each with figures and context.
- **Interpreting coefficients:** say the strength and direction, then put it into words for this context.
- **Reliability comments:** sample size (larger is more reliable), interpolation vs extrapolation, strength of correlation, whether the sample is random/representative.
- **Probability:** show the multiplication along branches and the addition of paths; give exact fractions or decimals, not ratios.
- **Don't leave blanks:** a method attempt may earn M1 even if you can't finish.

## Quick-fire revision

- Data types: qualitative/quantitative; discrete/continuous; primary/secondary.
- Sampling: random (sampling frame + random numbers), stratified (proportional), systematic (every kth), quota (not random).
- Comparative pie charts: r₂ = r₁ × √(n₂ ÷ n₁).
- Histogram: area = frequency; frequency = frequency density × width.
- Positive skew: Q₃ − Q₂ > Q₂ − Q₁; mean > median > mode.
- Line of best fit through (x̄, ȳ); y = a + bx; interpret a (value when x = 0) and b (change in y per unit of x).
- Spearman's: rank both the same way; Σd²; rs = 1 − 6Σd² ÷ (n(n² − 1)).
- 4-point moving average for quarterly data, plotted halfway between the 2nd and 3rd quarters it covers.
- Crude rate = events ÷ population × 1000; standardised rate = Σ(group rate × standard %) ÷ 100.
- Weighted index = Σ(index × weight) ÷ Σweights.
- Expected frequency = probability × number of trials.
