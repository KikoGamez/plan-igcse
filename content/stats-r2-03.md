# Mark with the mark scheme

> **Goal:** Mark your timed Paper 1 strictly with the official mark scheme, find out exactly why each mark was lost, and record every mistake in your error log so you can fix it before the mock.

## Before you start

- Your completed **Paper 1** from the last two sessions.
- The official **mark scheme** for the same series (Pearson Qualifications website). If available, also download the **examiner's report** for that series — it explains common errors question by question.
- A **red or green pen** (a different colour from your answers).
- Your **error log** (a notebook page or spreadsheet with columns: Question | Topic | Marks lost | Type of error | What I should have done | Fixed?).
- Calculator and ruler (to check plotted points and lines).

## Exam format reminder

- 4ST1 has two papers, each **1 hour 45 minutes**, each **50%** of the grade. Calculator allowed. All questions compulsory.
- Mark schemes use:
 - **M** – method mark: for a correct method, even with a wrong final answer.
 - **A** – accuracy mark: for a correct answer, only if the related M mark has been earned.
 - **B** – independent mark: for a correct statement or value, regardless of method.
 - **ft** – follow through: a later mark can be earned using your own wrong earlier answer, if the method is correct.
 - **oe** – or equivalent (other correct forms); **cao** – correct answer only; **awrt** – answer which rounds to; ranges such as "34 to 35" for graph readings.

## How to do it

1. **0–5 min** – Read the general marking guidance at the start of the mark scheme so you know how M, A, B and ft marks work.
2. **5–35 min** – Mark question by question.
 - Award marks exactly as the mark scheme says. Be strict: if the scheme wants context and you didn't give it, no mark.
 - For graphs, check plotted points against the scheme's tolerance (often ±½ small square) and that the line passes through the required point.
 - For answers outside the time limit (written in a different colour), mark them separately — do not include them in your timed score.
 - Write the marks for each part in the margin, then total each page.
3. **35–50 min** – Error log. For every part where you dropped a mark, write one line in the log (see checklist below). Read the examiner's report for those questions if you have it.
4. **50–55 min** – Calculate your percentage. Compare with your self-estimate from last session. Find the boundary for a grade on the grade boundaries document for that series (Pearson publishes them) to see roughly where you are.
5. **55–60 min** – Choose your **three** most costly topics (most marks lost). These are your targets for session 4 (*Weak topics and formulae*) and later revision.

## Exam technique for this paper

What examiners' reports for this qualification repeatedly comment on:
- **Context missing** in interpretations and comparisons — this is the single most common way to lose easy marks.
- **Comparisons with no figures**, or comparing two measures of location without a measure of spread.
- **Rounding too early**, especially in standard deviation, standardised scores and Spearman's.
- **Plotting errors**: cumulative frequency at midpoints, moving averages in the wrong place, line of best fit not through the mean point.
- **Vague sampling descriptions**: "pick people randomly" instead of using a sampling frame and random numbers.
- **Confusing correlation with causation** in explanation questions.
- **Probability**: adding instead of multiplying on tree diagrams; forgetting "without replacement".

## Checklist while marking

For each lost mark, classify the error type:

| Code | Error type | Example |
|---|---|---|
| K | Knowledge – didn't know the fact or method | didn't know the standardised rate method |
| R | Reading – misread the question | used 3-point instead of 4-point moving averages |
| C | Calculation slip | squared d wrongly in Spearman's |
| W | Working not shown | right answer, no method, lost M marks |
| X | Context/communication missing | "positive correlation" with no context |
| T | Time – not reached or rushed | last question blank |

Error log row example: *Q7(c) | Time series | 2 | R | Plotted 4-point MAs at Q2 instead of between Q2 and Q3 | ☐*

Then:
- Count marks lost by type. If most are **K**, you need content revision. If most are **X** or **W**, it's exam technique — easier to fix fast.
- Redo every question where you lost 2 or more marks, **without** looking at the scheme, then check again. Tick "Fixed?" only when you get it fully right.

## Quick-fire revision

- Context in every interpretation: name the variables and the people/objects in the question.
- Comparison = location + spread, each with values and context.
- Keep full calculator values until the final answer; 3 significant figures unless told otherwise.
- Spearman's rs must be between −1 and +1; standardised scores are usually between −3 and +3.
- Moving averages: plotted at the centre of their period; trend line through the moving averages.
- Seasonal variation = actual − trend.
- Line of best fit passes through (x̄, ȳ).
- Outlier test: 1.5 × IQR beyond the quartiles; state the conclusion.
- Random sample: number the sampling frame, generate random numbers, ignore repeats.
- Chain base index below 100 = a fall since the previous year; above 100 = a rise.
