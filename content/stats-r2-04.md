# Weak topics and formulae

> **Goal:** Fix the three calculation topics that most often cost marks — Spearman's rank, index numbers and moving averages — and make sure every formula in the course is memorised before the mock.

## Before you start

- Your **error log** from session 3 (*Mark with the mark scheme*) and the marked Paper 1.
- Topic pages: *Correlation and regression*, *Index numbers and rates*, *Time series*.
- For extra questions: a **Paper 2** from a series you have not done (e.g. June 2024 or June 2025) and its mark scheme — you will only use selected questions on these three topics, not the whole paper.
- Calculator, pencil, ruler, graph paper, blank flashcards.

## Exam format reminder

- Two papers, **1 h 45 min each**, **50% each**, calculator allowed; every topic can appear on either paper.
- Spearman's rank, index numbers and time series are usually tested as structured questions of roughly 6–12 marks with a calculation followed by interpretation in context. Pace: about 1 minute per mark.

## How to do it

1. **0–10 min – Formula test.** On a blank sheet, write every formula from the quick-fire list below from memory. Check, and make a flashcard for every one you got wrong or hesitated on.
2. **10–25 min – Spearman's rank drill.** Do this without notes, then check:
 Judge A ranks eight dishes 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8; Judge B ranks them 2, 1, 4, 3, 5, 8, 6, 7. Calculate rs and interpret it.
 *Answer:* d = −1, 1, −1, 1, 0, −2, 1, 1; Σd² = 10; rs = 1 − 60 ÷ (8 × 63) = 1 − 60/504 = **0.881**: strong positive agreement between the judges.
 Then find one Spearman's question in your Paper 2 and do it under timed conditions.
3. **25–40 min – Index numbers drill.**
 (a) A meal cost €12.50 in 2020 (base year) and €15.00 in 2025. Find the 2025 index. *Answer: 15.00 ÷ 12.50 × 100 = **120***
 (b) It cost €14.00 in 2023 and €14.70 in 2024. Find the chain base index for 2024 and interpret it. *Answer: **105**: the price rose by 5% from 2023 to 2024.*
 (c) Chain indices 104, 102, 97: overall change? *Answer: 1.04 × 1.02 × 0.97 = 1.029 → about **2.9% increase***
 Then do a standardised rate question from your Paper 2 or the topic page.
4. **40–55 min – Moving averages drill.**
 Quarterly data: 30, 44, 52, 26, 34, 48, 56, 30. Calculate the 4-point moving averages and say where to plot them.
 *Answer:* **38, 39, 40, 41, 42**, plotted halfway between the 2nd and 3rd quarter of each group of four (the first between Q2 and Q3 of year 1). Trend: increasing by about 1 per quarter.
 Plot the data and the moving averages on graph paper, draw a trend line, find the seasonal variation for the first Quarter 3 (the moving averages either side of it are 38 and 39, so the trend value there is 38.5: 52 − 38.5 = 13.5).
5. **55–60 min** – Update your error log: tick off anything now fixed, add anything new.

## Exam technique for this paper

- **Spearman's:** set out a table with columns: rank 1, rank 2, d, d². Rank both variables the same way (1 = highest for both). Write the substitution in full: 1 − (6 × Σd²) ÷ (n(n² − 1)). Interpret: strength + direction + context. If asked to compare with another coefficient, comment on which shows stronger correlation.
- **Index numbers:** always state the base year when interpreting. A chain index of 98 means a 2% **fall** since the previous year. A fall in chain index from 110 to 105 is still a rise in price.
- **Standardised rates:** use the group rates, not group populations; explain "takes account of different age structures".
- **Moving averages:** match the number of points to the cycle; plot at the middle of each period; draw the trend line through the moving averages only; prediction = trend value + mean seasonal variation; comment on reliability (extrapolation).
- In all three topics, interpretation marks need **context**.

## Checklist while marking

- Spearman's: correct ranks? Σd² correct? Answer between −1 and 1? Interpretation in context?
- Index numbers: right way round (current ÷ base)? × 100? Base year stated in interpretation?
- Moving averages: right number of points? Right plotting positions? Seasonal variation sign (actual − trend)?
- Record each remaining error in your error log with the correct method written in full, and schedule it for another attempt in a later session.

## Quick-fire revision

- Stratified sample size = (group ÷ population) × sample size.
- Mean = Σfx ÷ Σf; standard deviation σ = √(Σx² ÷ n − x̄²) or √(Σfx² ÷ Σf − (Σfx ÷ Σf)²).
- Quartile positions (list): (n + 1)/4, (n + 1)/2, 3(n + 1)/4; from a cumulative frequency graph: n/4, n/2, 3n/4.
- IQR = Q₃ − Q₁; outliers beyond Q₁ − 1.5 × IQR and Q₃ + 1.5 × IQR.
- Frequency density = frequency ÷ class width.
- Standardised score = (value − mean) ÷ standard deviation.
- Comparative pie chart radius: r₂ = r₁ × √(n₂ ÷ n₁).
- Spearman's: rs = 1 − 6Σd² ÷ (n(n² − 1)).
- Line of best fit y = a + bx, through (x̄, ȳ).
- Simple index = (value ÷ base-year value) × 100; chain base index = (value ÷ previous year's value) × 100.
- Weighted index = Σ(index × weight) ÷ Σweights.
- Crude rate = (events ÷ population) × 1000; standardised rate = Σ(group rate × standard population %) ÷ 100.
- Seasonal variation = actual − trend; prediction = trend + mean seasonal variation.
- P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B); independent: P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B); P(A | B) = P(A ∩ B) ÷ P(B).
